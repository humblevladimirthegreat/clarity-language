# Predication (classification and identity)
<a id="predication"></a>

English *is* does two jobs that look alike. You can say someone **is a kind or role** (*Uzozen is a dog*): they belong in that category. You can also say two names pick out **the same one** (*Olazun is Uzozen*): one person under two labels, not a category. Agebon uses a different word pattern for each.

## Beginner {#beginner}

### Property (*is ADJ*)
<a id="classification-property"></a>

You already write a property on `/ɡ/` after the noun (*a blue dog*). When you say someone *is* that quality (*Uzozen is challenging*), you use the same pair with no verb. You do not add a separate *is*; the name plus the `/ɡ/` word already make the sentence. The `/ɡ/` word is the piece English would put after *is*.

> `zuzozen gamahum.`
>
> z-Uzozen | g-challenge
>
> "Uzozen is challenging."

A `/ɡ/` word after the verb does not describe the subject the way *tired* does in English *arrived tired*. Give the state its own sentence instead (`zolazun godamul. zalamur velurul.`).

The same string is also the noun plus its adjective: `zuzozen gamahum` is both the full sentence *Uzozen is challenging* and the phrase *challenging Uzozen*.

To say someone belongs to a country or tradition (*Japanese*), use the **-m** ending, not the name ending **-n**. **-n** names the country itself (*Japan*), and **-m** says you belong to it.

> `zuzozen gazabanam.`
>
> z-Uzozen | g-japanese
>
> "Uzozen is Japanese."

### Kind / role {#classification}

To say someone **is a kind of thing** or **has a role** (*Uzozen is a dog*), put the kind word on `/ɡ/` right after the noun. It is the same pattern as a quality, again with no word for *is*: the `/ɡ/` word is what English puts after *is a*.

This use is called **classification**: it puts someone in a category rather than describing a quality.

> `zuzozen gudozol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | g-dog
>
> "Uzozen is a dog."

**Compare with:** *A dog walks* uses a noun plus a verb (`zudozol vowurel`). When the subject **is** that kind, keep the kind on `/ɡ/`.

### In the middle of an event {#in-progress}

Some roots name an event (*walk*, *fight*, *sing*). On `/ɡ/`, such a root says the noun is **in the middle of** that event, as the one doing it: English *walking*. The ending picks the sense as always, so **-l** is the everyday event and **-m** its abstract sense (`gavuvum`, *struggling*).

> `zuzozen gowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | g-walk
>
> "Uzozen is walking."

After a noun it works like any adjective. The event is under way at the time the sentence talks about, so the dog below is walking while Uzozen sees it.

> `zuzozen dudozol gowurel vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [d-dog | g-walk] | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sees a walking dog."

Only a sense that is an event reads this way. A thing root stays a kind (`gudozol`, *is a dog*), even when English has a verb from the same picture.

**Compare with:** the verb in `zuzozen vowurel.` reports the walking without saying it is under way.

### Existence (a lone noun) {#existence}

To say that something **exists** or is here (English *there is …*), write the noun alone, with no verb and no `/ɡ/` word after it.

> `zudozol.`
>
> z-dog
>
> "There is a dog."

The same holds with a `/ɡ/` word after the noun, when the noun is new: a common noun, or one of a name on **-ln**, but not a name on **-n** or a resumed noun on **-r**. The pair introduces the thing: *there is a G Z*.

> `zudozol gelazom.`
>
> z-dog | g-big
>
> "There is a big dog."

With a name or a resumed noun, the listener already knows the thing, so the `/ɡ/` word is a [property](#classification-property): `zuzozen gamahum.` is *Uzozen is challenging*, and `zudozor gelazom.` is *The dog is big*.

An object (`/d/`) needs a verb, so a noun plus a `/d/` word with no verb is not a sentence.

**Compare with:** `zudozol vowurel.` says what the dog does. `zuzozen gudozol.` puts Uzozen in a kind.

### Identity (**SAME**)
<a id="identity"></a>
<a id="same"></a>

When two names pick out **the same person or thing** (*Olazun is Uzozen*), you are not putting Olazun in a kind. You are saying the two labels match: they are one individual.

Write the special adjective `gorol` after the first name, then an extra noun (`/b/`) for the other label. Keep `gorol` and that `/b/` word **next to each other**. This pair states **identity**. The adjective’s English name is **`SAME`**; its root is **`oro`**. (cue: 🪙 *coin*: two faces, one substance)

> `zolazun gorol bluzozen.`
>
> z-Olazun | [g-SAME | bl-Uzozen]
>
> "Olazun is Uzozen." (one person, two names)

> `zagayul gorol bluzozen.`
>
> z-guard | [g-SAME | bl-Uzozen]
>
> "The guard is Uzozen."

The second label is a `/b/` whatever it is: a common noun or a pronoun works as well as a name.

> `zagayul gorol blobayel.`
>
> z-guard | [g-SAME | bl-police]
>
> "The guard is the police officer."

> `zagayul gorol bluwon.`
>
> z-guard | [g-SAME | bl-speaker]
>
> "The guard is me."

With **no** `/b/` after it, `gorol` points back to the one already mentioned: *the same one again*. Put `gorol` after the thing.

> `zolazun danal gorol varayel.`
>
> z-Olazun | [d-book | g-SAME] | v-write
>
> "Olazun is writing the same book." (the book already mentioned)

**Compare with:** *Uzozen is a dog* uses [kind / role](#classification) (`gudozol`). Use **`SAME`** when two labels name one person.

## Intermediate {#intermediate}

### Classification packaging {#classification-packaging}

You already put a kind on `/ɡ/` after the name (*Uzozen is a dog*). The extra choice here is how firmly the kind holds: hedge it, say it holds at every time, or deny it. The kind word stays on `/ɡ/`. Add a hedge on `/w/` before it (*sort of*), **`hual`** after the pair (*always*), or **`gul`** after it (*not*).

> `zuzozen gudozol gul.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [g-dog | g-not]
>
> "Uzozen is not a dog."

<!-- cheat-sheet: word-shape-clause -->
| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| `/w/` hedge before the classifying `/ɡ/` | soft / hedged class | *Uzozen is something of a dog* / *a dog, sort of* |
| `zuzozen gudozol hual` | characterizing / habitual | *Uzozen is always a dog* |
| `zuzozen gudozol gul` | negation | *Uzozen is not a dog* |

> `zuzozen woyurem gudozol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [w-degree | g-dog]
>
> "Uzozen is something of a dog."

**Related form:** habitual *always* uses [**hual**](restrictors.md).

**Related form:** *not a dog* uses [negation **u**](joins.md#negation-u) (`gul`).

### Commands to be ADJ {#command-property}

A command can ask for a state as well as an act. Put the [command word](speech-moves.md#speech-act-beginner) **`yel`** before a `/ɡ/` word with no verb: the listener is the unstated subject, and the pair reads *be ADJ!*, as a [verbless statement](#classification-property) does.

> `yel gayulem.`
>
> y-command | g-caution
>
> "Be careful!"

The `/ɡ/` word takes the verb's place in the [usual order](speech-moves.md#act-word-order), so it comes first. Name the listener after it to address one person (`yel gayulem zawan`), or use the verb root for the doing (`yel vayulem`, *take care*). **`yul`** turns the same pair into *don’t be ADJ*.

### **SAME** endings

Beginner already used **`gorol`** (*is the same as*). Its other ending finishes the map.

| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| **-l** | stand behind the match (closed) | *is* (the same as) |
| **-m** | leave the match open | *same for our purposes* / *basically* |

<!-- cheat-sheet: word-shape-clause -->
| Agebon | English |
|---------|---------|
| `zolazun gorol bluzozen` | *Olazun is Uzozen* (the same person) |
| `zolazun gorom bluzozen` | *Olazun is basically Uzozen* |
| `zolazun gorol bluzozen gul` | *Olazun is not Uzozen* (not the same person) |
| `zeyol gorol bluzozen` | inside a noun phrase: *the person who is Uzozen* |

**Compare with:** ordinary *coin* as a noun is `zorol`. Identity is **`gorol`** / **`gorom`** plus a following `/b/` label, kept next to each other.

### Label scope {#label-scope}

English *Olazun is angry* can mean one outburst or the kind of person Olazun is. The same word does both jobs, and a single act easily turns into a claim about someone's nature. To say what kind of claim a label is, write the root, then mid-word **`th`**, then a scope vowel, then the ending. The vowel says whether the label covers only this episode, a practiced role, one relationship, or a type that follows the person across scenes. The word keeps its usual slot, so a label on `/ɡ/` is still *is ADJ*, and a verb is still the verb.

> `zolazun gunemuthal.`
>
> z-Olazun | g-angry-th-episode
>
> "Olazun is angry this time."

A label with no scope vowel says nothing about how far it reaches. Use one when that reach matters, the way you would add *this time* or *a teacher* in English.

<!-- cheat-sheet: word-shape-clause -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| **`tha`** | this episode: this stretch of doing | *angry this time*, *lied (this once)* | **a** ≈ add (one instance added) |
| **`the`** | a practiced role: the office they inhabit | *a teacher*, *lying as their craft* | **e** ≈ order (a sequence of practice) |
| **`tho`** | this pair: true in one relationship | *a stranger to Uzozen*, *lying, in dealings with Olazun* | **o** ≈ one (that extra one) |
| **`thu`** | a type across scenes: the quality follows them | *an anxious type*, *a teacher type* | **u** ≈ undo (not only here) |

Mid-word **`th`** with a scope vowel goes on `/ɡ/`, `/z/`, `/d/`, `/b/`, `/v/`, and `/h/`, on a content root. It labels a content word, so a [special pronoun](pronouns.md#special-pronouns) with **-n** takes none (its root on **-l** is an ordinary word, `zuwothal` *a microphone, this time*). The [sake](sakes.md#sake-inventory) roots use this spot for their own stances instead. A [role compound](roles.md#one-time-or-a-practice) writes the same vowels after **`w`**, not after **`th`**.

To say **when** that claim counts (*only at school*, *always except when it rains*), put a [restrictor](restrictors.md) on `/w/` immediately before a scoped `/ɡ/` word, with the circumstance words on `/w/` too. The scope vowel says what kind of claim the label is; the restrictor says on which occasions it holds. A restrictor on **`/h/`** limits the whole sentence instead (*walks only when it rains*), not the label.

> `zolazun wanurul wal gwezuduyel.`
>
> z-Olazun | [w-night | w-only-when | g-role-agent-teach]
>
> "Olazun is a teacher only at night."

**Compare with:** **`hual`** / **`wual`** says the claim holds at every time. **`thu`** says you are treating them as that type across scenes, which can still have exceptions. **`wual gwezuduyel`** is the practiced role at every time, and **`ganumuthul`** is the type-claim. Using both together doubles down.

#### This episode (`tha`) {#scope-occasion}

> `zuzozen valamuthal.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-lie-th-episode
>
> "Uzozen lied this once."

#### Practiced role (`the`) {#scope-occupation}

> `zuzozen valamuthel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-lie-th-role
>
> "Uzozen lies as their craft."

On a verb, **`the`** is that act as their practice, not one outing.

**Compare with:** *usually* / *often* is a [restrictor](restrictors.md) (`huam`, `war`). **`thu`** is the type-claim (*a lying type*), which is not a practiced role.

#### This pair (`tho`) {#scope-relative}

**`tho`** says the label holds in one relationship: toward one other party. Put that party in a `bl-` word right after the word, on `/ɡ/`, `/h/`, and `/v/` alike. A verb's recipient stays a plain `/b/`. On a noun, **`tho`** stands alone; to name the party, say the label on `/ɡ/` instead.

> `zuzozen golayothom blolazun.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [g-strangeness-th-pair | bl-Olazun]
>
> "Uzozen is a stranger to Olazun."

> `zuzozen valamuthol blolazun.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [v-lie-th-pair | bl-Olazun]
>
> "With Olazun, Uzozen lies." (in that relationship)

With no `/b/`, the party is unstated: *a stranger (to someone)*.

On a [role compound](roles.md#one-time-or-a-practice) the same vowel sits after **`w`**: `gwozuduyel blolazun` is *is a teacher to Olazun*.

#### Type across scenes (`thu`) {#scope-type}

> `zolazun ganumuthul.`
>
> z-Olazun | g-anxious-th-type
>
> "Olazun is an anxious type."

On a [role compound](roles.md#one-time-or-a-practice) the same vowel sits after **`w`**: `zwuzuduyel` is *a teacher type*.

#### On a role compound {#scope-role}

These are the same vowels, written right after **`w`** on a [role compound](roles.md#role-compounds): there they scope the role, not the act. `gwezuduyel` is *is a teacher*, `gwazuduyel` *is one who teaches (this time)*, and `zwuzuduyel` *a teacher type*.

> `zwuzuduyel varuvel.`
>
> z-type-agent-teach | v-run
>
> "A teacher type runs."

A role word takes no **`th`** seam after its stem: its scope is written once, at the front. Scope on a plain verb stem (`vuduyethal`) scopes the teaching.

**Compare with:** the **`th`** seam scopes the word itself (the act on a verb, the quality on an adjective, the manner on an adverb), and the verb keeps the rest of its clause: `zuzozen valamuthal bolazun.` is *Uzozen lied to Olazun, this once*. A role compound names the person by their part: `zuzozen gwezalamul.` is *Uzozen is a liar*.

#### Stacked scope {#scope-stacks}

The four scope vowels also combine in pairs, the same pairs that [joins](joins.md#beginner) and [hooks](hooks.md#extra-noun) use. A pair that starts with **`u`** reads like a join's *everything but*: **`u`** is every scene, and the second vowel is the one set aside (*every time but this one*, *with everyone but Olazun*, *as themselves, not in the role*). The other pairs read like the extra-noun hooks: **`ao`** *over*, **`ae`** *using*, **`oe`** *toward*. Do not read them as the first vowel's meaning followed by the second's.

<!-- cheat-sheet: word-shape-clause -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| **`thao`** | the **situation**, not the person | *angry, but it's the situation*; *a teacher by circumstance* | **ao** ≈ over: the label hangs over the scene |
| **`thae`** | **using** the role for a purpose | *playing the teacher* | **ae** ≈ using |
| **`thoe`** | **toward** the role | *becoming a teacher* | **oe** ≈ toward |
| **`thua`** | type, **except** this episode | *a teacher type, except this time* | **ua** ≈ everything but: every time but this one |
| **`thuo`** | type, **except** this pair | *a teacher type, except with Olazun* | **uo** ≈ anything but: everyone but this one |
| **`thue`** | as themselves, **not in that role** | *not as a teacher* (off duty) | **ue** ≈ every scene but the role |

> `zolazun gunemuthaol.`
>
> z-Olazun | g-angry-th-situation
>
> "Olazun is angry, but it's the situation, not Olazun."

> `zolazun gunemuthaol blerulal.`
>
> z-Olazun | [g-angry-th-situation | bl-rain]
>
> "Olazun is angry, but it's the rain, not Olazun."

> `zuwon gwaezuduyel.`
>
> z-speaker | g-using-agent-teach
>
> "I'm playing the teacher." (taking on the role for a purpose)

> `zolazun gwoezuduyel.`
>
> z-Olazun | g-toward-role-agent-teach
>
> "Olazun is becoming a teacher."

> `zuzozen valamuthual.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-lie-th-except-episode
>
> "Uzozen is a lying type, except this time."

> `zuzozen gunemuthuol blolazun.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [g-angry-th-except-pair | bl-Olazun]
>
> "Uzozen is an angry type, except with Olazun."

> `zuzozen gwuezuduyel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | g-outside-role-agent-teach
>
> "Uzozen is not a teacher here."

> `zuzozen gwaozuduyel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | g-situation-agent-teach
>
> "Uzozen is a teacher by circumstance." (the situation made them one)

> `zwuazuduyel varuvel.`
>
> z-except-episode-agent-teach | v-run
>
> "A teacher type, except this time, runs."

> `zuzozen gwuozuduyel blolazun.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [g-except-pair-agent-teach | bl-Olazun]
>
> "Uzozen is a teacher type, except with Olazun."

> `zuzozen valamuthael.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-lie-th-using
>
> "Uzozen is using lying as a tool."

> `zolazun valamuthoel.`
>
> z-Olazun | v-lie-th-toward-role
>
> "Olazun is becoming a liar."

> `zuwon vezuyethuel bawan.`
>
> z-speaker | v-tell-th-outside-role | b-listener
>
> "I'm telling you this as myself, not in my role."

**`thuo`** takes its `bl-` party the same way **`tho`** does. On **`thao`**, a `bl-` word is the situation the label belongs to; it claims no cause, and without one the situation is the scene at hand. **`thue`** sets the role aside: it means off duty, never refusing the role or *fake teacher* (to deny the label, put [`gul`](joins.md#negation-u) after it). The same pairs on a role compound write the vowels after **`w`**.
