# Knowing {#knowing}

English *I think* can be a guess, a memory, or a conclusion from clues, and the listener has to work out which. Agebon keeps the ordinary verb and adds a `/th/` stance word that says which: **MAY** for what could be so, or a word for how you know it. So a guess never passes for a report, and a report says what it rests on.

## Beginner {#beginner}

### MAY {#may}

To say something **could be** so without claiming that it is (*Olazun may be leaving*), keep the ordinary verb and add **`oma`** plus an ending as a [stance](clause.md#stance-th) word on `/th/`. This word is called **MAY**. The sentence now says the leaving is possible, not that it happens, and it gives no reason for thinking so. This matters most for painful guesses (a rejection, a blow, a loss), which English *I think* easily turns into facts. Start with **`thomam`**, *may* / *could be*. (cue: 💭 *thought*: a balloon over the scene)

Without the `/th/` word, you are reporting that it happens. As a plain noun the root keeps its own meaning (`zomal` *a thought*); the verb *think* is a different root, `vevemol`.

> `zolazun thomam velurul dahelan.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-MAY | v-departure | d-Ahelan
>
> "Olazun may be leaving Ahelan."

**Compare with:** `zuzozen vevemol.` is *Uzozen thinks*, a report of the act of thinking; it does not mark anything as a guess. For a likelihood in numbers (*70% likely*), use the [stance number](numbers.md#number-as-stance-by-marker) **`th+N`**.

### Hold endings (MAY)
<a id="may-hold"></a>

The ending on MAY says whether you mean to follow up on the *could be*: with **-l** you will find out, with **-m** you leave that unsaid (the default), and with **-r** it is a passing thought you are not pursuing. The three run on the same settled-to-passing scale as [time horizon](sakes.md#settled-to-passing). No ending is better than the others: finding out, leaving it at *may*, and letting it go are all fine choices. With **-n**, the word is an ordinary [name](word-endings.md#proper-name--n).

| Agebon | Use | English | Same root as | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|--------------|-----|
| **`thomal`** | could be, and you are **committed to settling it** | *may, I'll find out* / *let's find out* | `omal` *thought* | 💭: balloon plus a magnifying glass (**-l** the full run) |
| **`thomam`** | could be (default; follow-up unstated) | *may* / *could be* | `omal` *thought* | 💭: balloon (**-m** middle) |
| **`thomar`** | could be, as a **passing thought** you are not pursuing right now | *may, who knows* | `omal` *thought* | 💭: balloon drifting off (**-r** ≈ right now) |

When you are not sure, use **`thomam`**: it says *could be* and nothing about following up.

> `zolazun thomal vanuvul duzozen.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-MAY-find-out | v-punch | d-Uzozen
>
> "Olazun may be punching Uzozen, I'll find out."

In a yes/no question, **`thomal`** says you are asking in order to find out:

> `yol zuzozen vanuvul thomal dolazun.`
>
> y-question | z-Uzozen | v-punch | th-MAY-find-out | d-Olazun
>
> "Might Uzozen be punching Olazun? Let's find out."

> `zahelan thomar vagamul.`
>
> z-Ahelan | th-MAY-who-knows | v-cry
>
> "Ahelan may be crying, who knows."

## Intermediate {#intermediate}

### Evidentiality {#evidentiality}
<a id="evidential"></a>
<a id="content-readings"></a>

English often says how you know a claim: *I saw it*, *I heard*, *I figured*. Agebon keeps the ordinary verb and adds a `/th/` stance word for that, called an **evidential**. It names the **channel** the claim reached you through: sensing it now, memory, a pattern of cases, clues, a proof from premises, a report, or a gut sense. So the same walk can be reported seven ways, and the listener always knows what your claim rests on. Under other role letters the channel roots are ordinary words (`vugol` *to attest*, `debal` *an ear*).

The seven channels come in four kinds. Two are **direct**: you sensed it yourself, now (LIVE) or earlier (MEMORY). Three are **reasoned**: you worked it out from similar cases (PATTERN), from clues about this case (CLUES), or from premises it must follow from (DEDUCED). One is **received**: someone else said or wrote it (REPORTED). One is **internal**: a sense you cannot fully spell out (INTUITION).

Verbs have **no past or future form**. Two channels place the event in time because of how you know it: **LIVE** means the scene is in your senses as you speak, and **MEMORY** means you remember sensing it (so it is past). The other channels do not place the event. When a reasoned or reported event happened at another time, add a when-frame: [*before* / *after* / *while*](dependents.md#time-poles), a clock or date, or a [signed offset](#dated-channel) on the channel. A claim that something **will** happen is a [forecast](#forecast): a channel plus a later offset, so a forecast always says how you know.

> `zuzozen thugom vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | th-LIVE | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen is walking, I can see it."

> `zuzozen thevum vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | th-MEMORY | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen walked, I remember it."

**For *could be* with nothing behind it, use:** [MAY](#may). When English *I think* means you have reasons (you worked it out, you heard it, you sense it), pick the channel for those reasons instead.

| Agebon | Use | English | Same root as | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|--------------|-----|
| **`thugom`** | LIVE: firsthand, sensed now | *live* / *from the scene* | `ugol` *attest* | 👁️‍🗨️: sensing-and-saying while it is in view, in earshot, or on your skin |
| **`thevum`** | MEMORY: firsthand, remembered | *from memory* | `evul` *fishing* | 🎣: you pull something back |
| **`thubem`** | PATTERN: similar cases | *from the pattern* | `ubel` *paw-prints* | 🐾: cases left behind |
| **`theyem`** | CLUES: reasoned from clues about this case | *from the clues* / *apparently* | `eyel` *investigate* | 🕵️: you work the clues |
| **`thanam`** | DEDUCED: it must hold if the premises hold | *it follows* / *by deduction* | `anal` *book* | 📖: a book already holds what it says; a deduction reads out only what the premises hold |
| **`thebam`** | REPORTED: someone else said or wrote it | *so they say* / *according to…* | `ebal` *ear* | 👂: you take in what others pass on |
| **`thabom`** | INTUITION: a gut sense | *my gut says* | `abol` *heart* | ❤️: knowing you cannot fully spell out |

Pick one channel for how you know.

> `zolazun thebam varayel.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-REPORTED | v-write
>
> "Olazun writes, so they say."

> `zolazun thabom vezuvol.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-INTUITION | v-sleep
>
> "Olazun is asleep, my gut says."

LIVE covers every sense, not only sight: a sound in earshot, cold on your skin, an ache in your knee. MEMORY is the same senses, remembered. A screen or a recording extends your senses, so footage is LIVE while you watch it and MEMORY once you have watched it, with the device as the [source](#source). A document is REPORTED: someone wrote it, so it reached you from someone else.

**Compare with:** the act of seeing is the ordinary verb **`vawebol`**; LIVE only says how you know the claim. In the same way, hearing a door is an ordinary `/v/` verb, not REPORTED. A feeling such as fear or hope is an [emotion compose](sakes.md#emotion-compose); INTUITION is a way of knowing, not a feeling about the event. Something a schedule says will happen is someone's [PLAN](intention.md#plan-predict), and a tale you tell for its own sake is [NOTIONAL](#notional).

**Related form:** to deny only the channel (*it happened, but I did not see it*), close the evidential with a [stance join](join-across-roles.md#stance-joins). Two channels you cannot choose between use the same kind of join.

### Evidence strength {#evidence-strength}

The ending on a channel says **how strong the evidence is**. Use **-m** by default. The test is what you could offer if someone asked *how do you know?*:

- **-l**: you could **show** it. You can hand over something checkable apart from you (the document, the clues, the source), and the hearer can weigh it themselves.
- **-m**: you could **describe** it. You can say what you saw, were told, or noticed, and the hearer takes your account on trust.
- **-r**: you could only **gesture** at it. You can point toward it but cannot state it precisely, so a follow-up question would find little behind it.

What counts as showing depends on the channel, so the table names what each channel grades.

Strength grades your evidence, not how sure you feel and not how likely the event is. Likelihood is a separate [stance number](numbers.md#number-as-stance-by-marker) (`th+70`), so a strong pattern can still give middling odds, and a rumor can claim certainty. Strength is also not distance from the event: that is [`th#N`](#hand-depth), so a compiled report that is several hands removed can still be **-l**.

<!-- cheat-sheet: knowing-intending -->
| Channel | Graded on | **-l** show | **-m** (default) describe | **-r** gesture |
|---------|-----------|-------------|---------------------------|----------------|
| LIVE | clarity of view | `thugol` a clear, full view | `thugom` | `thugor` a glimpse |
| MEMORY | vividness of memory | `thevul` a vivid memory | `thevum` | `thevur` a hazy memory |
| PATTERN | number and consistency of cases | `thubel` a well-established pattern | `thubem` | `thuber` a few cases |
| CLUES | strength of clues | `theyel` strong clues | `theyem` | `theyer` a weak clue |
| DEDUCED | how far you checked the steps | `thanal` every step could be shown | `thanam` | `thanar` it seems to follow, steps unchecked |
| REPORTED | reliability of the source | `thebal` a reliable source or an authoritative document | `thebam` | `thebar` a rumor or an unofficial note |
| INTUITION | how well you can state the basis | `thabol` a strong gut sense | `thabom` | `thabor` a faint hunch |

Two rows stretch the word *show*. A live view is gone once the moment passes, so for LIVE and MEMORY **-l** means you could narrate it in fine detail. INTUITION is private, so **-l** means you can say what the sense rests on, not that you can hand it over.

DEDUCED grades how far you checked the steps, not the premises. A step can be airtight and still start from a false premise, so a deduction is only as good as what it was deduced from ([deduction](#deduction)).

> `zolazun thebar velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-REPORTED.weak | v-departure
>
> "Rumor has it Olazun left."

> `zuzozen thevul vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | th-MEMORY.strong | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen walked, I remember it clearly."

### Second-hand and further (`th#N`) {#hand-depth}

English *second-hand*, *third-hand*, *a secondary source* and *a tertiary source* count the hands between the person who was there and you. A channel does not say that. Add a [stance number](numbers.md#number-as-stance-by-marker) with the rank marker after the channel: **`th#N`** (from 2) is *N-th hand*. The channel says what form the claim reached you in, and the number says how far that is from the event. So `thredul` (`th#2`) is one step removed: told by someone who was there, or written by someone who asked them. `threrel` (`th#3`) is two steps removed: told by someone who was told, or compiled from reports. (cue: **`e`** ≈ order, the place in the chain)

> `zolazun thebam threrel velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-REPORTED | th-3rd-hand | v-departure
>
> "I heard third-hand that Olazun left."

> `zolazun thebal bunonal thredul velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | [th-REPORTED.strong | b-newspaper] | th-2nd-hand | v-departure
>
> "Olazun left, according to a newspaper article by someone who interviewed people who were there."

Alone, `th#N` says the claim was passed on to you, without saying in what form. After a channel it adds the form: REPORTED (a retelling, a secondary source, or lore passed down), PATTERN (cases you know only from reports), or CLUES (clues you know only from reports). The number counts the hands on the evidence, never the reasoning steps: a conclusion drawn from your own clues is plain CLUES. First-hand has no number, because that is a channel: `th#N` with LIVE or MEMORY (`thugom`, `thevum`) is not used. The usual number endings apply: **-m** *about N-th hand* (`threrem`), **-r** with a digit *the same depth as before* (`threrer`). Digitless `threr` under a question asks *how many hands?*

**Compare with:** `th_N` names *which* numbered source the claim rests on; `th#N` counts *how many hands* it passed through. Reliability of the one source you heard from is the ending on the channel (`thebal` / `thebar`).

### Source (`/b/` after the channel) {#source}

English *according to Uzozen* and *going by the camera* name **who or what you got the claim from**. Put that noun in `/b/` right after the channel. The channel is its [host](clause.md#extra-nouns), so the `/b/` is the source, not someone who receives anything.

> `zolazun thebam buzozen velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | [th-REPORTED | b-Uzozen] | v-departure
>
> "Olazun left, according to Uzozen."

> `zolazun thugom bogewol vezuvol.`
>
> z-Olazun | [th-LIVE | b-camera] | v-sleep
>
> "Olazun is asleep, going by the camera." (watching the feed now)

What you put in that `/b/` decides what it does. A person or other noun is the source: a speaker or a document on REPORTED, a screen or a recording on LIVE and MEMORY. A time [measure phrase](numbers-applied.md#measure-phrases) is an offset that dates the event instead ([dated channel](#dated-channel)).

**Compare with:** the source is where the claim came to you from; the claim is still yours to pass on. A numbered footnote source is `th_N`.

### Mirative (`themom`) {#mirative}

English *it turns out*, *to my surprise*, and *so … after all* say the news ran **against what you expected**. Put **`themom`** on the clause. This `/th/` word is called **MIRATIVE**. It is not a channel: it says nothing about how you know, so add a channel beside it when you want to say that too.

> `zolazun themom velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-MIRATIVE | v-departure
>
> "Olazun left, it turns out."

The ending says how firm the expectation was that the news overturned. If you do not know, use **-m**.

| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| `themol` | it overturns a firm expectation | *astonishingly*, *I never expected that* | **-l** ≈ the expectation was locked in |
| `themom` | it runs against what you expected (default) | *it turns out*, *surprisingly* | **-m** ≈ the ordinary case |
| `themor` | it runs against a loose expectation | *oh*, *as it happens* | **-r** ≈ a passing guess |

> `zolazun thugom themol vowurel.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-LIVE | th-MIRATIVE.firm | v-walk
>
> "Olazun is walking, I see it, and I never expected that."

The mirative says only that the news was unexpected, not whether it is good or bad. To say how it lands, add a [feeling](sakes.md#emotion-compose): the mirative after **`goyem`** says the stimulus was unexpected.

> `zewal wawothamar goyem themom.`
>
> [z-present | [w-relatedness-met-any-term-INTERNAL-SURGING | g-stimulus]] | th-MIRATIVE
>
> "I'm pleasantly surprised by the present."

**Compare with:** *even* on the including hook ([`wemom al`](hooks.md#hook-w)) singles out the one unexpected member of a set. **`themom`** says the whole claim was unexpected. For *surprisingly* on one adjective (*surprisingly tired*), put the ordinary word **`wemom`** (*amazement*) on `/w/` right before it, the same word that grades the hook in *even*:

> `zuzozen wemom godamul vowurel.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | [w-amazement | g-tired]] | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen walks, surprisingly tired."

### Forecasts (`bral`) {#forecast}

English *will* can make a forecast: a claim that something will happen. Agebon has no *will* word for this. Use a channel and put **`bral`** right after it in `/b/`: a number with a plus sign and no digits, meaning *some time after now*. Because a forecast needs a channel, it always says what it rests on; for a hunch, that channel is INTUITION. The mirror, **`brul`**, is *some time before now*, for a past claim with no measured amount.

> `thubem bral verulal.`
>
> [th-PATTERN | b-later] | v-rain
>
> "It will rain, going by the pattern."

> `zolazun thabor bral velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | [th-INTUITION.weak | b-later] | v-departure
>
> "I have a faint hunch Olazun will leave."

<a id="forecast-question"></a>

In a [question](questions.md#question), the channel asks what the **listener's** answer rests on: *going by the pattern you see, will it rain?* The listener answers with that channel or names a different one. Pick the channel you expect them to have: CLUES (`theyem`) when they would work it out from clues, REPORTED (`thebam`) when they would go by what they heard or read.

> `yol thubem bral verulal.`
>
> y-question | [th-PATTERN | b-later] | v-rain
>
> "Will it rain, going by the pattern you see?"

On a [feeling](sakes.md#emotion-compose) (a sake word with a locus and a motion ending, before **`goyem`**), the channel and offset date the **stimulus**. On a [feeling with no object](sakes.md#feeling-no-object), they date a stimulus you leave unnamed. The feeling itself stays now.

**Compare with:** [PLAN](intention.md#plan-predict) (`thagem`) says what you **intend** to do. Your own intention needs no channel. A forecast is a claim about the world. *Could be* with nothing behind it is [MAY](#may), not a forecast.

### Evidence clause (`barl`) {#evidence-clause}

English *judging by*, *going by*, and *given that* name the **grounds** for a claim: the fact that tells you it holds. The three reasoned channels take grounds: CLUES (`theyem`, from clues), PATTERN (`thubem`, from cases that repeat) and DEDUCED (`thanam`, from premises). Each one takes **`barl`** as its `/b/`. The main sentence stops after `barl`, and the next sentence is the grounds ([dependent clauses](dependents.md#dependent-clauses)). You state the claim, the strength of your grounds, and the grounds themselves in one sentence.

> `zolazun vanuvul theyem barl zuzozen varemul.`
>
> z-Olazun | v-punch | [th-CLUES | b-that-clause] | z-Uzozen | v-scream
>
> "Olazun punched, judging by the fact that Uzozen is screaming."

Grounds are not a cause. With [`thahum barl`](causation.md#only-because), the clause after `barl` is what made the event happen. With a reasoned channel, it is only how you know the event happened: the screaming did not make Olazun punch.

> `zuzozen varemul thahum barl zolazun vanuvul.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-scream | [th-because | b-that-clause] | z-Olazun | v-punch
>
> "Uzozen screams because Olazun punches."

<!-- cheat-sheet: knowing-intending -->
| You say | The clause after `barl` is | Form |
|---------|----------------------------|------|
| what made it happen | the cause | `thahum barl` |
| what has to hold for it to happen | the condition | `thohom barl` / `thebom barl` / `thehem barl` |
| how you know it happened | your grounds | `theyem barl` / `thubem barl` / `thanam barl` |

The ending on the channel grades the grounds ([evidence strength](#evidence-strength)): **-l** for strong clues or a well-established pattern, **-m** by default, **-r** for a weak clue or a few cases. Strong clues still fall short of a proof: when the claim **must** hold if the grounds do, use DEDUCED ([deduction](#deduction)).

> `verulal theyel barl zugalal gegulol.`
>
> v-rain | [th-CLUES.strong | b-that-clause] | [z-cloud | g-gray]
>
> "It is raining, judging by the gray clouds." (strong clues)

For a **forecast**, keep the [offset](#forecast) and put `barl` after it. The pattern is what you go by, and the grounds clause says what you see now.

> `verulal thubel bral barl zugalal gegulol.`
>
> v-rain | [th-PATTERN.strong | [b-later | b-that-clause]] | [z-cloud | g-gray]
>
> "It will rain, going by the well-established pattern, given the gray clouds."

You assert the grounds, the way you assert a cause. If you are not sure of them, say so inside the grounds clause with its own channel:

> `zolazun vanuvul theyem barl zuzozen thugom varemul.`
>
> z-Olazun | v-punch | [th-CLUES | b-that-clause] | z-Uzozen | th-LIVE | v-scream
>
> "Olazun punched, judging by the fact that Uzozen is screaming, which I can hear."

**Compare with:** a noun in `/b/` after any channel is its [source](#source) (`thebam buzozen` *according to Uzozen*), and no other channel takes `barl`. A cause or condition word may not come before the channel. Put the channel first (`theyem thahum barl`) to say both that you inferred it and that it was caused. *Could be* with no grounds is [MAY](#may).

### Live vs memory {#live-vs-memory}
<a id="memory-record"></a>
<a id="memory"></a>
<a id="record-past"></a>
<a id="live"></a>
<a id="reconstructive-catch"></a>

English *I see it* and *I saw it* both report watching; they differ in when you watched. LIVE **`thugom`** is for a scene still in your senses as you speak. MEMORY **`thevum`** is for a scene you remember sensing, so the event is past. Do not put both on one claim. English *I witnessed it* is MEMORY, not LIVE. (cue: memory is fishing: you pull the episode back, and it may not be what you hoped, the way a line can come up with a boot)

> `zuzozen thevum vawebol dunobal.`
>
> z-Uzozen | th-MEMORY | v-see | d-boot
>
> "Uzozen saw a boot, as I remember it."

Footage works the same way. While you watch, it is LIVE with the device as the source; once you have watched it, it is MEMORY, because your memory of the footage can slip just as a memory of the scene can.

> `zuzozen thevum bogewol vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [th-MEMORY | b-camera] | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen walked, as I remember from the camera."

| Agebon | Use | English | Same root as | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|--------------|-----|
| **`thugom`** | concurrent observation, by any sense | *live* / *from the scene* | `ugol` *attest* | 👁️‍🗨️: sensing-and-saying while it is in view |
| **`thevum`** | reconstructive / recalled observation | *I remember* / *from memory* | `evul` *fishing* | 🎣: retrieval from the water |

### Carrying a channel forward {#carry-forward}
<a id="channel-frame-carry"></a>

English tells a story with one *they say* or *I remember* at the start, and every sentence after it rests on the same ground. Agebon does the same. Once you put a channel on a claim, your later claims with no channel keep it: the channel is your **frame**. You name the channel once, then keep talking.

> `zolazun thebam velurul. zolazun varuvel. zolazun vezuyol.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-REPORTED | v-departure . z-Olazun | v-run . z-Olazun | v-sing
>
> "Olazun left, so they say. Olazun ran. Olazun sang." (all three are what you heard)

The frame is **yours**. It covers only your own claims, and someone else who replies never inherits it. Their reply does not end it either: when you go on telling, your frame is still in force.

**Which sentences take the frame:** your statements with no channel of their own, including a statement after a call, an interjection, or an answer word (`yael zolazun varuvel.` *yes, Olazun ran* keeps your frame). A [question](questions.md#question) does not take it, because a channel in a question asks about the listener's grounds ([forecasts](#forecast-question)). A command, request, or prohibition does not take it, because it is not a claim. Neither ends the frame, so your next statement picks it up again.

> `zolazun thebam velurul. yol zolazun varuvel. zolazun vezuyol.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-REPORTED | v-departure . y-question | z-Olazun | v-run . z-Olazun | v-sing
>
> "Olazun left, so they say. Did Olazun run? Olazun sang." (the singing is hearsay too)

**What does not end it:** a new [topic](pronouns.md#topic) or a return to one, the [sentence linkers](dependents.md#sentence-linkers) (*therefore*, *however*, *meanwhile*, *next*, *but*), clause joins, asides, a [MIRATIVE](#mirative), or MAY. A story moves from one person to the next, and a surprise inside it is still part of it.

> `zuzozen thebam vowurel. xolazun zalon vuhohol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | th-REPORTED | v-walk . x-Olazun | z-TOPIC | v-sit
>
> "Uzozen walked, so they say. Now, about Olazun: Olazun sat." (the sitting is hearsay too)

**What ends it:**

- **A new frame.** Any channel replaces it, and so does [NOTIONAL](#notional). From there on, the new one carries.
- **The statement word, written out.** **`yal`** (or **`yam`** / **`yar`**) before a body says you are back in your own voice, with no frame. It is the one place where writing the usually silent [statement word](speech-moves.md#speech-act) changes the meaning. A rhetorical **`yal yol`** is a question, so it does not count.
- **`xuvedam`** *by the way*: a digression leaves what you were telling. To go back, name the channel again.
- **A goodbye**, which ends the conversation.

> `zolazun thebam velurul. yal zolazun vezuvol.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-REPORTED | v-departure . y-statement | z-Olazun | v-sleep
>
> "Olazun left, so they say. Olazun is asleep." (the second claim no longer rests on the report)

A [quote](spans.md#writing) keeps its speaker's frame: nothing carries into it from outside, and your own frame resumes after it.

### Residue and former climate
<a id="residue"></a>
<a id="former-climate"></a>
<a id="episode-standing"></a>

English *has left* and *used to tell* mix two things: when the event happened, and how it stands now. Agebon places events in time with other tools: [*before* / *after* / *while*](dependents.md#time-poles), a clock or date, [LIVE](#live-vs-memory) / [MEMORY](#live-vs-memory), or a [forecast](#forecast). Two `/h/` words keep the books on how it stands. **RESIDUE** says the outcome of an event is still in effect now. **FORMER** says a usual pattern is not one you are claiming for now. Neither says when anything happened, so use them alongside a channel or a when-frame, never as a past or present tense.

**RESIDUE** (`ara`) says the result still counts: an unpaid tab, a door that is still shut. Without it, you make no claim that anything is left over (the event is over and done, or nothing remains from it). As a plain noun, `zaram` is *debt*. (cue: 💰 a debt still on the books)

> `zolazun haram velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | h-RESIDUE | v-departure
>
> "Olazun has left and is still gone."

> `zolazun thevum haram velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-MEMORY | h-RESIDUE | v-departure
>
> "I remember Olazun leaving, and Olazun is still gone."

> `zolazun thugom haram velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-LIVE | h-RESIDUE | v-departure
>
> "Olazun has left and is still gone, I can see it." (what is left over is in view)

**Compare with:** [*after*](dependents.md#time-poles) (`halam bohol`) orders two events. **`haram`** does not say the leaving was earlier; it says the **outcome is still in effect**. [LIVE](#live-vs-memory) is how you know, not what is left over. A result as a property can stay ordinary `/ɡ/` (*the door is shut*) with no residue word.

**FORMER** (`ela`) says a usual pattern held, but you are **not** claiming it holds now: *Uzozen always sings* as a past habit. Without it, [always](restrictors.md) **`hual`** describes the pattern as it stands now. As a plain noun, `zelam` is *emptiness*. (cue: 🪹 the nesting season is over; a former climate, not today's weather)

> `zuzozen hual vezuyol helam.`
>
> z-Uzozen | h-always | v-sing | h-FORMER
>
> "Uzozen always used to sing." (no claim about now)

> `zuzozen hual vezuyol helam thevum.`
>
> z-Uzozen | h-always | v-sing | h-FORMER | th-MEMORY
>
> "Uzozen always used to sing, as I remember."

> `zuzozen hual vezuyol helam thubem bral.`
>
> z-Uzozen | h-always | v-sing | h-FORMER | [th-PATTERN | b-later]
>
> "Going by the pattern, always singing will be something Uzozen used to do."

**Compare with:** PATTERN **`thubem`** says how you know (from repeated cases); it does not say the pattern is over. **`helam`** is not a past tense either: it works with LIVE and in a forecast.

Do not write **`helam`** right before **`hual`**: that spot holds the restrictor's list of occasions (*always except when …*). Put **`hual`** first (bare *always*) and FORMER after the verb, where a channel goes on a [general claim](#universality).

| Agebon | Use | English | Same root as | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|--------------|-----|
| **`haram`** | outcome still in effect | *still counts* / residue | `aram` *debt* | 💰: unpaid balance still on the books |
| **`helam`** | usual pattern not claimed for now | *former climate* | `elam` *emptiness* | 🪹: the nesting season is over |

Put RESIDUE and FORMER on `/h/`, anywhere in the sentence: like an [intention adverb](clause.md#intention-adverbs), each covers the whole clause. Use `/w/` only when the word applies to the one `/ɡ/` adjective right after it. Both always end in **-m**.

### Already, still, not yet, no longer {#phasal}

English *already*, *still*, *not yet*, and *no longer* do not place an event in time. Each compares what is true **now** with a change: has it come, or not? Like RESIDUE, that is about how things stand now, not when something happened, so Agebon uses four `/h/` words, not a tense. (cue: each root pictures the stage the change is at)

| Agebon | Use | English | Same root as | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|--------------|-----|
| **`havom`** | the change has happened | *already* | `avol` *hatch* | 🐣: the chick is already out |
| **`hawum`** | the state goes on | *still* | `awul` *anchor* | ⚓: the ship is held where it was |
| **`helem`** | the change is expected but has not come | *not yet* | `elel` *seedling* | 🌱: not grown yet |
| **`huvum`** | the state has stopped | *no longer* / *not anymore* | `uvul` *wilt* | 🥀: no longer fresh |

> `zuzozen hawum vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | h-still | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen is still walking."

> `zuzozen helem vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | h-not-yet | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen hasn't walked yet."

> `zuzozen huvum vowurel ol bahehal.`
>
> z-Uzozen | h-no-longer | v-walk | [at | b-house]
>
> "Uzozen doesn't walk at the house anymore."

The ending says how settled the current stage is, on the same settled-to-passing scale as [time horizon](sakes.md#settled-to-passing). Use **-m** when it could change back, or when you do not know. Use **-l** when it is not expected to. Use **-r** when it holds only for now: it is likely to change back soon, or, on *not yet*, the change may never come.

<!-- cheat-sheet: knowing-intending -->
| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| `havol` | done, and it will stay done | *already, for good* |
| `hawul` | goes on, with no end in sight | *still, and it will keep on* |
| `helel` | not yet, but it is sure to come | *not yet, but it will* |
| `huvul` | stopped, and it will not come back | *never again* |
| `havor` | done, but likely to come undone | *already, for now* |
| `hawur` | goes on, but about to end | *still, for now* |
| `heler` | not yet, and it may never come | *not yet, maybe never* |
| `huvur` | stopped for now; expected to resume | *paused* / *not for now* |

> `zolazun havol velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | h-already.lasting | v-departure
>
> "Olazun has already left, for good."

> `zuzozen huvur vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | h-no-longer.for-now | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen has stopped walking for now." (expected to start again)

When only one `/ɡ/` adjective is still or no longer so, write the same word under `/w/` immediately before that adjective.

> `zuzozen dagozol wawum guzemol vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [d-cat | [w-still | g-sleepy]] | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sees a cat that is still sleepy."

**Compare with:** RESIDUE **`haram`** says the outcome of a finished event is **still in effect**, not that the act goes on. FORMER **`helam`** retires a usual **pattern**; **`huvum`** says any state or act has stopped.

#### Ever / never {#ever-never}

English *has ever* / *has never* asks whether something happened at least once, at no particular time. Use *already* **`havom`** with *sometimes* **`har`** ([restrictors](restrictors.md)): the change has come, on some unnamed occasion. For *has never*, use bare **`hal`** (*never*).

> `zuzozen havom vazuvel har.`
>
> z-Uzozen | h-already | v-swim | h-sometimes
>
> "Uzozen has swum at some point."

> `zuzozen vazuvel hal.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-swim | h-never
>
> "Uzozen has never swum."

### Notional (as-if framing)
<a id="notional"></a>
<a id="notional-mood"></a>

To talk about a scene that is only imagined (*imagine …*, *as if …*, *for the sake of argument*), keep the ordinary verb and add **`eyu`** plus an ending on `/th/`. This word is called **NOTIONAL**. The sentence now describes an imagined scene and makes no claim that it happens, so a pretend walk still uses the ordinary verb *walk*. (cue: 🎭 *theater*: a stage mask)

> `zuzozen theyum vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | th-NOTIONAL | v-walk
>
> "Imagine Uzozen walking."

**For *could be*, use:** [MAY](#may).

NOTIONAL [carries forward](#carry-forward) like a channel, so a story you make up, or a supposition you reason through, runs over several sentences. *Once upon a time* is NOTIONAL on the first sentence.

> `zuzozen theyum vowurel. zuzozen vuhohol. zuzozen vezuyol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | th-NOTIONAL | v-walk . z-Uzozen | v-sit . z-Uzozen | v-sing
>
> "Imagine Uzozen walking. Uzozen sits down. Uzozen sings."

A legend that people pass on as true is not NOTIONAL: it is REPORTED, often with a [hand count](#hand-depth).

**Compare with:** *walks like a duck* is a real walk; only its manner matches the model ([simile](relations.md#similative) `hewom`). *Tells on behalf of Olazun* is a real telling, and Uzozen is still the teller ([proxy](relations.md#proxy) `hanom`).

### Hold endings (NOTIONAL)
<a id="notional-hold"></a>

The ending on NOTIONAL says what the imagining is for: with **-l** you rehearse a scene to prepare for doing it, with **-m** you picture it with no further purpose (the default), and with **-r** you toy with a *what if* for the moment. The three run on the same settled-to-passing scale as [time horizon](sakes.md#settled-to-passing), like the endings on [PLAN](intention.md#plan-predict). With **-n**, the word is an ordinary [name](word-endings.md#proper-name--n).

| Agebon | Use | English | Same root as | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|--------------|-----|
| **`theyul`** | rehearse a possible scene to prepare for action | *let’s practice…* / *try acting as if…* | `eyul` *theater* | 🎭: practice a scene |
| **`theyum`** | imagine a scene without a further purpose (default) | *imagine…* / *as if…* | `eyul` *theater* | 🎭: picture a scene |
| **`theyur`** | toy with a possibility or assumption, for now | *suppose…* / *what if…?* | `eyul` *theater* | 🎭: try on a mask for a moment |

When you are not sure, use **`theyum`**.

> `zahelan theyul vezuyol.`
>
> z-Ahelan | th-NOTIONAL-rehearse | v-sing
>
> "Ahelan practices singing." (an imagined run-through, to prepare)

> `zuzozen theyur varayel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | th-NOTIONAL-suppose | v-write
>
> "Suppose Uzozen writes."

### Deduction (`thanam`) {#deduction}
<a id="deduced"></a>

English *it follows that*, *P, so Q* and *by deduction* claim more than good evidence: if the premises hold, the conclusion **must** hold. Put DEDUCED (**`thanam`**) on the conclusion and the premises after **`barl`**, as in any [evidence clause](#evidence-clause). Join several premises with a [clause join](joins.md#clause-joins); a join after a stand-in stays in the dependent, so every premise is grounds. You assert the premises and the conclusion.

> `zuzozen vuduyel thanam barl zual gaxuduyethel vuduyel xam zuzozen gaxuduyethel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-teach | [th-DEDUCED | b-that-clause] | [[z-everything | g-agent-x-teach-th-role] | v-teach | x-and.open | z-Uzozen | g-agent-x-teach-th-role]
>
> "Uzozen teaches, since every teacher teaches and Uzozen is a teacher."

To claim only that the conclusion follows, put a [pole](causation.md#only-because) between the channel and **`barl`**. The pole is the ordinary *if*: the premises are enough for the conclusion, and you do not claim them. DEDUCED says you know that link by deduction. Now you assert nothing but the link.

> `zuzozen vuduyel thanam thohom barl zual gaxuduyethel vuduyel xam zuzozen gaxuduyethel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-teach | th-DEDUCED | [th-if | b-that-clause] | [[z-everything | g-agent-x-teach-th-role] | v-teach | x-and.open | z-Uzozen | g-agent-x-teach-th-role]
>
> "If every teacher teaches and Uzozen is a teacher, it follows that Uzozen teaches."

<!-- cheat-sheet: knowing-intending -->
| You claim | Form | You assert |
|-----------|------|------------|
| *P, so Q* | Q `thanam barl` P | P and Q |
| *Q follows from P* | Q `thanam thohom barl` P | only the link |
| *P and Q come to the same thing* | Q `thanam thehem barl` P | only the link, both ways |
| *P rules out Q* | Q denied with `vul`, then `thanam thohom barl` P | only that P and Q cannot both hold |

To say a conclusion **does not** follow (*that doesn't follow*, *a non sequitur*), deny the channel and the pole together with a [stance join before `barl`](join-across-roles.md#stance-join-before-barl). You deny the link and claim nothing about P or Q: either may still be true.

The ending grades how far you checked the steps ([evidence strength](#evidence-strength)): **`thanal`** when you could write out every step, **`thanam`** when you could name the premises and the rule, **`thanar`** when it only seems to follow.

When the premises come first, open the conclusion with the linker **`xahel`** *it follows that* ([sentence linkers](dependents.md#sentence-linkers)). It reaches back to the one sentence before it, so join every premise into that sentence.

> `zual gaxuduyethel vuduyel xam zuzozen gaxuduyethel. xahel zuzozen vuduyel.`
>
> [[z-everything | g-agent-x-teach-th-role] | v-teach | x-and.open | z-Uzozen | g-agent-x-teach-th-role] . x-it-follows | z-Uzozen | v-teach
>
> "Every teacher teaches, and Uzozen is a teacher. It follows that Uzozen teaches."

A linker is not a channel, so the conclusion keeps your [frame](#carry-forward): a deduction from a report is still REPORTED. To reason from a premise you do not grant, suppose it with [NOTIONAL](#notional) **`theyur`**. The frame carries, so every step after it stays supposed. To keep what you found, state the link with its own channel, which ends the supposition:

> `zuzozen theyur gaxuduyethel. xahel zuzozen vuduyel. zuzozen vuduyel thanam thohom barl zuzozen gaxuduyethel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | th-NOTIONAL-suppose | g-agent-x-teach-th-role . x-it-follows | z-Uzozen | v-teach . z-Uzozen | v-teach | th-DEDUCED | [th-if | b-that-clause] | [z-Uzozen | g-agent-x-teach-th-role]
>
> "Suppose Uzozen is a teacher. Then Uzozen teaches. So if Uzozen is a teacher, it follows that Uzozen teaches."

**Compare with:** strong clues (`theyel`) make a claim likely, but it can still fail with every clue true. A deduction cannot fail unless a premise does. *Because* (`thahum`) says what made it happen; a deduction says only what must be true.

### Channels as comparison bars {#channel-bars}

English *sleepier than usual* and *bigger than it looks* compare against an expectation. Put the channel right before the rank word (**`zel`** / **`zuel`** / **`zoel`**) as the comparison's [bar](comparatives.md#bars). The bar is then **what that channel led you to expect**, not how you know the claim. After the comparison, the same word is the claim's channel again.

> `zuzozen thubem zel guzemol.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | th-PATTERN | z-rank/more | g-sleepy]
>
> "Uzozen is sleepier than usual."

> `zuzozen zel guzemol thubem.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-rank/more | g-sleepy] | th-PATTERN
>
> "Uzozen is the sleepiest, going by the pattern."

| Channel as the bar | Ranks against | English |
|--------------------|---------------|---------|
| LIVE `thugom` | how it looks | *than it looks* |
| MEMORY `thevum` | how you remember it | *than I remember* |
| PATTERN `thubem` | the trail of cases | *than usual* |
| CLUES `theyem` | what was worked out | *than calculated* |
| REPORTED `thebam` | what was said or written | *than advertised* / *than the stories say* |
| INTUITION `thabom` | your gut expectation | *than I expected* |
| FORMER `helam` | how it used to be | *than it used to be* |
| NOTIONAL `theyum` | the imagined case | *than imagined* |

MAY and MIRATIVE give no expected value to compare against, so neither can be a bar. *Than scheduled* ranks against a plan: that is the [PLAN bar](comparatives.md#bars) (`thagem`).

### Channels after `uem` {#channel-frame}

English *contrary to what I was told* and *against my gut* say the event went against what a source led you to expect. Put the channel after the hook **`uem`** (*contrary to*), the same way as a ban or a plan ([contrary to a stance](sakes.md#contrary-to-stance)). The pair names what the event goes **against**; the channel then says nothing about how you know the claim.

> `zuzozen vowurel uem thebam.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [contrary-to | th-REPORTED]
>
> "Uzozen walks, contrary to what I was told."

> `zuzozen vowurel uem thabom.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [contrary-to | th-INTUITION]
>
> "Uzozen walks, against my gut."

| After **`uem`** | Goes against | English |
|-----------------|--------------|---------|
| LIVE `thugom` | how it looks | *contrary to appearances* |
| MEMORY `thevum` | how you remember it | *contrary to what I remember* |
| PATTERN `thubem` | the trail of cases | *contrary to habit* / *unusually* |
| CLUES `theyem` | what was worked out | *contrary to my calculations* |
| REPORTED `thebam` | what was said or written | *contrary to what I was told* / *contrary to legend* |
| INTUITION `thabom` | your gut expectation | *against my gut* |

*Off schedule* goes against a plan: **`uem thagem`** ([contrary to a stance](sakes.md#contrary-to-stance)). MIRATIVE already says the claim ran against what you expected, so it does not follow **`uem`**. MAY, NOTIONAL, RESIDUE, and FORMER do not either: none of them is something the event could go against.

**Compare with:** `zuzozen themom vowurel.` says the walking surprised you. `zuzozen vowurel uem thabom.` says your gut expected otherwise, whether or not the news surprised you.

### Whose view (holder) {#holder}
<a id="holder-seam"></a>

English *Uzozen thinks…*, *she's upset*, and *he doubts it* report what is in someone else's mind as if you could see it. In Agebon, you name whose view a sentence gives (the **holder**) by joining their name onto a channel, [MAY](#may), or [NOTIONAL](#notional). Keep that word's own last letter (**-l**, **-m**, or **-r**) and write the name straight after it: `theyem` plus `uzozen` is `theyemuzozen`. The joining letter is the **seam**. This is the only way to name a holder, so you can never report someone's view without saying how you know it. (cue: the name hangs off the way you know it)

> `zolazun theyemuzozen velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-CLUES-Uzozen | v-departure
>
> "I gather Uzozen thinks Olazun left."

> `zolazun thebamuzozen velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-REPORTED-Uzozen | v-departure
>
> "I hear Uzozen thinks Olazun left."

> `zolazun thomamuzozen velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-MAY-Uzozen | v-departure
>
> "Maybe Uzozen thinks Olazun left."

> `zolazun theyumuzozen velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-NOTIONAL-Uzozen | v-departure
>
> "Imagine it as Uzozen sees it: Olazun left."

The seam letter keeps its meaning: [evidence strength](#evidence-strength) on a channel, the [hold](#may-hold) on MAY or NOTIONAL. The name keeps its own **-n** at the end. **-n** is never a seam.

> `zolazun theyeluzozen velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-CLUES.strong-Uzozen | v-departure
>
> "I have strong clues that Uzozen thinks Olazun left."

> `zolazun thebaruzozen velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-REPORTED.weak-Uzozen | v-departure
>
> "Rumor has it Uzozen thinks Olazun left."

A holder [carries forward](#carry-forward) like a channel. After it, your bare claims go on giving that person's view, until a new frame, so you can tell a stretch of the story as they see it.

> `zolazun theyemuzozen velurul. zolazun vezuvol.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-CLUES-Uzozen | v-departure . z-Olazun | v-sleep
>
> "I gather Uzozen thinks Olazun left. Olazun is asleep." (the sleeping is Uzozen's view too)

The holder word ends the way a noun for that person would. **-n** is a name. **-r** [resumes](pronouns.md#resume-r) someone already named, which is English *he* / *she* / *they*, and a [role pointer](pronouns.md#role-pointers) on **-r** works too (`theyemaxar`, *I gather the latest doer thinks*). Add [associative **-x**](plurality.md#associative) after the ending for the person and their associates. A kind of person on **-lx** is some people of that kind.

> `zuzozen vowurel. zolazun theyemuzozer velurul.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk . z-Olazun | th-CLUES-←Uzozen | v-departure
>
> "Uzozen walks. I gather they think Olazun left."

> `zuzozen vowurel. zolazun theyemaxar velurul.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk . z-Olazun | th-CLUES-←agent.same | v-departure
>
> "Uzozen walks. I gather they think Olazun left." (the latest doer: Uzozen)

> `zolazun theyemuzozenx velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-CLUES-Uzozen-x | v-departure
>
> "I gather Uzozen and co. think Olazun left."

> `zolazun theyemeyolx velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-CLUES-person-x | v-departure
>
> "I gather some people think Olazun left."

The holder may be the [topic pronoun](pronouns.md#topic-pronoun), whoever the conversation is about now, or the [generic pronoun](pronouns.md#generic-pronoun), the generic view. The generic pronoun takes no **-x**.

> `xuzozen theyemalon zolazun velurul.`
>
> x-Uzozen | th-CLUES-TOPIC | z-Olazun | v-departure
>
> "Now, about Uzozen: I gather Uzozen thinks Olazun left."

> `theyemeyon zolazun velurul.`
>
> th-CLUES-ONE | z-Olazun | v-departure
>
> "I gather one would think Olazun left."

**Someone else's plan** goes the same way. [PLAN](intention.md#plan-predict) alone is yours; after a holder it is theirs, and the seam word says how you know what they plan. After **`uem`** ([contrary to a stance](sakes.md#contrary-to-stance)), write the holder word, then the plan, for *against their plan*.

> `zuzozen thebamuzozen thagem vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | th-REPORTED-Uzozen | th-plan-itinerary | v-walk
>
> "I hear Uzozen plans to walk."

> `zuzozen theyemolazun thagem vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | th-CLUES-Olazun | th-plan-itinerary | v-walk
>
> "I gather Olazun plans for Uzozen to walk."

The seam word keeps its `/b/` source like any channel. A schedule is not a forecast: it is a plan you know from a record, so put the record in `/b/`:

> `zuzozen thebaluzozen bagorel thagem vezuyel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [th-REPORTED.strong-Uzozen | b-calendar] | th-plan-itinerary | v-tell
>
> "Going by the official calendar, Uzozen plans to speak."

> `zuzozen vowurel uem theyemolazun thagem.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [contrary-to | th-CLUES-Olazun | th-plan-itinerary]
>
> "Uzozen walks, against Olazun's plan, as far as I can tell."

**Someone else's motive** goes the same way. A [motive](sakes.md#sake-preference) word alone names what the act serves for you; it does not say why the actor does it. After a holder, the motive is the holder's, and the seam word says how you know their reason.

> `zuzozen vezuyel thawothom theyemuzozen.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-tell | th-relatedness-motive-any-term | th-CLUES-Uzozen
>
> "I gather Uzozen talks to feel connected."

**The seam word is yours; the rest of the clause is the holder's.** The word that carries the name says how **you** know about the holder's view: you inferred it, heard it, guess it, or are imagining it. You claim nothing about whether the holder is right, and nothing about your own view. Everything else in the clause, wherever it sits, belongs to the holder:

| In the clause | With no holder | With a holder |
|---------------|----------------|---------------|
| The event | you assert it | what the holder takes to be so; you do not assert it |
| MAY, MIRATIVE, CAUSE poles, PLAN, prescription | yours | the holder's |
| [Sake](sakes.md) words and [emotion compose](sakes.md#emotion-compose), with their endings | your stake and feeling | the holder's stake and feeling |
| A `/ɡ/` sake's *my* reading ([personal possession](sakes.md#personal-possession)) | your belonging | the holder's belonging |
| Another evidential | how you know | how the holder knows |
| The grounds after an inferring or pattern host's [`barl`](#evidence-clause) | grounds you assert | the holder's grounds; you do not assert them |
| A comparison [bar](comparatives.md#bars) | your expectation | the holder's expectation |

> `zonal wawothumol goyem bolazun theyemuzozen.`
>
> [z-memo | [[w-relatedness-unmet-modifiable-AIMED-STILL | g-stimulus] | b-Olazun]] | th-CLUES-Uzozen
>
> "I gather Uzozen resents Olazun over the memo, holding it in."

> `zolazun theyemuzozen thugom velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-CLUES-Uzozen | th-LIVE | v-departure
>
> "I gather Uzozen is watching Olazun leave."

> `zolazun themom theyemuzozen velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-MIRATIVE | th-CLUES-Uzozen | v-departure
>
> "I gather Olazun's leaving surprised Uzozen." (the surprise is Uzozen's)

A holder on a comparison bar names **whose expectation** you rank against. Inside the fence the seam covers only the bar: the ranking itself is still your claim.

> `zuzozen thabomolazun zel guzemol.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | th-INTUITION-Olazun | z-rank/more | g-sleepy]
>
> "Uzozen is sleepier than Olazun expected."

A holder on a channel after [**`uem`**](#channel-frame) names **whose view** the event goes against. The seam covers only that pair: the event is still your claim.

> `zuzozen vowurel uem thebamolazun.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [contrary-to | th-REPORTED-Olazun]
>
> "Uzozen walks, contrary to what I hear Olazun thinks."

A [feeling with no object](sakes.md#feeling-no-object) works the same way: the holder makes it someone else's feeling.

> `thuwathuruor theyemolazun.`
>
> th-competence-unmet-passing-UNPLACED-SURGING | th-CLUES-Olazun
>
> "I gather Olazun is anxious."

Pronouns and speech moves stay yours: **`uwon`** is still you, and the sentence is still your turn. The holder's view is their view **now**. A channel with a holder still takes its own `/b/`, so `thebamuzozen bral` is *I hear Uzozen expects it*. A holder is not a [source](#source): a source in `/b/` is where a claim came to you from, and the claim stays yours to pass on; a holder makes the claim the holder's view.

**One clause, one holder.** The holder covers its clause and any dependent sentence inside it; a new `/x/` sentence is yours again unless it has its own seam word. A dependent with its own seam word switches holder for that sentence only. That is how you take a stance on someone else's stance: your stance in the main sentence, theirs in a dependent.

> `wawothum goyem zarl zolazun wawothum goyem theyemuzozen.`
>
> [w-relatedness-unmet-modifiable | g-stimulus] | z-that-clause | [z-Olazun | [w-relatedness-unmet-modifiable | g-stimulus]] | th-CLUES-Uzozen
>
> "It sits badly with me that, as I gather, Olazun sits badly with Uzozen."

The first sake word has no holder, so it is yours. The second is Uzozen's.

Only the seven channels, MAY, and NOTIONAL take a holder: they are the ways you can have access to someone else's view. When they told you in their own words, a [cite](spans.md#writing) keeps their wording instead.

**Compare with:** being upset **for** Uzozen is your own feeling about Uzozen's stake (the ON-BEHALF locus in [emotion compose](sakes.md#emotion-compose)); with a holder the feeling is Uzozen's. *On behalf of* Uzozen is [proxy](relations.md#proxy) (`hanom`). A `/b/` on [permission](sakes.md#permission) or [consent](sakes.md#consent) names someone who granted or agreed openly, not a holder whose mind you report. A [dictionary compound](compounds.md#lexical-compounds) also joins with an ending letter, but it is a listed word; the holder seam is built on the spot, and only after these closed hosts.

## Advanced {#advanced}

### Mood on one adjective
<a id="mood-on-adjective"></a>

The `/th/` words on this page (MAY, the channels, NOTIONAL) and the `/h/` words RESIDUE and FORMER cover the whole sentence. When one applies only to a single `/ɡ/` adjective, put the same word on `/w/` right before that adjective instead.

Only words about **how you know** or **how things stand** move to `/w/` this way: the ones above, [CAUSE](causation.md#cause), the [*already* / *still* words](#phasal), and [*can* with `oda`](intention.md#ability-fallback). A plan, decision, try, or want stays on its own letter (`/th/`, or `/h/` for a try): on `/w/` the same spelling is the ordinary word of its root (`wagem` *as planned*, `wogom` *decidedly*, `wowam` *tentatively*, `walem` *wishfully*).

> `zuzozen danobal wevum gerulel vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [d-hat | [w-MEMORY | g-red]] | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sees a hat that I remember as red."

> `zuzozen danobal wugom gerulel vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [d-hat | [w-LIVE | g-red]] | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sees a hat that I can see is red."

> `zuzozen waram godamul vowurel.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | [w-RESIDUE | g-tired]] | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen walks, still tired." (the tiredness from earlier has not worn off)

### Dated channel (signed offset) {#dated-channel}
<a id="ago"></a>
<a id="time-offset"></a>

English *three hours ago* and *in three hours* count from now. Agebon counts from the channel instead: put a [measure phrase](numbers-applied.md#measure-phrases) in `/b/` immediately after the evidential. Any other noun there is the [source](#source); a time measure is the offset. The amount is **signed**: **`-`** is earlier than now, **`+`** is later than now. The offset always dates the **event**, not the moment you learned about it. There is no bare *ago* word, so a dated claim always says how you know it.

> `zuzozen thevum bagorum grurel vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [th-MEMORY | [b-hour | g-minus-three]] | v-walk
>
> "I saw Uzozen walk three hours ago."

> `zolazun thebam bagorum grarel velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | [th-REPORTED | [b-hour | g-three]] | v-departure
>
> "I hear Olazun leaves in three hours."

Some channels only point one way:

| Channel | Sign | Why |
|---------|------|-----|
| **`thevum`** MEMORY | **`-`** only | you already observed it |
| **`thugom`** LIVE | no offset | the scene is in view now |
| **`thubem`** PATTERN | **`-`** or **`+`** | **`-`**: it happened then, if the pattern held; **`+`**: it will, if the pattern holds |
| **`theyem`** CLUES, **`thebam`** REPORTED, **`thabom`** INTUITION | **`-`** or **`+`** | the channel does not fix the time |
| [PLAN](intention.md#plan-predict) **`thagem`** | **`+`** only | the intended act is later |

The wrong sign on a one-way word (**`thevum`** with **`+`**, **`thagem`** with **`-`**) is not a sentence.

A [carried](#carry-forward) channel keeps its offset. Date the first sentence of a story, and the bare sentences after it happen then too, so a past story needs no tense and no offset on every line. A new offset starts a new frame.

> `zuzozen thevum buzurem gruwol vowurel. zuzozen vuhohol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [th-MEMORY | [b-day | g-minus-one]] | v-walk . z-Uzozen | v-sit
>
> "I saw Uzozen walk yesterday. Uzozen sat down." (the sitting was yesterday too, as I remember)

A signed measure in the `/b/` of a [time pole](dependents.md#time-poles) (**`harum`** *until*, **`hevahum`** *by*, **`habam`** *before*, **`halam`** *after*, **`herem`** *during*) counts from now, the same way. The pole itself needs no channel, but its clause must already be a command, a request, or a plan, or carry a channel of its own (a carried frame does not count). So a command or a plan can name a day without saying how anyone knows, while a plain statement cannot use a pole to slip in a past or future with no warrant. An absolute date in the pole (`harum b_#22,7`) needs none of this.

> `yel vuhamum zawan harum buzurem grawol.`
>
> y-command | v-confidentiality | z-listener | [h-until | [b-day | g-one]]
>
> "Keep the secret until tomorrow."

For *just* and *about to*, use the [just-short](numbers.md#just-short) amount with no unit: **`brubul`** is *a hair before now*, **`brabul`** is *a hair after now*.

> `zuzozen thevum brubul vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [th-MEMORY | b---e-] | v-walk
>
> "I just saw Uzozen walk."

> `theyem brabul verulal.`
>
> [th-CLUES | b-+-e-] | v-rain
>
> "It looks like it's about to rain."

For *soon*, soften that hair with open **-m**: **`brabum`** is *a short while after now*. It is later than *about to* (`brabul`) but still close, unlike open-ended *later* (`bral`).

> `theyem brabum verulal.`
>
> [th-CLUES | b-+-e-.about] | v-rain
>
> "It looks like it will rain soon."

The mirror, **`brubum`**, is *a short while before now*: *recently*.

> `zuzozen thevum brubum vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [th-MEMORY | b---e-.about] | v-walk
>
> "I saw Uzozen walk recently."

> `zolazun thubem bagorum grurel vezuvol.`
>
> z-Olazun | [th-PATTERN | [b-hour | g-minus-three]] | v-sleep
>
> "Going by the pattern, Olazun slept three hours ago."

A channel used as a comparison [bar](comparatives.md#bars) takes the same offset, which dates the bar: MEMORY with a day before is *than yesterday*.

> `zuzozen thevum buzurem gruwol zel guzemol.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | [th-MEMORY | [b-day | g-minus-one]] | z-rank/more | g-sleepy]
>
> "Uzozen is sleepier than yesterday."

With an [as-of](relations.md#as-of) pair, the offset counts from the as-of moment instead of from the moment you speak.

**Compare with:** an absolute clock or date (`h_#22,7`) dates the event without a channel. [RESIDUE](#residue) and [FORMER](#former-climate) do not locate time, so they take no offset.

#### Now and today {#now}

Agebon has no short *now* word. Say *now* with an offset of **zero**: the event sits no time away from the moment you speak. The channel stays, so *now* still says how you know it.

> `zolazun theyem bagorum grazol vezuvol.`
>
> z-Olazun | [th-CLUES | [b-hour | g-zero]] | v-sleep
>
> "Olazun must be asleep right now."

The unit sets how wide *now* is. Zero hours is *right now*; zero [days](numbers-applied.md#stock-units) is *today*.

> `zuzozen thebam buzurem grazol vezuyol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [th-REPORTED | [b-day | g-zero]] | v-sing
>
> "I hear Uzozen sings today."

Use zero on a channel that takes either sign. **LIVE** `thugom` already means *now in view*, so it needs no offset. **MEMORY** takes **`-`** only and PLAN takes **`+`** only, so zero is not a sentence on them.

### Channels on a generalization {#universality}
<a id="universality-mood"></a>
<a id="generalization"></a>

English *always*, *usually*, *every*, and *never* make a general claim. The [universal fences](joins.md#universals-domains-generics) (`zual` / `zuam`) and the [restrictors](restrictors.md) (`hual` / `huam` / `hal`) already say **who** or **when** the claim covers, and their ending says how many exceptions it allows: closed **-l** allows none, and open **-m** leaves them open, as on any list (*usually*, *as a rule*). No other word is needed for the claim itself.

> `zuzozen huam vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | h-always.open | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen usually walks."

To say **how you know** the general claim, add a [channel](#evidentiality). On a clause with a universal fence or restrictor, the channel is your warrant for the whole generalization, not for one case, and its [ending](#evidence-strength) says how much backs it.

> `zuzozen hual vowurel thuber.`
>
> z-Uzozen | h-always | v-walk | th-PATTERN.weak
>
> "Uzozen always walks, from the few cases I have seen."

> `zual gaxuduyethel vuduyel thanal.`
>
> [z-everything | g-agent-x-teach-th-role] | v-teach | th-DEDUCED.strong
>
> "It follows that every teacher teaches." (every step could be shown)

> `zolazun hal vanuvul thebal bazunal.`
>
> z-Olazun | h-never | v-punch | [th-REPORTED.strong | b-soccer]
>
> "Olazun never punches, per the soccer rules."

The fence says how far the claim reaches, and the channel says what it rests on. Keep the two apart: *always, as far as I have checked* is closed **`hual`** with a weak channel, because you claim no exceptions on thin evidence. Open **`huam`** does not claim that: it leaves the exceptions open.

| English | Fence or restrictor | Channel | Example |
|---------|---------------------|---------|---------|
| *usually*, *as a rule* | `huam` / `zuam` | any, or none | `zuzozen huam vowurel.` |
| *always, as far as I have checked* | `hual` / `zual` | a weak channel | `zuzozen hual vowurel thuber.` |
| *it follows that every …* | `zual` | DEDUCED.strong `thanal` | `zual gaxuduyethel vuduyel thanal.` |
| *by definition* | `zual` + `thehem barl` | none | `zual geyol gaxuduyethel thehem barl zeyor vuduyel.` |
| *under the rules of …* | `hal` / `hual` | REPORTED.strong with the rules in `/b/` | `zolazun hal vanuvul thebal bazunal.` |
| *cats are sleepy* (in general) | `zuam` + kind | any, or none | `zuam gagozol guzemol thubem.` |

A [cause or condition](causation.md) on a general claim says how things work. It holds for **each** member or occasion, the same way the verb does. Inside the sentence after **`barl`**, resume the kind with **-r** (`zagozor`) to mean *that same one*: each cat, in turn.

> `zual gagozol vezuvol thewem thohom barl zagozor guzemol.`
>
> [z-everything | g-cat] | v-sleep | th-CAUSE | [th-if | b-that-clause] | [z-←cat | g-sleepy]
>
> "Every cat sleeps when it is sleepy; that is how it works."

A **definition** works both ways: whatever has the property is that kind, and every member of the kind has it. Write it on a closed fence with **`thehem`** (*if and only if*, from [causation](causation.md#only-because)). The grounds sentence is the defining property. A definition needs no channel, because it is a decision about a word, not a report about the world.

> `zual geyol gaxuduyethel thehem barl zeyor vuduyel.`
>
> [z-everything | g-person] | g-agent-x-teach-th-role | [th-iff | b-that-clause] | z-←person | v-teach
>
> "By definition, a person is a teacher if and only if they teach."

The channel stays on the main sentence and covers the whole generalization. Put it before the pole, as with the [evidence clause](#evidence-clause): `zual gagozol vezuvol thubel thohom barl …` is *going by a well-established pattern, every cat sleeps when …*.

**Compare with:** a general claim you are **not** giving as today's report is [FORMER](#former-climate) (`helam`). *Every cat must sleep* as a rule someone sets is [REQUIRE](sakes.md#requirement) (`thonam`), not a law of how things work. *Maybe every cat sleeps* is [MAY](#may) on the same clause.
