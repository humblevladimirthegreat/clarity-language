# Initials, rays, named ranges {#initials-rays-named-ranges}

### Initials {#initials}

English shortens a long title to its initials (*FBI*). Agebon does too: take each root of a long [hyphenated name](proper-names.md#multipart-names), in order, and write its **first consonant** as a [letter code](numbers-applied.md#letter-codes) with **`@`** (**-n**, an official designation). A root always starts with a vowel, so its first letter would leave only four choices; its first consonant is the letter you hear.

> `z@_nl vezuyel.`
>
> 🔊 `zronulan vezuyel.`
>
> z-_nl.named | v-tell
>
> "The NL announces." (the office whose full title is *Book-Courage*)

| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| `zanaxolazun` | long title (`ana` × `olazu`) | *Book-Courage* |
| `z@_nl` (🔊 `zronulan`) | initials: `n` from `ana`, `l` from `olazu` | *the NL*, the same office |
| `z=_nl` (🔊 `zronular`) | resume | *that office again* |

- **Any title works.** Each initial is its own syllable, so two roots with the same first consonant give a doubled letter (`zanaxanumun` → `z@_nn`, 🔊 `zronunun`), and a title of any length gives one letter per root.
- **Initials can be shared,** as in English. Context sorts out which office is meant. When you need a name nobody else has, give the office a [proper name](proper-names.md#proper-name--n) of its own instead: one published root + **-n**.
- **Say it mid-sentence.** The whole rule is *say each root's first consonant by its letter name*, so you can make the initials while you talk.

### Rays {#rays}
<a id="numeric-thresholds"></a>
<a id="greater-less-than"></a>

English *5 or more* / *less than 5* is a **ray**: a band with only one end. Write it as a [range](numbers-applied.md#ranges) whose other end is infinity: **`zrawal`** (+∞) or **`zruwal`** (−∞), the [digitless exponents](special-values.md#special-values). The same **`al`** / **`ul`** rules apply, so the hook excludes the endpoint after it.

> `zrazal al zrawal.`
>
> z-five | through | z-plus-infinity
>
> "5 or more."

> `zruwal ul zrazal.`
>
> z-minus-infinity | through-excluding | z-five
>
> "Less than 5."

<!-- cheat-sheet: numbers -->
| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| **`zrazal al zrawal`** | 5 up to +∞ | *5 or more* (`≥ 5`) |
| **`zrawal ul zrazal`** | +∞ down to 5, 5 left out | *more than 5* (`> 5`) |
| **`zruwal al zrazal`** | −∞ up to 5 | *5 or less* / *at most 5* (`≤ 5`) |
| **`zruwal ul zrazal`** | −∞ up to 5, 5 left out | *less than 5* (`< 5`) |
| **`zrazal am zrawal`** | soft | *about 5 or more* |

For counts, −∞ reads as *no lower limit*: `zagozolx gruwal al grazal` is *up to five cats*.

Ranks take the first and last place as ends. **`grewal`** is the last place and **`grewul`** the first, so `zredol al zrewal` is *2nd through last*.

**Compare with:** a rank join on one number is not a ray: `zrazal zel` is *only 5 matters*, and `zrazal zael` is *start with 5*.

### Named and unknown ranges {#named-ranges}
<a id="half-open-exclude-the-high-end-only"></a>

The range hook takes the other hook endings too.

<!-- cheat-sheet: numbers -->
| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| `z+13 an z+19` | **-n**: a named band | *the teens* |
| `zragel ar zrazal` | **-r**: some value in the band | *some value from 3 to 5* |
| `zragel ur zrazal` | **-r**: some value in the band, 5 left out | *some value from 3 up to 5* |

> `z+13 an z+19.`
>
> 🔊 `zrabagel an zrabanal.`
>
> NAME[z-13 | through | z-19]
>
> "The teens."

With **-r**, the hook stands in for one value you are not naming. Under a [question](questions.md#fill-ask-r) it asks for that value, as `zar` asks *who?*:

> `zuzozen dagozolx gragel ar grazal vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [d-cat-x | g-three] | some.through | g-five | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sees some number of cats from 3 to 5."

The [range hooks](ending-grids.md#ranges) take **-r** the same way: `zragel uar zrazal` is *some value strictly between 3 and 5*, and `zragel uer zrazal` *some value outside 3–5*.

### Ordinal generation: digitful exponent on **`#`** / **`#-`**
<a id="ordinal-generation"></a>
<a id="generation-ordinal"></a>

On a rank number (forward **`#`** / **`re`** or end-relative **`#-`** / **`rue`**), an exponent with digits is not a power of ten. It says **how many generations away** from the person, version, or outline point in focus. Use it when English would say *the third grandchild* or *the 3rd sub-point*.

The marker still counts **from the start** or **from the end**, but only among the members of that generation (its cohort). The exponent **`eK`** names the generation K steps deeper (descendants), and **`e-K`** the generation K steps up (ancestors). The mantissa picks which member; leave it off to name the whole cohort. Leave the exponent off for the focus's **own** generation (`gregel` = 3rd peer). Writing **`e0`** says *current generation* outright, which is usually redundant.

| Piece | Job |
|-------|-----|
| **Marker `#` / `#-`** | Count **from the start** / **from the end** within that generation’s cohort |
| **Digitful exp `eK` / `e-K`** | Generation **+K** (descendants / nested deeper) or **−K** (ancestors / enclosing layer) |
| **Mantissa** | Which **slot** in that cohort (omit mantissa = bare OoM: the generation as a whole) |
| **Exp omitted** | **Same generation** as the focus (`g#3` = 3rd peer); formal default |
| **Named `e0`** | **Assert current generation** (gen 0); usually redundant when omit already means current |

<!-- cheat-sheet: numbers -->
| Form | Reading |
|------|---------|
| **`g#3`** | 3rd of the focus’s own generation (from the start) |
| **`g#-3`** | 3rd of the focus’s own generation (from the end) |
| **`g#3e0`** | 3rd of the **current** generation, exp asserted (gen 0) |
| **`g#e0`** | **current generation** cohort as a whole (member unspecified) |
| **`g#3e2`** | 3rd from the start **in generation +2** (e.g. 3rd in the grandchild cohort) |
| **`g#3e-2`** | 3rd from the start **in generation −2** (e.g. 3rd in the grandparent cohort) |
| **`g#-1e2`** | last of generation +2 (end-framed within that cohort) |
| **`g#e2`** | generation +2 cohort as a whole (member unspecified) |
| **`g#e-1`** | generation −1 / parent layer (member unspecified) |
| **`g#1e-1`** | 1st of generation −1 (often *the parent* when the cohort is singleton-framed) |

On `/x/`, generations are levels of an outline: `x#3e2` is the *3rd sub-sub-point under the current point*, and **`xrewamol`** is *the point or points on the current level*. `/h/`, `/v/`, and `/y/` read generations the same way when the series comes in levels (*for the 3rd time at level +2*, *place 3rd at level +2*, …). For a plain count of occasions, use **`h#N`**.

#### Kin {#kin-generations}

Generations form a **tree**, like an outline. A cohort is only the members **under the same node**, so the plain forms name close family and never side branches:

- **`grewubal`** is the layer directly above the focus: the **parents**, not aunts or uncles.
- **`grewamol`** is the others under the same parent: the **siblings**, not cousins. It never includes the focus.
- **`grewabal`** / **`grewadol`** are the focus's own **children** / **grandchildren**, not nieces or nephews.

The mantissa is **birth order** within that cohort: **`g#1e0`** is the eldest sibling, **`g#-1e0`** the youngest. To say whose family it is, put the anchor person in a `bl-` word right after the number. (cue: the number says how far up or down the tree; `/b/` says from whom)

> `zolazun grewamol bluzozen vowurel.`
>
> [z-Olazun | [g-#-e0 | bl-Uzozen]] | v-walk
>
> "Olazun, Uzozen's sibling, walks."

With no `bl-` word after a joined subject, each member is a sibling of the others.

> `zuzozen zolazun zal grewamol.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-Olazun | z-and | g-#-e0]
>
> "Uzozen and Olazun are siblings."

> `zolazun g#1e0 bluzozen vowurel.`
>
> 🔊 `zolazun grewamoyabal bluzozen vowurel.`
>
> [z-Olazun | [g-#-1e0 | bl-Uzozen]] | v-walk
>
> "Olazun, Uzozen's eldest sibling, walks."

> `zahoyel g#2e1 bluzozen vowurel.`
>
> 🔊 `zahoyel grewabayadol bluzozen vowurel.`
>
> [z-child | [g-#-2e1 | bl-Uzozen]] | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen's second child walks."

For a side branch, nest a second anchor: first step to the relative, then from there. *Aunt or uncle* is a sibling of a parent.

> `zolazun grewamol bleyol g#1e-1 bluzozen vowurel.`
>
> 🔊 `zolazun grewamol bleyol grewubayabal bluzozen vowurel.`
>
> [z-Olazun | [g-#-e0 | [bl-person | [g-#-1e-1 | bl-Uzozen]]]] | v-walk
>
> "Olazun, Uzozen's aunt or uncle, walks." (a sibling of the first of Uzozen's parents)

**Compare with:** friends, bosses, and other non-kin relations are not generations, so these forms do not name them.
