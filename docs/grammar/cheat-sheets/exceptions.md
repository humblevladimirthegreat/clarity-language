# Exceptions cheat sheet

A quick look at the places where Agazan breaks one of its own patterns. Each row gives the usual pattern, the words that break it, and a link to the page that teaches them.

## `-n` that is not a name {#n-not-a-name}

On a content word, the ending **-n** marks a name: this person, place, work, or brand, not a kind of thing ([word endings](../word-endings.md#proper-name--n)). These closed word families also end in **-n** without naming anything:

| Words | What **-n** does there | Example |
|-------|------------------------|---------|
| [special pronouns](../pronouns.md#special-pronouns), the [generic pronoun](../pronouns.md#generic-pronoun), and the [topic pronoun](../pronouns.md#topic-pronoun) | the usual ending on these person words | `zamagon` *I*, `zoben` *one*, `zozan` *the topic* |
| [stand-ins](../dependents.md#stand-in-roles) | **-rn** (single vowel) or **-n** (stacked vowel) names a kind of sentence content (statement, question, command, prohibition); on `/v/`, the matching response verb | `darn` *a statement*, `varn` *state* |
| [join words](../joins.md) with a `/v/`, `/x/`, `/ɡ/`, or `/h/` first letter | join content: a [join-act](../join-across-roles.md#join-act-verbs) verb, clause [sequence](../join-across-roles.md#clause-sequence), or a [join-relation](../join-across-roles.md#join-relations) | `van` *include*, `xan` *and then*, `gan` *including* |

These still count as names:

| Words | Why it is still a name |
|-------|------------------------|
| a phrase join under `/z/`, `/d/`, or `/b/` | **-n** titles the bundle ([titled phrases](../word-endings.md#titled-phrases)) |
| a hook | **-n** titles a proper-name phrase ([titled phrases](../word-endings.md#titled-phrases)) |
| a number word | **-n** is a conventional designation, such as an official label or *the Second* ([number-word exception](../word-endings.md#number-word-exception)) |
