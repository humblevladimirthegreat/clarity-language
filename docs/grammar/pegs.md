# Number Pegs

A long number, such as a PIN or a phone number, is easier to remember as a few pictures than as a row of digits. In Agebon, each digit is spoken with its own consonant ([counts](./numbers.md#counts)), and ordinary roots are built from the same consonants. So every root's consonants spell a number. To remember a number, cut it into one- or two-digit pieces and, for each piece, pick a root whose consonants spell those digits. A root used this way is a **peg**: you picture the thing it names, and later read the digits back off its spelling.

## Reading a word as digits {#reading}

Each digit has one consonant, the first letter of its spoken syllable:

| Digit | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 0 |
|-------|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Letter | b | d | g | v | z | h | l | r | n | m |

Only the root spells digits, so first take off the letters around it:

1. Drop the role letter, the first letter that says the word's part in the sentence (`z` for the subject, `v` for the verb, and so on). A word that starts with `gl-` drops both letters.
2. Drop the ending (**-l**, **-m**, **-n**, **-r**) and anything after it, such as plural **-x**.
3. In the root that is left, skip the vowels and the letters `w` and `y`. Read the other consonants as digits, left to right.

`zudozol` *dog* is 25: drop `z` and **-l**, and the root's consonants are `d` and `z`. The role letter and the ending change what the word does in the sentence, not the thing you picture, so `dudozom` is the same dog and still 25.

`w` and `y` fill out a syllable without spelling a digit, so a five-letter root with one of them spells a single digit: `erowol` *radio* is 8, and `anewul` *knife* is 9. A root whose only consonants are `w` and `y`, like `oyayul` *ogre*, spells no number.

Vowels never count, so roots that differ only in their vowels spell the same number: `agedal` *cutlery*, `agedol` *croissant* and `ogodal` *coconut* are all 32. When a number has several roots, pick the picture you find most vivid.

Longer words spell longer numbers:

- A [dictionary word](./compounds.md#lexical-compounds) made of two roots counts its joining letter like any other consonant: `oholeboral` *doorbell* is 6718.
- A word glued with a mid-word `x` reads each root in turn, and the `x` spells nothing: `zubedaxuyudal` *peanut butter* is 12, then 2.

Number words (`gragol` *three*), pronouns and other grammar words are not pegs. Read them for what they say.

## Peg sizes {#peg-sizes}

| Root | Digits | Peg for |
|------|--------|---------|
| three letters (`ohol` *door*) | one | 0 to 9 |
| five letters with `w` or `y` (`erowol` *radio*) | one | 0 to 9 |
| five letters, two digit consonants (`abedol` *pear*) | two | 00 to 99 |
| dictionary word (`oholeboral` *doorbell*) | three or more | a longer piece |

The dictionary has at least one root for every digit and for every pair from 00 to 99. On a five-letter root the last consonant also says what kind of thing the root names ([root tails](./compounds.md#root-tails)), so the second digit of a pair tells you what sort of picture to look for: 2 is a food or drink, 5 an animal, 9 something with a title, such as a newspaper or a sport.

## Remembering a number {#remembering}

1. Cut the number into pairs. When a group has an odd number of digits, its last digit is a piece on its own: 555 is 55, then 5.
2. For each piece, pick a root that spells exactly those digits.
3. Picture the thing each root names (its emoji in the [lexicon](./lexicon.md) is a good start). Place the pictures one by one along a route you know well, such as the rooms of your home.

2381 is 23, then 81: `udugul` *triangle*, then `orebol` *ring*. Picture a triangle threaded through a ring.

Each place on the route holds one piece, so you know how many digits it gives back. If a place that held a pair gives you three digits, you counted the ending or a `w` or `y`. If it gives you one, you skipped a consonant of the root.

For a number with a decimal point, use two places: one for the whole part, one for the part after the point.

## Person, action, object {#person-action-object}

To hold six digits in one picture, make a sentence with a subject, an object and a verb, each a two-digit root. Read the digits in the sentence's usual order: subject, object, verb.

> `zudozol dabedol vanuvul.`
> z-dog | d-pear | v-punch
>
> "The dog punches a pear."

This scene holds 25 12 94. Pick a verb with two digit consonants; a verb spelled only with `w` and `y` spells nothing.
