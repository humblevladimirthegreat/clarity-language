# Questions

This page teaches how to ask a question and how to answer one.

You can ask for *yes* or *no*, or you can leave a blank for *who* / *what* (a **fill-ask**). After a *yes* / *no* question, you can answer with a short *yes* / *no* / *sure* word (**polar stance**). The same words answer a command or a request.

## Beginner {#beginner}

### Ask (`yol` / `yom`) {#question}

A **question** asks for a reply instead of stating a fact. English marks it by moving words around (*Uzozen walks* → *Does Uzozen walk?*). Agebon marks it with the act word **`yol`** at the start of the turn, followed by the body (subject, verb, and so on). Every word keeps the form it would have in a statement. Only the usual order changes: the verb comes [second](speech-moves.md#act-word-order), right after the subject.

Soft **`yom`** asks the same question more gently (wondering rather than pressing). End a written question with a period, like any sentence.

When every person and action in the clause is already named, the expected reply is *yes* or *no*. (cue: **o** ≈ one: a menu you pick from.)

> `yol zuzozen vowurel.`
>
> y-question | z-Uzozen | v-walk
>
> "Does Uzozen walk?"

> `yom zolazun vezuvol.`
>
> y-soft-question | z-Olazun | v-sleep
>
> "Is Olazun sleeping, I wonder?"

> `yol zuzozen vawebol dagozol.`
>
> y-question | z-Uzozen | v-see | d-cat
>
> "Does Uzozen see a cat?"

With no body at all, the question asks about what was just said: *Huh?* / *What?* Soft **`yom.`** is gentler (*Hm?*).

> `yol.`
>
> y-question
>
> "Huh?"

A question whose subject is *nobody* (`zal`), or whose whole clause is *nothing happened* (`xal`), still asks for *yes* or *no*, not *who*.

> `yol zal vowurel.`
>
> y-question | z-none | v-walk
>
> "Does nobody walk?"

### Fill-ask
<a id="fill-ask-r"></a>

Sometimes you do not want *yes* or *no*. You want the other person to **name** someone or something: *who?*, *what?*, *do what?*, *what happened?* That kind of question is a **fill-ask**.

Keep **`yol`** or **`yom`** at the start. In the slot you want filled, use a join word that ends in **-r** (`zar`, `var`, `xar`, …). That **-r** is the blank ([unspecified-member **-r**](joins.md#unspecified-member-r-phrase)). (cue: **-r** names this member; **a** ≈ add for *who/what*, **u** ≈ undo for *who/what else*.)

> `yol zar vowurel.`
>
> y-question | z-wh | v-walk
>
> "Who walks?"

Put the blank **first** in the body, and the other words in their statement order after it. The listener hears what you are asking before anything else, and the blank itself marks the turn as a question even if **`yol`** is missed.

> `yol dar zuzozen vawebol.`
>
> y-question | d-wh | z-Uzozen | v-see
>
> "What does Uzozen see?"

A blank that completes another word comes first together with that word: *like whom?* is `hewom bar`, so `yol hewom bar zuzozen vowurel.` asks *Whom does Uzozen walk like?*

In the word-by-word line under an example, a fill-ask blank shows as *wh*, short for whichever question word fits the slot (*who*, *what*, *where*, and so on).

If the reply is just the fill, not a full sentence, write it as a [citation](word-endings.md#citation-forms): root + ending, no role letter. Leave no join **-r** in the answer; that **-r** was the blank. A full-sentence answer uses ordinary role letters.

> `uzozen.`
>
> "Uzozen."

| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| **a** (`zar` · `var` · `xar`) | ask for who or what | *Who/what?* / *What happened?* | **a** ≈ add |
| **u** (`zur` · `vur` · `xur`) | ask for who or what else | *Who else?* / *What else?* | **u** ≈ undo |

> `yol zur vezuvol.`
>
> y-question | z-wh-else | v-sleep
>
> "Who else sleeps?"

> `olazun.`
>
> "Olazun."

**Compare with:** pointing back at someone already named uses resume **-r** on a whole stem or a role pointer ([pronouns](pronouns.md)). A fill-ask uses only **join** **-r**. The spelling tells them apart: a resume spells a whole stem, and a role pointer has a role vowel, **`x`**, and a pointer vowel. A join word has no stem, only its role letter, its join vowel or vowels, and its ending.

### Question tone (`?`) {#question-tone}

The act word **`yol`** / **`yom`** already makes the sentence a question, so the tone mark `?` is never required. It is still **recommended**, because it shows the raised voice in writing:

- **Yes/no question:** write a free-standing `?` right after the `/y/` words, so it applies to the whole rest of the sentence.
- **Fill-ask:** attach `?` to the front of each fill-ask word (the join **-r** blank), so the raised voice falls on what you want filled.

> `yol ? zuzozen vowurel.`
>
> y-question | ? | z-Uzozen | v-walk
>
> "Does Uzozen walk?" (with the raised voice)

> `yol ?zar vowurel.`
>
> y-question | ?z-wh | v-walk
>
> "Who walks?" (with the raised voice on *who*)

The sentence still ends in a period.

### Fill-all {#fill-all}

To ask *who sees what?*, put join **-r** in every slot you want filled, and put all the blanks first. Each of those **-r** words is a blank, and the answer is expected to **fill all** of them, in the order the question gave them.

> `yol zar dar vawebol.`
>
> y-question | z-wh | d-wh | v-see
>
> "Who sees what?"

> `uzozen udozol.`
>
> Uzozen | dog
>
> "Uzozen, a dog."

To ask for several members of one slot (*who all?*), put [**-x**](joins.md#unspecified-member-r-phrase) after the blank. The answer lists them with a join.

> `yol zarx vowurel.`
>
> y-question | z-wh-x | v-walk
>
> "Who all walks?"

> `zuzozen zolazun zam.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-Olazun | z-and.open]
>
> "Uzozen and Olazun, among others."

To ask only about people, put the kind before the blank: `yol zeyol zarx vowurel.` is *Which people walk?*

If only one slot is unknown, put join **-r** only there. Write the known thing as an ordinary word (`-l` / `-m`).

> `yol zar vawebol dudozol.`
>
> y-question | z-wh | v-see | d-dog
>
> "Who sees a dog?"

> `uzozen.`
>
> "Uzozen."

### Polar stance (*yes* / *no* / *sure*) {#polar-stance}
<a id="answers"></a>

After a *yes* / *no* question, you often answer with a short word that means *yes*, *no*, or *I’ll take that*. That answer is a **polar stance**. It can stand alone as a whole turn, or it can sit at the start of a turn before a sentence body.

Write **`y`**, then two vowels, then **-l** (firm) or **-m** (softer).

**`yael`** means *yes* / *true*: the claim matches. Soft twin **`yaem`**: *I think so* / *yeah*.

**`yuel`** means *no* / *false*: the claim does not match. Soft twin **`yuem`**: *I don’t think so* / *nah*.

**`yaol`** means *sure* / *I’ll take that*: you take up the offer. Soft twin **`yaom`**: *okay* / *sure, I guess*.

When a sentence body follows *yes*, write **`yael`** then the body. You do not also write the statement word **`yal`**.

A turn holds one polar word. Do not stack two, and do not put one before an act word: an answer followed by a question is two turns.

| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
<!-- cheat-sheet: talking -->
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| **`yael`** / soft **`yaem`** | the claim matches, or *got it* | *yes* / *true* · *I think so* | **a** ≈ add + **e** ≈ order (the claim lines up) |
| **`yuel`** / soft **`yuem`** | mismatch | *no* / *false* · *I don’t think so* | **u** ≈ undo + **e** ≈ order (the claim does not line up) |
| **`yaol`** / soft **`yaom`** | take up an offered option | *sure* / *I’ll take that* · *okay* | **a** ≈ add (accept) + **o** ≈ this option |

> `yael.`
>
> "Yes." / "True." / "Got it."

> `yaem.`
>
> y-yes-soft
>
> "I think so." / "Yeah."

> `yuel.`
>
> "No." / "False."

> `yuem.`
>
> y-no-soft
>
> "I don’t think so." / "Nah."

> `yaol.`
>
> "Sure." / "I’ll take that."

> `yaom.`
>
> y-sure-soft
>
> "Okay." / "Sure, I guess."

To back up your own statement with *yes*, give **`yael`** a turn of its own: finish the statement with a period, then write **`yael.`**

> `zuzozen vowurel. yael.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk . y-yes
>
> "Uzozen walks. Yes."

To ask the listener to confirm instead (*…, right?*), put the question word **`yol`** before it: **`yol yael.`** A short question like this after a statement is a **tag**. Soft **`yom yael.`** is *…, isn't it?*

> `zuzozen vowurel. yol yael.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk . y-question | y-yes
>
> "Uzozen walks, right?"

**Compare with:** a command *Don’t walk* uses **`yul`**. **`yuel`** answers whether a claim is true.

Inside the clause, *not X* is the join **`zul`** / **`vul`**, not a polar word.

### Embedded *whether* {#embedded-whether}

English *whether* reports a yes/no question inside a statement (*Uzozen sees whether Olazun walks*). Write the stand-in [**`dorl`**](dependents.md#dependent-clauses) in the object slot, with the inner question right after it. The whole sentence is still a statement, so do not write **`yol`** at its start.

> `zuzozen vawebol dorl zolazun vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-see | d-whether-clause | z-Olazun | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen sees whether Olazun walks."

**Compare with:** a direct question (*Does Uzozen walk?*) puts **`yol`** at the start of the turn.

## Intermediate {#intermediate}

### Polar stance: fuller inventory

Beginner already used *yes* / *true* (**`yael`**), *no* / *false* (**`yuel`**), and *sure* (**`yaol`**). The rest of the map is *reject this option*, *reject the question itself*, and *decline to answer*. The first vowel names the family: **`u`** undo, **`a`** accept, **`o`** pass. The second vowel says what you are answering: **`e`** the claim or the order, **`o`** this option, **`a`** the question's own premise. You can also hear the family: the three undo answers [fall](phonology.md#phonotactics) and the other three rise.

| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| **`ae`** (`yael`) | the claim matches, or *got it*; comply with an order | *true* · *will do* | **a** ≈ add + **e** ≈ order (judged in line) |
| **`ue`** (`yuel`) | flip / mismatch; refuse an order | *false* · *I won't* | **u** ≈ undo + **e** ≈ order (judged out of line) |
| **`ao`** (`yaol`) | take up an offered option | *sure* | **a** ≈ add (accept) + **o** ≈ this option |
| **`uo`** (`yuol`) | reject this option | *not that* | **u** ≈ undo + **o** ≈ one |
| **`ua`** (`yual`) | reject the question's premise | *neither: the question doesn't apply* | **u** ≈ undo + **a** ≈ add (undo what the question added) |
| **`oe`** (`yoel`) | decline to answer | *I won't answer* | **o** ≈ one + **e** ≈ order (pass to the next one in line) |

**`yual`** answers a question that assumes something false. *Have you stopped lying?* has no honest *yes* or *no* if you never lied. **`yual.`** says so in one word, without accepting the frame. It judges the question, not the speaker's willingness: for a refusal to answer, use **`yoel`** (below).

> `yol zuzozen vowurel ul bahehal. yual.`
>
> y-question | z-Uzozen | v-walk | [from | b-house] . y-reject-frame
>
> "Did Uzozen walk away from the house?" "The question doesn't apply." (Uzozen was never at the house)

#### Three endings {#polar-endings}

Each stance word takes one of three endings, on the same settled-to-passing scale as [time horizon](sakes.md#settled-to-passing). **-l** holds the stance firmly. **-m** is the soft twin. **-r** marks a stance you hold only *for now*: provisional, and you expect to revisit it.

<!-- cheat-sheet: talking -->
| | **-l** firm | **-m** soft | **-r** for now |
|--|-------------|-------------|--------------------|
| **`ae`** | `yael` *true* | `yaem` *I think so* | `yaer` *true, for now* / *I'll take that on board for now* |
| **`ue`** | `yuel` *false* | `yuem` *I don't think so* | `yuer` *not so, for now* (I'm holding off, and may reconsider) |
| **`ao`** | `yaol` *sure* | `yaom` *okay* | `yaor` *fine, for now* / *let's try it* |
| **`uo`** | `yuol` *not that* | `yuom` *nah* | `yuor` *not that, for now* / *not right now* |
| **`ua`** | `yual` *the question doesn't apply* | `yuam` *it's more complicated than that* | `yuar` *it doesn't apply, for now* (the premise fails at the moment) |
| **`oe`** | `yoel` *I won't answer* | `yoem` *I'd rather not say* | `yoer` *pass, for now* |

**`yaer`** takes a claim on board without committing to it. English *oh, I see* often sounds like agreement; **`yaer`** says only that you hold the claim for now. You can restate the claim after the stance word:

> `yaer zuzozen vowurel.`
>
> y-yes-for-now | z-Uzozen | v-walk
>
> "All right, so Uzozen walks, for now."

**`yuar`** says the question's premise fails at the moment, though it may hold later (cue: a door that is not there yet). **`yuer`** holds off on the claim; **`yuar`** holds off on what the question took for granted.

> `yol zuzozen vowurel ul bahehal. yuar.`
>
> y-question | z-Uzozen | v-walk | [from | b-house] . y-reject-frame-for-now
>
> "Is Uzozen walking away from the house?" "The question doesn't apply, for now." (Uzozen isn't at the house yet)

**`yoel`**, **`yoem`** and **`yoer`** decline to answer without saying *no* and without judging the question. Nobody should hear them as a denial or as a rejected frame.

> `yol zolazun vezuvol. yoer.`
>
> y-question | z-Olazun | v-sleep . y-decline-for-now
>
> "Is Olazun asleep?" "I'll pass for now."

#### Answering a command or request {#answer-command}

A command (**`yel`**) or request (**`yem`**) is not a claim, so *true* and *false* do not fit it. The same stance words answer it, but now they say whether you will do what was asked. The **e** in **`yael`** / **`yuel`** is the order vowel of **`yel`** itself.

| Stance | **-l** firm | **-m** soft | **-r** for now |
|--------|-------------|-------------|----------------|
| **`ae`** accept the order | `yael` *will do* | `yaem` *okay, I'll try* | `yaer` *will do, for now* |
| **`ue`** refuse the order | `yuel` *I won't* | `yuem` *I'd rather not* | `yuer` *I won't, for now* |
| **`ao`** take it up as an offer | `yaol` *sure, I'll take that on* | `yaom` *okay, fine* | `yaor` *fine, for now* |
| **`uo`** reject this option | `yuol` *not that* | `yuom` *nah* | `yuor` *not that, for now* |
| **`ua`** reject the premise | `yual` *that can't be done* | `yuam` *it's more complicated than that* | `yuar` *it doesn't apply, for now* |
| **`oe`** decline to answer | `yoel` *I won't say* | `yoem` *I'd rather not say* | `yoer` *pass, for now* |

After a flat command, `yael` / `yuel` are the plain comply / refuse. After a request (**`yem`**), the **o** words fit better, because you are choosing whether to take it on.

> `yel vowurel zawan. yael.`
>
> y-command | v-walk | z-listener . y-yes
>
> "Walk." "Will do."

> `yem vowurel zawan. yuor.`
>
> y-request | v-walk | z-listener . y-refuse-option-for-now
>
> "Please walk." "Not right now."

For a stronger stance, use the [tone mark](speech-moves.md#tone-marks) **`!!`**, not a different word. **`!!yaol`** is *Absolutely!* / *Yes, please!*, and **`!!yuol`** is *Hell no!*

> `!!yaol.`
>
> !!y-sure
>
> "Absolutely!"

> `!!yuol.`
>
> !!y-refuse-option
>
> "Hell no!"

| Ask flavor | Firm (**-l**) | Soft (**-m**) |
|------------|----------------|----------------|
| *Is it so?* / confirm the claim | **`yael`**, deny with **`yuel`** | **`yaem`**, deny with **`yuem`** |
| *Want this?* / offer | **`yaol`**, reject-this with **`yuol`** | **`yaom`**, reject-this with **`yuom`** |
| Command (**`yel`**) | **`yael`**, refuse with **`yuel`** | **`yaem`**, refuse with **`yuem`** |
| Loaded question | **`yual`** | **`yuam`** |
| Declined question | **`yoel`** | **`yoem`** |

A stance word can stand alone (`yael.`), sit before a body (`yael zuzozen vowurel.`), or come as a confirm tag in the next turn after `.`. After a stance word plus a body, continue with **`/x/`**; write a second polar word only in a new turn. When you think aloud on your own, **`yuel`** can mark a correction, **`yaol`** the next step, and **`yael`** a point you settle.

**Compare with:** *not* inside the clause uses **`zul`** / **`vul`**. Polar **`yuel`** / **`yuol`** answer a *yes* / *no* or an offer.

### Tags: asking for an answer {#tags}

Beginner closed a claim with the tag **`yol yael.`** (*…, right?*). Every polar word works as a tag the same way: after your sentence, write **`yol`** and the polar word as a turn of their own. The tag asks the listener for that answer. Naming an answer makes it easy to give, so pick the one you want to make easy.

After a request or a suggestion, **`yol yaol.`** asks *okay?*: will they take it up? **`yol yuol.`** asks *or would you rather not?*, so they can turn it down without having to push back. After a claim, **`yol yuel.`** asks *or am I wrong?*: it invites a correction, where **`yol yael.`** leans toward agreement.

> `yem vowurel zayan. yol yaol.`
>
> y-request | v-walk | z-interlocutors . y-question | y-sure
>
> "Let's walk, okay?"

> `yem vowurel zawan. yol yuol.`
>
> y-request | v-walk | z-listener . y-question | y-refuse-option
>
> "Please walk, or would you rather not?"

> `zuzozen vowurel. yol yuel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk . y-question | y-no
>
> "Uzozen walked, or am I wrong?"

| Agebon | Use | English |
<!-- cheat-sheet: talking -->
|--------|-----|---------|
| `yol yael.` | ask them to confirm a claim | *…, right?* |
| `yol yuel.` | invite a correction | *…, or am I wrong?* |
| `yol yaol.` | ask them to take up a request or an offer | *…, okay?* / *…, deal?* |
| `yol yuol.` | offer them the refusal | *…, or would you rather not?* |
| `yol yaer.` | ask them to accept it for now | *…, can we go with that for now?* |
| `yol yoem.` | after a question, offer them a pass | *…, or would you rather not say?* |

Soft **`yom`** asks more gently (`yom yaol.` *…, if that's okay?*), and the other polar words and endings make tags the same way.

**Compare with:** the answer itself is the polar word alone (`yaol.` *sure*). The tag puts **`yol`** first, so it asks for that answer instead of giving it.

### Confirming a negative {#confirming-a-negative}

When the question already contains *not* (*Didn’t Uzozen run?*), *yes* and *no* still say whether **that claim** matches. **`yael`** confirms the denial (*true: they didn’t*). **`yuel`** says the denial is false (*they did*). Restate the body after the stance word to make the answer unmistakable. Offer words (**`yaol`** / **`yuol`**) still answer *take this?*, not true/false.

> `yol zuzozen varuvel vul. yael.`
>
> y-question | z-Uzozen | [v-run | v-not] . y-yes
>
> "Didn’t Uzozen run?" "True, Uzozen didn’t."

> `yael varuvel vul.`
>
> y-yes | [v-run | v-not]
>
> "True, they didn’t run."

### Fill-ask arity {#fill-ask-arity}

Beginner already used *who/what* (**…ar**) and *what else* (**…ur**). Two more vowels (**…or**, **…er**) and the number of items listed before the blank (none, one, or several) complete the map, which matches [single-item / standalone](joins.md#single-item-phrase).

<!-- cheat-sheet: talking -->
| Items listed | **…ar** | **…or** | **…er** | **…ur** |
|--------|---------|---------|---------|---------|
| **0** | *Who/what?* | *Anything?* | *What’s the priority?* | *What else?* |
| **1** | *What/which (unknown) X?* | *Any X?* | *Which X is first?* | *What else (besides X)?* |
| **2+** | *Which of these?* | *Any of these?* | *Which ranks highest?* | *What else?* / *Other than these?* |

With nothing listed, **-r** in the asked-about slot is an open *who* / *what*. Bare **`var`** / **`xar`** is *what did they do?* / *what happened?*. Bare **`har`** is *when?*.

### What kind? {#what-kind}

To ask what something is **like** (*What color is it?*, *What kind of dog?*), put the blank on an adjective: **`gar`**. Answer with the adjective as a [citation](word-endings.md#citation-forms).

> `yol gar zudozol.`
>
> y-question | g-wh | z-dog
>
> "What is the dog like?"

> `gelazom.`
>
> "Big."

| Agebon | English |
<!-- cheat-sheet: talking -->
|--------|---------|
| **`gar`** | *What is it like?* / *What kind?* |
| **`gor`** | *Like anything in particular?* |
| **`gur`** | *What else is it like?* |

**Compare with:** *like what?* is the similative blank, **`hewom bar`**: it asks for a model to resemble, not a property.

### When? {#when}

To ask *when?* / *in what case?*, put the blank **`har`** in the `/h/` slot (or **`war`** on `/w/`).

> `yol har zahelan vowurel.`
>
> y-question | h-when | z-Ahelan | v-walk
>
> "When does Ahelan walk?"

| Agebon | English (statement, bare) | English (under question, bare) |
|--------|---------------------------|--------------------------------|
| **`har`** / **`war`** | *sometimes* | *When?* / *In what case?* |

### Where? {#where}

To ask *where?*, write an [extra-noun hook](hooks.md#extra-noun) (a short word like *at*, *in*, or *from* that brings in a `/b/` noun), then the blank **`bar`** in the `/b/` slot after it. The hook picks which *where* you mean.

> `yol ol bar zahelan vowurel.`
>
> y-question | [at | b-wh] | z-Ahelan | v-walk
>
> "Where does Ahelan walk?"

> `yol al bar zudozol vezuvol.`
>
> y-question | [in | b-wh] | z-dog | v-sleep
>
> "What does the dog sleep in?"

| Agebon | English |
<!-- cheat-sheet: talking -->
|--------|---------|
| **`ol bar`** | *Where (at)?* |
| **`al bar`** | *In what?* |
| **`ul bar`** | *Where from?* |
| **`el bar`** | *For what?* |
| **`ol bor`** | *Anywhere?* |
| **`ol bur`** | *Where else?* |

Answer with the hook and the landmark, as a [citation](word-endings.md#citation-forms): `ol bahehal.` ("At the house.").

**Compare with:** *when?* is **`har`** on `/h/`. Occasions are times or cases, so a place never goes there.

### Whose? {#whose}

To ask *whose?*, write the hook **`em`** (*used by*) after the thing, then the blank **`bar`**: the same shape as *where?*. Because **`em`** says the `/b/` noun uses the thing, the question asks *who uses it?*

> `yol zudozol em bar vowurel.`
>
> y-question | [z-dog | [used-by | b-wh]] | v-walk
>
> "Whose dog walks?"

Answer with the hook and the user, as a [citation](word-endings.md#citation-forms): `em buzozen.` ("Uzozen's."). For ownership rather than use, see [`gogunum`](joins.md#scope-fence-p-join).

### How? {#how}

To ask *how?*, use the same shape as *where?*: a word that says how the answer relates to the event, then the blank **`blar`**. For manner (*in what way?*), that word is [**`hewom`**](relations.md) *like*, so the question asks *like what?*

> `yol hewom bar zuzozen vowurel.`
>
> y-question | h-like | b-wh | z-Uzozen | v-walk
>
> "How does Uzozen walk?" (*like what?*)

To ask about means (*with what?*), use the *using* hook with the blank.

### Why? {#why}

To ask *why?*, write a [condition word](causation.md) (*because*, *so that*, *in case*), then the blank **`blar`**. Pick the word for the kind of reason you want.

> `yol thahum bar zuzozen vowurel.`
>
> y-question | th-because | b-wh | z-Uzozen | v-walk
>
> "Why does Uzozen walk?" (*because of what?*)

> `yol hovam bar zuzozen vowurel.`
>
> y-question | h-so-that | b-wh | z-Uzozen | v-walk
>
> "What does Uzozen walk for?"

| Agebon | English |
<!-- cheat-sheet: talking -->
|--------|---------|
| **`thahum bar`** | *Why?* (*because of what?*) |
| **`hovam bar`** | *What for?* (*for what purpose?*) |
| **`thohom bar`** | *Under what condition?* / *In what case?* |

### A blank inside a dependent {#blank-in-dependent}

English puts *who* in different places in *Do you know who walks?* and *Who do you think walks?*. In the first, the inner clause asks; in the second, the main question asks. In Agebon the blank stays in the inner clause both times (it never moves to the front of the outer sentence), and the stand-in's vowel says which question it belongs to. **`dorl`** (*whether*) is itself question-like, so its blank belongs to it, and the outer **`yol`** is yes/no. **`darl`** (*that*) is statement-like and cannot ask, so its blank belongs to the outer **`yol`**.

> `yol zawan vanam dorl zar vowurel.`
>
> y-question | z-listener | v-knowledge | d-whether-clause | z-wh | v-walk
>
> "Do you know who walks?"

> `yol zawan vevemol darl zar vowurel.`
>
> y-question | z-listener | v-think | d-that-clause | z-wh | v-walk
>
> "Who do you think walks?"

If the outer sentence has a blank of its own, that blank is the main question and the inner one stays with `dorl`: `yol zar vanam dorl zar vowurel.` is *Who knows who walks?* Under a statement, `dorl` plus a blank is a [reported question](#embedded-whether).

### Yes/no with single-item / standalone
<a id="yes-no-single-item-standalone"></a>
<a id="under-question"></a>

A *yes/no* question can also check a **named** item: put a single-item join after it instead of a fill-ask blank. Single-item **-l** (or usual **-n**) asks the listener to confirm that this one item alone is meant (*Just Uzozen?*, *Has to be Uzozen?*). Single-item **-m** offers it (*How about Ahelan?*). **`…ul`** / **`…um`** / **`…un`** confirm or offer a denial (*Not Uzozen?*). Standalone **-l** / **-m** check emptiness (*Nothing?*, *Got nothing?*). Fill-ask still uses **…ar** / **…or** / **…er** / **…ur**.

> `yol zuzozen zal.`
>
> y-question | [z-Uzozen | z-and]
>
> "Just Uzozen?"

> `yol zahelan zam.`
>
> y-question | [z-Ahelan | z-and.open]
>
> "How about Ahelan?"

**Compare with:** *who* / *which* still uses join **-r** ([fill-ask](#fill-ask-r)).

### Four kinds of question {#four-kinds-of-question}

English *who?* can ask four different things. You may not know the answer, or know it and want to see whether the listener does, or have known it once together and forgotten, or the answer may not exist yet because nobody has chosen. Put a [kind indefinite](joins.md#kind-indefinites) in the blank: its knowledge vowel says which of the four you are asking, and its kind letter says what kind of answer fits.

> `yol zwoyar dogewel vumewol.`
>
> y-question | z-wh.person.withheld | d-key | v-find
>
> "Who found the key?" (I know; will you say?)

| Agebon | Kind of question | English | Cue |
|--------|------------------|---------|-----|
| `yol ol bwuhar zuzozen vagedal.` | a real question: there is an answer, and I don't know it | *Where does Uzozen eat?* | **u** ≈ unsaid |
| `yol ol bwohar zuzozen vagedal.` | a quiz: I know the answer; do you? | *Where does Uzozen eat?* (I know) | **o** ≈ one I hold |
| `yol ol bwehar zayan vagedal.` | remind me: we both knew it | *Where was it we ate, again?* | **e** ≈ echo of what we both held |
| `yol ol bwahar zayan vagedal.` | a choice: nothing is picked yet, so you choose | *Where shall we eat?* | **a** ≈ add: any that fits could be added |

Asking what you already know, to catch someone out, is a common move in an argument. An `o` blank puts the asker's knowledge on the table, so the trap becomes an open quiz. Plain `yol zar` already asks a real question, so a `u` blank adds only the kind (*who?*, not *what?*).

The join vowel keeps its [arity](#fill-ask-arity): `yol ol bwuhur zuzozen vagedal.` is *Where else does Uzozen eat?* **-x** after the blank asks for several, as on `zarx`.

> `yol zwuyarx vowurel.`
>
> y-question | z-wh.person.unknown-x | v-walk
>
> "Who all walks?" (several, I expect)

On `/v/` the blank asks *do what?*, and its kind letter is always `v`.

> `yol vwavar zayan.`
>
> y-question | v-wh.unpicked | z-interlocutors
>
> "What shall we do?"

Like a join word, a kind indefinite has no stem. The `w` right after the role letter shows it, and the consonant after its first vowel tells it from a [tag](pronouns.md#tag-pronouns).

#### Refusing the question {#refusing-a-blank}

A typed blank takes something for granted: `zwuyar` that a person did it, `bwehar` that there is a place you both know. The listener can refuse that with the same kind indefinite on **-l** or **-m**.

> `yol zwuyar dogewel vumewol.`
>
> y-question | z-wh.person.unknown | d-key | v-find
>
> "Who found the key?"

> `zwuyal.`
>
> z-none.person.here
>
> "No person did."

> `ol bweham.`
>
> [at | b-none.place.shared.open]
>
> "Nowhere we both know, as far as I know." (so say which)

### Rhetorical question {#rhetorical}

A **rhetorical question** is shaped like a question, but you are not waiting for a reply: you think the answer is obvious. Write statement **`yal`** right before **`yol`**. The statement word says you already hold the answer; the question word gives the body its question shape. The body is an ordinary yes/no question or fill-ask.

> `yal yol zar vegemal.`
>
> y-statement | y-question | z-wh | v-care
>
> "Who cares?" (nobody does)

> `yal yol zuzozen vowurel.`
>
> y-statement | y-question | z-Uzozen | v-walk
>
> "Doesn't Uzozen walk?" (of course Uzozen does)

Soft **`yam yol`** muses rather than insists: you lean toward an answer but put it forward lightly, as if thinking aloud.

> `yam yol zar vegemal.`
>
> y-soft-statement | y-question | z-wh | v-care
>
> "Who really cares, though?"

The listener may still reply, but no reply is owed. Only **`yal`** or **`yam`** stacks before the question word **`yol`** / **`yom`** this way.

A [typed blank](#four-kinds-of-question) after **`yal yol`** keeps the speaker's claim to the answer. `o` repeats that claim, `e` says the answer is one you both know, and `a` says the obvious answer is a choice. A `u` blank would say the speaker does not know which one, so it never follows **`yal yol`**.

> `yal yol ol bwehar zayan vagedal.`
>
> y-statement | y-question | [at | b-wh.place.shared] | z-interlocutors | v-eat
>
> "Where did we eat, as if you didn't know?"

**Compare with:** a [tag](#tags) after the sentence (`…. yol yael.`) asks the listener to confirm. A rhetorical question asks for nothing.
