# Sakes (`/ɡ/` / `/th/` / `/w/`)
<a id="sakes"></a>
<a id="sake-ascription"></a>

English *good for me* and *costs me* say what something does for a person. Agebon names that payoff as a **sake**: something good for a person, a psychological payoff you can name (autonomy, competence, understanding, purpose, relatedness, beneficence, pleasure, physical, or an unspecified sake). It means *for your sake* (what is good for you), not *for argument's sake*. A sake word also carries your **stance**, how the thing stands toward that sake: it **serves** the sake or **detracts from** it. An unmet sake is a cost to name, not a lack in you.

## Beginner {#beginner}

Put the sake word on `/ɡ/` after a **noun you keep**, to say how that belonging stands toward the sake. Put it on `/th/` to say how the whole **event** stands toward it. When the noun is not yours, personal possession shows how to point at it instead.

### Sake inventory {#sake-inventory}

Nine published roots are the sakes you can name, and no other root takes the sake form. Pick one row. With mid-word **`th`** and a stance vowel after it, that root means the sake in the English column, not the everyday object in **Same root as**.

| Agebon | Use | English | Same root as | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|--------------|-----|
| **`una`** | autonomy sake | *autonomy* (choice, agency, self-direction) | `unal` *ballot* | 🗳️: voting is choosing for yourself |
| **`uwa`** | competence sake | *competence* (efficacy, skill, getting things to work) | `uwal` *tool* | 🧰: the kit that gets things working |
| **`owo`** | understanding sake | *understanding* (making sense, learning, discovery) | `owol` *lightbulb* | 💡: the light goes on when it makes sense |
| **`ure`** | purpose sake | *purpose* (meaning, direction, an aim that matters) | `urel` *compass* | 🧭: a heading worth following |
| **`awo`** | relatedness sake | *relatedness* (connection, belonging, care) | `awol` *knot* | 🪢: ties people together |
| **`ewa`** | beneficence sake | *beneficence* (making someone better off) | `ewal` *present* | 🎁: a gift leaves someone better off |
| **`odo`** | pleasure sake | *pleasure* (enjoyment, comfort, aesthetic payoff) | `odol` *strawberry* | 🍓: sweetness you enjoy |
| **`oba`** | physical sake | *physical* (health, exercise, food, sleep, shelter, safety from harm) | `obal` *lungs* | 🫁: breathing easy means the body is well |
| **`uda`** | a sake without picking among the eight | *unspecified sake* | `udal` *egg* | 🥚: not yet a specific kind |

**Compare with:** `zunal` is a *ballot*, and `gunal` is *ballot-like*. With mid-word **`tha`** / **`thu`**, **`una`** is the *autonomy* sake.

**Compare with:** *when* / *always* / *never* on `/h/` is a [restrictor](restrictors.md). This page names a **sake**.

### Which sake? {#which-sake}

Each sake answers a different question about what pays off. Ask the question, then pick the row.

| Sake | What pays off |
|------|---------------|
| autonomy | *I chose it.* |
| competence | *It worked; I can do this.* |
| understanding | *It makes sense; I learned something.* |
| purpose | *It matters.* |
| relatedness | *We're connected.* |
| beneficence | *Someone is better off because of me.* |
| pleasure | *It feels good.* |
| physical | *My body is cared for and safe.* |

**Compare with:** competence is how **well** it goes; purpose is whether it is **worth doing**. A failed try at something that matters still serves purpose, and it detracts from competence. Mastering a pointless puzzle serves competence, not purpose.

**Compare with:** competence is whether it **works**; understanding is whether it **makes sense**. You can fix a machine you do not understand, and understand stars you can never change. A task that is too easy can meet competence and still leave understanding unmet: that is the boredom of nothing new to learn.

**Compare with:** something new for its own sake (new sights, variety) is pleasure. Understanding pays off when the new thing **fits**: an hour of headlines can serve pleasure and leave understanding unmet.

**Compare with:** relatedness lives in the **bond**, and it runs both ways. Beneficence lives in the **effect**: your act left someone better off, with or without a bond. An anonymous donation serves beneficence and leaves relatedness alone; an easy evening with friends serves relatedness with no one helped. Being helped is not your beneficence: it serves whichever sake of yours the help met.

**Compare with:** purpose needs an aim that matters to you; beneficence needs someone who is actually better off. Writing a novel serves purpose with no one helped; fixing a stranger's tire serves beneficence with little purpose. When an act does both, name both.

**Compare with:** autonomy is *I chose it*; purpose is *it matters*. Being handed a task you believe in serves purpose and can still detract from autonomy.

### Met (`tha`): serves the sake {#met}

To say that a belonging of yours **serves** a sake (pays it off), write that thing as a noun, then a `/ɡ/` word: sake root, mid-word **`th`**, **`a`**, then an ending. That adjective describes the noun. The ending says the payoff's **time horizon**: whether the payoff lasts or arrives right away. With **-m**, you leave the time horizon unstated:

> `zahehal guwatham.`
>
> z-house | g-competence-met-any-term
>
> "My house serves competence."

### Time horizon (endings on met) {#time-horizon-endings-on-met}
<a id="sake-contact"></a>
<a id="settled-to-passing"></a>

On a *serves* word, the ending says **when the payoff lands**: **-l** lasting, **-m** unstated, **-r** immediate. That order, from **-l** (most settled) through **-m** (the default) to **-r** (most passing), is the **settled-to-passing scale**, and other sake endings reuse it. Neither end is better; a good day needs both. If you do not know, use **-m**.

<!-- cheat-sheet: why-allowed -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| `…thal` | serves the sake over time | *lasting* | **-l** ≈ lasting |
| `…tham` | serves the sake; time horizon unstated (soft default if unknown) | *serves* | **-m** ≈ open |
| `…thar` | serves the sake right away | *immediate* | **-r** ≈ right now |

> `zewal gawothal.`
>
> z-present | g-relatedness-met-lasting
>
> "My gift serves relatedness in the long term."

> `zanal guwathar.`
>
> z-book | g-competence-met-immediate
>
> "My book serves competence right now."

An immediate payoff is often exactly what is needed:

> `zodol godothar.`
>
> z-strawberry | g-pleasure-met-immediate
>
> "My strawberry is a pleasure right now."

### Unmet (`thu`): detracts from the sake
<a id="sake-changeability"></a>

To say that a belonging of yours **detracts from** a sake, write that thing as a noun, then a `/ɡ/` word: sake root, mid-word **`th`**, **`u`**, then an ending.

That ending is **changeability**: how lasting the harm to the sake is, on the same settled-to-passing scale as met. If you do not know, use **-m**.

| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| `…thul` | the detriment is fixed as far as you can tell | *irreversible* | **-l** ≈ lasting |
| `…thum` | effort or circumstance may soften the detriment (soft default if unknown) | *modifiable* | **-m** ≈ open to change |
| `…thur` | the detriment has no lasting impact | *passing* | **-r** ≈ right now |

> `zanal guwathum.`
>
> z-book | g-competence-unmet-modifiable
>
> "My book detracts from competence." (modifiable)

One noun can carry both a **`tha`** word and a **`thu`** word. The pair can run either way: *good now, costly later* (an immediate **`tha`** word and a lasting **`thu`** word), or *costly now, good later*:

> `zanal godothur guwathal.`
>
> [z-book | g-pleasure-unmet-passing | g-competence-met-lasting]
>
> "My book is a slog for now, but it builds lasting competence."

### Word shape {#word-shape}
<a id="sake-stake"></a>

A sake word is a sake root with mid-word **`th`**, a stance vowel, and an ending. On `/ɡ/` it describes a **noun you keep**; on `/th/` it describes the **event**. A noun that is not yours takes the stimulus word, with the same sake word on `/w/` immediately before it.

A sake word ends in **-l**, **-m**, or **-r**, from that stance’s table. It never takes **-n**. The stance itself is one vowel after **`th`**.

<!-- cheat-sheet: why-allowed -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| **`tha`** | the belonging or event pays off this sake | *serves* / *met* | **a** ≈ add (the payoff is in) |
| **`thu`** | the belonging or event subtracts from this sake | *detracts from* / *unmet* | **u** ≈ undo (the sake is taken down) |

On `/th/`, the sake word says how the event itself stands toward the sake:

> `zuzozen vezuyel thuwatham.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-tell | th-competence-met-any-term
>
> "Uzozen talks, and that boosts my confidence."

Without mid-word **`th`**, the root keeps its everyday sense: `gunal` is *ballot-like*, and `hodol` is the adverb from *strawberry*.

### Personal possession {#personal-possession}
<a id="stimulus"></a>

A sake word on `/ɡ/` also says the noun is **yours**, with no separate word for *my*. That shortcut makes thanks and costs cheap to say about the things you already call *my X*. To say who owns something without naming a sake, use `gogunum` plus `/b/`. Someone else's thing uses that pair too, never a sake word alone on `/ɡ/`.

When the noun is **not** yours (weather, a gathering, another person’s church), follow it with the adjective **`goyem`**, *as stimulus*: the thing you react to, not something you keep. Put the same met or unmet word you would use on `/ɡ/` on `/w/` immediately before **`goyem`**, as extra detail on it. (cue: ☝️ *point*: you point at the thing; you do not hold it)

> `zabowam wunathur goyem.`
>
> z-gathering | [w-autonomy-unmet-passing | g-stimulus]
>
> "The gathering detracts from autonomy right now." (passing)

The same `/w/` sake word can sit before any adjective. Then it is about **that property**: the tea's heat is what serves pleasure, not the tea as a whole. **`goyem`** is the case where the property is the noun itself, as the thing you react to.

> `zududal wodotham gohamul.`
>
> z-tea | [w-pleasure-met-any-term | g-hot]
>
> "The tea is pleasantly hot."

As a plain noun, `zoyel` is *emphasis*. **`goyem`** with no `/w/` sake word before it names no sake. An event (*this raining*, *this telling*) takes its sake word on `/th/` on the clause, whether or not it is yours.

| Agebon | Use | English | Same root as | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|--------------|-----|
| **`goyem`** | unowned noun as sake-target | *as stimulus* | `oyel` *emphasis* | ☝️: you point at it rather than keep it |

## Intermediate {#intermediate}

### Emotion compose {#emotion-compose}

English emotion words (*anxious*, *resentful*, *proud*) often do **judgment or explanation** work: one label packs in what is at stake and why it feels that way. In Agebon you name the pieces instead, on **one word**: the [sake stance](#sakes) on the situation, a **locus** (where the feeling sits or what it points at), and a **motion ending** (how the feeling moves). The sake word keeps its stance vowel and its ending letter, then adds the locus (the vowels of a [hook](hooks.md#extra-noun)) and the motion ending. You can then say what you make of a situation without leaning on a single emotion word.

Put that word where any sake word goes: on `/ɡ/` after a belonging, on `/w/` immediately before [stimulus](#stimulus) **`goyem`** when the noun is not yours, or on `/th/` by itself when the feeling has no object. For the bare noun *emotion*, with no judgment to explain, use the compound `zabolohalal` (the water of the heart).

> `zezuyel wuwathuraor goyem.`
>
> z-speech | [w-competence-unmet-passing-CIRCUM-SURGING | g-stimulus]
>
> "I'm anxious about the dialogue." (my competence is at stake for now, the feeling hangs over the room, and it comes in waves)

Build it from the plain sake word: `wuwathur` (competence, unmet, passing) + **`ao`** (*over*: CIRCUM) + **-r** (SURGING) = `wuwathuraor`. The sake word's own ending (**-r** here) now sits mid-word and keeps its meaning.

The locus uses the hook vowels you already know. A **placement** locus says where the feeling sits:

| Locus | Hook | Use | English |
|-------|------|-----|---------|
| **`a`** | *in* | INTERNAL | *mine, held inside* |
| **`ao`** | *over* | CIRCUM | *the atmosphere of the situation* |
| **`uo`** | *through* | UNPLACED | *I can't place where it comes from* (no single source) |

A **direction** locus says what the charge points at:

| Locus | Hook | Use | English |
|-------|------|-----|---------|
| **`o`** | *at* | AIMED | *the charge lands on a target* (*annoyed at*, *grateful to*, *pressing for*) |
| **`oe`** | *toward* | SEEKING | *turning to someone for comfort or safety* |
| **`ue`** | *against* | RESISTING | *pushing back* (*objecting to*, fighting) |
| **`e`** | *for* | ON-BEHALF | *felt for someone's sake* (*upset for*) |
| **`u`** | *from* | AWAY | *moving off* (*avoiding*, fleeing, disengaging, hiding) |
| **`ua`** | *out of* | COLLAPSE | *out of fight: giving up* (*defeated by*) |
| **`ae`** | *using* | FAWN | *using yourself to placate* (*appeasing*) |

A direction names the **dominant** pull when feelings mix; use two feeling words for two pulls. AIMED lands on a party or a goal; RESISTING opposes an outcome or a demand. ON-BEHALF is your own feeling about someone else's stake; FAWN is placating someone for your own safety.

Name the landmark with a `bl-` word right after the feeling (after **`goyem`** when the feeling is on `/w/`): the target of a direction, or the situation for CIRCUM. INTERNAL and UNPLACED take no `/b/`: they have no landmark. A direction with no `/b/` points at the stimulus noun when there is one, otherwise at someone unnamed.

> `zonal wawothumol goyem blolazun.`
>
> z-memo | [[w-relatedness-unmet-modifiable-AIMED-STILL | g-stimulus] | bl-Olazun]
>
> "I resent Olazun over the memo, holding it in."

The motion ending says how the feeling moves, on the [settled-to-passing scale](#settled-to-passing): **-l** is the most settled, **-m** is the ordinary case, and **-r** the most passing, lasting only right now. (cue: a feeling is water: still, a current, or a wave)

| Motion ending | Use | English | Cue |
|---------------|-----|---------|-----|
| **-l** | STILL | not moving: held, frozen, numb | **-l** ≈ *lasting*: still water |
| **-m** | FLOWING | moving steadily, at a pace you can ride | **-m** ≈ the ordinary case: a current |
| **-r** | SURGING | moving in waves or spikes: rising, falling, swinging | **-r** ≈ *right now*: a wave |

Motion is how the feeling moves, not how strong it is. Strong but steady anger is flowing, and so is calm contentment. Fury held in check and a numb flatness are both still. Surging and still are information, not verdicts: surging can be a cue to ground; still can be a cue to rest, or to re-engage gently. Freezing is STILL: AWAY with **-l** is *frozen, wanting out*.

Say how **strong** the feeling is with a [degree word](clause.md#degree-w) on `/w/` right before the feeling word. Before a `/ɡ/` feeling it grades that adjective as usual. Before a `/w/` feeling, it grades the feeling, not the stimulus. With no degree word, strength is unstated.

> `zebewem wohalal guwathamar.`
>
> z-draft | [w-ocean | g-competence-met-any-term-INTERNAL-SURGING]
>
> "I'm overwhelmingly proud of my draft."

> `zonal wamezom wawothumom goyem blolazun.`
>
> z-memo | [[w-small | w-relatedness-unmet-modifiable-AIMED-FLOWING | g-stimulus] | bl-Olazun]
>
> "I feel a mild, steady irritation at Olazun over the memo."

The locus plus the motion ending is the **emotion tail**. It goes on met **`tha`**, motive **`tho`**, and unmet **`thu`** words. Prescription **`the`** is advice, not a feeling, so it takes no tail. A word with no tail (`wuwathur`) is an ordinary sake word.

<!-- cheat-sheet: why-allowed -->
| Agebon | English |
|---------|---------|
| `zezuyel wuwathuraor goyem` | *anxious about the dialogue* (competence at stake, temporary; hangs over the room; surging) |
| `zozowum wawothumual goyem` | *given up over the division* (unmet relatedness; out of fight; still) |
| `zebewem guwathamar` | *proud of the draft* (met competence; held inside; surging) |
| `zonal wawothumem goyem bluzozen` | *upset for Uzozen about the memo* (unmet relatedness; on Uzozen's behalf; flowing) |
| `zezuyel wawothumum goyem blolazun` | *avoiding Olazun over the dialogue* (unmet relatedness; moving off from Olazun; flowing) |
| `zezuyel wawothumaem goyem blolazun` | *placating Olazun about the dialogue* (unmet relatedness; appeasing Olazun; flowing) |
| `zonal wawothumoem goyem bluzozen` | *upset about the memo and turning to Uzozen for comfort* (unmet relatedness; seeking Uzozen; flowing) |
| `zonal wawothumuer goyem` | *objecting to the memo* (unmet relatedness; pushing against the memo; surging) |
| `zezuyel wurethumal goyem` | *the dialogue feels pointless* (unmet purpose; held inside; still) |
| `zebewem gewathamam` | *glad my draft helped* (met beneficence; held inside; flowing) |
| `zezuyel wamezom wuwathuruom goyem` | *a little uneasy about the dialogue, and I can't say why* (competence at stake, temporary; slightly; unplaced; flowing) |
| `zezuyel wuwathumuer guzezam` | *the dialogue is frustratingly slow* (unmet competence on the slowness; pushing against it; surging) |

Raw feeling (contacting a sensation without judgment) may go unlabeled. To name a feeling without explaining it, its lexicon root works as an ordinary [property](predication.md) word: `zuwon gunemul` (*I am angry*). Use the full compose when an emotion word would have done judgment work.

**Compare with:** *could be* uses [MAY](knowing.md#may) (`thomam`). *Because of* is a causal claim with the because pole. The stimulus says what the feeling is **about**; a direction `/b/` says what it is **aimed at**; the because pole says what **caused** it.

### Prescription (`the`): ought this act for this sake
<a id="sake-force"></a>

When English puts *should* / *ought to* on **doing** something for a named sake (*Ahelan ought to sing; it's good for their health*), put a `/th/` word on the clause: sake root, mid-word **`th`**, **`e`**, then an ending. The clause's verb names the act that ought to count toward the sake. The sentence then says the act *ought* to happen; it does not report that the act is already happening.

The ending says why you think the move is welcome. Use **-l** when the person invited this move, explicitly or through a clear standing invitation. Use **-m** for an unsolicited offer meant to serve the sake. Use **-r** for a trial: the move is worth trying to see whether it serves the sake, not a settled recommendation. These endings describe why the move is offered and what it aims at, not whether it succeeds. How firmly you put the act on the listener is a separate choice, made by the speech-act word on `/y/` (**`yel`** / **`yem`**, **`yal`** / **`yam`**). If none of the three reasons applies, drop **`the`**.

**Compare with:** a [command](speech-moves.md#speech-act-beginner) **`yel`** tells someone to act outright, without naming the sake or why the move is wanted. **`the`** is an *ought* tied to a sake (*ought to, to advance this sake*), with the reason on the ending.

**Compare with:** to ask someone to act for **your** sake, make it a [request](speech-moves.md#speech-act) (**`yem`**) with motive **`tho`**. **`the`** is advice.

<!-- cheat-sheet: why-allowed -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| `…thel` | the person invited this kind of move | *invited* | **-l** ≈ lasting: their invitation stands |
| `…them` | an unsolicited offer meant to serve the sake | *offered* | **-m** ≈ open offer |
| `…ther` | a move worth trying, to see whether it serves the sake | *worth a try* | **-r** ≈ right now (a trial run) |

A sake word with no `/b/` is about **your** sake, so advice for the other person's own good names them in a `bl-` word right after the sake word. In the same sentence, that is the [themself](pronouns.md#themself) pointer **`baxer`**. The `/b/` says whose stake the act serves, not why they would do it.

> `zuzozen vezuyel thawothem blaxer.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-tell | [th-relatedness-ought-offered | bl-←agent.self]
>
> "Uzozen ought to talk; it would help them feel connected." (unasked advice)

> `zahelan vezuyol thobathel blaxer.`
>
> z-Ahelan | v-sing | [th-physical-ought-invited | bl-←agent.self]
>
> "Ahelan ought to sing; it's good for their health." (Ahelan asked for advice)

> `zolazun vabuyel thawother blaxer.`
>
> z-Olazun | v-bow | [th-relatedness-ought-trial | bl-←agent.self]
>
> "Olazun could try bowing, to see whether it helps them feel connected."

A move meant to **prevent** harm to the sake is a *so that … not* dependent ([so that](dependents.md#so-that)): **`hovam`** + **`blurl`**, then the outcome to keep off.

> `zolazun vabuyel thawothem blaxer hovam blurl zuzozen velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | v-bow | [th-relatedness-ought-offered | bl-←agent.self] | [h-so-that | bl-lest-clause] | z-Uzozen | v-departure
>
> "Olazun ought to bow to stay connected, so that Uzozen does not leave."

### Motive (`tho`): time horizon
<a id="sake-preference"></a>

English *doing this for …* and *for the sake of …* name a **motive**: why the action is happening, as a description, not a *should*. Write `/th/` on the clause with the sake root, mid-word **`th`**, **`o`**, then an ending. That ending is the **time horizon**, on the same settled-to-passing scale as [met](#time-horizon-endings-on-met): whether the act serves the sake over time or right away. If you do not know, use **-m**. To say this is the usual reason, add **`hual`** after the motive word (**`…thom`** plus **`hual`**).

> `zuwon vezuyel thawothom.`
>
> z-speaker | v-tell | th-relatedness-motive-any-term
>
> "I talk to feel connected."

The reason is yours. On someone else's act, a motive word still names what the act serves **for you**: `zuzozen vezuyel thawothom` is *Uzozen talks, and to me it is about feeling connected*. It does not say why Uzozen talks; their reasons are in their mind, and Agebon has no way to say a reason belongs to whoever is acting.

**Not the same job as:** prescription **`the`**, which says the act *ought* to serve this sake. **`tho`** describes why the act is happening: *doing it for this sake*.

On `/ɡ/`, the same stance gives **your** noun’s purpose (*my gift for relatedness*).

**For other English *for*, use:**

- an intended **event** (*walks so that Olazun sits*): [so that](dependents.md#so-that) (**`hovam`**), not a sake
- the other side of a swap (*a book for a hammer*): [exchange](relations.md#exchange) (`heham`)
- acting for someone (*tells on behalf of Olazun*): [proxy](relations.md#proxy) (`hanom`)

A reason held **to keep a cost off** is a *so that … not* dependent ([so that](dependents.md#so-that), **`hovam blurl`**), as with prescription.

<!-- cheat-sheet: why-allowed -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| `…thol` | does this for the sake over time | *lasting* | **-l** ≈ lasting |
| `…thom` | does this for the sake; time horizon unstated (soft default if unknown) | *for* | **-m** ≈ open |
| `…thor` | does this for the sake right away | *immediate* | **-r** ≈ right now |

Neither end is better; a good day needs both, and doing something for a payoff right now is often exactly what is needed. When the **situation** is what pulls the reason, say so with the [because pole](causation.md) and the situation as `/b/`.

A motive word takes the [emotion tail](#emotion-compose) too. The feeling is then a pull toward the payoff, as English *curious* or *eager* is:

> `zezuyel wowothomom goyem.`
>
> z-speech | [w-understanding-motive-any-term-AIMED-FLOWING | g-stimulus]
>
> "I'm curious about the dialogue."

### Which ending table?

Beginner already used *serves* and *detracts from*. Intermediate adds *ought this act for* and *doing for*. Pick the ending table that matches the stance vowel.

<!-- cheat-sheet: why-allowed -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| **`tha`** | [time horizon](#time-horizon-endings-on-met) endings | *serves* / *met* | **a** ≈ add |
| **`the`** | [invited / offered / trial](#sake-force) endings | *ought this act for this sake* | **e** ≈ order (instruct / ought) |
| **`tho`** | [time horizon](#sake-preference) endings | *doing for this sake* | **o** ≈ one (one sake as the reason) |
| **`thu`** | [changeability](#sake-changeability) endings | *detracts from* / *unmet* | **u** ≈ undo |

### Attachment sites

Beginner already attached `/ɡ/` after a noun and `/th/` on the clause for met and unmet. The rest of the map:

| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| `g…tha…` after a noun | praise: **your** noun serves the sake | *my gift (serves relatedness)* (`gawotha…`) |
| `g…thu…` after a noun | criticism: **your** noun detracts from the sake | *my book (detracts from competence)* (`guwathu…`) |
| `g…tho…` after a noun | **your** noun’s purpose is this sake | *my gift for relatedness* (`gawotho…`) |
| `/w/` sake + `goyem` | the same stances on a noun that is **not** yours | *the gathering (detracts from autonomy)* (`wunathu… goyem`) |
| `/w/` sake + another adjective | the stance on that one property | *pleasantly hot* (`wodotham gohamul`) |
| `gl-` + sake word | the same adjective before the **belonging** | `glawothal zewal` |
| `th…tha…` on the clause | the event serves the sake | *tells: competence is met* (`thuwatha…`) |
| `th…the…` on the clause | prescription: the act ought to serve the sake | *ought … for this sake* (`thawothe…`); ending = [invited / offered / trial](#sake-force) |
| `th…tho…` on the clause | motive | *I talk (to feel connected)* (`thawotho…`) |
| `/w/` before a sake `/ɡ/` | extra detail on that adjective | *very relatedness-serving* |

Prescription **`the`** goes on `/th/` only: it is about an act, and a noun is not an act. For what a noun is **for**, use **`tho`**. On **`the`**, use **-l** for an invitation, **-m** for an offer, and **-r** for a trial. On **`thu`** and **`tho`**, prefer **-m** when you cannot tell which ending fits.

For several sakes, write several `/ɡ/` or `/th/` words (`guwathal gawothal`), one sake each. The noun or verb the sake word describes keeps its own ending, which says whether it is concrete or abstract.

A `/w/` word before a sake word on `/ɡ/` grades that adjective. A `/w/` sake word immediately before **`goyem`** is the sake itself.

> `zuwon vezuyel thuwathal thawothom.`
>
> z-speaker | v-tell | th-competence-met-lasting | th-relatedness-motive-any-term
>
> "I talk to feel connected, and it builds my confidence for the long run."

### Whose stake {#whose-stake}

A sake word with no `/b/` is about **your** sake. To say it is someone else's, put that person in a `bl-` word right after the sake word. This works on every stance and every placement: met, unmet and motive on a noun, and met, unmet, prescription and motive on the clause.

This holds on someone else's act too. `zuzozen vezuyel thawotham` is *Uzozen talks, and that helps me feel connected*: what the act serves is your stake, as you see it. A `/b/` names whose stake the act serves, not whose reason it is.

> `zuzozen vezuyel thawothom blolazun.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-tell | [th-relatedness-motive-any-term | bl-Olazun]
>
> "Uzozen talks for Olazun's sake." (Olazun's relatedness)

> `zolazun vabuyel thawothem bluzozen.`
>
> z-Olazun | v-bow | [th-relatedness-ought-offered | bl-Uzozen]
>
> "Olazun ought to bow, for Uzozen's relatedness." (unasked advice)

> `zewal gawothal blolazun.`
>
> z-present | [g-relatedness-met-lasting | bl-Olazun]
>
> "My gift serves Olazun's relatedness in the long term."

> `zabowam wawothul goyem blolazun.`
>
> z-gathering | [[w-relatedness-unmet-irreversible | g-stimulus] | bl-Olazun]
>
> "The gathering does lasting harm to Olazun's relatedness."

The `/b/` names whose **stake** it is, not who **owns** the noun. On `/ɡ/` the noun is still yours: `zewal gawothal blolazun` is *my gift, good for Olazun*. Someone else's thing is `gogunum` plus `/b/`, or **`goyem`**.

A feeling word with a direction tail already uses its `/b/` for the landmark ([emotion compose](#emotion-compose)), so it cannot also name whose stake.

**Compare with:** the *for* hook (`el bolazun`) names who the act is for without naming which sake.

### Feeling with no object {#feeling-no-object}

On the clause, a feeling word is about the clause's event, so English *moved that …* or *glad that …* puts the feeling on the clause it names:

> `zolazun vowurel thawothamar.`
>
> z-Olazun | v-walk | th-relatedness-met-any-term-INTERNAL-SURGING
>
> "I'm moved that Olazun walks."

With no event at all, a feeling word on `/th/` is a whole sentence about **you**: how you feel right now, with nothing as its object.

> `thuwathuruor.`
>
> th-competence-unmet-passing-UNPLACED-SURGING
>
> "I'm anxious."

A lone feeling can still name what caused it. The cause is not the object: the feeling is not *about* the rain.

> `thuwathuruor thahum blerulal.`
>
> th-competence-unmet-passing-UNPLACED-SURGING | [th-because | bl-rain]
>
> "I'm anxious, and the rain caused it."

**Compare with:** a lone noun before the feeling is an [existence](predication.md#existence) clause, so the noun's being there is the stimulus: `zolazun thuwathuruor.` is *anxious that Olazun is here*, not *Olazun is anxious*.

### Thanks and sorry {#thanks-sorry}

Agebon has no set phrase for *thank you*. Say that your sake **is met**: a lone `/th/` met word is a whole sentence about you, the same shape as a [feeling with no object](#feeling-no-object), and saying it to the one who acted is the thanks. Pick the sake that was actually served; the ending says how long the payoff lasts ([endings on met](#time-horizon-endings-on-met)). (cue: thanks = *my sake is met*)

> `thawotham.`
>
> th-relatedness-met-any-term
>
> "Thank you, that meant a lot to me."

> `thuwathal.`
>
> th-competence-met-lasting
>
> "Thanks, that sets me up for the long run."

Add an emotion tail to say how the thanks moves you:

> `thawothamar.`
>
> th-relatedness-met-any-term-INTERNAL-SURGING
>
> "Thank you, I'm so moved."

<!-- cheat-sheet: why-allowed -->
| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| `…thal` | the help lasts | *that will stay with me* |
| `…tham` | the help, horizon unstated (default) | *thank you* |
| `…thar` | the help landed right when needed | *thanks, that got me through* |

*Sorry* is about **their** sake, not yours. A lone unmet word would say *my* sake went unmet, so name whose sake in a `bl-` word right after it. The ending is the same [changeability](#sake-changeability) as on any unmet word, so it says how serious the harm is. (cue: sorry = *that detracted from your sake*)

> `thawothum blawan.`
>
> [th-relatedness-unmet-modifiable | bl-listener]
>
> "I'm sorry; that hurt your sense of connection."

<!-- cheat-sheet: why-allowed -->
| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| `…thul blawan` | the harm is lasting | *I'm deeply sorry* |
| `…thum blawan` | the harm can be softened (default) | *I'm sorry* |
| `…thur blawan` | the harm is passing | *my bad* |

The `/b/` can name anyone: `thawothum blolazun.` owns up to a harm to Olazun, even when you are telling someone else.

**Compare with:** a met word with a `/b/` person (`thawotham blolazun.`) says the act met **Olazun's** sake: *Olazun appreciated it*, not your thanks.

### Hopefully and other attitudes {#speaker-attitude}

English *hopefully*, *luckily*, and *worryingly* say how **you**, the speaker, feel about the whole claim. In Agebon, put the content root for that feeling on `/th/` with **-m**. It is always the speaker's attitude, never the subject's. For the subject's manner (*Uzozen walks hopefully*), use `/h/` (`hoguyem`).

| Agebon | Use | English | Same root as |
|--------|-----|---------|--------------|
| **`thoguyem`** | you want it, and it is still open | *hopefully* | `oguyel` *crossed-fingers* |
| **`tholezom`** | it went well by chance | *luckily* | `olezol` *ladybug* |
| **`thewemum`** | it concerns you | *worryingly* | `ewemul` *worried* |

> `zuzozen thoguyem vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | th-hope | v-walk
>
> "Hopefully Uzozen is walking."

Hope is not a way of **knowing**, so it cannot hold up a forecast by itself. To say *hopefully … will*, add a real evidence channel as well. Hope says how you feel about the claim; the channel says what backs it.

### Say words: permission, requirement, consent {#say-words}
<a id="permission"></a>
<a id="requirement"></a>
<a id="consent"></a>

English *may*, *must*, *may not*, and *they agreed* each say that someone has a say over an act. Agebon puts that in one **say word** on `/h/`. A grant, a ban, a demand, or a yes is said openly, so a say word needs no channel; like an [intention adverb](clause.md#intention-adverbs), it covers the whole clause. Write **`h`**, **`w`**, a scope vowel, whose say it is, the answer, and an ending.

> `zuzozen vowurel hwavam.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | h-episode-event-ALLOW-said
>
> "Uzozen may walk; I'm allowing it."

| Piece | Says | In `hwavam` |
|-------|------|-------------|
| `w` | a closed word with no root, as on a [tag](pronouns.md#tag-pronouns) | `w` |
| scope vowel | how far the say reaches: **`a`** this act (more below) | `a` |
| whose say | **`v`** someone over the act and outside it; **`z`** the doer; **`d`** the one it's done to; **`b`** the recipient | `v` |
| answer | **`a`** yes, allow (as in `yael`); **`u`** no, forbid (as in `yuel`); **`e`** require, ask for (**e** ≈ order); **`o`** their call (**o** ≈ pick) | `a` |
| ending | **-l** a rule, or a binding agreement (≈ law); **-m** a person said so (≈ mouth); **-r** nobody said: tolerated, assumed, or customary (≈ read the room) | **-m** |

If you do not know how it stands, use **-m**.

#### Someone over the act (`v`)

**`v`** is the say of someone outside the act: a rule, a person with the standing to decide, or custom. On **-m** that person is the speaker, unless a `bl-` word right after names someone else.

> `zuzozen vowurel hwavam blolazun.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [h-episode-event-ALLOW-said | bl-Olazun]
>
> "Olazun lets Uzozen walk."

> `zahelan vuhohol hwaval.`
>
> z-Ahelan | v-sit | h-episode-event-ALLOW-rule
>
> "The rules allow Ahelan to sit."

> `zolazun vezuvol hwaver.`
>
> z-Olazun | v-sleep | h-episode-event-REQUIRE-tacit
>
> "Olazun is supposed to sleep."

| Answer | **-l** the rules | **-m** a person | **-r** nobody said |
|--------|------------------|-----------------|--------------------|
| **`a`** | `hwaval` *the rules allow* | `hwavam` *you may* / *I'm letting you* | `hwavar` *no one's stopping it* |
| **`u`** | `hwavul` *the rules forbid* | `hwavum` *you may not* | `hwavur` *people won't like it* |
| **`e`** | `hwavel` *the rules require* | `hwavem` *you have to* | `hwaver` *it's customary* / *is supposed to* |
| **`o`** | `hwavol` *the rules leave it optional* | `hwavom` *it's up to you* | `hwavor` *nobody cares either way* |

When the speaker is in the clause, the speaker's own say is their part, not **`v`**, so a bare **`v`** on **-m** has no reading there. So *I have to walk* says who requires it: the rules (`zuwon vowurel hwavel.`), a person (`zuwon vowurel hwavem blolazun.`), custom (`zuwon vowurel hwaver.`), or you yourself (`zuwon vowurel hwazem.` *I'm making myself walk*). Telling an outside demand from one you put on yourself is the point of the split.

To **ask** for permission, put the clause under [ask](questions.md#question): `yom zuwon vuhohol hwavam.` (*May I sit?*). The question asks the listener, so the speaker may be in it. Grant it with [**`yaol.`**](questions.md#polar-stance) (*go ahead*).

**Compare with:** [prescription](#sake-force) **`the`** says the act *ought* to serve a named sake, a reason you can check; a say word says only who allows or demands it. A [command](speech-moves.md#speech-act-beginner) **`yel`** is the speaker telling the listener now; **`hwavem`** reports a demand, which may be someone else's.

#### Someone in the act: consent (`z` / `d` / `b`)

Consent is the say of someone **in** the act: their body, their things, their time. Write the role letter that person has in the clause: **`d`** the one it's done to, **`b`** the recipient, **`z`** the doer. They are already in the clause, so the say word needs no `bl-` word.

> `zuzozen vezuyel bahelan hwabam.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-tell | b-Ahelan | h-episode-recipient-ALLOW-said
>
> "Uzozen tells Ahelan, and Ahelan said yes to it."

> `zuzozen dolazun vawebol hwadam.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-Olazun | v-see | h-episode-patient-ALLOW-said
>
> "Uzozen looks at Olazun, and Olazun agreed."

> `zuzozen vezuyel bahelan bolazun bal hwabar.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-tell | [b-Ahelan | b-Olazun | b-and] | h-episode-recipient-ALLOW-tacit
>
> "Uzozen tells Ahelan and Olazun, assuming they are OK with it."

> `zolazun vezuvol hwazam.`
>
> z-Olazun | v-sleep | h-episode-agent-ALLOW-said
>
> "Olazun lets themself sleep."

The ending says how binding the yes is. **-l** is a binding agreement: they signed on in advance, and withdrawing has terms. **-m** is a yes they can take back at any time. **-r** is a yes nobody said, taken as given; it is allowed, but choosing it makes *I assumed* part of the sentence instead of hiding it inside *they were fine with it*.

| Answer | **`z`** the doer | **`d`** the one it's done to | **`b`** the recipient |
|--------|------------------|------------------------------|-----------------------|
| **`a`** | `hwazam` *lets themself* | `hwadam` *they agreed* | `hwabam` *the recipient agreed* |
| **`u`** | `hwazum` *won't let themself* | `hwadum` *they refused* | `hwabum` *the recipient refused* |
| **`e`** | `hwazem` *makes themself* | `hwadem` *at their request* | `hwabem` *at the recipient's request* |
| **`o`** | `hwazom` *their own choice* | `hwadom` *it's their call* | `hwabom` *the recipient's call* |

| Answer | **-l** binding | **-r** assumed |
|--------|----------------|----------------|
| **`a`** | `hwadal` *they signed on*, `hwazal` a vow | `hwadar` *they're probably OK with it* |
| **`e`** | `hwadel` *they're owed it*, `hwazel` *a rule they set themself* | `hwader` *they'd want it*, `hwazer` *feels they have to* |
| **`o`** | `hwadol` *their call, by agreement* | `hwazor` *presumably their own choice* |

- **`hwazer`** is the unspoken *should*: *I feel I have to* when nobody asked. Beside `hwavem blolazun` (someone asked), `hwavel` (the rules), and `hwazem` (pushing yourself), it shows where the pressure comes from.
- **`hwazel`** is a personal rule (*I don't drink on weekdays*). Naming a rigid rule as self-set makes it something to examine.
- **`hwader`** (*they'd want to know*) is a common excuse for acting on someone's behalf; **-r** marks it as an assumption, as `hwadar` does for consent.
- **`o`** hands the choice to that party: neither allowed, forbidden, nor required, but up to them. `hwazom` (*chose it freely*) differs from `hwazam` (*let themself*), which suggests a reluctance overcome. *No answer yet* needs no vowel: ask it (`yom zuzozen dolazun vawebol hwadam.`).

> `zolazun vezuvol hwazom.`
>
> z-Olazun | v-sleep | h-episode-agent-CHOICE-said
>
> "Olazun sleeps, by their own choice."

**Promise:** a vow (`hwazal`) commits you to an act, and said to someone it is a promise to them. `zuwon vowurel hwazal.`, said to someone, is *I promise (you) to walk*. A self-set rule (`hwazel`) is a policy you hold toward yourself, not a promise. The weaker endings stay weaker on purpose: `hwazam` is *I'll do it* (you can still back out), and `hwazar` cannot pass for a promise.

To check in, ask: `yom zuwon vezuyel bawan hwabam.` (*Are you still OK with me telling you?*).

A say word takes no **-x**: a group's say comes from a group in the clause.

#### How far the say reaches (scope) {#say-scope}

The scope vowel is [label scope](predication.md#label-scope), the same vowels as after **`th`** on a label:

- **`a`:** this act. `hwadam` is a yes to this act, which is why consent is asked for each time.
- **`e`:** standing. `hwedam` is standing consent (*they're generally fine with this*), `hwevam` standing permission (*you may, any time*), `hwevem` a standing duty, and `hwezem` a discipline. Standing is not binding: `hwedam` can still be taken back, and only **-l** binds.
- **`o`:** toward one party, named in a `bl-` word. `zuwon vowurel hwozal blahelan.` is a vow toward Ahelan: *I promise Ahelan I'll walk*. On **`v`**, the `bl-` word is still who has the say, and **`o`** limits it to their own part: `hwavam blolazun` is *Olazun lets Uzozen walk*, and `hwovam blolazun` is *fine as far as Olazun is concerned*; others may still object. Bare `hwovam` is *fine by me*, without speaking for anyone else.
- **`u`:** a type across scenes. `hwudam` is *they're the type who's fine with it*, and `hwuvem` *their kind has to*. These are the inferences consent and fairness warn against; the form makes them visible instead of hiding them.
- **Pairs** compose as on any label: `hwuevam` is *you may, but not in my official role*.

> `zuwon vowurel hwozal blahelan.`
>
> z-speaker | v-walk | [h-pair-agent-ALLOW-rule | bl-Ahelan]
>
> "I promise Ahelan I'll walk."

Only **`v`** and pair scope **`o`** take a `bl-` word.

### Contrary to a stance (`uem`) {#contrary-to-stance}

English *against the rules*, *against Olazun's will*, and *contrary to the plan* say that the event happens even though a rule, a person, or a plan says otherwise. Put the hook **`uem`** *contrary to* right before a `/th/` stance word, a [say word](#say-words), or an [intention adverb](clause.md#intention-adverbs). That word then says what the event goes against, and it no longer applies to the claim. `zuzozen vowurel hwavul.` says Uzozen is not allowed to walk; `zuzozen vowurel uem hwavul.` says Uzozen walks anyway. (cue: **ue** ≈ undo aimed at; **-m** the opposing frame, which the stance spells out)

> `zuzozen vowurel uem hwavul.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [contrary-to | h-episode-event-FORBID-rule]
>
> "Uzozen walks against the rules."

The stance keeps its endings and its `bl-` word. On a refusal, the pair is English *against their will*:

> `zuzozen vezuyel bolazun uem hwabum.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-tell | b-Olazun | [contrary-to | h-episode-recipient-FORBID-said]
>
> "Uzozen tells Olazun, against Olazun's will." (Olazun said no)

> `zahelan vuhohol uem hwavem blolazun.`
>
> z-Ahelan | v-sit | [contrary-to | [h-episode-event-REQUIRE-said | bl-Olazun]]
>
> "Ahelan sits, against Olazun's orders."

A [plan](intention.md#plan) after **`uem`** is your own, like any stance. A [speaker attitude](#speaker-attitude) after **`uem`** is what you were hoping for.

> `zuzozen vowurel uem thagem.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [contrary-to | th-plan-itinerary]
>
> "Uzozen walks, contrary to my plan."

> `zuzozen vowurel uem thoguyem.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [contrary-to | th-hope]
>
> "Uzozen walks, against my hopes."

Only the stance right after **`uem`** is what the event goes against. A stance anywhere else in the clause still applies to the claim:

> `zuzozen vowurel uem hwavul hwavam.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [contrary-to | h-episode-event-FORBID-rule] | h-episode-event-ALLOW-said
>
> "Uzozen walks against the rules, and I'm allowing it."

The word after **`uem`** has to say something the event can go against: a ban, a requirement, a refusal, a plan, a decision, a want, or what you hoped.

| After **`uem`** | English |
|-----------------|---------|
| ban **`hwavul`** / **`hwavum`** / **`hwavur`** | *against the rules* / *though I forbade it* / *though people frown on it* |
| requirement **`hwavel`** / **`hwavem`** / **`hwaver`** | *contrary to regulations* / *against my orders* / *contrary to custom* |
| refusal **`hwadul`** / **`hwadum`** / **`hwadur`** | *against their will* (they opted out / they said no / they probably don't want it) |
| plan **`thager`** / **`thagem`** / **`thagel`** | *contrary to my plan* |
| speaker attitude, such as **`thoguyem`** | *against my hopes* |

A yes only lifts a restriction, so no say word with answer **`a`** follows **`uem`**: not permission (`hwavam`), and not consent (`hwadam`, `hwazal`), where a listener who misses **`uem`** would hear that the person agreed. *Their call* (**`o`**) sets nothing to go against either. A yes that was taken back is a no, so use the refusal: `zuzozen vezuyel bahelan uem hwabum.` To say you broke a promise, give the promise its own sentence after **`hezom blarl`**: `zuwon vezuvol hezom blarl zuwon vowurel hwazal.` (*I sleep, although I promised to walk.*) Sake words cannot follow **`uem`** either: to say an act goes against someone's interest, put an [unmet](#sake-changeability) sake word on the clause (`zuzozen vowurel thudathum blolazun.` *Uzozen's walking harms Olazun's interests*).

**Compare with:** *despite the rain* is **`uem`** or **`hezom`** with a noun in `/b/` (`zuzozen vowurel uem berulal.`). *Although Olazun said no* as a full sentence is **`hezom blarl`** plus that sentence. The sentence after **`blarl`** needs an event or a thing, so a stance word alone cannot fill it; use **`uem`** with the stance instead.

### Enough and too (sake bars) {#sake-bars}

English *enough* and *too much* compare an amount with **what a need requires**. Write a met sake word on `/th/` as the [bar](comparatives.md#bars) right before a rank join: it stands for what that sake needs, and the rank join compares the amount with it. The equal-rank join **`zoel`** is *enough*. Rank **`zel`** is *too much*, and **`zuel`** is *not enough*. Use the unspecified sake **`uda`** when you do not name which sake.

> `zududal thudatham zoel gral.`
>
> [z-tea | th-sake-met-any-term | z-equal-rank | g-amount]
>
> "There is enough tea."

> `zududal thudatham zel gral.`
>
> [z-tea | th-sake-met-any-term | z-rank/more | g-amount]
>
> "There is too much tea."

> `zududal thudatham zuel gral.`
>
> [z-tea | th-sake-met-any-term | z-rank/less | g-amount]
>
> "There is not enough tea."

The bar keeps its [time-horizon ending](#time-horizon-endings-on-met), which now says **how long the payoff has to last**: **`thudathal`** is *enough to last*, and **`thudathar`** is *enough for now*.

> `zududal thudathar zoel gral.`
>
> [z-tea | th-sake-met-immediate | z-equal-rank | g-amount]
>
> "There is enough tea for now."

Name a sake to say which need sets the limit. With a quality as the scale, the same pattern gives *too ADJ* and *ADJ enough*. To say **whose** stake it is, put that person in a `bl-` word right after the bar.

> `zududal thobatham zel gohamul.`
>
> [z-tea | th-physical-met-any-term | z-rank/more | g-hot]
>
> "The tea is too hot to be safe."

> `zududal thobatham blolazun zel gohamul.`
>
> [z-tea | [th-physical-met-any-term | bl-Olazun] | z-rank/more | g-hot]
>
> "The tea is too hot for Olazun's health."

On the [time scale](comparatives.md#time-scale), a sake bar is *too late* and *too early*:

> `zuzozen thudatham zuel bral vovurel.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | th-sake-met-any-term | z-rank/less | b-later] | v-arrival
>
> "Uzozen arrives too early."

| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| met sake bar + **`zoel`** | reaches what the sake needs | *enough* / *ADJ enough* |
| met sake bar + **`zel`** | past what the sake needs | *too much* / *too ADJ* / *too late* |
| met sake bar + **`zuel`** | short of what the sake needs | *not enough* / *too early* |
| bar ending **-l** / **-m** / **-r** | how long the payoff has to last | *enough to last* / *enough* / *enough for now* |
| `bl-` word right after the bar | whose stake | *too hot for Olazun* |

**Compare with:** the same met word after the fence is about the whole claim: `zududal zel gohamul thobatham` is *the tea is the hottest, and that serves my health*.
