# Relations

Some English prepositions need a content root of their own (*like*, *for* in a swap, *on behalf of*, *between*, *part of*, *as of*). Agebon writes each one as a **relation** word on `/h/` (or `/ɡ/` on a noun), followed by an extra noun in `/b/` that completes it. Everyday *in* / *at* / *from* / *for* (a get) / *using* are [hooks](hooks.md#extra-noun) instead.


You already write *like Uzozen* as a hosted pair (`hewom bluzozen`): a relation word, then the extra noun as a `bl-` word right after it. The same shape covers the other prepositions on this page: pick the relation root, then put a `bl-` word after it. A `/w/` word that grades the relation sits immediately before the `/h/` or `/ɡ/` word.

### Like (*resembles*) {#like-resembles}
<a id="similative"></a>
<a id="like"></a>

When English says something **resembles** a model (*walks like a duck*, *a house like Uzozen’s*), keep the same hosted pair as on the [clause](clause.md#extra-nouns) page. The relation word is **`hewom`**. Put the model in a `bl-` word right after it. Optional `/w/` sits immediately before the relation word. The event or thing itself is real; the `bl-` word is only what it is compared to. (cue: 🪞 *mirror*: you see a reflection, not the same object twice)

> `zuzozen hewom bludozal vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [h-like | bl-duck] | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen walks like a duck."

> `zuzozen welazom hewom bludozal vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [[w-very | h-like] | bl-duck] | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen walks very much like a duck."

On a noun, the same root is **`gewom`**:

> `zahehal gewom bluzozen.`
>
> z-house | [g-like | bl-Uzozen]
>
> "A house like Uzozen’s."

When the model is an event (*walks the way a duck swims*), put [**`blarl`**](dependents.md#poles) after **`hewom`** and say that event as the next sentence. Keep the pair last, as with *because*. You are not claiming that the model event happened; it is only the comparison.

> `zuzozen vowurel hewom blarl zudozal vazuvel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [h-like | bl-that-clause] | z-duck | v-swim
>
> "Uzozen walks the way a duck swims."

As a plain noun, `zewol` is *a mirror*.

With no `bl-` word after a joined subject, the model is the other members: each one resembles the others. That is English *alike* / *similar to each other*. Every hosted relation follows this rule.

> `zuzozen zolazun zal gewom.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-Olazun | z-and | g-like]
>
> "Uzozen and Olazun are alike."

> `zuzozen zolazun zal hewom vowurel.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-Olazun | z-and] | h-like | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen and Olazun walk alike."

**Compare with:** a `/b/` word with no relation word before it is the recipient. *In a house* is a [hook](hooks.md#extra-noun) (`al bahehal`). *Using* a tool is also a [hook](hooks.md#extra-noun). Giving **one** person two labels is not resemblance, so it does not use this pair. *As sleepy as* compares how far two things have a quality, not whether one resembles the other. *As if they walk* says the walk may not be real; with *like*, the walk is real.

<!-- cheat-sheet: linking-clauses -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Same root as | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|--------------|-----|
| **`hewom` / `gewom`** | resemblance to a model | *like* | `ewol` *mirror* | 🪞: the image is of the model |

### Exchange (*for*) {#exchange}
<a id="in-exchange-for"></a>

When English names the **other side of a swap** (*a book for a hammer*), use the same hosted pair as *like*. The relation word is **`heham`**. Put what is given in return in a `bl-` word right after it. That extra noun is the other half of the trade, not who receives the book. (cue: 💱 *currency-exchange*: the arrows swap one thing for another; `/b/` is the other side)

> `zuzozen danal heham blehowol veham.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-book | [h-in-exchange-for | bl-hammer] | v-convertibility
>
> "Uzozen trades a book for a hammer."

On a noun, the same root is **`geham`** (`danal geham blehowol` *a book for a hammer*). As a plain noun, `zehal` is *a currency exchange*. As a verb, `veham` is *to trade* (the convertibility act).

**Compare with:** a `/b/` word with no relation word before it is the recipient (*a book for Olazun* as who gets it). *Using* a tool is a [hook](hooks.md#extra-noun). *Like a duck* is [simile](#similative). *In a house* is a hook (`al`).

<!-- cheat-sheet: linking-clauses -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Same root as | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|--------------|-----|
| **`heham` / `geham`** | consideration / countervalue | *in exchange for* | `ehal` *currency-exchange* | 💱: the arrows swap one side for the other |

### Proxy (*on behalf of*) {#proxy}
<a id="on-behalf-of"></a>

When English says someone **acts as another person’s agent** (*tells on behalf of Olazun*), use the same hosted pair as [*like*](#similative). The relation word is **`hanom`**. Put that person in a `bl-` word right after it. The subject does the act; `/b/` is the person they act for, not who hears the telling. (cue: 🪪 *id*: the card stands as an authorized face; `/b/` is that person)

> `zuzozen hanom blolazun vezuyel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [h-on-behalf-of | bl-Olazun] | v-tell
>
> "Uzozen speaks on behalf of Olazun."

A recipient `/b/` can follow once the pair is complete: `zuzozen hanom blolazun vezuyel bahelan` (*tells Ahelan on behalf of Olazun*). On a noun, the same root is **`ganom`** (`danal ganom blolazun` *a book on behalf of Olazun*). As a plain noun, `zanol` is *an id*.

**Compare with:** a `/b/` word with no relation word before it is who hears the telling. *Using* a tool is a [hook](hooks.md#extra-noun). *A book for a hammer* as a swap is [exchange](#exchange). *In a house* is a hook (`al`).

<!-- cheat-sheet: linking-clauses -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Same root as | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|--------------|-----|
| **`hanom` / `ganom`** | authorized agency | *on behalf of* | `anol` *id* | 🪪: the card stands as their face |
