# Predication {#predication}

English *is* does two jobs that look alike. You can say someone **is a kind or role** (*Azozon is a dog*): they belong in that category. You can also say two names pick out **the same one** (*Alazun is Azozon*): one person under two labels, not a category. Agebon uses a different word pattern for each.


### Property (*is ADJ*)
<a id="classification-property"></a>

You already write a property on `/ɡ/` after the noun (*a blue dog*). When you say someone *is* that quality (*Azozon is challenging*), you use the same pair with no verb. You do not add a separate *is*; the name plus the `/ɡ/` word already make the sentence. The `/ɡ/` word is the piece English would put after *is*.

> `zazozon gamahum.`
>
> z-Azozon | g-challenge
>
> "Azozon is challenging."

A `/ɡ/` word after the verb does not describe the subject the way *tired* does in English *arrived tired*. Give the state its own sentence instead (`zalazun gadamul. zulamur vudurul.`).

The same string is also the noun plus its adjective: `zazozon gamahum` is both the full sentence *Azozon is challenging* and the phrase *challenging Azozon*.

To say someone belongs to a country or tradition (*Japanese*), use the **-m** ending, not the name ending **-n**. **-n** names the country itself (*Japan*), and **-m** says you belong to it.

> `zazozon gazabanam.`
>
> z-Azozon | g-japanese
>
> "Azozon is Japanese."

### Kind / role {#classification}

To say someone **is a kind of thing** or **has a role** (*Azozon is a dog*), put the kind word on `/ɡ/` right after the noun. It is the same pattern as a quality, again with no word for *is*: the `/ɡ/` word is what English puts after *is a*.

This use is called **classification**: it puts someone in a category rather than describing a quality.

> `zazozon godozel.`
>
> z-Azozon | g-dog
>
> "Azozon is a dog."

**Compare with:** *A dog walks* uses a noun plus a verb (`zodozel vowurol`). When the subject **is** that kind, keep the kind on `/ɡ/`.

### In the middle of an event {#in-progress}

Some roots name an event (*walk*, *fight*, *sing*). On `/ɡ/`, such a root says the noun is **in the middle of** that event, as the one doing it: English *walking*. The ending picks the sense as always, so **-l** is the everyday event and **-m** its abstract sense (`guvuvum`, *struggling*).

> `zazozon gowurol.`
>
> z-Azozon | g-walk
>
> "Azozon is walking."

After a noun it works like any adjective. The event is under way at the time the sentence talks about, so the dog below is walking while Azozon sees it.

> `zazozon dodozel gowurol vayebel.`
>
> z-Azozon | [d-dog | g-walk] | v-see
>
> "Azozon sees a walking dog."

Only a sense that is an event reads this way. A thing root stays a kind (`godozel`, *is a dog*), even when English has a verb from the same picture.

**Compare with:** the verb in `zazozon vowurol.` reports the walking without saying it is under way.

### Existence (a lone noun) {#existence}

To say that something **exists** or is here (English *there is …*), write the noun alone, with no verb and no `/ɡ/` word after it.

> `zodozel.`
>
> z-dog
>
> "There is a dog."

The same holds with a `/ɡ/` word after the noun, when the noun is new: a common noun, or one of a name on **-ln**, but not a name on **-n** or a resumed noun on **-r**. The pair introduces the thing: *there is a G Z*.

> `zodozel gelazem.`
>
> z-dog | g-big
>
> "There is a big dog."

With a name or a resumed noun, the listener already knows the thing, so the `/ɡ/` word is a [property](#classification-property): `zazozon gamahum.` is *Azozon is challenging*, and `zodozer gelazem.` is *The dog is big*.

An object (`/d/`) needs a verb, so a noun plus a `/d/` word with no verb is not a sentence.

**Compare with:** `zodozel vowurol.` says what the dog does. `zazozon godozel.` puts Azozon in a kind.

### Identity (**SAME**)
<a id="identity"></a>
<a id="same"></a>

When two names pick out **the same person or thing** (*Alazun is Azozon*), you are not putting Alazun in a kind. You are saying the two labels match: they are one individual.

Write the special adjective `gorel` after the first name, then an extra noun (`/b/`) for the other label. Keep `gorel` and that `/b/` word **next to each other**. This pair states **identity**. The adjective’s English name is **`SAME`**; its root is **`ore`**. (cue: 🪙 *coin*: two faces, one substance)

> `zalazun gorel blazozon.`
>
> z-Alazun | [g-SAME | bl-Azozon]
>
> "Alazun is Azozon." (one person, two names)

> `zagayul gorel blazozon.`
>
> z-guard | [g-SAME | bl-Azozon]
>
> "The guard is Azozon."

The second label is a `/b/` whatever it is: a common noun or a pronoun works as well as a name.

> `zagayul gorel blebayol.`
>
> z-guard | [g-SAME | bl-police]
>
> "The guard is the police officer."

> `zagayul gorel bluwen.`
>
> z-guard | [g-SAME | bl-speaker]
>
> "The guard is me."

With **no** `/b/` after it, `gorel` points back to the one already mentioned: *the same one again*. Put `gorel` after the thing.

> `zalazun danal gorel varuyol.`
>
> z-Alazun | [d-book | g-SAME] | v-write
>
> "Alazun is writing the same book." (the book already mentioned)

**Compare with:** *Azozon is a dog* uses [kind / role](#classification) (`godozel`). Use **`SAME`** when two labels name one person.
