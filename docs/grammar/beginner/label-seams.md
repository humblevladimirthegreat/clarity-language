# How far a label reaches {#how-far-a-label-reaches}

### Label scope {#label-scope}

English *Olazun is angry* can mean one outburst or the kind of person Olazun is. The same word does both jobs, and a single act easily turns into a claim about someone's nature. To say what kind of claim a label is, write the root, then mid-word **`th`**, then a scope vowel, then the ending. The vowel says whether the label covers only this episode, a practiced role, one relationship, or a type that follows the person across scenes. The word keeps its usual slot, so a label on `/ɡ/` is still *is ADJ*, and a verb is still the verb.

> `zolazun gunemuthal.`
>
> z-Olazun | g-angry-th-episode
>
> "Olazun is angry this time."

A label with no scope vowel says nothing about how far it reaches. Use one when that reach matters, the way you would add *this time* or *a teacher* in English.

<!-- cheat-sheet: word-shape-clause -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| **`tha`** | this episode: this stretch of doing | *angry this time*, *lied (this once)* | **a** ≈ add (one instance added) |
| **`the`** | a practiced role: the office they inhabit | *a teacher*, *lying as their craft* | **e** ≈ order (a sequence of practice) |
| **`tho`** | this pair: true in one relationship | *a stranger to Uzozen*, *lying, in dealings with Olazun* | **o** ≈ one (that extra one) |
| **`thu`** | a type across scenes: the quality follows them | *an anxious type*, *a teacher type* | **u** ≈ undo (not only here) |

Mid-word **`th`** with a scope vowel goes on `/ɡ/`, `/z/`, `/d/`, `/b/`, `/v/`, and `/h/`, on a content root. It labels a content word, so a [special pronoun](pronouns.md#special-pronouns) with **-n** takes none (its root on **-l** is an ordinary word, `zuwothal` *a microphone, this time*). The [sake](sakes.md#sake-inventory) roots use this spot for their own stances instead. A [role compound](roles.md#one-time-or-a-practice) writes the same vowels after **`w`**, not after **`th`**.

To say **when** that claim counts (*only at school*, *always except when it rains*), put a [restrictor](restrictors.md) on `/w/` immediately before a scoped `/ɡ/` word, with the circumstance words on `/w/` too. The scope vowel says what kind of claim the label is; the restrictor says on which occasions it holds. A restrictor on **`/h/`** limits the whole sentence instead (*walks only when it rains*), not the label.

> `zolazun wanurul wal gwezuduyel.`
>
> z-Olazun | [w-night | w-only-when | g-role-agent-teach]
>
> "Olazun is a teacher only at night."

**Compare with:** **`hual`** / **`wual`** says the claim holds at every time. **`thu`** says you are treating them as that type across scenes, which can still have exceptions. **`wual gwezuduyel`** is the practiced role at every time, and **`ganumuthul`** is the type-claim. Using both together doubles down.

#### This episode (`tha`) {#scope-occasion}

> `zuzozen valamuthal.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-lie-th-episode
>
> "Uzozen lied this once."

#### Practiced role (`the`) {#scope-occupation}

> `zuzozen valamuthel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-lie-th-role
>
> "Uzozen lies as their craft."

On a verb, **`the`** is that act as their practice, not one outing.

**Compare with:** *usually* / *often* is a [restrictor](restrictors.md) (`huam`, `war`). **`thu`** is the type-claim (*a lying type*), which is not a practiced role.

#### This pair (`tho`) {#scope-relative}

**`tho`** says the label holds in one relationship: toward one other party. Put that party in a `bl-` word right after the word, on `/ɡ/`, `/h/`, and `/v/` alike. A verb's recipient stays a plain `/b/`. On a noun, **`tho`** stands alone; to name the party, say the label on `/ɡ/` instead.

> `zuzozen golayothom blolazun.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [g-strangeness-th-pair | bl-Olazun]
>
> "Uzozen is a stranger to Olazun."

> `zuzozen valamuthol blolazun.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [v-lie-th-pair | bl-Olazun]
>
> "With Olazun, Uzozen lies." (in that relationship)

With no `/b/`, the party is unstated: *a stranger (to someone)*.

On a [role compound](roles.md#one-time-or-a-practice) the same vowel sits after **`w`**: `gwozuduyel blolazun` is *is a teacher to Olazun*.

#### Type across scenes (`thu`) {#scope-type}

> `zolazun ganumuthul.`
>
> z-Olazun | g-anxious-th-type
>
> "Olazun is an anxious type."

On a [role compound](roles.md#one-time-or-a-practice) the same vowel sits after **`w`**: `zwuzuduyel` is *a teacher type*.

#### On a role compound {#scope-role}

These are the same vowels, written right after **`w`** on a [role compound](roles.md#role-compounds): there they scope the role, not the act. `gwezuduyel` is *is a teacher*, `gwazuduyel` *is one who teaches (this time)*, and `zwuzuduyel` *a teacher type*.

> `zwuzuduyel varuvel.`
>
> z-type-agent-teach | v-run
>
> "A teacher type runs."

A role word takes no **`th`** seam after its stem: its scope is written once, at the front. Scope on a plain verb stem (`vuduyethal`) scopes the teaching.

**Compare with:** the **`th`** seam scopes the word itself (the act on a verb, the quality on an adjective, the manner on an adverb), and the verb keeps the rest of its clause: `zuzozen valamuthal bolazun.` is *Uzozen lied to Olazun, this once*. A role compound names the person by their part: `zuzozen gwezalamul.` is *Uzozen is a liar*.

#### Stacked scope {#scope-stacks}

The four scope vowels also combine in pairs, the same pairs that [joins](joins.md) and [hooks](hooks.md#extra-noun) use. A pair that starts with **`u`** reads like a join's *everything but*: **`u`** is every scene, and the second vowel is the one set aside (*every time but this one*, *with everyone but Olazun*, *as themselves, not in the role*). The other pairs read like the extra-noun hooks: **`ao`** *over*, **`ae`** *using*, **`oe`** *toward*. Do not read them as the first vowel's meaning followed by the second's.

<!-- cheat-sheet: word-shape-clause -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| **`thao`** | the **situation**, not the person | *angry, but it's the situation*; *a teacher by circumstance* | **ao** ≈ over: the label hangs over the scene |
| **`thae`** | **using** the role for a purpose | *playing the teacher* | **ae** ≈ using |
| **`thoe`** | **toward** the role | *becoming a teacher* | **oe** ≈ toward |
| **`thua`** | type, **except** this episode | *a teacher type, except this time* | **ua** ≈ everything but: every time but this one |
| **`thuo`** | type, **except** this pair | *a teacher type, except with Olazun* | **uo** ≈ anything but: everyone but this one |
| **`thue`** | as themselves, **not in that role** | *not as a teacher* (off duty) | **ue** ≈ every scene but the role |

> `zolazun gunemuthaol.`
>
> z-Olazun | g-angry-th-situation
>
> "Olazun is angry, but it's the situation, not Olazun."

> `zolazun gunemuthaol blerulal.`
>
> z-Olazun | [g-angry-th-situation | bl-rain]
>
> "Olazun is angry, but it's the rain, not Olazun."

> `zuwon gwaezuduyel.`
>
> z-speaker | g-using-agent-teach
>
> "I'm playing the teacher." (taking on the role for a purpose)

> `zolazun gwoezuduyel.`
>
> z-Olazun | g-toward-role-agent-teach
>
> "Olazun is becoming a teacher."

> `zuzozen valamuthual.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-lie-th-except-episode
>
> "Uzozen is a lying type, except this time."

> `zuzozen gunemuthuol blolazun.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [g-angry-th-except-pair | bl-Olazun]
>
> "Uzozen is an angry type, except with Olazun."

> `zuzozen gwuezuduyel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | g-outside-role-agent-teach
>
> "Uzozen is not a teacher here."

> `zuzozen gwaozuduyel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | g-situation-agent-teach
>
> "Uzozen is a teacher by circumstance." (the situation made them one)

> `zwuazuduyel varuvel.`
>
> z-except-episode-agent-teach | v-run
>
> "A teacher type, except this time, runs."

> `zuzozen gwuozuduyel blolazun.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [g-except-pair-agent-teach | bl-Olazun]
>
> "Uzozen is a teacher type, except with Olazun."

> `zuzozen valamuthael.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-lie-th-using
>
> "Uzozen is using lying as a tool."

> `zolazun valamuthoel.`
>
> z-Olazun | v-lie-th-toward-role
>
> "Olazun is becoming a liar."

> `zuwon vezuyethuel bawan.`
>
> z-speaker | v-tell-th-outside-role | b-listener
>
> "I'm telling you this as myself, not in my role."

**`thuo`** takes its `bl-` party the same way **`tho`** does. On **`thao`**, a `bl-` word is the situation the label belongs to; it claims no cause, and without one the situation is the scene at hand. **`thue`** sets the role aside: it means off duty, never refusing the role or *fake teacher* (to deny the label, put [`gul`](joins.md#negation-u) after it). The same pairs on a role compound write the vowels after **`w`**.
