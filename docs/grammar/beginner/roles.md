# Role compounds {#roles}

When English would name a person or thing from an event (*a teacher* from *teach*, *the one told*, *a building*), Agebon builds that name from the event’s stem. Write a person or thing letter, then **`w`**, then a scope vowel, then a part consonant, then the stem, then an ordinary [word ending](word-endings.md). The part consonant is the role letter the participant had in the event's clause: **`z`** the doer, **`d`** what the event acts on or brings into being, and **`b`** the extra `/b/` party (who is told, or the other party of a relation).

The scope vowel says how far the label reaches. **`u`** is this episode: one act is enough (*someone who teaches*). **`o`** is a practiced role, the kind English names as a job (*a teacher*). Every role word picks one, so calling someone *a fighter* after a single fight takes a separate, visible choice. The ending decides whether you mean anyone in that part or the one from a particular event: with **-r**, it is the participant of the latest matching event (*the one teaching*).


### Role compounds: the doer (`z`) {#role-compounds}
<a id="agent-place-patient-recipient"></a>

English *someone who teaches* names a person from what they do, from the same idea as *teach*. Write a person or thing letter (`/z/` `/d/` `/b/`), then **`w`**, then the scope vowel **`u`**, then the part consonant **`z`**, then the event root, then an ordinary [word ending](word-endings.md). The result is one content word (a **role compound**) that you can use as a participant. The **`z`** is the doer's own role letter: the one who did it sat in `/z/`.

> `zwuzuduyel varuvel.`
>
> z-episode-agent-teach | v-run
>
> "Someone who teaches runs."

The last letter is the ordinary [word ending](word-endings.md): **-l** takes the stem’s everyday concrete sense, **-m** its abstract sense. `zwuzuduyel` is *someone who teaches*; `zwuzavuvul` is *someone who fights*; `zwuzavuvum` is *someone who struggles*. No teaching or fight needs to come up earlier in the conversation, and **`u`** says nothing about how often: one lesson is enough.

When the event is a dictionary word built from two roots, the role compound takes the whole word as its stem: `zwuzanalawebol` is *someone who reads*, from `analawebol` *read*.

A noun that specifies the event goes in the same stem, before the event and joined to it with mid-word **`x`**: `zwuzudozoxowurel` is *someone who walks dogs* (the doer of *dog-walking*). The noun's part in the event is as open as in English (*dog walker*, *coffee maker*); for an exact part, use a [hook](hooks.md#extra-noun) or a second sentence. **-r** resumes the whole stem.

> `zwuzudozoxowurel varuvel.`
>
> z-episode-agent-dog-x-walk | v-run
>
> "Someone who walks dogs runs."

A role compound has one part consonant, right after its scope vowel. It is never one piece of another compound.

### One time or a practice (`u` / `o`) {#one-time-or-a-practice}
<a id="usual-role"></a>

English *a teacher* says more than *someone who teaches*: teaching is that person's practiced role, often a job. Write **`o`** in the scope slot instead of **`u`**. (cue: **o** ≈ medium (a life they inhabit))

> `zwozuduyel varuvel.`
>
> z-role-agent-teach | v-run
>
> "A teacher runs."

`zwozavuvul` is *a fighter*, and `zwozudozoxowurel` is *a dog walker*. With **`u`**, a role compound never makes that claim, so one fight gives `zwuzavuvul` (*someone who fought*), not a fighter.

| Agebon | Scope | English | Cue |
|--------|-------|---------|-----|
| `zwuzuduyel` | **`u`** this episode | *someone who teaches* | **u** ≈ none (a speck of time) |
| `zwozuduyel` | **`o`** practiced role | *a teacher* | **o** ≈ medium (a life they inhabit) |

### This instance (`-r`)

English *the one teaching* points at the doer of an event already mentioned. Put **-r** on the role compound: once the conversation has a matching event (*Uzozen teaches*), **-r** on the same stem means **that** doer, not just anyone who teaches.

> `zuzozen vuduyel.`
> `zwuzuduyer vezuvol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-teach
> z-←episode-agent-teach | v-sleep
>
> "Uzozen teaches. The one teaching sleeps."

To build the **-r** form, take the whole stem of the earlier verb. In `vuhohol` (*sit*), the stem is **`uhoho`**, so *the one sitting* is `zwuzuhohor`: subject **`z`**, then **`wu`**, the doer **`z`**, the stem **`uhoho`**, and **-r**.

> `zuzozen vuhohol. zwuzuhohor vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-sit . z-←episode-agent-sit | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen sits. The one who sat walks."

The scope vowel reads the same on **-r**: `zwozuduyer` is *the one teaching, in their practiced role*.

### The undergoer (`d`) {#the-undergoer-d}
<a id="the-undergoer-u"></a>

English *the one seen* or *the one fought* names the person the event happens to. That person sat in `/d/`, so write **`d`** as the part consonant. With **-l**, it is anyone it happens to (*someone seen*); with **-r**, it is the undergoer of the latest matching event.

> `zuzozen dagozol vawebol.`
> `zwudawebor varuvel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-cat | v-see
> z-←episode-patient-see | v-run
>
> "Uzozen sees a cat. The one seen runs."

What an event brings into being sits in `/d/` too (*Olazun builds a house*), so **`d`** names it as well: `dwudeguyel` is *something built*. A verb's `/d/` is either what it acts on (*see*) or what it makes (*construct*), and the verb says which.

> `zolazun veguyel. zuzozen dwudeguyer vawebol.`
>
> z-Olazun | v-construct . z-Uzozen | d-←episode-patient-construct | v-see
>
> "Olazun builds. Uzozen sees what Olazun built."

### The extra `/b/` party (`b`) {#the-extra-b-party-b}
<a id="the-extra-b-party-o"></a>

English *the one told* names who gets the telling. In a sentence, that person is the **recipient**: a `/b/` word with no relation word before it ([extra nouns](clause.md#extra-nouns)). To name them from the verb stem, write **`b`** as the part consonant.

> `zuzozen vezuyel.`
> `zwubezuyer varuvel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-tell
> z-←episode-recipient-tell | v-run
>
> "Uzozen speaks. The one spoken to runs."

You can name that party with no prior telling: `zwubezuyel` is someone told something (the **-l** sense of *speech*), and `zwobezuyel` is *a confidant*, someone regularly told things.

Some stems name a **relation** (who is bound to whom), not an action. The other person of that relation is the `/b/` word right after the relation word. The same **`b`** names that person from the relation stem (*the other party of the bond*).

> `zuzozen gawom blolazun.`
> `zwubawor varuvel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [g-bond | bl-Olazun]
> z-←episode-recipient-bond | v-run
>
> "Uzozen is bound to Olazun. The one Uzozen is bound to runs."

**Compare with:** *for someone* (who the act is meant to benefit) is the extra-noun hook **`ol`** plus `/b/`, not the recipient.

| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| `…wuzROOT…` | names the doer of an event | *someone who teaches* / *the one teaching* | **z**: the doer sat in `/z/` |
| `…wudROOT…` | names what the event acts on or makes | *someone seen* / *the one seen* (this seeing); *something built* | **d**: it sat in `/d/` |
| `…wubROOT…` | names the extra `/b/` party | *someone told* / *the one told* | **b**: they sat in `/b/` |

A role compound fills `/z/`, `/d/`, `/b/`, or `/ɡ/`. Under `/y/` with **-n** or **-r** it calls someone (`ywozobebun` calls the doctor).

Its stem is an event or a state: `zwuzunemul` is *someone who is angry*. A [special pronoun](pronouns.md#special-pronouns) such as `uwon` (*the speaker*) names a person, not an event, so `zwuzuwon` is not a word. The same root on **-l** is an ordinary content word with a verb sense (*perform*), so it can be a stem: `zwuzuwol` is *someone who performs*.

With **-l**, **-m**, or **-n**, the word is **any** such participant: someone who does it, something it acts on or makes, someone told. **-r** looks back to the latest verb, event noun, or relation with that whole stem (including a stem already inside a role compound) and names **this instance**. The part consonant still picks which part you mean: doer, undergoer, or extra `/b/` party.

### Without the stem: role pointers {#role-pointers-family}

A [role pointer](pronouns.md#role-pointers) is this same word with the event stem left out: **`w`**, the scope vowel, the part consonant, a pointer vowel, and an ending (**-r** the participant itself, **-l** a new one of its kind, **-m** its part in the event). Instead of naming the event, the pointer vowel picks it by position: **`e`** the latest one, **`a`** the other one, **`o`** this sentence’s own, **`u`** the one that left the part unsaid. `zwuzuhohor` is *the one who sat*; `zwuzer` is *whoever did the latest thing*. After the pointer vowel and ending the word ends, while a compound's stem goes on (`zwuzer` against `zwuzarayel`, *someone who writes*).

A pointer looks back to whoever last had that part, skipping events without one ([role pointers](pronouns.md#role-pointers)).

### The event itself

English *a fight* names the event itself, not anyone in it. Put the event root on a thing letter with no **`w`** and part: `davuvul` is *a fight*. Use **`wuz`**, **`wud`**, or **`wub`** only when you mean who fights, who is fought, or the extra `/b/` party.

> `zuzozen davuvul vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-fight | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sees a fight."

**Related form:** a verb’s own **-r** resumes that [event](pronouns.md) (`vavuvur` *that fighting*). A role compound names the people.

### Saying someone does it
<a id="saying-someone-is-a-teacher"></a>

To say someone **is one who teaches**, put the same role compound on `/ɡ/`, as in [classification](predication.md#classification). With **`u`**, the word says only that they do it, with no claim about how often. With **-r** on `/ɡ/`, it says they are the doer of the matching event (*is the one teaching*).

> `zuzozen gwuzuduyel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | g-episode-agent-teach
>
> "Uzozen is someone who teaches."

> `zuzozen gwuzavuvum.`
>
> z-Uzozen | g-episode-agent-struggle
>
> "Uzozen is someone who struggles."

To say someone **is** a teacher, put the practiced-role word on `/ɡ/`:

> `zuzozen gwozuduyel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | g-role-agent-teach
>
> "Uzozen is a teacher."

After another noun, the same `/ɡ/` word picks out which one you mean by that role.

> `zuzozen dagayul gwuzavuvul vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [d-guard | g-episode-agent-fight] | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sees a guard who fights."

**Compare with:** the plain root on `/ɡ/` is the act [in progress](predication.md#in-progress): `dagayul gavuvul` is *a fighting guard*, in the middle of a fight. English *the guard who fought (that time)* with extra participants or a place is [two sentences](dependents.md#which-noun), not this role word.
