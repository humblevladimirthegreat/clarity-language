# Hooks {#hooks}


English uses small words such as *including*, *instead*, *in*, and *for* to attach one piece to another. Agebon does this with **hooks**: short words with **no role letter** (no `z` / `v` / `g` / …), just a vowel (or two vowels) plus an ending. A hook attaches the piece on its right to something already in play: a phrase in the same role, the sentence before, or the clause itself as an extra noun.

The same spellings work in all three places. You tell them apart by what comes right **after** the hook, and by whether a `/b/` word comes right **before** it.

### Including (`am` / `al`)
<a id="including"></a>

You named something (A), and you want to say English *including B* about it. Put a hook between two phrases that play the **same role** in the clause (both subjects, both objects, both adjectives, …): `A HOOK B`. For everyday *including*, the hook is **`al`**. B is added to A's slot, but A and B do not become a two-item list. (cue: **a** ≈ add.)

```text
A HOOK B
```

> `zavayal al zuzozen.`
>
> z-family | including | z-Uzozen
>
> "The family, including Uzozen."

Because a hook starts with a vowel, the [last consonant](phonology.md#word-edges) of the word before it may slide over and start the hook's syllable, in speech and in song: `zavayal al` can sound like *za-va-ya-lal*. That is fine. They are still two words, and the listener can still tell where the hook starts.

**Compare with:** packaging two members as a list uses a [join](joins.md) (`zudozol zagozol zam` *a dog and a cat*). A hook adds B to A's slot; it does not name a pair.

### Rather (`el`)
<a id="rather"></a>

To correct your own wording, English says *A, or rather B*. Use the same `A HOOK B` shape with vowel **e**: **`el`**. B is a better way to say A. It describes the **same** thing, so it is not an extra member (as with *including*) and not a different thing in A's place (as with *instead*). (cue: **e** ≈ order.)

> `zuzozen godamul el guzemol.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | g-tired] | rather | g-sleepy
>
> "Uzozen is tired, or rather, sleepy."

### Instead (`ol`)
<a id="instead"></a>

For English *B instead of A*, use the same shape with vowel **o**: `A ol B`. A, on the left, is what you named or expected first. B, on the right, is what really belongs in that slot. The order is the reverse of the English. (cue: **o** ≈ one.)

> `dududal ol dugudal.`
>
> d-tea | instead | d-coffee
>
> "Coffee instead of tea."

A and B can play any role, as long as both play the same one. They can even be **verbs**:

> `zuzozen varuvel ol vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-run | instead | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen walks instead of running."

**Compare with:** *Uzozen tells on behalf of Olazun* keeps Uzozen as the teller; **`ol`** puts someone else in that slot.

### Except (`ul`)
<a id="except"></a>

For English *A except B*, use vowel **u**: `A ul B`. What you say holds for A with B taken out. (cue: **u** ≈ undo.)

> `zavayal ul zuzozen.`
>
> z-family | except | z-Uzozen
>
> "The family except Uzozen."

The left side can be the clause standalone **`xual`** *everything* ([clause joins](joins.md#clause-joins)). The right side is then a clause:

> `xual ul zuzozen vowurel.`
>
> x-everything | except | z-Uzozen | v-walk
>
> "Everything happened except that Uzozen walked."

<!-- cheat-sheet: vowels -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| **a** | add a member in the same slot | *including*: A, including B | **a** ≈ add |
| **e** | offer a better wording | *rather*: A, or rather B | **e** ≈ order |
| **o** | swap the item | *instead*: B in place of A | **o** ≈ one |
| **u** | subtract an exception | *except*: A except B | **u** ≈ undo |

### Closed and open endings

The ending on an `A HOOK B` hook says whether B is complete. **-l** says B is the whole of it, like plain English *including B* or *except B*. **-m** says there may be more than B, like adding *and maybe more*. When you are unsure, prefer **-m**.

<!-- cheat-sheet: joins-hooks -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| **-l** (`al` / `el` / `ol` / `ul`) | B is the whole story you stand behind | unmarked (*including B*, *except B*, …) | **l** ≈ locked |
| **-m** (`am` / `em` / `om` / `um`) | B is not the only one | *and maybe more* | **m** ≈ maybe more |

> `zavayal am zuzozen.`
>
> z-family | including.open | z-Uzozen
>
> "The family, including Uzozen and maybe more."

> `zavayal um zuzozen.`
>
> z-family | except.open | z-Uzozen
>
> "The family except Uzozen, and maybe others."

### Glue this sentence to prior talk {#discourse-hooks}

English can open a sentence with *Additionally*, *In other words*, *Instead*, or *Except* to say how it connects to what was said before (this sentence-to-sentence connective is the *glue*). Put the same hook at the **front** of the sentence. What was already said plays A's role, and the whole new sentence is B. The vowels keep their meanings: add, say another way, replace, take out. (cue: same **a** / **o** / **e** / **u** map as in-clause.)

| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| `al …` | add another point you stand behind | *Additionally, …* | **a** ≈ add |
| `el …` | say the last point another way | *In other words, …* | **e** ≈ order |
| `ol …` | this claim replaces the last one | *Instead, …* / *Actually, …* | **o** ≈ one |
| `ul …` | an exception to what was just said | *Except, …* | **u** ≈ undo |

> `al zuzozen vowurel.`
>
> additionally | z-Uzozen | v-walk
>
> "Additionally, Uzozen walks."

> `ol zolazun vezuvol.`
>
> instead | z-Olazun | v-sleep
>
> "Instead, Olazun sleeps."

Use the same **`ol …`** for *actually* when you correct what someone expected: the new claim replaces the old one.

> `el zahelan gudozol.`
>
> in.other.words | z-Ahelan | g-dog
>
> "In other words, Ahelan is a dog."

The hook is glue only when the next word is not a `/b/` word. With a `/b/` word right after it, even the stand-in **`barl`**, it is an extra-noun hook instead.

**Compare with:** *however* / *therefore* use [continue](dependents.md#continue-x) linkers. A hook at the front only adds, rephrases, replaces, or takes out.

### Extra noun (`/b/` after the hook) {#extra-noun}

English prepositions such as *in*, *at*, *from*, and *for* add a place, a source, or someone who benefits. In Agebon, put a hook right before a `/b/` [extra noun](clause.md#extra-nouns). The hook says how that noun, its **landmark**, relates to the event (or to the noun just before the hook). This is not the recipient, which takes no hook (`zuzozen vezuyel bolazun` *tells Olazun*).

So the test is: `/b/` right after the hook, and no recipient `/b/` right before it. Right after a recipient, the hook is same-role *including* (`buzozen al bolazun`). The hook already marks its landmark, so the landmark is a plain `/b/` word, never a [`bl-` word](clause.md#extra-nouns).

The four one-vowel hooks name the everyday extras:

<!-- cheat-sheet: joins-hooks -->
| Agebon | English | Use | Cue |
|--------|---------|-----|-----|
| **`al`** | *in* | containment | **a** ≈ add (into a volume) |
| **`ol`** | *at* | coincidence with a point | **o** ≈ one (the one point) |
| **`ul`** | *from* | source of a path | **u** ≈ undo (leave) |
| **`el`** | *for* | who or what the act is meant for | **e** ≈ order (aim the act) |

> `zudozol vezuvol al bahehal.`
>
> z-dog | v-sleep | [in | b-house]
>
> "A dog sleeps in a house."

To ask *where?*, put the fill-ask blank **`bar`** after the hook (`ol bar` *where?*, `ul bar` *where from?*).

> `zolazun vuhohol ol bedohol.`
>
> z-Olazun | v-sit | [at | b-table]
>
> "Olazun sits at a table."

> `zuzozen dawudal vobuvul ul bedodal.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-wine | v-pour | [from | b-teapot]
>
> "Uzozen pours wine from a teapot."

> `zuzozen vuguyel el bavayal.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-cook | [for | b-family]
>
> "Uzozen cooks for a family."

When `/b/` is a person or group, **`el`** names who the act is meant to benefit, as in *cooks for Olazun*: what the act brings is meant to reach them.

> `zuzozen vuguyel el bolazun.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-cook | [for | b-Olazun]
>
> "Uzozen cooks for Olazun."

These hooks take **-l**: the landmark is the exact place, point, source, or goal.

To say where something is (*there is a dog in a house*), write the noun with no verb, then the hook and its `/b/`. A noun alone says the thing [exists](predication.md#existence), and the hook places it.

> `zudozol al bahehal.`
>
> z-dog | [in | b-house]
>
> "There is a dog in a house."

**Compare with:** *Additionally, Uzozen walks* is a hook at the front whose next word is **not** `/b/` (`al zuzozen vowurel`).

### Since (`ul` on a time) {#since}

For *since* (from a starting point, and still true now), use **`ul`** *from* with a time instead of a place: *from the thunderstorm on*. [**`halam`**](dependents.md#time-poles) *after* only places the event later. **`ul`** says the event has gone on from that point until now. (cue: **u** ≈ undo: leave the starting point behind)

> `zuzozen vuhohol ul benulul.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-sit | [from | b-thunderstorm]
>
> "Uzozen has been sitting since the thunderstorm."

For *since* before a sentence, put **`barl`** after **`ul`** and the starting event after it.

> `zuzozen vowurel ul barl zolazun vezuvol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [from | b-that-clause] | z-Olazun | v-sleep
>
> "Uzozen has been walking since Olazun fell asleep."

English often puts *since* first (*Since Olazun fell asleep, Uzozen has been walking*). Agebon keeps **`ul barl`** after the main clause, because the sentence after **`barl`** runs to the period. As a short answer, the pair and the starting event can stand alone:

> `ul barl zolazun vezuvol.`
>
> [from | b-that-clause] | z-Olazun | v-sleep
>
> "Since Olazun fell asleep."

**`ul`** is the only hook that takes **`barl`**. Every other hook needs a real noun in `/b/`.

**Compare with:** *since* meaning *because* is **`thahum blarl`**. Agebon keeps the time reading and the cause reading apart.

#### Pick one {#beginner-pick-one}

**1.** *Olazun pours wine from a teapot.* `zolazun dawudal vobuvul ul bedodal.` or `zolazun dawudal vobuvul al bedodal.`

::: details Show answer
`zolazun dawudal vobuvul ul bedodal.`

z-Olazun | d-wine | v-pour | [from | b-teapot]

**`ul`** before `/b/` is the source, *from*; **`al`** would be *in* the teapot.
:::

**2.** *The family except Olazun, and maybe others, cooks.* `zavayal um zolazun vuguyel.` or `zavayal ul zolazun vuguyel.`

::: details Show answer
`zavayal um zolazun vuguyel.`

z-family | except.open | z-Olazun | v-cook

Open **-m** leaves room for more exceptions; **`ul`** says Olazun is the only one.
:::
