### Adjectives before the noun (`gl-`) {#left-bound-adjectives}

English puts the adjective before the noun (*an angry dog*). Agebon can too: add **`l`** as the second letter (`/ɡ/` + **l** + root + ending), and that adjective describes the **next** noun instead of the one before it. This lets you choose the order for meter or emphasis, and it frees the spot after the noun for another adjective. (cue: **l** ≈ lean: the word leans toward the noun ahead.)

> `glunemul zudozol.`
>
> [gl-angry | z-dog]
>
> "an angry dog"

A `bl-` word the adjective hosts still follows it, and a `/w/` word still sits immediately before it (`welazom glunemul zudozol` = *a very angry dog*). On `/ɡ/`, this extra **`l`** only moves the adjective; the word means the same either way. On `/b/`, [`bl-`](clause.md#extra-nouns) is a different job, not a different order. A noun must come after it: with no noun to lean on, write a plain `/ɡ/` adjective after the noun instead (`zudozol gunemul`).

A [family name](proper-names.md#multipart-names) is a `/ɡ/` word too, so **`gl-`** puts it first, for a culture that names people family first. It leans on a [call](speech-moves.md#vocative) the same way.

> `glalowen zalen vowurel.`
>
> [gl-Alowen | z-Alen] | v-walk
>
> "Alowen Alen walks."

> `glalowen yalen.`
>
> [gl-Alowen | y-Alen]
>
> "Alowen Alen!" (calling Alowen Alen)

### Complex chaining {#complex-chaining}

A relation adjective and its `bl-` word (*the same as Uzozen*: **`gorol`** + `bl-`) work as one unit after a noun. A `/w/` word that grades the relation goes immediately before the `/ɡ/` word, as usual. You can keep chaining: a plain adjective after that pair describes the **extra noun**, not the first noun. (cue: 🪙 *coin*: two faces of one thing.)

> `zagayul welazom gorol bluzozen.`
>
> z-guard | [[w-very | g-SAME] | bl-Uzozen]
>
> "a guard who is the very same person as Uzozen"

> `zagayul gorol bluzozen guzemol.`
>
> z-guard | [g-SAME | [bl-Uzozen | g-sleepy]]
>
> "a guard who is the same person as sleepy Uzozen"

The same holds after an `/h/` or `/th/` host: an adjective after its `/b/` describes that extra noun.

> `zuzozen vezuvol hewom bludozol godamul.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-sleep | [h-like | [bl-dog | g-tired]]
>
> "Uzozen sleeps like a tired dog."

Because a second relation after the pair would attach to the extra noun, you cannot put two relation adjectives **after** the same first noun. To keep both on that noun in one clause, put one [before the noun](#left-bound-adjectives) with **`gl-`**; its `/b/` still follows it. Otherwise use [another sentence](dependents.md#which-noun).

> `glorol bluzozen zagayul gorol blobayel.`
>
> [[gl-SAME | bl-Uzozen] | z-guard] | [g-SAME | bl-police]
>
> "a guard who is both Uzozen and the police officer"

A verb can take several `/h/` or `/th/` units. Each plain word counts as one unit, and so does a host with its `/b/` (plus any `/w/` before it).

**Compare with:** a [hook](hooks.md#extra-noun) followed by `/b/` means *in* / *at* / *for*, not the person who receives.

### How much: degree words on `/w/` {#degree-w}

Beginner used **`welazom`** *very* before an adjective. The same `/w/` slot takes a few stock degree words, and each one works before an adverb as well as an adjective.

<!-- cheat-sheet: word-shape-clause -->
| Agebon | English | Cue |
|--------|---------|-----|
| `welazom` | *very* | 🐘: big, as a degree |
| `wohalal` | *extremely* / *overwhelmingly* | 🌊: an ocean of it |
| `wamezom` | *slightly* / *a bit* | 🐁: small, as a degree |
| `wadowom` | *quite* / *fairly* | 🔉: medium volume, not loud or soft |
| `wuhuram` | *completely* / *totally* / *entirely* / *fully* | 💯: a hundred is the whole |

> `zudozol welazom hedurem vowurel.`
>
> z-dog | [w-very | h-haste] | v-walk
>
> "A dog walks very hastily."

> `zudozol wohalal gunemul vowurel.`
>
> [z-dog | [w-ocean | g-angry]] | v-walk
>
> "An extremely angry dog walks."

> `zudozol wamezom gunemul vowurel.`
>
> [z-dog | [w-small | g-angry]] | v-walk
>
> "A slightly angry dog walks."

> `zudozol wadowom gunemul vowurel.`
>
> [z-dog | [w-quite | g-angry]] | v-walk
>
> "A fairly angry dog walks."

Two stock words go in `/h/` and describe the verb itself. Use **`hoyurem`** *kind of* / *sort of* to hedge the action, and **`hurugem`** for *again*.

<!-- cheat-sheet: word-shape-clause -->
| Agebon | English | Cue |
|--------|---------|-----|
| `hoyurem` | *kind of* / *sort of* | 📈: some degree, not a full one |
| `hurugem` | *again* | 🔁: the same thing once more |

> `zuzozen hoyurem vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | h-kind-of | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen kind of walks."

> `zudozol hurugem varuvel.`
>
> z-dog | h-again | v-run
>
> "The dog runs again."

### Leaving out who acts {#no-subject}

English uses the passive (*The cat was seen*, *Mistakes were made*) to avoid naming who acted. Agebon simply leaves out the `/z/` word. The verb and its object stay the same, and the sentence says nothing about who did it.

> `duzozen vawebol.`
>
> d-Uzozen | v-see
>
> "Uzozen was seen."

**Compare with:** [`zumon`](pronouns.md#special-pronouns) *someone* says that somebody did it, without saying who. With no subject at all, the sentence does not mention a doer. A missing subject never means the topic: to make the topic the doer, write `zalon`.

**1.** *Uzozen sees a police officer who is the same person as sleepy Ahelan.* `zuzozen dobayel gorol blahelan guzemol vawebol.` or `zuzozen dobayel guzemol gorol blahelan vawebol.`
