# Plurality

English *they* and *the Smiths* often mean a named person plus people around them, not a counted list. Egabon marks that **associative group** with optional **-x** after the [word ending](word-endings.md).

## Beginner {#beginner}

### Associative group (`-x`) {#associative}

Without **-x**, the word picks **one** person or thing. With **-x** after the ending, you name an **anchor plus nearby associates** (family, coworkers, unit, household, and so on). Who counts as an associate comes from the conversation and the situation; on a name it reads as *Uzozen and associates*. (cue: **-x** ≈ set with the named anchor)

> `zuzozenx vewurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen-x | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen and associates walk."

A name already ends in **-n**, so a name plus associates is **-nx** (`zuzozenx`). The same **-nx** also marks a group that has its own name or title (*Team Alpha*).

To point back to that group (or to a prior person **and** their associates), add **-x** after resume **-r**:

> `zuzozerx vuhohol.`
>
> z-←Uzozen-x | v-sit
>
> "They sit." (Uzozen and associates)

A [role pointer](pronouns.md#role-pointers) takes **-x** the same way: that one and associates, or the whole group when the earlier slot held a joined list.

> `zuzozen vewurel. zaxarx vuhohol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk . z-←agent.same-x | v-sit
>
> "Uzozen walks. They sit." (Uzozen and associates)

To introduce several things of one kind (English *some cats*), use **-lx** (concrete) or **-mx** (abstract). Plain **-l** / **-m** introduce one thing. With **-x** added, they introduce an **indefinite group**: a set of that kind in this situation, with no anchor and no associates.

> `zagozolx vuhohol.`
>
> z-cat-x | v-sit
>
> "Some cats sit."

> `zuzozen dudozolx vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-dog-x | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sees some dogs."

An extra noun takes **-x** the same way. Here the recipient is Olazun and associates:

> `zuzozen vezuyel bolazunx.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-tell | b-Olazun-x
>
> "Uzozen talks to Olazun and associates."

**Compare with:** English *cats* can mean the kind in general. **-lx** means only a set in this situation.

| Egabon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| **-lx** | indefinite group (a set in this situation), concrete | *some cats* (a set in this situation) |
| **-mx** | indefinite group (a set in this situation), abstract | same grouping on a **-m** root |
| **-nx** | named / titled group | *Team Alpha*; *Uzozen and associates* as a title |
| **-lnx** | indefinite group of things a name applies to ([one of a name](word-endings.md#name-instance--ln)) | *some Azawans* (beds of that brand) |
| **-rx** | resume someone already mentioned and associates, or a prior group | *they* (that one and associates) |

### Person-role **-x** {#person-role-x}
<a id="clusivity"></a>

English *we* and *you* do not say whether the listener is in the group (the split between *we including you* and *we without you* is called **clusivity**). Egabon puts **-x** on the speaker and listener roots. It still adds associates, but who is in the group follows each root's place in the conversation. Speaker **-x** is the speaker plus that person’s people, without the listener. Listener **-x** is the **address set**: everyone you are talking to right now, and not the speaker.

Inclusive *you and I* is **`aha`** ([special pronouns](pronouns.md#special-pronouns)). With **-x** it adds the speaker’s and listener’s shared people (below).

> `zehonx vewurel.`
>
> z-listener-x | v-walk
>
> "You all walk." (everyone I'm talking to)

> `zamunx vuhohol.`
>
> z-speaker-x | v-sit
>
> "My people and I sit." (not you)

> `zolazunx vuhohol.`
>
> z-Olazun-x | v-sit
>
> "You and your associates sit." (said to Olazun)

> `zahan vewurel.`
>
> z-interlocutors | v-walk
>
> "We walk." (you and I)

The same roots take the role letter of the slot they fill. As a direct object they are **`damunx`**, **`dehonx`**, **`dahan`**:

> `zuzozen damunx vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-speaker-x | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sees me and my people." (not you)

> `zuzozen dehonx vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-listener-x | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sees you all." (everyone I'm talking to)

> `zuzozen dahan vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-interlocutors | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sees us." (you and I)

<!-- cheat-sheet: people-pointing -->
| Egabon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| **`amunx`** | speaker plus that person’s people (listener not assumed); `zamunx` as subject, `damunx` as object | *I and my people* (not you) |
| **`ehonx`** | all current addressees; `zehonx` as subject, `dehonx` as object | *you all* |
| **`ahan`** | the interlocutor set; `zahan` as subject, `dahan` as object | *we* (you and I) |
| **`ahanx`** | the interlocutor set plus associates beyond this conversation | *we all*, *us and our people* (you included) |
| name **-nx** / resume **-rx** | that person plus associates | *X and associates* |

> `zahanx vewurel.`
>
> z-interlocutors-x | v-walk
>
> "We all walk." (you, I and our people)

**Compare with:** inclusive *we* uses **`aha`** (`zahan`), not **`amunx`**. **`amunx`** leaves you out, **`ehonx`** leaves the speaker out, and **`ahanx`** takes in both and the people around you. Nonspecific *someone* (**`una`**) names no group, so it takes no **-x**: *some people* is `zobelx`.

For *you and yours* (one listener plus that person's people), put **-x** on the listener's name (`zolazunx`). **`ehonx`** means only the address set.

## Intermediate {#intermediate}

### Associate-set resolution
<a id="associate-resolution"></a>

English *Uzozen and them* does not say who belongs. Egabon **-x** is the same: who counts is **open** unless you say. When the list matters, name the members with a [join](joins.md). When it does not, the listener guesses in this order: a group already named in the conversation, then the obvious group in the scene (this meeting, this household).

> `zuzozenx vewurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen-x | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen and associates walk."

If the wrong reading would matter, say so. Use **`aha`** or a join for *you and I*. For *not you*, use speaker **-x**, or subtract the listener with the *except* [hook](hooks.md) **`ul`**:

> `zuzozenx ul zehon vewurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen-x | except | z-listener | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen and associates, not counting you, walk."

| Priority | Use | English |
|----------|-----|---------|
| Explicit join / except | listed members win | *Uzozen and Olazun*; associates minus the listener |
| Recently named cohort | family, team title already in the conversation | that introduced group |
| Institutional frame | this meeting, this household | the scene’s default group |
| Open | no further cue | the listener may or may not be in the set |

For the **address set** (`ehonx`), count everyone called by name at the start of this turn, plus anyone still being addressed from before.

### Groups as the topic

A [topic word](pronouns.md#topic-groups) takes **-x** like any noun: `xuzozenx` makes *Uzozen and associates* the topic, `xudozolx` makes *some dogs* the topic, and `xuzozerx` returns to the group. The published [linkers](dependents.md#sentence-linkers) take no **-x**.

### Verbs (`/v/`): collective {#verbs-v}

English *they walk* does not say whether it was one shared outing. Put **-x** on the **verb** (after its ending) when the event is **one shared act** (a **collective** act). Without verb **-x**, a group subject leaves open whether they acted together or separately, except with a counted object (below). Noun **-x** says **who** is in the group; verb **-x** says they did it as one act.

> `zuzozenx vezuyolx.`
>
> z-Uzozen-x | v-sing-x
>
> "They sing as one performance." (Uzozen and associates)

| Egabon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| subject set + singular `/v/` | how they act is left open | *they walk* (together, separately, or don’t care) |
| subject set + `/v/`…**-x** | collective: one shared act | *they walk as one outing* |
| named person + `/v/`…**-x** | joint doing; unnamed others share the act | *Uzozen sings jointly* (not a solo) |
| group-kind singular (*the family*) + `/v/`…**-x** | the group acts as one unit | *the family walks as one unit* |

<!-- cheat-sheet: people-pointing -->
| Egabon | English |
|--------|---------|
| `zuzozenx vezuyol` | *Uzozen and associates sing* (together or each, or don’t care) |
| `zuzozenx vezuyolx` | *they sing as one performance* |
| `zuzozen vezuyolx` | *Uzozen sings jointly* (not a solo) |
| `zavayal vewurelx` | *the family walks as one unit* |

**Counts with a set subject:** when a set subject has a counted object, a singular verb gives the count to **each member** (*apiece*). Verb **-x** makes the count the **total** for the shared act.

> `zuzozen zolazun zal vawebol dagozolx grarel.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-Olazun | z-and] | v-see | [d-cat-x | g-three]
>
> "Uzozen and Olazun each see three cats."

> `zuzozen zolazun zal vawebolx dagozolx grarel.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-Olazun | z-and] | v-see-x | [d-cat-x | g-three]
>
> "Uzozen and Olazun see three cats between them."

**-rx** on `/v/` resumes a prior **collective** verb (*still as one shared act*).

**Related form:** *three times* uses a [number](numbers.md) under `/h/`; *always* uses **`hual`**.

### Adjectives (`/ɡ/`): collective {#adjectives-g}
<a id="collective"></a>

English *the boxes are heavy* can mean each box or the pile. Put **-x** on the **adjective** (after its ending) when the property holds of the noun's group **as one unit**. Without **-x**, the adjective leaves open whether it holds of each member or of the whole pile. With **-x**, a pile can be heavy when no single box is.

> `zavogalx garolomx.`
>
> z-file-box-x | g-heavy-x
>
> "The file-boxes are heavy as a pile." (but not individually)

| Egabon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| plural noun or list + singular `/ɡ/` | each member, or don’t care | *the boxes are heavy* |
| plural noun or list + `/ɡ/`…**-x** | collective: the set as one pile | *heavy as a pile* |

After nouns joined into a list with **`a`** ([joins](joins.md)), one plain `/ɡ/` word applies to **each member**. With **-x**, it applies to the list **as one unit**.

<!-- cheat-sheet: people-pointing -->
| Egabon | English |
|--------|---------|
| `zavogal zulol zal garolom` | *the file-box and the tool are each heavy* |
| `zavogal zulol zal garolomx` | *the file-box and the tool are heavy together* (collective) |
| `zavayal gagalam` | *the family is vast* (group-level size) |
| `zavogalx garolom` | *the file-boxes are heavy* (members / plain) |
| `zavogalx garolomx` | *the file-boxes are heavy collectively* |

**-rx** on `/ɡ/` resumes a prior **collective** adjective (*still collectively so*).

For *red books*, put **-x** on the **noun** and leave the color word plain, since each book is red on its own. A character trait is likewise a plain `/ɡ/` word, plus **`hual`**.

**Related form:** who is in the set uses **-x** on the noun.

**Compare with:** a scale in [comparatives](comparatives.md) is one line, so that adjective is singular.

### Vocatives (`/y/`)

To call a group at the start of a turn, put **-x** on the call word (the vocative, `/y/`) after its ending, the same way you mark associates on a noun. That names who you are talking to as an associative group. A call ends in **-n** (or resumes with **-r**), so a kind you address takes **-n** as a title. An interjection (**-l** / **-m**) calls no one, so it takes no **-x**.

> `yagozonx.`
>
> y-Agozon-x
>
> "Hey, cats!"

| Egabon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| **-nx** | a kind or a title as the group | *hey, kids*; *Team Alpha* |
| **-rx** | prior addressee and associates | *hey, you (that one) and associates* |
| **`yehonx`** | current addressees, no name | *hey, you all* |

**For *everyone*, use:** [universals](joins.md#universals-domains-generics) (`…ual`).

### Where **-x** sits

Beginner already used **-x** on nouns and person-role specials. The rest of the words that take it:

| Word | Use | English |
|------|-----|---------|
| nouns `/z/` `/d/` `/b/` | associative group (name / resume); indefinite group (kind **-lx** / **-mx**) | *Uzozen and associates*; *some dogs* |
| vocatives `/y/` | group address | *hey, cats*; *Team Alpha* |
| person-role specials | speaker’s people / address set | *I and my people*; *you all* |
| `/v/`…**-x** | collective event | one shared act |
| `/ɡ/`…**-x** | collective property | the set as one pile |
| inner words of a non-opaque span | group reference inside the span | **-x** on those inner words |

**Related form:** a count uses a [number](numbers.md) on `/ɡ/` (digitless **`gral`** = more than one; `grarel` = three). **-x** and a count can combine (`zagozolx grarel`).

**For *every K*, use:** [universals](joins.md#universals-domains-generics) (`zual gagozol`; habitual **`hual`**). For the kind itself (*the dodo is extinct*), use [`zuan`](joins.md#kind-reference).
