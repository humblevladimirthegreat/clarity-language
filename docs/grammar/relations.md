# Hosted relations

Some English prepositions need a content root of their own (*like*, *for* in a swap, *on behalf of*, *between*, *part of*, *as of*). Agebon writes each one as a **relation** word on `/h/` (or `/ɡ/` on a noun), followed by an extra noun in `/b/` that completes it. Everyday *in* / *at* / *from* / *for* (a get) / *using* are [hooks](hooks.md#extra-noun) instead.

## Beginner {#beginner}

You already write *like Uzozen* as a hosted pair (`hewom bluzozen`): a relation word, then the extra noun as a `bl-` word right after it. The same shape covers the other prepositions on this page: pick the relation root, then put a `bl-` word after it. A `/w/` word that grades the relation sits immediately before the `/h/` or `/ɡ/` word.

### Like (*resembles*) {#like-resembles}
<a id="similative"></a>
<a id="like"></a>

When English says something **resembles** a model (*walks like a duck*, *a house like Uzozen’s*), keep the same hosted pair as on the [clause](clause.md#extra-nouns) page. The relation word is **`hewom`**. Put the model in a `bl-` word right after it. Optional `/w/` sits immediately before the relation word. The event or thing itself is real; the `bl-` word is only what it is compared to. (cue: 🪞 *mirror*: you see a reflection, not the same object twice)

> `zuzozen hewom bludozal vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [h-like | bl-duck] | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen walks like a duck."

> `zuzozen welazom hewom bludozal vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [[w-very | h-like] | bl-duck] | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen walks very much like a duck."

On a noun, the same root is **`gewom`**:

> `zahehal gewom bluzozen.`
>
> z-house | [g-like | bl-Uzozen]
>
> "A house like Uzozen’s."

When the model is an event (*walks the way a duck swims*), put [**`blarl`**](dependents.md#poles) after **`hewom`** and say that event as the next sentence. Keep the pair last, as with *because*. You are not claiming that the model event happened; it is only the comparison.

> `zuzozen vowurel hewom blarl zudozal vazuvel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [h-like | bl-that-clause] | z-duck | v-swim
>
> "Uzozen walks the way a duck swims."

As a plain noun, `zewol` is *a mirror*.

With no `bl-` word after a joined subject, the model is the other members: each one resembles the others. That is English *alike* / *similar to each other*. Every hosted relation follows this rule.

> `zuzozen zolazun zal gewom.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-Olazun | z-and | g-like]
>
> "Uzozen and Olazun are alike."

> `zuzozen zolazun zal hewom vowurel.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-Olazun | z-and] | h-like | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen and Olazun walk alike."

**Compare with:** a `/b/` word with no relation word before it is the recipient. *In a house* is a [hook](hooks.md#extra-noun) (`al bahehal`). *Using* a tool is also a [hook](hooks.md#extra-noun). Giving **one** person two labels is not resemblance, so it does not use this pair. *As sleepy as* compares how far two things have a quality, not whether one resembles the other. *As if they walk* says the walk may not be real; with *like*, the walk is real.

| Agebon | Use | English | Same root as | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|--------------|-----|
| **`hewom` / `gewom`** | resemblance to a model | *like* | `ewol` *mirror* | 🪞: the image is of the model |

### Exchange (*for*) {#exchange}
<a id="in-exchange-for"></a>

When English names the **other side of a swap** (*a book for a hammer*), use the same hosted pair as *like*. The relation word is **`heham`**. Put what is given in return in a `bl-` word right after it. That extra noun is the other half of the trade, not who receives the book. (cue: 💱 *currency-exchange*: the arrows swap one thing for another; `/b/` is the other side)

> `zuzozen danal heham blehowol veham.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-book | [h-in-exchange-for | bl-hammer] | v-convertibility
>
> "Uzozen trades a book for a hammer."

On a noun, the same root is **`geham`** (`danal geham blehowol` *a book for a hammer*). As a plain noun, `zehal` is *a currency exchange*. As a verb, `veham` is *to trade* (the convertibility act).

**Compare with:** a `/b/` word with no relation word before it is the recipient (*a book for Olazun* as who gets it). *Using* a tool is a [hook](hooks.md#extra-noun). *Like a duck* is [simile](#similative). *In a house* is a hook (`al`).

<!-- cheat-sheet: linking-clauses -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Same root as | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|--------------|-----|
| **`heham` / `geham`** | consideration / countervalue | *in exchange for* | `ehal` *currency-exchange* | 💱: the arrows swap one side for the other |

### Proxy (*on behalf of*) {#proxy}
<a id="on-behalf-of"></a>

When English says someone **acts as another person’s agent** (*tells on behalf of Olazun*), use the same hosted pair as [*like*](#similative). The relation word is **`hanom`**. Put that person in a `bl-` word right after it. The subject does the act; `/b/` is the person they act for, not who hears the telling. (cue: 🪪 *id*: the card stands as an authorized face; `/b/` is that person)

> `zuzozen hanom blolazun vezuyel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [h-on-behalf-of | bl-Olazun] | v-tell
>
> "Uzozen speaks on behalf of Olazun."

A recipient `/b/` can follow once the pair is complete: `zuzozen hanom blolazun vezuyel bahelan` (*tells Ahelan on behalf of Olazun*). On a noun, the same root is **`ganom`** (`danal ganom blolazun` *a book on behalf of Olazun*). As a plain noun, `zanol` is *an id*.

**Compare with:** a `/b/` word with no relation word before it is who hears the telling. *Using* a tool is a [hook](hooks.md#extra-noun). *A book for a hammer* as a swap is [exchange](#exchange). *In a house* is a hook (`al`).

<!-- cheat-sheet: linking-clauses -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Same root as | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|--------------|-----|
| **`hanom` / `ganom`** | authorized agency | *on behalf of* | `anol` *id* | 🪪: the card stands as their face |

## Intermediate {#intermediate}

### Exactly like (`hewol` / `gewol`) {#exactly-like}

Beginner used **`hewom`** / **`gewom`** for *like*: the event or thing resembles the model in `/b/`. When the match is exact (*walks exactly like a duck*, *a house just like Uzozen's*), end the same relation word in **-l** instead: **`hewol`** on the event, **`gewol`** on a noun. The model sits in a `bl-` word right after it, as before, and the event or thing is still real. (cue: **-l** ≈ locked: the reflection matches the model line for line)

> `zuzozen hewol bludozal vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [h-exactly-like | bl-duck] | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen walks exactly like a duck."

> `zahehal gewol bluzozen.`
>
> z-house | [g-exactly-like | bl-Uzozen]
>
> "A house just like Uzozen's."

**Related form:** a degree word before **`hewom`** grades the match instead (`wamezom hewom bludozal` *a bit like a duck*).

### Locative relations {#locative-relations}

Everyday *in* / *on* / *at* / *from* / *toward* / *through* / *out of* are [extra-noun hooks](hooks.md#extra-noun-intermediate). To say where someone moves from and to, use one hook for each landmark (*from* one, *toward* another), and write the **source** before the **goal**.

> `zuzozen ul buyahel vowurel oel bedavel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [from | b-station] | v-walk | [toward | b-train]
>
> "Uzozen walks from a station toward a train."

> `zuzozen ul bahehal vowurel ol buyahel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [from | b-house] | v-walk | [at | b-station]
>
> "Uzozen walks from a house to a station."

> `zolazun ual bahehal varuvel al bedavel.`
>
> z-Olazun | [out-of | b-house] | v-run | [in | b-train]
>
> "Olazun runs out of a house into a train."

*The guard in a bank* uses the same hook on a noun, not a [*who*-clause](dependents.md#which-noun):

> `zagayul al babohal vuhohol.`
>
> z-guard | [in | b-bank] | v-sit
>
> "A guard in a bank sits."

<a id="spatial-path"></a>

*Between* needs a **root**, not a hook: the position depends on **two** landmarks at once. Use hosted **`hedam`** (or **`gedam`** on a noun) and put both landmarks in `/b/` as a [join](joins.md). (cue: 🥪 *sandwich*: the filling sits between two sides)

> `zolazun vuhohol hedam bledavel bluyahel bal.`
>
> z-Olazun | v-sit | [h-between | [bl-train | bl-station | b-and]]
>
> "Olazun sits between a train and a station."

The `bl-` word after the relation word is **one slot**. A join (`bal`) or a [plural](plurality.md) fills it whole: each item takes `bl-`, the join word does not, and the landmarks do not each need their own relation word. The same holds on a noun:

> `zuzozen gedam bledavel bluyahel bal vuhohol.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | [g-between | [bl-train | bl-station | b-and]]] | v-sit
>
> "Uzozen, between a train and a station, sits."

As a plain noun, `zedal` is *a sandwich*.

<a id="across"></a>

*Across* also needs a root. The hook **`uol`** *through* passes through the inside of the landmark (a crowd, a tunnel). **`hohem`** goes from one edge to the far edge (a street, a river). Put the landmark in a `bl-` word right after it. (cue: 🌉 *bridge*: a bridge spans the gap)

> `zuzozen vowurel hohem bluyahel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [h-across | bl-station]
>
> "Uzozen walks across the station."

On a noun it is **`gohem`**: *Uzozen, across the station, sits.* As a plain noun, `zohel` is *a bridge*.

*Around* needs a root too: the person or thing is on **all sides** of one landmark, like a ring. Use hosted **`hevam`** (or **`gevam`** on a noun) with the landmark in `/b/`. (cue: 🎠 *carousel*: the horses ride on all sides of the center pole)

> `zagozolx vuhohol hevam bledavel.`
>
> z-cat-x | v-sit | [h-around | bl-train]
>
> "Cats sit around a train."

As a plain noun, `zeval` is *a carousel*. **`om`** *near* says only that the person or thing is close, not that it surrounds the landmark.

<!-- cheat-sheet: linking-clauses -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Same root as | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|--------------|-----|
| **`hedam` / `gedam`** | position between two landmarks | *between* | `edal` *sandwich* | 🥪: the filling sits between two sides |
| **`hohem` / `gohem`** | path from one edge of the landmark to the far edge | *across* | `ohel` *bridge* | 🌉: a bridge spans the gap |
| **`hevam` / `gevam`** | on all sides of one landmark | *around* | `eval` *carousel* | 🎠: the horses ride on all sides of the pole |

**Compare with:** a `/b/` word with no relation word before it is the recipient. Numeric *from 3 to 5* is a [range](numbers-applied.md#ranges). *Like* is [simile](#similative). *For a hammer* as a swap is [exchange](#exchange). *On behalf of Olazun* is [proxy](#proxy). English *of* that is not a place is [of relations](#of-relations).

### Of relations (*part of*, *cup of*, *made of*, *from*) {#of-relations}
<a id="noun-relations"></a>

English *of* also names how one noun sits toward another (*a hand of Olazun*, *a teapot of tea*, *a house of wood*, *Olazun from the city*). Use the same hosted pair as *like*: the relation word on `/ɡ/` (or `/h/` on the event), and the extra noun in a `bl-` word right after it. The `/b/` word is what comes after *of* in English (the whole, the filling, the material, or the origin), not a recipient.

> `zuzozen dahebal gobem blolazun vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [d-hand | [g-part-of | bl-Olazun]] | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sees one of Olazun's hands."

> `zuzozen dedodal gadam blududal vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [d-teapot | [g-contents | bl-tea]] | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sees a teapot full of tea."

> `zuzozen dahehal gulem blulel vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [d-house | [g-material | bl-wood]] | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sees a house made of wood."

> `zolazun gaham blezahel vowurel.`
>
> [z-Olazun | [g-origin | bl-skyline]] | v-walk
>
> "Olazun, who is from the city, walks."

On an event, the same roots take `/h/` (`zuzozen vuguyol hulem blulel` *Uzozen crafts out of wood*; `zolazun vowurel haham blezahel` *Olazun walks, originating from the city*). As plain nouns, the same roots name ordinary things (`zobel` *a bone*; `zadal` *a jar*; `zulel` *wood*; `zahal` *a country*).

For *a piece of* a mass, put the substance first and `gozowum` right after it. With no `/b/`, the whole it was cut from is left open.

> `zuzozen debodol gozowum vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [d-bread | g-division] | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sees a piece of bread."

Do not flip the pair: *tea of the teapot* (tea that sits in that pot) is the *in* hook (`al`), not `gadam`.

<!-- cheat-sheet: linking-clauses -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Same root as | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|--------------|-----|
| **`hobem` / `gobem`** | constitutive piece | *part of* | `obel` *bone* | 🦴: the piece that holds the body’s shape |
| **`hadam` / `gadam`** | filling of a vessel | *contents of* | `adal` *jar* | 🫙: the vessel named by what it stores |
| **`hulem` / `gulem`** | constitution | *made of* | `ulel` *wood* | 🪵: the stuff the thing is made of |
| **`haham` / `gaham`** | identity source / provenance | *from* (origin) | `ahal` *country* | 🗾: a map of one country as where it comes from |
| **`hozowum` / `gozowum`** | portion cut from a whole | *a piece of* / *a slice of* | `ozowul` *saw* | 🪚: the saw cuts a piece off |

**Compare with:**

- Plain *Uzozen's*, with no particular tie, is the hook [`em`](hooks.md#genitive) (`zudozol em buzozen`). Ownership is `gogunum` + `/b/` ([joins](joins.md#scope-fence-p-join)). A need from [sakes](sakes.md) on `/ɡ/` also says the thing is the speaker's.
- Place *in* is the hook `al`; path *from* is the hook `ul` ([locative relations](#locative-relations)).
- *A house like Uzozen’s* is [simile](#similative) (`gewom`), not *made of*. *A book for a hammer* as a swap is [exchange](#exchange) (`geham`).
- `gobem` is a part that belongs to the thing's structure (a bone of the body); `gozowum` is a piece taken off it (a slice of bread).
- A portion *of* a class is [percent](numbers-applied.md#percent-and-percentage-points). A person and their associates take plural **-x**, not *part of*.
- *From* as the origin of a whole kind (`ROOTl#e-`) is [numeric derivation](numeric-derivation.md), not this *from*, which is about one particular thing.

### Social relations (*Uzozen's friend*, *boss*) {#social-relations}

English *'s* also names a social tie (*Uzozen's friend*, *Uzozen's boss*). Put the root for the tie on `/ɡ/` with **-m** (the abstract sense), and put the other person in a `bl-` word right after it, as with the *of* relations. `/b/` is the person the tie points to.

> `zolazun gumayam bluzozen.`
>
> z-Olazun | [g-companionship | bl-Uzozen]
>
> "Olazun is Uzozen's friend."

> `zuzozen dolazun gugobem bluzozen vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [d-Olazun | [g-leadership | bl-Uzozen]] | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sees Olazun, Uzozen's boss."

With no `bl-` word after a joined subject, the tie points at the other members, as with `gewom` ([similative](#similative)). The other party is left out.

> `zuzozen zolazun zal gumayam.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-Olazun | z-and | g-companionship]
>
> "Uzozen and Olazun are friends."

Any root whose abstract sense is a tie works this way (`gohuyam` *partner of*, `gawom` *bound to*). To name the other side of the tie without repeating anyone, use the [role compound](roles.md#role-compounds) with **`o`**: after `zolazun gumayam bluzozen`, `zoxumayar` is *the other party of that friendship* (Uzozen).

<!-- cheat-sheet: linking-clauses -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Same root as |
|--------|-----|---------|--------------|
| **`gumayam`** + `/b/` | companion of `/b/` | *friend of* | `umayal` *Mrs Claus* |
| **`gugobem`** + `/b/` | the one `/b/` answers to | *boss of* | `ugobel` *crown* |
| **`gohuyam`** + `/b/` | partner of `/b/` | *partner of* | `ohuyal` *holding hands* |
| **`gobolom`** + `/b/` | the one `/b/` looks after | *in the care of* (*Uzozen's patient*, *team*) | `obolol` *house plant* |

A tie of care (*Uzozen's patient*, *Uzozen's team*) is **`gobolom`**: `/b/` looks after the person. It says Uzozen is responsible for them, not that Uzozen owns or uses them. (cue: 🪴 a house plant needs someone to tend it)

> `zolazun gobolom bluzozen.`
>
> z-Olazun | [g-tending | bl-Uzozen]
>
> "Olazun is in Uzozen's care."

**Compare with:** kin uses generation numbers ([kin generations](numbers-applied.md#kin-generations)). Plain *Uzozen's*, with the tie left unsaid, is the hook [`em`](hooks.md#genitive). Ownership is `gogunum`. *Part of* is `gobem`.

## Advanced {#advanced}

### As-of (whose now) {#as-of}
<a id="as-of-ledger"></a>
<a id="as-of-bookmark"></a>

English *as of Friday*, *would*, *was going to*, and *if he had* mix two times: **when the event happens**, and **which “now”** the rest of the sentence is measured from (what is still left over, what used to be the pattern, what is planned, what is forecast). Call that second one *whose-now*.

Agebon keeps the two apart. With no extra marking, whose-now is **speech-now**, the moment you are speaking. To move it, write a hosted pair: **`henam`** when you claim that moment as real (a dated entry in the record), **`hunem`** when it is only a placeholder you are not claiming. Put the moment (the snapshot) in `/b/` immediately after. (cue: 📒 *ledger* stamps a dated line in the books; 🔖 *bookmark* holds the place without stamping)

> `zolazun haram henam bl_#22,7 velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | h-RESIDUE | [h-as-of.ledger | bl-_22,7] | v-departure
>
> "As of 22 July, Olazun had left and was still gone."

> `zuzozen henam bl_#22,7 vuhohol al bahehal.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [h-as-of.ledger | bl-_22,7] | v-sit | [in | b-house]
>
> "As of 22 July, Uzozen is sitting in a house."

With nothing else marked, the sitting happens at that now.

The pair covers only its own clause. To keep the same whose-now in a following `/x/` clause, repeat the **same word** with **-r** and no `/b/` (`henar` / `huner`). A new pair with its own `/b/` replaces whose-now, whether it repeats the word or switches between `henam` and `hunem`. A clause (or a noun or adjective, below) takes at most one *as-of* pair.

> `zolazun haram henam bl_#22,7 velurul. xuzozen haram henar vowurel.`
>
> z-Olazun | h-RESIDUE | [h-as-of.ledger | bl-_22,7] | v-departure . x-Uzozen | h-RESIDUE | h-as-of.ledger | v-walk
>
> "As of 22 July, Olazun had left and was still gone. As of that same day, Uzozen's walk still counted too."

The role letter says what the snapshot applies to: `/h/` the whole clause, `/ɡ/` one noun (`genam` *the tab as of Friday*), and `/w/` the `/ɡ/` adjective immediately after the pair (`wenam b_#22,7 gamahum`). These are separate, so one clause may carry a clause snapshot and an adjective snapshot at once. A noun or adjective snapshot does not reach the verb: if the **verb** should also count from that now, the clause needs its own `/h/` *as-of* (or a resume).

The `bl-` word after the pair can be:

- a clock time or date digit string
- an event noun
- **`blarl`**, with the next sentence as whose-now (the pair then goes last)
- extra-noun **-r**, which points back to a time already said (on a date, **-r** is written `=`: `bl=_#22,7` is *that 22 July again*; see the example below where Olazun walks on 22 July)

It is never an offset from speech-now such as *three hours ago*: that would date the event without saying how you know the date, so an undated past event needs a [dated channel](knowing.md#dated-channel). The *as-of* word already carries the date, so do not also write a separate `h_#22,7` for the same snapshot. A bare `h_#…` with no pair dates the event itself ([time](numbers-applied.md#time)). A clock time, *before*, or *after* may sit beside an *as-of* pair.

Do not put a bare person in `/b/` to mean “from Olazun’s now.” A person after `hanom` is [proxy](#proxy). To mean *as of when Olazun told it*, use **`blarl`** and a sentence: `henam blarl zolazun vezuyel`.

Once whose-now is set, these count from it:

- A [forecast](knowing.md#forecast) offset (`blral`) counts forward **from** that now.
- [RESIDUE](knowing.md#residue) is what is still left over **at** that now.
- [FORMER](knowing.md#former-climate) means the pattern no longer held **at** that now.
- A bare verb happens **at** that now.

[MAY](knowing.md#may), [CAUSE](causation.md#cause), and every other stance still count from speech-now. Evidentials, apart from LIVE at the snapshot, still say how *you* know at speech-now.

> `zuzozen zolazun zel wenam b_#22,7 gamahum.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-Olazun | z-rank/more | [[w-as-of.ledger | b-_22,7] | g-challenge]]
>
> "As of 22 July, Uzozen is more challenging than Olazun."

Here the snapshot covers only *challenging*. It does not reach a verb: a verb in this clause would still count from speech-now unless the clause had its own `/h/` pair.

> `zaral haram henam blelurul.`
>
> z-money | h-RESIDUE | [h-as-of.ledger | bl-departure]
>
> "As of the departure, the money still stood."

> `zuzozen henam bl_#22,7 habam blelurul vuhohol al bahehal.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [h-as-of.ledger | bl-_22,7] | [h-before | bl-departure] | v-sit | [in | b-house]
>
> "As of 22 July, Uzozen is sitting in a house before the departure."

*Before the departure* places the sitting itself; *as of 22 July* only sets the now the clause is measured from. The two times are separate.

> `zolazun vowurel ol b_#22,7. xolazun haram henam bl=_#22,7 velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | v-walk | [at | b-_22,7] . x-Olazun | h-RESIDUE | [h-as-of.ledger | bl-←_22,7] | v-departure
>
> "Olazun walked on 22 July. As of that day, Olazun had left and was still gone."

> `zolazun haram velurul henam blarl zolazun vezuyel.`
>
> z-Olazun | h-RESIDUE | v-departure | [h-as-of.ledger | bl-that-clause] | z-Olazun | v-tell
>
> "As of when Olazun spoke, Olazun had left and was still gone."

The speaking only sets the now; it is not what is left over.

> `zolazun haram henam bl_#22,7 velurul. xuzozen thubem blral hunem bl_#23,7 vowurel.`
>
> z-Olazun | h-RESIDUE | [h-as-of.ledger | bl-_22,7] | v-departure . x-Uzozen | [th-PATTERN | bl-later] | [h-as-of.bookmark | bl-_23,7] | v-walk
>
> "As of 22 July, Olazun had left and was still gone. Taking 23 July as now, Uzozen would walk, going by the pattern."

The second sentence's own pair replaces the 22 July now.

An *as-of* pair inside a finished inner clause cannot be resumed later in the main clause. `henar` on its own parses, but write it only after a clause that set whose-now with `henam` and a `/b/`.

**Compare with:** [*after*](dependents.md#time-poles) **`halam`** and extra-noun **`ol`** *at* place the event in time or next to a landmark; they do not set whose-now. [Causal *if*](causation.md#if) **`thohom`** is a condition judged at speech-now, not English *if he had* (`hunem`). [NOTIONAL](knowing.md#notional) is pretend, not a placeholder now. [Scope islands](joins.md#scope-islands-join) limit how far a join reaches; they do not carry an *as-of* forward. On the next clause, repeat the evidential, resume the *as-of*, or both.

| Agebon | Use | English | Same root as | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|--------------|-----|
| **`henam` / `genam` / `wenam`** | asserted whose-now | *as of* (dated ledger) | `enal` *ledger* | 📒: a dated line in the books |
| **`hunem` / `gunem` / `wunem`** | unasserted whose-now | *as of* (placeholder now) | `unel` *bookmark* | 🔖: hold the place without stamping |

### Stance as-of {#stance-as-of}

A stance is yours at speech-now: `thomam` is how unsure you are **as you speak**, and a sake or feeling word is how you stand **now**. English *I was sure then*, *back then it stung*, and *I'd be relieved* date the stance itself. Put the same *as-of* pair on `/th/`: **`thenam`** for a real now, **`thunem`** for a now you are not claiming, then the snapshot in `/b/`. Every stance word in the clause, including MAY, WANT, CAUSE, sakes, and feelings, then reads from that now. A [PLAN](intention.md#plan-predict) is then planned **from** that now, and a [DECISION](intention.md#decision) is held **at** it.

> `zolazun thenam bl_#22,7 thomam velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | [th-as-of.ledger | bl-_22,7] | th-MAY | v-departure
>
> "On 22 July, I thought Olazun might leave." (no claim about what I think now)

> `zolazun thomam velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-MAY | v-departure
>
> "Olazun might leave."

`/th/` *as-of* moves only the stances. `/h/` *as-of* moves the event's books. A clause may carry one of each, so a past stance can sit next to a past event:

> `zolazun thenam bl_#22,7 thomam henam bl_#22,7 velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | [th-as-of.ledger | bl-_22,7] | th-MAY | [h-as-of.ledger | bl-_22,7] | v-departure
>
> "On 22 July, I thought Olazun might have left by then."

With **`thunem`**, the stance is one you would hold, not one you held:

> `zolazun thomam velurul thunem blarl zuzozen vezuyel.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-MAY | v-departure | [th-as-of.bookmark | bl-that-clause] | z-Uzozen | v-tell
>
> "Once Uzozen had spoken, I'd think Olazun might leave."

Resume works as on `/h/`: **`thenar`** / **`thuner`** with no `/b/` on the next `/x/` clause keeps the same stance-now.

Unlike the event pair, stance *as-of* also takes a signed [offset](knowing.md#dated-channel) from speech-now in its `/b/`, because it dates only your stance, not the event. Digitless **`blrul`** is *back then*, with no amount claimed:

> `zolazun thenam blrul thomam velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | [th-as-of.ledger | bl-earlier] | th-MAY | v-departure
>
> "Back then, I thought Olazun might leave."

Changing your mind is then a change of now, not a contradiction: *I was fairly sure on the 22nd* (`thenam`) and *I'm unsure now* (no pair) are both true.

**Compare with:** an evidential is how you know **at speech-now**, even under `/th/` *as-of*. Someone else's stance takes a [holder](knowing.md#holder); with one, `thenam` dates **their** stance. *I remember feeling it* is a remembering channel (`thevum`) plus `thenam`.

| **`thunem`** | stances as of a now you are not claiming | *I would think / I'd feel* | 🔖: a held place |
| **`thenar` / `thuner`** | resume that stance-now | *still as I saw it then* | **-r** restates |
