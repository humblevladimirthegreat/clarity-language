# Compounds
<a id="x-compounds"></a>
<a id="compound-parser"></a>

Sometimes one Agebon word is two smaller meanings with **`x`** in the middle. The last letter (**-l** / **-m** / **-n** / **-r**) is still the ending of the **whole** word. What sits left and right of that **`x`** tells you which kind of word it is.

## Beginner {#beginner}

### Two roots, one word

English often treats two words as one idea (*peanut butter*), or joins two names with a hyphen. In Agebon you write both dictionary roots in **one** word and put **`x`** between them. The first letter of the word is still the [role letter](clause.md#beginner) (subject, verb, and so on). The [ending](word-endings.md) belongs to the whole word, not to the piece before **`x`** alone. (cue: **x** ≈ plus: two pieces make one word)

> `zubedaxuyudal.`
>
> z-peanut-x-butter
>
> "peanut butter"

The last root is the kind of thing; the left root specifies it (what field, what extra label). You get one thing in the sentence, not two.

> `gagamaxolamal.`
>
> g-crush-x-love
>
> "in love" (the crush kind)

| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| full root **`x`** full root | two roots, one word | `zubedaxuyudal` *peanut butter* | **x** ≈ plus |

**Not the same job as:** a [role compound](roles.md#role-compounds) opens with **`w`**, a scope vowel, and a part consonant (`zwazuduyel`). Only a noun inside its stem takes **`x`**.

**Not the same job as:** a word that **starts** with **`x`** [keeps the same speech move going](dependents.md#continue-x). That **`x`** is not this glue in the middle.

**Not the same job as:** mid-word **`th`**. The [stance](clause.md#stance-th) letter joins pieces when the second piece is **your view** rather than more of the same thing, such as a sake and how it stands ([sakes](sakes.md): `guwathal`). **`x`** adds; **`th`** says from where you see it. (cue: **th** ≈ *think*)

### Words you look up, not build
<a id="lexical-compounds"></a>

English *bedroom*, *doorbell*, and *textbook* feel like one word. Agebon lists those as **one dictionary word** too. You look the word up. You do not make it on the spot by putting **`x`** between *door* and *bell*.

Inside the dictionary spelling you can still see the two old roots, with a join letter instead of **`x`**. That letter is the [word ending](word-endings.md) of the **left** root: **-l** when that piece is the everyday kind, **-m** when it is the abstract sense (named **-n** or resume **-r** if that left piece is a name or a resume). The last letter of the word is still the ending of the **whole** entry. In a sentence you add a role letter and that whole-word ending, the same way you do for *dog* or *hammer*.

> `zoholeboral.`
>
> z-doorbell
>
> "a doorbell"

<!-- cheat-sheet: roles-comparing -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| `zoholeboral` | door **-l** bell | doorbell | left piece everyday |
| `zuzahalanal` | school **-l** book | textbook | left piece everyday |
| `zerulalogobal` | rain **-l** coat | raincoat | left piece everyday |
| `…m…` | left root in its [abstract](word-endings.md#abstract-m) sense | same two roots, join **-m** | **m** ≈ meaning |

If the pairing is not in the dictionary, either glue with **`x`** (`zubedaxuyudal` *peanut butter*) or use two words.

### One thing or two?
<a id="compound-vs-separate"></a>

Ask how many things you mean. *A hammer and a wrench* is two tools, so two Agebon words (and a [join](joins.md) if you need *and*). *Peanut butter* is one food, so one word. *Bedroom* is also one thing, but that spelling is already in the dictionary, so there is no **`x`**.

<!-- cheat-sheet: roles-comparing -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| two words | two things, or a property already marked another way | `zehowol zerewol zam` *a hammer and a wrench*; `zudozol gubulel` *a blue dog* (`/ɡ/`) | |
| **`x`** in the middle | one thing; you can still hear both roots | `gagamaxolamal` *in love* (the crush kind); `zubedaxuyudal` *peanut butter*; `zuzozexolazun` (one person) | **x** ≈ plus |
| dictionary word | one familiar kind, listed as a single entry | `zobahohal` *bedroom*; `zozalal` *sunflower* | join letter in the entry, not **`x`** |

**Compare with:** a property on a noun uses `/ɡ/` (or `/w/`). A list uses a [join](joins.md).

**Related form:** *someone who teaches* (who does / where it happens / who undergoes / who is told; **-r** *the one teaching*) uses a [role compound](roles.md#role-compounds).

## Intermediate {#intermediate}

### Left and right of `x` {#families-by-shape}

Beginner already used two roots glued with **`x`** and dictionary words with no **`x`**. After the role letter, look at both sides of the **first** **`x`**. That pair picks the family. A [role compound](roles.md#role-compounds) or [role pointer](pronouns.md#role-pointers) is not one of these families: it opens with **`w`**, a scope vowel, and a part consonant (`zwazuduyel`, `zwazar`), and only a noun inside its stem takes **`x`**.

<!-- cheat-sheet: roles-comparing -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| name **`x`** **`a`** / **`o`** / **`e`** / **`u`** + **-n** (conversation length) | conversation length | *Uzozen, I have time* | vowel on the name |
| longer root **`x`** **`a`** / **`e`** / **`o`** / **`u`** | ability | *can’t sing right now* | extra vowel after the root |
| full roots on **both** sides, maybe more **`x`** + root | ordinary compound | `zubedaxuyudal`; `zalexalowen` ([hyphenated names](word-endings.md#multipart-names)) | **x** ≈ plus |
| sake root **`th`** **`a`** / **`e`** / **`o`** / **`u`** | [sakes](sakes.md) | `guwathal` | **th** ≈ *think* (your view of the sake) |
| other root **`th`** **`a`** / **`e`** / **`o`** / **`u`** (or a stack) | [label scope](predication.md#label-scope) | *angry this time*; *a teacher* | **th** ≈ *think* (what kind of claim the label is) |
| direction root **`th`** facing person | [viewpoint lateral](roles.md#viewpoint-laterals) | `gowuhethuzozen` *Uzozen’s left* | **th** ≈ *think* (whose point of view) |

A root is at least three letters, so one or two vowels left of **`x`** are never a root.

### Adding another piece {#ordinary-compound-order}

To narrow a compound further, or to build a hyphenated name from several labels, add another root with another **`x`**. Keep the English order in **one** word (*crush* then *love*; a shop named after three drinks). The **last** root is the kind, and each earlier root narrows it. The ending stays at the end of the whole word. In a name (a person or a shop), the roots are simply labels in order, and none of them is the kind.

> `gagamaxolamal.`
>
> g-crush-x-love
>
> "in love" (the crush kind)

> `zugudaxududaxowulan.`
>
> z-Uguda-x-Ududa-x-Owulan
>
> "Coffee-Tea-Water" (one named shop)

[Hyphenated names](word-endings.md#multipart-names) use the same order (*Mary-Kate*: first piece first). A family name is not part of this stack; it is a separate `/ɡ/` word after the given name. [Body left / right](roles.md#viewpoint-laterals) is not this stack: it joins the direction and whose facing counts with **`th`** (`gowuhethuzozen`), because the second root is a point of view, not a kind.

A [role compound](roles.md#role-compounds) can take such a stack as its stem (`zwazudozoxowurel` *someone who walks dogs*), but is never one piece of a stack.

**Compare with:** a short office **handle** is one root plus [**-n**](word-endings.md#proper-name--n), not this stack. A dictionary compound is one listed word, not a live list you extend.

### Which family is this?
<a id="decision-order"></a>

Use the table above. If the word has a mid-word **`th`**, it is a [sake](sakes.md) word (a [sake root](sakes.md#sake-inventory), then a vowel, with an optional [emotion tail](sakes.md#emotion-compose)), a [label scope](predication.md#label-scope) word (any other root, then a vowel), or a [viewpoint lateral](roles.md#viewpoint-laterals) (a root follows **`th`**). The root before **`th`** decides: after a sake root, letters that look like a root are always the emotion tail (`guwathamol`), never a lateral. Otherwise read both sides of the first **`x`** in this order:

- A name (one root or a [hyphenated name](word-endings.md#multipart-names)), **`x`**, a single vowel **`a`** / **`o`** / **`e`** / **`u`**, named **-n**, as a conversation-length bid → conversation length.
- A longer root, then **`x`**, then a single vowel **`a`** / **`e`** / **`o`** / **`u`** → **ability**.
- Full roots on **both** sides of the first **`x`** (and maybe more **`x`** + root) → an ordinary compound (sense or name).

### Conversation length (name **`x`** vowel) {#conversation-length}

English *I can stay a while* or *I only have a minute* tells the other person how long you can talk. In Agebon, add **`x`** and one vowel before **-n** on a [greeting](word-endings.md#greeting) (your own name with a period, `SELFn.`) or a [call](speech-moves.md#vocative) (their name under **`/y/`**, `yolazun.`). At arrival, the vowel sets how long you expect the conversation to last. At departure, the same form says how soon you are leaving. The vowel is about the conversation, not a grade of the person.

> `SELFxan.`
>
> SELF-presence
>
> "I have time; we can stay."

> `yolazuxen.`
>
> y-Olazun-minutes
>
> "Olazun, I have a few minutes."

<!-- cheat-sheet: roles-comparing -->
| Form | At arrival | At departure | Cue |
|------|------------|--------------|-----|
| `SELFxan` | open-ended: *we can stay* | none | **a** ≈ add (time is in) |
| `SELFxen` | a few minutes: *I have a little time* | leaving soon | **e** ≈ order (the slot has an end) |
| `SELFxon` | one slot: *one ask or comment* | about to leave | **o** ≈ one (one last slot) |
| `SELFxun` | passing: *just acknowledging; not opening a talk* | leaving right now | **u** ≈ undo (lift the talk) |

On a [full name](word-endings.md#multipart-names), the vowel goes on the given name (`alexen galowen.`); a family name takes no bid, since **`x`** + vowel on a `/ɡ/` word is ability. Only one of these vowels per name. These forms use the same scale throughout a conversation: a smaller level narrows the remaining time. **-n** is still the [named](word-endings.md#named-n-beginner) ending.

At arrival, the other person can answer with their own bid. The **lesser** bid sets the shared limit: **a** (open-ended) is more than **e** (a few minutes), **e** is more than **o** (one slot), and **o** is more than **u** (passing). If you offer open-ended time and Olazun offers one slot, you have one slot.

> `SELFxan.`
>
> SELF-presence
>
> "I have time; we can stay."

> `olazuxon.`
>
> Olazun-ask
>
> "Olazun here. I only have time for one thing."

**Compare with:** the same **`x`** + vowel on a verb or adjective is ability (*can’t sing right now*). A [sake](sakes.md) word takes **`th`** + vowel instead (`guwathal`). Conversation-length bids sit on a **named** citation or a **`/y/`** call, with **-n**. In a clause body, a name with **`x`**, a vowel, and **-n** is not a word.

**Compare with:** a plain named citation with a period is the basic hello (`SELFn.`). The bid says how much conversation the person is open to.

At departure, use your own name with the reduced level:

> `SELFxen.`
>
> SELF-minutes
>
> "I’m leaving soon."

> `SELFxon.`
>
> SELF-ask
>
> "I’m about to leave."

> `SELFxun.`
>
> SELF-passing
>
> "I’m leaving now."

## Advanced {#advanced}

### Root tails {#root-tails}

A root ends in a **tail**, its last two letters, that hints at what it names. The last consonant says what **kind** of thing the root's concrete sense is, and the last vowel says what **job** it does. On a five-letter root the first three letters keep it apart from others of the same kind; on a three-letter root the first vowel does.

The tail is a memory aid. No grammar reads it, and you never build a root from the tail. It just means that if you know what a thing is, you already know two letters of its root, and an unknown word in a text still tells you roughly what it names. A country or territory name has no tail: it is spelled from the English sound ([place-name roots](phonology.md#place-name-roots)).

Each letter reuses a job it already has somewhere in the grammar:

| Second consonant | Kind | Example | Why this letter |
|------------------|------|---------|-----------------|
| `z` | creatures: animals, microbes | `agozo` *cat* | [`/z/`](clause.md#role-letters) is the one who acts |
| `y` | people: persons, kin, roles, and what only a person does | `eboye` *baby* | [`/y/` + name](speech-moves.md#vocative) calls someone |
| `m` | mind and feeling: faces, emotions, mental states | `evemu` *fear* | [**-m**](word-endings.md#abstract-m) is the abstract ending |
| `b` | the body and what goes on it: parts, clothes, what you carry, medicine | `aheba` *hand* | [`/b/`](clause.md#role-letters) is the bolted-on piece |
| `v` | doing, playing and moving: actions, toys, vehicles | `aruve` *run* | [`/v/`](clause.md#role-letters) is the action |
| `h` | places and fixtures: buildings, landscapes, rooms, headings | `aheha` *house* | [`/h/`](clause.md#role-letters) is where |
| `l` | nature and raw stuff: plants, weather, sky, materials, colors | `edale` *tree* | [**-l**](word-endings.md#concrete-l) is the concrete ending |
| `d` | food and drink, with dishes and kitchen gear | `oledo` *apple* | [`/d/`](clause.md#role-letters) is what is done to (eaten) |
| `w` | tools and things you use | `anewu` *knife* | [`/w/`](clause.md#role-letters) is *with* |
| `g` | signs and marks: symbols, letters, religion marks | `awugu` *warning* | [`/ɡ/`](clause.md#role-letters) is a grade, a mark on something |
| `r` | counting, time, money and rhythm | `egore` *clock* | `r` after a role letter starts a [number](numbers.md#counts) |
| `n` | titles: mail, paper, labels, and named sports | `unona` *newspaper*, `azuna` *soccer* | [**-n**](word-endings.md#proper-name--n) is the name ending |

The last vowel is the job, with the same vowel series as [joins](joins.md#beginner): **a** add, **o** one, **e** order, **u** undo.

| Last vowel | Job | Examples |
|------------|-----|----------|
| `a` | holds or gathers: containers, groups, masses, what you go inside; or hollow, like a bowl | `abowa` *basket*, `oruda` *rice*, `uneza` *ant* (it lives in a colony) |
| `o` | one whole thing: round or compact, like a ball | `agozo` *cat*, `oledo` *apple* |
| `e` | order: ranked, in sequence, a stage or cycle, a path you travel or are led along; or long, like a road | `orahe` *road*, `egore` *clock*, `aruve` *run*, `urule` *rainbow* |
| `u` | takes away or works against: cuts, removes, harms, stings, forbids; or sharp, like a blade | `anewu` *knife*, `ezozu` *snake*, `evemu` *fear* |

When more than one job fits, try **u** first, then **a**, then **e**. A knife is one tool, but it cuts, so its root ends in `u`. When no job fits, the thing's outline decides: hollow `a`, long `e`, sharp `u`, round or compact `o`. A banana ends in `e` and an apple in `o`. For a feeling, it's which way the feeling points (toward others `a`, inward `o`); for an action, how it unfolds (with others `a`, along a course `e`).

In every kind but feelings, the **middle vowel** usually says how big the thing is:

| Middle vowel | Size | Examples |
|--------------|------|----------|
| `a` | about your size or bigger | whale, ship, bed, bicycle |
| `o` | between your hand and you | dog, chair, guitar |
| `e` | no bigger than your hand | ant, key, apple, phone |
| `u` | nothing you could measure: a stuff, an act, a symbol, a time | rice, red, run, arrow, night |

This is a hint, not a rule: a few roots take another vowel to stay apart from a neighbor. Feelings have no size, so a feeling's middle vowel carries no meaning.

A [dictionary word](#lexical-compounds) made of two roots ends in its head root, so it keeps the head's tail. A few dictionary words end in a bare tail instead of a second root.

### Tail suffixes {#tail-suffixes}

A few dictionary words end in a bare tail instead of a second root. *Friend* is *hug* plus `yo`: `y` for a person, `o` for one thing marked by the left piece, so *a person known by hugs*. The two letters already say what kind of thing the word names, so it needs no second root such as *person*.

> `zahuyayol.`
>
> z-friend
>
> "a friend"

| Agebon | Pieces | English |
|--------|--------|---------|
| `zahuyayol` | *hug* + `yo` | friend |
| `zobahohal` | *bed* + `ha` | bedroom |
| `zubezozal` | *bird* + `za` | flock |
| `zevogoyol` | *flag* + `yo` | citizen |
| `zehahayol` | *hut* + `yo` | neighbor |
| `zogewoyol` | *camera* + `yo` | celebrity |

The left piece is always a five-letter root, and there is no join letter: five letters, then the tail, then the ending. The tail vowel does the same jobs as on a root: **a** the thing holds or gathers the left, **o** it is one thing marked by the left, **e** it follows the left in order or along a path, **u** it takes the left away or works against it. The tail consonant is any kind but nature `l`, mind `m` and counting `r`; those words take a full second root.

When the thing is a more specific kind than its tail, the dictionary word keeps two full roots: *doorbell* ends in *bell*, not in a bare tool tail.

Like every dictionary word, you look these up. You never add a tail to a root on the spot; to join two meanings yourself, use **`x`**.
