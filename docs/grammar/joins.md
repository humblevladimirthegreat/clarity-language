# Joins

When English would pack several things that play the same role into one slot (*a dog and a cat*, *walks and runs*), Agebon writes those items in a row and then one join word after them. That last word is how you know the list has ended. This pattern of items, then the join, is called a **right-close fence**. The join starts with the same role letter as the items (`z` for subjects, `v` for verbs, `x` for whole clauses).

Sometimes you only care that the items belong together, with no ranking among them (*and*, *or*, *not*): that is a **set**. Sometimes you care which item comes first (*X first*, *A before B*): that is a **rank**.

The join’s vowel says how the items combine. It uses the same vowel cues as the [speech-act words](speech-moves.md): **a** adds, **o** picks one, **e** orders, **u** undoes.

## Beginner {#beginner}

### And-lists (`a`)

When English would say *and*, write every item, then the join. Use vowel **a** on that join: you are adding the items as a group. (cue: **a** ≈ add.)

The join’s first letter matches the items: `zam` after two `/z/` nouns, `vam` after two `/v/` verbs.

The ending says whether the list is complete. Closed **-l** means these are the only items, so the English needs no extra words. Open **-m** means other members may exist: add *(and possibly more)*. When unsure, prefer **-m**.

> `zudozol zagozol zal.`
>
> [z-dog | z-cat | z-and]
>
> "a dog and a cat."

> `zudozol zagozol zam.`
>
> [z-dog | z-cat | z-and.open]
>
> "a dog and a cat." (and possibly more)

> `zuzozen zolazun zam.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-Olazun | z-and.open]
>
> "Uzozen and Olazun." (and possibly more)

> `vowurel varuvel vam.`
>
> [v-walk | v-run | v-and.open]
>
> "walks and runs." (and possibly more)

Whatever the join, the whole list fills one role in the clause. A [role pointer](pronouns.md#role-pointers) back to that role picks up the whole group:

> `zuzozen zolazun zal vowurel. zwazar vuhohol.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-Olazun | z-and] | v-walk . z-←episode-agent.same | v-sit
>
> "Uzozen and Olazun walk. They sit." (both of them)

### Right-close fence {#right-close}
<a id="join-series-ending-shared"></a>

Write the items first, then the join. Readers treat the items up to that join as one role in the clause, filled by several members. A flat list needs only one join, at its end.

The join is never optional. Two nouns of the same role side by side, or two verbs, must close with one, because only the join says how the items combine: *Uzozen and Olazun walk* is `zuzozen zolazun zal vowurel.`, never the two names alone. Two verbs in one clause take the join even with other words between them. The join takes every item right before it, back to the last word of another role. Adjectives and adverbs are different: a row of them needs no join, because each one describes on its own ([clause](clause.md#adjectives-ɡ)).

Two full clauses are the one exception: the join that starts with `x` goes **between** them, so you hear how the clauses relate before the second one starts.

Objects and extra nouns list the same way. Two `/d/` objects close with `dal`; two `/b/` recipients close with `bal`.

> `zuzozen dudozol dagozol dal vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [d-dog | d-cat | d-and] | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sees a dog and a cat."

> `zuzozen vezuyel bolazun bahelan bal.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-tell | [b-Olazun | b-Ahelan | b-and]
>
> "Uzozen tells Olazun and Ahelan."

### Choice (`o`)
<a id="empty-allowed-om"></a>

When English would offer a menu and ask to *pick one*, or leave the pick *(optional)*, use vowel **o**. Closed **-l** (`zol`) means pick one of the listed items. Open **-m** (`zom`) means the pick is optional. (cue: **o** ≈ one.)

> `zududal zugudal zol.`
>
> [z-tea | z-coffee | z-or-exactly-one]
>
> "tea or coffee." (pick one)

> `zugudal zom.`
>
> [z-coffee | z-or.open]
>
> "coffee." (optional)

### How many items {#standalone-phrase}
<a id="single-item-phrase"></a>

The join still comes last when there is only one item, or none. How many items sit before it is the **arity**:

| Arity | Agebon | Use | English |
|-------|--------|-----|---------|
| **List** (2+) | `A B zam` | close a multi-item set | ordinary *and* / *or* |
| **Single-item** (1) | `A zal` | that one item is the whole story | *just X* |
| **Standalone** (0) | `zam` | the join alone | *nothing, as far as I know* |

> `zuzozen zal.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-and]
>
> "just Uzozen."

Single-item **-l** also covers English *just* in the sense *merely*: the one item is all there is, with nothing more to it.

> `zuzozen dagozol dal vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [d-cat | d-and] | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sees just a cat, nothing more."

A standalone join fills its slot with no items. In the object slot, `dal` is *nothing*; in the extra-noun slot, `bal` is *nobody*.

> `zuzozen dal vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-none | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sees nothing."

> `zuzozen vezuyel bal.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-tell | b-none
>
> "Uzozen tells nobody."

Right after a word that takes a [`bl-` partner](clause.md#extra-nouns), a standalone join is that whole partner, so it takes `bl-` too. Written plain, it would be the one who receives.

> `zuzozen vowurel hewom blal.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [h-like | bl-none]
>
> "Uzozen walks like nobody."

### Negation (`u`) {#negation-u}

When English would deny the listed items, use vowel **u**. One item before `zul` is *not X*. Two items before `zul` is *neither A nor B*; more items is *none of these*. The join alone (`zul` / `zum`) is *no*. (cue: **u** ≈ undo.)

> `gamahum gul.`
>
> [g-challenge | g-not]
>
> "not challenging."

> `zuzozen zul.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-not]
>
> "not Uzozen."

> `zududal zugudal zul.`
>
> [z-tea | z-coffee | z-not]
>
> "neither tea nor coffee."

### Everything (`ua`)

When English would say *everything* / *everyone*, write a standalone join with two vowels, **u** then **a** (`zual`). With one item before it, the same join means *everything but* that item. Both vowels sit in one word: say them in order, then the ending. (cue: **u** ≈ undo, then **a** ≈ add.)

> `zual.`
>
> z-everything-but
>
> "everything" / "everyone."

### Rank (`e`)
<a id="set-joins-and-rank-joins"></a>

The *and* / *or* / *not* joins above are **set** joins: the items share a slot as a group, and writing one before the other does not rank them. A **rank** join is the other English job: you care which item comes first (priority, favorite, *A before B*).

Write the items in that order, then a join with vowel **e**. The earlier item outranks the later. One item before `zel` means *only X matters*; before `zem`, *X first* / *mainly X*. (cue: **e** ≈ order.)

> `zuzozen zel.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-rank/more]
>
> "only Uzozen matters."

### Unspecified member (`-r`) {#unspecified-member-r-phrase}

When English says *something* or *someone*, or *one of these* without saying which, put ending **-r** on the join. The join then stands for one member of its list that you are not naming. **-r** goes only on a join with one vowel (**a** / **o** / **e** / **u**), not on a two-vowel join such as **ua**.

| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| `zar` / `var` / `xar` | unspecified member of an add-set | *something* / *do something* / *something happened* | **a** ≈ add |
| `zor` / `vor` / `xor` | unspecified member of a menu | *anything* / *do anything* | **o** ≈ one |
| `zer` / `ver` / `xer` | unspecified member of a ranking | *whatever ranks highest* | **e** ≈ order |
| `zur` / `vur` / `xur` | unspecified leftover | *something else* / *someone else* | **u** ≈ undo |

> `zar.`
>
> z-something
>
> "something" / "someone."

> `zeyol zar.`
>
> [z-person | z-something]
>
> "some person." (unknown who)

> `zududal zugudal zar.`
>
> [z-tea | z-coffee | z-something]
>
> "one of tea and coffee."

Write A and B, then `zar`: the join still closes the list, and **-r** means some member of that pair (you are not saying which).

When English says *some of them* or *some people*, more than one, add [**-x**](plurality.md#associative) after the **-r**. The join then stands for a group of members you are not naming.

> `zarx vowurel.`
>
> z-something-x | v-walk
>
> "Some of them walk."

> `zuzozen zolazun zahelan zarx vowurel.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-Olazun | z-Ahelan | z-something-x] | v-walk
>
> "Some of Uzozen, Olazun, and Ahelan walk."

**-x** goes after **-r** only. A closed or open empty set already covers every member.

**Compare with:** on a content word, **-r** [resumes](pronouns.md) the last match. On a join, **-r** is an unspecified member. Standalone **`-rl` / `-rm`** (`darl`, `barl`) is a stand-in for the next sentence, not a join and not *something*.

### Vowels and endings {#join-type-vowel-series}

| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| **a** | add the items | *and* | **a** ≈ add |
| **o** | exclusive choice | exclusive *or* | **o** ≈ one |
| **u** | deny the listed | *not* / *neither* / *none of* / standalone *no* | **u** ≈ undo |
| **ua** | universal; one listed item is the leftover | standalone *everything*; one item *everything but* | **u** ≈ undo, then **a** ≈ add |
| **e** | rank among items | *A before B*; one item *only X matters* | **e** ≈ order |

| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| **-l** | the listed items are the only ones in play | (no extra words) | **l** ≈ locked list |
| **-m** | other items may exist | *(and possibly more)*; empty: *as far as I know* | **m** ≈ maybe more |
| **-r** | unspecified member of the join | *someone* / *something* in the list | **r** ≈ member you have not named |
| **-rx** | unspecified group of members | *some ones* / *some of them* | **r** ≈ member you have not named, **x** ≈ more than one |

### Clause joins (`/x/`) {#clause-joins}
<a id="clause-join-position"></a>

A clause is long, so a join that waits until the end would leave you holding two whole sentences before you learn how they relate. A clause join that starts with `x` therefore goes **between** the clauses: first clause, join, second clause. Every other join still closes its list on the right.

> `zuzozen vowurel xam zolazun varuvel.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | v-walk | x-and.open | z-Olazun | v-run]
>
> "Uzozen walks and Olazun runs." (and possibly more)

> `zuzozen vowurel xol zolazun varuvel xol zahelan vezuvol.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | v-walk | x-or-exactly-one | z-Olazun | v-run | x-or-exactly-one | z-Ahelan | v-sleep]
>
> "Uzozen walks, or Olazun runs, or Ahelan sleeps: exactly one of the three."

Repeating the same join word keeps the list flat. Switching to a **different** join word closes everything before it as one group: `A xol B xal C` is *(A or B) and C*.

So a group always sits on the left. The set joins **a** / **o** / **u** ignore order, so you can usually move the group to the front. When the group has to come last, end the sentence and start the next one with the join: the join then takes the **whole** next sentence.

After a stand-in such as **`dorl`** or **`blarl`**, every clause joined in the same sentence belongs to the dependent clause. A clause meant for the main sentence also starts a new sentence.

> `zuzozen vowurel. xan zolazun varuvel xol zahelan vezuvol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk . x-and-then | [z-Olazun | v-run | x-or-exactly-one | z-Ahelan | v-sleep]
>
> "Uzozen walks. Then Olazun runs or Ahelan sleeps."

A clause join never sits on one clause alone. To deny or single out one clause, put the join on the part you mean: **`vowurel vul`** *does not walk*, **`zuzozen zal`** *only Uzozen*.

A join word with no clause before it stands for a clause of its own: `xal.` *nothing happened*, `xar` *something happened*, `xur` *something else happened*, `xual` *everything happened*. That join word can also be one item of a clause list:

| Agebon | English |
|--------|---------|
| `zuzozen vowurel xam xar.` | *Uzozen walks, for one* (and something else happened too) |
| `zuzozen vowurel xol xal.` | *Uzozen may walk* (Uzozen walks, or nothing happens) |
| `zuzozen vowurel xel xur.` | *mainly, Uzozen walks* (that outranks anything else) |

**Compare with:** a sentence linker (*therefore*, *however*) also starts the next sentence, but it is a content word with its own meaning ([continue](dependents.md#continue-x)).

## Intermediate {#intermediate}

### SHARED after the join
<a id="scope-fence-p-join"></a>

When an adjective or extra noun is meant for the **whole list** (*both challenging*, *Uzozen's dog and cat*), write it **immediately after** the join. Material in that spot is called SHARED: it describes each member of the list. A modifier that belongs to one item only stays on that item (after its head, or `gl-` before it).

> `zudozol zagozol zal gamahum.`
>
> [z-dog | z-cat | z-and | g-challenge]
>
> "a challenging dog and a challenging cat."

A hook belongs to one item the same way. Put the hook and its `/b/` right after that item, before the join word: **`em`** plus a resume gives *Uzozen and his dog*.

> `zuzozen zudozol em buzozer zal vowurel.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | [z-dog | [used-by | b-←Uzozen]] | z-and] | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen and his dog walk."

Only the item just before the join word takes a hook this way. A hook anywhere else in the clause is an extra noun of the clause.

> `zudozol zagozol zam gogunum bluzozen.`
>
> [z-dog | z-cat | z-and.open | [g-ownership | bl-Uzozen]]
>
> "Uzozen's dog and cat." (and possibly more)

A plain `/ɡ/` after that SHARED pair describes its `/b/`, as in [complex chaining](clause.md#complex-chaining).

> `zudozol zagozol zam gogunum bluzozen guzemol.`
>
> [z-dog | z-cat | z-and.open | [g-ownership | [bl-Uzozen | g-sleepy]]]
>
> "Sleepy Uzozen's dog and cat." (and possibly more)

**Compare with:** a [sakes](sakes.md) need on `/ɡ/` already means the speaker’s belonging (`guwathal` *my … serves competence*).

| Join family | Use of SHARED `/ɡ/` |
|-------------|----------------------|
| **a** | distributive property (*both ADJ*) or bundle description |
| **a** + `/ɡ/`…**-x** | [collective](plurality.md#adjectives-g) (*ADJ together*) |
| **oe** + SHARED scale | [equative](comparatives.md#equatives) (`/ɡ/` or `/h/`) |
| **e** / **ue** on NP | [comparison scale](comparatives.md) (`/ɡ/` or `/h/`) |
| **ae** on NP | sorted low to high (`/ɡ/` or `/h/`) |
| **ua** / **uo** | kind / domain for universals |

More items of the same role go *before* the join, not after it.

SHARED material has to be able to describe what the join lists. After a noun join, a `/ɡ/` describes every noun, and an `/h/` is only a scale after rank **`e`** / **`ue`**, equative **`oe`**, or sequence **`ae`**. After a verb join, a `/d/` object, a recipient `/b/`, or an `/h/` adverb is SHARED: every verb takes it. Nothing describes a list of adjectives, so after a `/ɡ/` join the next `/ɡ/` is simply the next item. Any other `/h/` after a join is an ordinary adverb on the verb:

> `zuzozen zolazun zal huhunem vowurel.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-Olazun | z-and] | h-intensity | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen and Olazun walk intensely."

### Rank joins {#rank-joins}
<a id="priority-ranking-e"></a>

When English would say two people are *as ADJ as* each other, use a tie: a rank join with two vowels, **o** then **e** (**`zoel`**, or open **`zoem`**). Write the names, then the tie, then the adjective right after it, so the adjective grades the whole list. In a tie, the order of the names does not matter.

> `zuzozen zolazun zoel gamahum.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-Olazun | z-equal-rank | g-challenge]
>
> "Uzozen is as challenging as Olazun."

**Compare with:** *more / most* and *least* use **`e`** / **`ue`** plus the same shared adjective ([comparatives](comparatives.md)).

### Invert (**`ua`** / **`uo`** / **`ue`**)
<a id="invert-u-stacks"></a>

Beginner already used *everything but* (**`ua`**). A leading **u** inverts **o** and **e** the same way: **uo** offers everything outside the list, and **ue** reverses a ranking.

| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| **ua** | invert add | *everything but* the listed | **u** ≈ undo + **a** ≈ add |
| **uo** | invert menu | *anything but* the listed (free choice outside) | **u** ≈ undo + **o** ≈ one |
| **ue** | invert rank | *A after B after C* (last first) | **u** ≈ undo + **e** ≈ order |

What *everything* or *anything* ranges over comes from context, or from a [SHARED](#shared-after-the-join) `/ɡ/` that names the kind. Only plain **`…om`** means *optional*; on **uo**, **-m** leaves the list open as usual. **-r** still attaches only to one-vowel **a** / **o** / **e** / **u**.

> `zududal zugudal zual.`
>
> [z-tea | z-coffee | z-everything-but]
>
> "everything but tea and coffee."

> `zuzozen zolazun zahelan zuel.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-Olazun | z-Ahelan | z-rank/less]
>
> "Uzozen, Olazun, Ahelan, from last to first."

**Compare with:** plain **`u`** is *not* / *none of* the listed, not an invert stack. *Every cat* uses SHARED kind after **`zual`**: `zual gagozol`.

### Sequence (**`ae`**) {#sequence-ae}

Rank **`e`** puts the first item at the **top** (*A matters more than B*). When English would instead say *A, then B* (an itinerary, a turn order, *from 3 to 5*), the first item is the **start**, not the winner. Write the items in that order and close with **`ae`**.

> `zuzozen zolazun zahelan zael.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-Olazun | z-Ahelan | z-in-order]
>
> "Uzozen, then Olazun, then Ahelan."

<!-- cheat-sheet: joins-hooks -->
| Arity | Closed **-l** | Open **-m** |
|-------|---------------|-------------|
| Multi (2+) | `A B zael` *A, then B* | `A B zaem` *roughly A, then B* |
| Single-item | `X zael` *start with X* / *from X on* | `X zaem` *maybe start with X* |
| Standalone | `zael` *in no particular order* / *any order* | `zaem` *not sure where to start* |

A sequence does not say which item is better. With a SHARED quality it sorts the list from low to high. Two numbers in a sequence are two values in order (`zragel zrazal zael` = *3, then 5*). (cue: **a** ≈ add + **e** ≈ order: *and then*)

### Respectively (`wubam`) {#respectively}

Two *and*-lists in one clause do not say who goes with what. `zuzozen zolazun zal vawebol dagozol dudozol dal.` could mean each of them saw both animals. When English says *respectively* (the first item goes with the first, the second with the second), put **`wubam`** right before the join word of the second list.

> `zuzozen zolazun zal vawebol dagozol dudozol wubam dal.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-Olazun | z-and] | v-see | [d-cat | d-dog | w-respectively | d-and]
>
> "Uzozen and Olazun saw a cat and a dog, respectively."

Uzozen saw the cat, and Olazun saw the dog. The lists pair up by position, so they must have the same number of items:

> `zuzozen zolazun zahelan zal vawebol dagozol dudozol dubezol wubam dal.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-Olazun | z-Ahelan | z-and] | v-see | [d-cat | d-dog | d-bird | w-respectively | d-and]
>
> "Uzozen, Olazun, and Ahelan saw a cat, a dog, and a bird, respectively."

| Rule | Pattern |
|------|---------|
| Where | right before the `a` join word (**-l** or **-m**) of the later list |
| Partner | another `a` list in the same clause with the same number of items |
| Only there | **`wubam`** goes nowhere else, and no other `/w/` word goes before a join word |

The lists can be of different roles. A list of adjectives or verbs pairs with the subject list the same way: put **`wubam`** before the join word that closes it.

> `zuzozen zolazun zal gelazom gamezom wubam gal.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-Olazun | z-and] | [g-big | g-small | w-respectively | g-and]
>
> "Uzozen and Olazun are big and small, respectively."

> `zuzozen zolazun zal vowurel varuvel wubam val.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-Olazun | z-and] | [v-walk | v-run | w-respectively | v-and]
>
> "Uzozen and Olazun walk and run, respectively."

Uzozen is big and Olazun is small; Uzozen walks and Olazun runs. Without **`wubam`**, an adjective right after a noun join is [SHARED](#shared-after-the-join): it describes every noun. When **`wubam`** and `gal` close the adjectives, they are a list of their own, so the first one is not shared.

Inside such a list, a plain `/ɡ/` after an item's hosted `/b/` is the next item, not a description of that `/b/`. The closing join decides it: with `gal` ending the row, every `/ɡ/` in it belongs to the list and so to the subjects.

> `zuzozen zolazun zal gewom blagayul guzemol wubam gal.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-Olazun | z-and] | [[g-like | bl-guard] | g-sleepy | w-respectively | g-and]
>
> "Uzozen is like the guard, and Olazun is sleepy." (respectively)

With no closing `gal`, the row is not a list. The `/ɡ/` after a noun join is SHARED, and the plain `/ɡ/` after its `/b/` describes that `/b/` ([complex chaining](clause.md#complex-chaining)): `zuzozen zolazun zal gewom blagayul guzemol.` is *both are like the sleepy guard*.

### Exclusivity and denying a whole list

When English would allow several listed items at once (*and/or*), stack **a** then **o** (**`ao`**). **`o`** alone is still at most one: closed **-l** is pick one; open **-m** is *(optional)*. On **`ao`**, even **-m** still needs at least one of the listed items.

When English would deny a list as a whole (*not both*), close the list with its own join, then add a **u** join after it. The inner list is the one item before the **u** join, so this is single-item *not X* with the whole list as X. To deny each item instead (*neither A nor B*), put the items straight before the **u** join, as in Beginner [negation](#negation-u).

> `vowurel varuvel val vul.`
>
> [[v-walk | v-run | v-and] | v-not]
>
> "not both walk and run."

> `vowurel varuvel vol vul.`
>
> [[v-walk | v-run | v-or-exactly-one] | v-not]
>
> "not exactly one of walk and run." (both, or neither)

| Agebon | English |
|--------|---------|
| `vowurel varuvel vul` | *neither walk nor run* |
| `vowurel varuvel val vul` | *not both walk and run* |
| `vowurel varuvel vol vul` | *not exactly one of walk and run* |

### Universals, domains, and generics {#universals-domains-generics}
<a id="generics"></a>
<a id="every-k"></a>

When English would say *every cat* rather than *everything but the cat*, write **`ua`** and put the kind as a shared adjective after the join (`zual gagozol`). Any nouns listed before that join are leftovers excluded from that kind. The same **`ua`** join covers both *every K* and *everything but X*.

<!-- cheat-sheet: joins-hooks -->
| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-------|---------|
| standalone `zual` | empty invert-add | *everything* / *everyone* |
| `zual` + SHARED `/ɡ/` | closed every-kind | *every K* (`zual gagozol` = *every cat*) |
| `zuam` + SHARED `/ɡ/` | open every-kind | *every K, as far as I know*; English often says *K in general* |
| `zuan` + SHARED `/ɡ/` | the kind itself | *the K* as a species |
| single-item `zual X` (no SHARED) | leftover only | *everything but X* |

> `zual gagozol.`
>
> [z-everything | g-cat]
>
> "every cat."

The kind word after **`ua`** / **`uo`** takes no **-x**: *every cat* already covers every member.

With a plain verb, *every cat* acts one by one: **`zual gagozol vuhohol.`** is *each cat sits*. For all of them acting together, put [collective **-x**](plurality.md) on the verb (`vuhoholx`).

*Every cat* covers the cats the situation is about. An extra-noun [hook](hooks.md#extra-noun) after the kind is a clause extra, as after any noun: it places the act, and it does not change which cats count.

> `zual gagozol ol bahehal vuhohol.`
>
> [z-everything | g-cat] | [at | b-house] | v-sit
>
> "Every cat sits at the house."

*No cat* is the same shape with **`u`**: **`zul`** plus the kind.

> `zul gagozol vuhohol.`
>
> [z-no | g-cat] | v-sit
>
> "No cat sits."

The same shape with no verb says there is **none** of that kind (*there is no …*), like a lone noun saying a thing [exists](predication.md#existence). Keep the order: `zudozol zul.` puts the dog before the join and means *not a dog*.

> `zul gudozol.`
>
> [z-no | g-dog]
>
> "There is no dog."

#### Not every (`zual` … `zul`) {#not-every}

To deny *every cat* as a whole (*not every cat*, *not all cats*), close the **`zual`** fence with **`zul`**. The `ua` fence is the one item before the **`u`** join, so the join denies that item, as **`zul`** denies any single item. The claim is that the cats do not all sleep: some cat may still sleep.

> `zual gagozol zul vezuvol.`
>
> [[z-everything | g-cat] | z-not] | v-sleep
>
> "Not every cat sleeps."

Where the denial goes changes the claim:

| Agebon | What it says | English |
|--------|--------------|---------|
| `zul gagozol vezuvol.` | none of the cats sleeps | *No cat sleeps.* |
| `zual gagozol vezuvol vul.` | each cat, one by one, does not sleep | *Every cat stays awake* (= no cat sleeps) |
| `zual gagozol zul vezuvol.` | the cats do not all sleep | *Not every cat sleeps.* |
| `zagozol vezuvol vul.` | a cat does not sleep | *Some cat doesn't sleep.* |

The last two come to the same thing: if not every cat sleeps, some cat stays awake. *Not every* denies the general claim, and the last row names one case of the denial.

With **`zual`** / **`zuam`**, a further `/ɡ/` after the kind is a [property](predication.md#classification-property) of every member: *every K is ADJ*. The listener can already pick out *every fire*, so the clause does not say *there is …*.

> `zual gavulul gohamul.`
>
> [z-everything | g-fire] | g-hot
>
> "Every fire is hot."

Open **`zuam`** makes the same claim but leaves the list of exceptions open, the way **-m** leaves any list open: *every cat, as far as I know*. You do not say whether you know of exceptions; you only do not rule them out. English says this as the everyday general claim (*cats are sleepy*, *cats in general*). A resumed noun, by contrast, is one cat already in the conversation.

> `zuam gagozol guzemol.`
>
> [z-everything.open | g-cat] | g-sleepy
>
> "Cats are sleepy."

> `zagozol vowurel. zagozor guzemol.`
>
> z-cat | v-walk . z-←cat | g-sleepy
>
> "A cat walks. The cat is sleepy."

In the sentence after a [`blarl`](dependents.md#poles) on a general claim, a resume of the kind means **the same member**, one at a time: `zual gagozol vezuvol thohom blarl zagozor guzemol.` is *every cat sleeps if it is sleepy*, each cat with its own sleepiness.

With **`zul`** the two readings say the same thing: `zul gudozol gubulol.` is both *no dog is black* and *there is no black dog*.

When a joined subject shares one thing marked [**`SAME`**](predication.md#identity) with no `/b/`, the members are compared with each other: they all have *the same one*.

> `zuzozen zolazun zal danal gorol varayel.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-Olazun | z-and] | [d-book | g-SAME] | v-write
>
> "Uzozen and Olazun write the same book."

For English *whoever* / *whatever*, use a bare [role compound](roles.md#role-compounds), which names anyone who does it: *whoever walks* is *everyone who walks*. Use the agent compound (**`a`**) for *whoever*, and the undergoer compound (**`u`**) for *whatever*.

> `zual gwazowurel vezuyol.`
>
> [z-everything | g-episode-agent-walk] | v-sing
>
> "Whoever walks sings."

Use closed `zual` plus kind when the claim allows no exceptions, and open `zuam` plus kind when you leave the exceptions open. Free-choice *any cat will do* uses `zor`.

**For *always*, use:** bare habitual **`hual`** ([restrictors](restrictors.md)).

Plural **-x** ([plurality](plurality.md)) is different: it names an anchor plus nearby associates, not *every K*.

#### The kind itself (`zuan`) {#kind-reference}

English *the dodo is extinct* and *someone invented the megaphone* are not about any one dodo or megaphone, or about every one. They are about the **kind**: the one thing that lasts while its members come and go. Write **`zuan`** and put the kind as a shared adjective after it (`zuan gedozol`). The **-n** names the kind the way it names a person: one individual, not a set of members.

> `zuan gedozol gedozom.`
>
> [z-the-kind | g-dodo] | g-obsolescence
>
> "The dodo is extinct."

> `zar duan gemowel vobuyem.`
>
> z-something | [d-the-kind | g-megaphone] | v-innovation
>
> "Someone invented the megaphone."

The kind is one individual, so its verb and adjectives stay singular, and the kind word takes no **-x**. The kind is a known thing, so a further `/ɡ/` is a property claim, not *there is …*.

To choose, ask whether one member could have the property. If none could, the claim is about the kind: no single wolf is rare, extinct, or invented. *Wolves are sleepy* is about the members, since one wolf can be sleepy, so it takes `zual` or `zuam`.

> `zuan gowozul gayazum.`
>
> [z-the-kind | g-wolf] | g-rarity
>
> "The wolf is rare."

<!-- cheat-sheet: joins-hooks -->
| Agebon | Covers | English |
|--------|--------|---------|
| `zual gagozol` | each cat in this situation, no exceptions | *every cat* |
| `zuam gagozol` | the members, as a rule | *cats in general* |
| `zuan gagozol` | the kind itself, one individual | *the cat* (as a species) |
| `zual gagozol` + `/v/`…**-x** | all the members in one shared act | *all the cats together* |
| `zual gagozol zul` | the members, denied as a whole | *not every cat* |

A [cause or condition](causation.md) on a `zuan` clause is about the kind, not each member: why the kind is the way it is.

#### People in general (`eyon`) {#generic-neighbors}

English *one*, generic *you*, and *people* make a claim about people as a rule. Agebon has a pronoun for this as well as the every-kind joins. The [generic pronoun](pronouns.md#generic-pronoun) `eyon` works like a one-word version of the open every-kind `zuam geyol`: a default that allows exceptions, not a claim about everyone.

| Form | Picks | English |
|------|-------|---------|
| `awan` | the person actually listening | *you* |
| `umon` | one unidentified individual who exists | *someone* |
| `eyon` | nobody in particular; true of people as a rule, with exceptions | *one*, generic *you* |
| `zuam geyol` | people in general, as far as I know | *people in general* |
| `zual geyol` | every person, no exceptions | *everyone* |
| `zuan geyol` | humankind as a kind | *people* (the species) |

For *everyone, no exceptions*, use `zual geyol`. As a topic, `xeyon` (*now, about people in general*) makes the [topic pronoun](pronouns.md#topic-pronoun) mean *one*.

#### Every and none, by set {#kind-domains}

*Everyone hates me* and *nobody ever listens* hide how far they reach. To name the set a claim covers, put a SHARED word after the join that bounds it. *Everyone we know* is the undergoer role compound of *know* with [pair scope](predication.md#scope-relative): `gwodanam blayan` is *known to the two of us*.

> `zual gwodanam blayan vezuvol.`
>
> [z-everything | [g-pair-patient-knowledge | bl-interlocutors]] | v-sleep
>
> "Everyone we know sleeps."

The join does the rest, as on any SHARED word:

| Agebon | Covers | English |
|--------|--------|---------|
| `zual geyol` | the people in this situation | *everyone* (here) |
| `zual gwodanam blayan` | the people we both know | *everyone we know* |
| `zuam gwodanam blayan` | the people we both know, exceptions left open | *everyone we know, as far as I know* |
| `zal gwodanam blayan` | none of the people we both know | *nobody we know* |
| `zarx gwodanam blayan` | some of the people we both know | *some people we know* |
| `zual gwodanam blayan zul` | the people we both know, denied as a whole | *not everyone we know* |
| `zual gwodanam bluwon` | the people I know | *everyone I know* |

With `blayan`, the other party is the two of you together: people both of you know. The listener can name someone in that set, which an unbounded *everyone* never allows.

### Fence nesting {#fence-nesting}

When one list sits inside another (*(tea or coffee) and water*), write the inner items, then their join, then the outer item, then the outer join. Each join sits after only the stretch it closes. You nest by stacking right-close joins; a flat list still uses a single join after all its members. Clause joins group by position instead ([clause joins](#clause-joins)).

| Pattern | Agebon | English |
|---------|--------|---------|
| flat | `zududal zugudal zowulal zol` | *tea or coffee or water* |
| nested | `zududal zugudal zol zowulal zal` | *either tea or coffee, and water* |
| nested adjectives | `gerulel gamezom gul gelazom gal` | *big, and neither red nor small* |

## Advanced {#advanced}

### Scope islands {#scope-islands-join}

A join takes every same-role item before it. When English groups a small list inside a bigger one of the same role (*Uzozen and (just water)*), wrap the small list and its join in **`{ … }`**, called a scope island. The join inside the island takes members only from between those marks. One island per clause; material outside needs its own outer join. An island holds at most one phrase and may close partway through it ([scope islands](spans.md#scope-islands)).

| Agebon | English |
|--------|---------|
| `zuzozen { zowulal zal } zam` | *Uzozen and (just water)* |
| `zuzozen { zal } zam` | *Uzozen and nothing* |
| `{ zowulal zal }` | *just water* (alone in the slot) |

> `zuzozen { zowulal zal } zam.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | SCOPE[z-water | z-and] | z-and.open]
>
> "Uzozen and (just water)."

**Compare with:** an island limits only what a join or an `/h/` word covers.

### Named phrase **`-n`**
<a id="named-list"></a>
<a id="named--conventional-ending-phrase-level-all-join-vowels"></a>

When a list is a familiar set phrase (*Stop–Drop–Roll*), end its join with **-n** (under `/z/`, `/d/`, or `/b/`). The **-n** names the whole list as one title, the way it names a person ([titled phrases](word-endings.md#titled-phrases)). Members keep their usual endings unless a member is itself a name.

> `zebodol zuyudal zan.`
>
> NAME[z-bread | z-butter | z-and]
>
> "bread and butter" (the familiar bundle)

Standalone **…an** (no items) is *null* / *void*. Under `/ɡ/` `/h/`, **-n** forms join-derived relations; see [Join across roles](join-across-roles.md#join-relations).

**Compare with:** clause **`xan`** is *and then*, not a named list.

### Allowed joins by role letter {#allowed-joins-by-pos}
<a id="phrase-reserved-forms"></a>

A join has at most two vowels. Which endings it can take depends on its role letter:

| Role | Join endings | Notes |
|-------|----------------|-------|
| `/z/` `/d/` `/b/` | **-l** / **-m** / **-n** / **-r** ( **-r** only on **a** / **o** / **e** / **u**), **-rx** | phrase lists; **-n** is a named package; **-rx** is an [unnamed group](#unspecified-member-r-phrase) |
| `/w/` | none | not a phrase list: the only `/w/` before a join word is [`wubam`](#respectively), with **-l** or **-m** |
| `/ɡ/` `/h/` | same series as restrictors allow | [restrictors](restrictors.md#defined-core-full); **-n** forms are [join-relations](join-across-roles.md#join-relations) |
| `/v/` | **-l** / **-m** / **-r**, each with **-x** | **-x** is one [shared act](plurality.md#verbs-v); parallel **-n** forms are [join-act verbs](join-across-roles.md#join-act-verbs) |
| `/th/` | **-l** / **-m** / **-r** ( **-r** only on **a** / **o** / **e** / **u**) | [stance joins](join-across-roles.md#stance-joins) on stance words (*not because…*) |
| `/x/` | **-l** / **-m** / **-n** / **-r** ( **-r** only on **a** / **o** / **e** / **u**) | between clauses, not after them ([clause joins](#clause-joins)); forms: [Join across roles](join-across-roles.md) |

Word shape: [phonology](phonology.md#phonotactics).

### Constraints

These limits apply to every join.

| Limit | Pattern |
|-------|---------|
| One slot per chain | one subject, object, plain `/b/` recipient, `/ɡ/` stack, or `/w/` / `/h/` / `/th/` unit; hosted `/b/` stays on its `/ɡ/`, `/h/`, or `/th/` |
| Matching role letter | every item head and the join share the first letter |
| One right-close per flat list | nest as [fence nesting](#fence-nesting) |
| Shared modifiers | only immediately after each join |
| Phrase **-r** | only **…ar** / **…or** / **…er** / **…ur**, plus **-x** for a group on `/z/` `/d/` `/b/` |
| Join **-x** | after **-r** on `/z/` `/d/` `/b/` (a group); after any ending on `/v/` (one shared act) |
| Verb+object packages | verb-phrase `/v/`; full sentences use `/x/` ([forms](join-across-roles.md)) |
| Revising one item | in-clause *including* / *rather* / *instead* / *except* are [hooks](hooks.md) |
| Several manners on `/h/` `/w/` | juxtaposition (`hedurel halozul`) |

**Compare with:** prefix-less **`al`** / **`am`** / … are [hooks](hooks.md). Under `/h/` `/w/`, the unit is a [restrictor](restrictors.md), not sibling *and*.

*Because* / *if* use `/th/` plus `/b/` [**`blarl`**](dependents.md#poles). An `/h/` or `/th/` word between the items of a noun, adjective, or `/b/` list does not end the list. For how far an `/h/` reaches in a verb list, see [VP and clause forms](join-across-roles.md#vp-clause-forms). Each item of a clause join is a full clause.

### Rare arities and edge readings

Most other phrase joins need two or more items. These edge readings keep a defined sense at every arity, or mix two joins by nesting.

| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| **…ul** / **…um** / **…un** | deny at every arity | standalone *no*; one item *not X*; multi *none of* |
| **…ar** / **…or** / **…er** / **…ur** | unspecified member at every arity | [unspecified **-r**](#unspecified-member-r-phrase) |
| **…oen** / **…en** / **…an** standalone | stock empty rank / named empty | *it's a draw*; *no favorite* (stock); *null* / *void* |
| **…ual** / **…uol** standalone | empty invert | *everything*; *anything (goes)* |
| `zeyol` vs `zeyol zar` | content **-l** vs join **-r** | *a person* vs *some person, unknown who* |
| `A B C zor` vs `… zer` | menu vs rank unspecified | any of them is fine vs pick by ranking |
| `A B zar` / `zur` / `zual` | among / leftover / invert | some one among; some one other than; *everything but* |
| `A B zel` vs `A B zael` | rank vs [sequence](#sequence-ae) | A matters more than B vs A, then B |

> `zududal zugudal zowulal zor.`
>
> [z-tea | z-coffee | z-water | z-anything]
>
> "any of tea, coffee, or water is fine."

**For *except*, use:** hook **`ul`** / **`um`** ([hooks](hooks.md)). Prefixed **`zur`** is unspecified *something else*. Nest when you need a rare mix (pick one of a closed list, but the pick is optional: `zududal zugudal zol zom`).
