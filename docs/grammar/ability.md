# Ability, decision, and try {#ability-decision-and-try}

### Decision (revisability)
<a id="decision"></a>
<a id="decision-mood"></a>

To say that you have decided on something, put **`thogo`** plus an ending on `/th/` before the ordinary verb. This word is called **DECISION**. Like PLAN, it is yours when nothing else is marked, and it does not say the act happens. Its ending says whether the decision is final, still open to change, or only for now, so you can say how open a choice is without changing the verb. (Cue: ✅ A checkmark confirms a choice.)

As a plain noun or verb, the root keeps its own meaning (`zogol` *a checkmark*; `vogol` *to check / tick*).

> `zuwon thogom vezuyol.`
>
> z-speaker | th-DECISION-modifiable | v-sing
>
> "I've decided to sing, and can still change that."

Unlike PLAN, DECISION carries **no time**. It says what you chose, not that the act still lies ahead: the sentence above fits *I've decided to sing* before you start and *I'm singing by choice* while you sing. So DECISION takes no `/b/`, not even a time offset. To say the act is still ahead, add PLAN.

**Compare with:** [PLAN](intention.md#plan-predict) says how fully you have planned a later act. DECISION says how readily you may change a choice, whenever the act falls.

**Compare with:** a [say word](feelings-permission.md#say-words) on yourself is a yes you give openly. `zuwon vowurel hwazal.` is *I promise to walk*, which others can hold you to; `zuwon thogol vowurel.` is *I've decided for good to walk*, a choice only you can see.

#### Endings: changeability
<a id="decision-changeability"></a>

The endings on DECISION run on the same settled-to-passing scale as [changeability](sakes.md#sake-changeability) on an unmet sake: **-l** irreversible, **-m** modifiable, **-r** temporary. With **-n**, the word is an ordinary [name](proper-names.md#proper-name--n). If you do not know how settled the decision is, use **-m**. How firm a choice will turn out is hard to know even for yourself, so **-m** claims nothing either way.

| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| **`thogol`** | **irreversible** | *have decided for good to …* | **-l** ≈ lasting (for good) |
| **`thogom`** | **modifiable** (soft default if unknown) | *have decided to …, and can still change that* | **-m** ≈ open to change |
| **`thogor`** | **temporary** | *have decided for now to …* | **-r** ≈ right now (for now only) |

To say both how detailed a plan is and how firm the decision is, put DECISION after [PLAN](intention.md#plan-predict):

> `zuwon thagem thogol vowurel.`
>
> z-speaker | th-plan-itinerary | th-DECISION-irreversible | v-walk
>
> "I plan to walk, and that choice is final."

PLAN and DECISION endings run the same way: **-l** is the most settled, **-m** the default, and **-r** the most passing.

> `zuwon thager thogor vowurel.`
>
> z-speaker | th-plan-sketch | th-DECISION-temporary | v-walk
>
> "I intend to walk, details open, and I've decided that for now."

To say an event goes against a decision (*against my decision*), put DECISION after the hook **`uem`** ([contrary to a stance](prescription.md#contrary-to-stance)).

> `zuzozen vowurel uem thogom.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [contrary-to | th-DECISION-modifiable]
>
> "Uzozen walks, against my decision."

> `zuwon vowurel uem thogol.`
>
> z-speaker | v-walk | [contrary-to | th-DECISION-irreversible]
>
> "I walk, against my own final decision."

### Try {#try}
<a id="attempt"></a>

To say that someone is trying to do something, put **`howa`** plus an ending on `/h/` before the ordinary verb. It is an [intention adverb](clause.md#intention-adverbs): it covers the whole clause wherever it sits. Unlike PLAN, an attempt is something others can watch, so it is not a stance and needs no holder. The sentence says the attempt happens, not whether it works. The ending says how committed the attempt is. (Cue: 🧪 A test tube tries a sample to see what happens.)

As a plain noun, the root keeps its own meaning (`zowal` *a test tube*).

> `zuzozen howam varayel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | h-ATTEMPT-unstated | v-write
>
> "Uzozen tries to write."

#### Endings: commitment
<a id="attempt-commitment"></a>

On **`owa`**, **-l / -m / -r** say how far the attempt will go. **-n** is ordinary [proper](proper-names.md#proper-name--n). If you do not know, use **-m**.

| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| **`howal`** | **committed** | *commits to trying …* / *keeps trying to …* until it works or is ruled out | **-l** the full run |
| **`howam`** | **unstated** (soft default) | *tries to …* | **-m** middle |
| **`howar`** | **trial run** | *tries out …* / *gives … a shot*: a low-stakes probe, and failing is useful to know | **-r** ≈ right now (a small sample) |

> `zolazun howar vezuyol.`
>
> z-Olazun | h-ATTEMPT-trial | v-sing
>
> "Olazun gives singing a try."

> `zolazun howal vowurel.`
>
> z-Olazun | h-ATTEMPT-committed | v-walk
>
> "Olazun keeps trying to walk."

A trial run (**-r**) plans for failure: if it fails, that is what the probe was for. Use it when you want to lower the stakes, for yourself or for someone else.

#### Fallback (`xon`) {#fallback}
<a id="or-else"></a>

For *try A; failing that, B*, put the clause join **`xon`** between the attempt and the backup. The backup comes into play only if the attempt before it fails, so exactly one of them ends up holding.

> `zuzozen howar vowurel xon zuzozer vezuyol.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | h-ATTEMPT-trial | v-walk | x-or-else | z-←Uzozen | v-sing]
>
> "Uzozen gives walking a try; failing that, Uzozen sings."

An intention adverb covers only its own clause, so the backup above is a plain *Uzozen sings*. Each clause takes its own, so you can probe the first option and commit to the backup:

> `zuzozen howar vowurel xon zuzozer howal vezuyol.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | h-ATTEMPT-trial | v-walk | x-or-else | z-←Uzozen | h-ATTEMPT-committed | v-sing]
>
> "Uzozen gives walking a try; failing that, Uzozen keeps trying to sing."

A fully prepared [PLAN](intention.md#plan-predict) (**`thagel`**) already has backups. To name them, chain the clauses with **`xon`**, first choice first, and put PLAN on each one:

> `zuwon thagel vowurel xon zuwon thagel vezuyol xon zuwon thagel vezuvol.`
>
> [z-speaker | th-plan-atlas | v-walk | x-or-else | z-speaker | th-plan-atlas | v-sing | x-or-else | z-speaker | th-plan-atlas | v-sleep]
>
> "My plan is to walk; failing that, to sing; failing that, to sleep."

**Compare with:** [ability](can-and-cant.md#ability) says whether someone **can** do something; an attempt makes no claim either way. [PLAN](intention.md#plan-predict) is intention before acting; an attempt is acting with the outcome open.

**Not the same job as:** plain *or*, **`xol`** ([clause joins](joins.md#clause-joins)), which has no first choice and no backup.

### Want {#want}
<a id="desire"></a>

To say that you **want** something to happen, put **`thale`** plus an ending on `/th/` before the ordinary verb. This word is called **WANT**. It is a stance: like any `/th/` word with nothing else marked, the want is **yours**, the speaker's. The sentence says you want the act; it does not say the act happens. The ending says how long the wanting lasts. (Cue: 🌠 A shooting star is where you make a wish.)

> `zuzozen thalem vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | th-WANT-unstated | v-walk
>
> "I want Uzozen to walk."

A want is in someone's mind, so someone else's want (*Uzozen wants to walk*) is their **view**: you say whose it is and how you know it with a [holder](evidentiality.md#holder), not with WANT alone.

To want a person or thing (*want Uzozen*), name the act that would get it (*see*, *take*) and put the person or thing in `/d/` as usual.

> `zolazun thalem vawebol duzozen.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-WANT-unstated | v-see | d-Uzozen
>
> "I want Olazun to see Uzozen."

#### Endings: lastingness {#want-endings}

On **`ale`**, **-l / -m / -r** say how long the wanting lasts. **-n** is ordinary [proper](proper-names.md#proper-name--n). If you do not know, use **-m**.

| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| **`thalel`** | a want that keeps coming back | *really want …* / *have long wanted …* | **-l** ≈ lasting (a steady star) |
| **`thalem`** | duration unstated (soft default) | *want …* | **-m** ≈ open |
| **`thaler`** | a want that will pass | *feel like …* / *have a whim to …* | **-r** ≈ right now (a streak) |

> `zolazun thaler vezuyol.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-WANT-passing | v-sing
>
> "Right now I feel like having Olazun sing."

A want, a [plan](intention.md#plan-predict), and a [decision](#decision) are three different steps, so they stack in that order:

> `zuwon thalel thagem vowurel.`
>
> z-speaker | th-WANT-lasting | th-plan-itinerary | v-walk
>
> "I have long wanted to walk, and I plan to."

For *against my wishes*, put WANT after the hook **`uem`** ([contrary to a stance](prescription.md#contrary-to-stance)): the event goes against that want. For someone else's wishes, a [holder](evidentiality.md#holder) goes between **`uem`** and WANT.

> `zuzozen vowurel uem thalem.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [contrary-to | th-WANT-unstated]
>
> "Uzozen walks, against my wishes."

**Compare with:** [hopefully](feelings-permission.md#speaker-attitude) (`thoguyem`) is your hope about whether a claim turns out true; **`thalem`** is wanting the act itself. [Motive](prescription.md#sake-preference) says which sake the act serves, not that it is wanted. In [emotion compose](feelings-permission.md#emotion-compose), SEEKING is a feeling turning toward someone for comfort, not a plain want.

### Someone else's want, plan, or decision {#whose-intention}

WANT, PLAN, and DECISION are all in a mind, and with nothing else marked that mind is yours. English *Olazun plans for Uzozen to walk* and *Uzozen has decided to walk* report what is in someone else's mind as if you could see it. None of the three takes a `/b/` for whose it is. Someone else's want, plan, or decision is their view: you give it with a [holder](evidentiality.md#holder), which says how you know it. That keeps a guess about someone's intentions from sounding like a fact.

After a holder, WANT and DECISION are the holder's, just as PLAN is. After **`uem`**, write the holder word, then the stance, for *against their wishes*.

> `zuzozen thebamuzozen thalem vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | th-REPORTED-Uzozen | th-WANT-unstated | v-walk
>
> "I hear Uzozen wants to walk."

> `zuzozen theyemuzozen thogom vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | th-CLUES-Uzozen | th-DECISION-modifiable | v-walk
>
> "I gather Uzozen has decided to walk, and can still change that."

> `zolazun vezuvol uem theyemuzozen thalem.`
>
> z-Olazun | v-sleep | [contrary-to | th-CLUES-Uzozen | th-WANT-unstated]
>
> "Olazun sleeps, against Uzozen's wishes, as far as I can tell."

**Compare with:** [requirement](feelings-permission.md#requirement) `zuzozen vowurel hwavem blolazun.` (*Olazun makes Uzozen walk*) puts a demand on Uzozen, who now owes the walk. **`zuzozen thogom vowurel.`** (*I've decided that Uzozen walks*) says only that you chose how things go, the way a coach decides who plays, with nothing asked of Uzozen.

### Plan or forecast
<a id="predict-evidentiality"></a>

English *will* can state a plan or a forecast. Your own plan uses [PLAN](intention.md#plan-predict) and needs no evidence. A forecast about the world, or about what someone else will do, uses a [channel](evidentiality.md#evidentiality), a `/th/` word that says how you know, plus the later offset **`blral`** ([forecasts](evidentiality.md#forecast)). So a forecast always says what it rests on.

> `thubem blral verulal.`
>
> [th-PATTERN | bl-later] | v-rain
>
> "It will rain, going by the pattern."

> `zolazun theyel blral vuhohol.`
>
> z-Olazun | [th-CLUES.strong | bl-later] | v-sit
>
> "It looks like Olazun will sit down." (strong clues)

> `zuwon thagem blral vuhohol.`
>
> z-speaker | [th-plan-itinerary | bl-later] | v-sit
>
> "I plan to sit down later."

**Compare with:** without an offset, a channel is about what is happening now or already so (**`thugom`** if you are watching it now; **`thevum`** if you remember watching it). Add **`blral`** when the claim is about a **later** event.
