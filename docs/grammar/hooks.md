# Hooks (*including* / *in* / *instead* / *for*)
<a id="hooks"></a>

## Beginner {#beginner}

English uses small words such as *including*, *instead*, *in*, and *for* to attach one piece to another. Agebon does this with **hooks**: short words with **no role letter** (no `z` / `v` / `g` / …), just a vowel (or two vowels) plus an ending. A hook attaches the piece on its right to something already in play: a phrase in the same role, the sentence before, or the clause itself as an extra noun.

The same spellings work in all three places. You tell them apart by what comes right **after** the hook, and by whether a `/b/` word comes right **before** it.

### Including (`am` / `al`)
<a id="including"></a>

You named something (A), and you want to say English *including B* about it. Put a hook between two phrases that play the **same role** in the clause (both subjects, both objects, both adjectives, …): `A HOOK B`. For everyday *including*, the hook is **`al`**. B is added to A's slot, but A and B do not become a two-item list. (cue: **a** ≈ add.)

```text
A HOOK B
```

> `zavayal al zuzozen.`
>
> z-family | including | z-Uzozen
>
> "The family, including Uzozen."

Because a hook starts with a vowel, the [last consonant](phonology.md#word-edges) of the word before it may slide over and start the hook's syllable, in speech and in song: `zavayal al` can sound like *za-va-ya-lal*. That is fine. They are still two words, and the listener can still tell where the hook starts.

**Compare with:** packaging two members as a list uses a [join](joins.md) (`zudozol zagozol zam` *a dog and a cat*). A hook adds B to A's slot; it does not name a pair.

### Rather (`el`)
<a id="rather"></a>

To correct your own wording, English says *A, or rather B*. Use the same `A HOOK B` shape with vowel **e**: **`el`**. B is a better way to say A. It describes the **same** thing, so it is not an extra member (as with *including*) and not a different thing in A's place (as with *instead*). (cue: **e** ≈ order.)

> `zuzozen godamul el guzemol.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | g-tired] | rather | g-sleepy
>
> "Uzozen is tired, or rather, sleepy."

### Instead (`ol`)
<a id="instead"></a>

For English *B instead of A*, use the same shape with vowel **o**: `A ol B`. A, on the left, is what you named or expected first. B, on the right, is what really belongs in that slot. The order is the reverse of the English. (cue: **o** ≈ one.)

> `dududal ol dugudal.`
>
> d-tea | instead | d-coffee
>
> "Coffee instead of tea."

A and B can play any role, as long as both play the same one. They can even be **verbs**:

> `zuzozen varuvel ol vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-run | instead | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen walks instead of running."

**Compare with:** *Uzozen tells on behalf of Olazun* keeps Uzozen as the teller; **`ol`** puts someone else in that slot.

### Except (`ul`)
<a id="except"></a>

For English *A except B*, use vowel **u**: `A ul B`. What you say holds for A with B taken out. (cue: **u** ≈ undo.)

> `zavayal ul zuzozen.`
>
> z-family | except | z-Uzozen
>
> "The family except Uzozen."

The left side can be the clause standalone **`xual`** *everything* ([clause joins](joins.md#clause-joins)). The right side is then a clause:

> `xual ul zuzozen vowurel.`
>
> x-everything | except | z-Uzozen | v-walk
>
> "Everything happened except that Uzozen walked."

| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| **a** | add a member in the same slot | *including*: A, including B | **a** ≈ add |
| **e** | offer a better wording | *rather*: A, or rather B | **e** ≈ order |
| **o** | swap the item | *instead*: B in place of A | **o** ≈ one |
| **u** | subtract an exception | *except*: A except B | **u** ≈ undo |

### Closed and open endings

The ending on an `A HOOK B` hook says whether B is complete. **-l** says B is the whole of it, like plain English *including B* or *except B*. **-m** says there may be more than B, like adding *and maybe more*. When you are unsure, prefer **-m**.

<!-- cheat-sheet: joins-hooks -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| **-l** (`al` / `el` / `ol` / `ul`) | B is the whole story you stand behind | unmarked (*including B*, *except B*, …) | **l** ≈ locked |
| **-m** (`am` / `em` / `om` / `um`) | B is not the only one | *and maybe more* | **m** ≈ maybe more |

> `zavayal am zuzozen.`
>
> z-family | including.open | z-Uzozen
>
> "The family, including Uzozen and maybe more."

> `zavayal um zuzozen.`
>
> z-family | except.open | z-Uzozen
>
> "The family except Uzozen, and maybe others."

### Glue this sentence to prior talk {#discourse-hooks}

English can open a sentence with *Additionally*, *In other words*, *Instead*, or *Except* to say how it connects to what was said before (this sentence-to-sentence connective is the *glue*). Put the same hook at the **front** of the sentence. What was already said plays A's role, and the whole new sentence is B. The vowels keep their meanings: add, say another way, replace, take out. (cue: same **a** / **o** / **e** / **u** map as in-clause.)

| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| `al …` | add another point you stand behind | *Additionally, …* | **a** ≈ add |
| `el …` | say the last point another way | *In other words, …* | **e** ≈ order |
| `ol …` | this claim replaces the last one | *Instead, …* / *Actually, …* | **o** ≈ one |
| `ul …` | an exception to what was just said | *Except, …* | **u** ≈ undo |

> `al zuzozen vowurel.`
>
> additionally | z-Uzozen | v-walk
>
> "Additionally, Uzozen walks."

> `ol zolazun vezuvol.`
>
> instead | z-Olazun | v-sleep
>
> "Instead, Olazun sleeps."

Use the same **`ol …`** for *actually* when you correct what someone expected: the new claim replaces the old one.

> `el zahelan gudozol.`
>
> in.other.words | z-Ahelan | g-dog
>
> "In other words, Ahelan is a dog."

The hook is glue only when the next word is not a `/b/` word. With a `/b/` word right after it, even the stand-in **`barl`**, it is an extra-noun hook instead.

**Compare with:** *however* / *therefore* use [continue](dependents.md#continue-x) linkers. A hook at the front only adds, rephrases, replaces, or takes out.

### Extra noun (`/b/` after the hook) {#extra-noun}

English prepositions such as *in*, *at*, *from*, and *for* add a place, a source, or someone who benefits. In Agebon, put a hook right before a `/b/` [extra noun](clause.md#extra-nouns). The hook says how that noun, its **landmark**, relates to the event (or to the noun just before the hook). This is not the recipient, which takes no hook (`zuzozen vezuyel bolazun` *tells Olazun*).

So the test is: `/b/` right after the hook, and no recipient `/b/` right before it. Right after a recipient, the hook is same-role *including* (`buzozen al bolazun`). The hook already marks its landmark, so the landmark is a plain `/b/` word, never a [`bl-` word](clause.md#extra-nouns).

The four one-vowel hooks name the everyday extras:

<!-- cheat-sheet: joins-hooks -->
| Agebon | English | Use | Cue |
|--------|---------|-----|-----|
| **`al`** | *in* | containment | **a** ≈ add (into a volume) |
| **`ol`** | *at* | coincidence with a point | **o** ≈ one (the one point) |
| **`ul`** | *from* | source of a path | **u** ≈ undo (leave) |
| **`el`** | *for* | who or what the act is meant for | **e** ≈ order (aim the act) |

> `zudozol vezuvol al bahehal.`
>
> z-dog | v-sleep | [in | b-house]
>
> "A dog sleeps in a house."

To ask *where?*, put the fill-ask blank **`bar`** after the hook (`ol bar` *where?*, `ul bar` *where from?*).

> `zolazun vuhohol ol bedohol.`
>
> z-Olazun | v-sit | [at | b-table]
>
> "Olazun sits at a table."

> `zuzozen dawudal vobuvul ul bedodal.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-wine | v-pour | [from | b-teapot]
>
> "Uzozen pours wine from a teapot."

> `zuzozen vuguyel el bavayal.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-cook | [for | b-family]
>
> "Uzozen cooks for a family."

When `/b/` is a person or group, **`el`** names who the act is meant to benefit, as in *cooks for Olazun*: what the act brings is meant to reach them.

> `zuzozen vuguyel el bolazun.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-cook | [for | b-Olazun]
>
> "Uzozen cooks for Olazun."

These hooks take **-l**: the landmark is the exact place, point, source, or goal.

To say where something is (*there is a dog in a house*), write the noun with no verb, then the hook and its `/b/`. A noun alone says the thing [exists](predication.md#existence), and the hook places it.

> `zudozol al bahehal.`
>
> z-dog | [in | b-house]
>
> "There is a dog in a house."

**Compare with:** *Additionally, Uzozen walks* is a hook at the front whose next word is **not** `/b/` (`al zuzozen vowurel`).

### Since (`ul` on a time) {#since}

For *since* (from a starting point, and still true now), use **`ul`** *from* with a time instead of a place: *from the thunderstorm on*. [**`halam`**](dependents.md#time-poles) *after* only places the event later. **`ul`** says the event has gone on from that point until now. (cue: **u** ≈ undo: leave the starting point behind)

> `zuzozen vuhohol ul benulul.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-sit | [from | b-thunderstorm]
>
> "Uzozen has been sitting since the thunderstorm."

For *since* before a sentence, put **`barl`** after **`ul`** and the starting event after it.

> `zuzozen vowurel ul barl zolazun vezuvol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [from | b-that-clause] | z-Olazun | v-sleep
>
> "Uzozen has been walking since Olazun fell asleep."

English often puts *since* first (*Since Olazun fell asleep, Uzozen has been walking*). Agebon keeps **`ul barl`** after the main clause, because the sentence after **`barl`** runs to the period. As a short answer, the pair and the starting event can stand alone:

> `ul barl zolazun vezuvol.`
>
> [from | b-that-clause] | z-Olazun | v-sleep
>
> "Since Olazun fell asleep."

**`ul`** is the only hook that takes **`barl`**. Every other hook needs a real noun in `/b/`.

**Compare with:** *since* meaning *because* is **`thahum blarl`**. Agebon keeps the time reading and the cause reading apart.

## Intermediate {#intermediate}

### Ending grids

Beginner already used closed **-l** and open **-m** for how complete B is, or how this sentence attaches to prior talk.

#### In-clause

<!-- cheat-sheet: joins-hooks -->
| | **-l** | **-m** |
|--|--------|--------|
| **a** (*including*) | `al`: including B | `am`: including B, and maybe more |
| **e** (*rather*) | `el`: or rather B | `em`: or rather B, and maybe more |
| **o** (*instead*) | `ol`: instead (B in place of A) | `om`: B instead of A, B being one of several possible replacements |
| **u** (*except*) | `ul`: except B | `um`: except B, and maybe more |

#### Discourse

At the front of a sentence, **-m** says this sentence is one of several you could say there: one more point among others, one way to reword, one possible replacement, one exception among others.

<!-- cheat-sheet: joins-hooks -->
| | **-l** | **-m** |
|--|--------|--------|
| **a** (*additionally*) | `al`: *Additionally, …* | `am`: *Among other things, …* |
| **e** (*in other words*) | `el`: *In other words, …* | `em`: *To put it one way, …* |
| **o** (*instead*) | `ol`: *Instead, …* | `om`: *Instead, for instance, …* |
| **u** (*except*) | `ul`: *Except, …* | `um`: *Except, among other exceptions, …* |

To make a whole `A HOOK B` phrase into a name (a store, a title, a handle), give the hook **-n** ([titled phrases](word-endings.md#titled-phrases)). Otherwise use **-l** or **-m**. The two sides keep their usual endings (kinds **-l**, abstracts **-m**, a name **-n**).

> `dududal on dugudal.`
>
> NAME[d-tea | instead | d-coffee]
>
> "Coffee-Instead-of-Tea" (a cafe name)

**Compare with:** *no dog* / *not X* uses a prefixed [join](joins.md#join-series-ending-shared) (`zudozol zul`). The hook **`ul`**, with no role letter, always takes a B out of A.

> `zudozol zul.`
>
> [z-dog | z-not]
>
> "No dog."

### Extra noun: stacked vowels and loose **-m**
<a id="extra-noun-intermediate"></a>

Beginner already used the one-vowel extra-noun hooks (*in* / *at* / *from* / *for*). Hooks with two vowels name the rest of the everyday extras. The test is the same: `/b/` comes right after the hook, and the word right before the hook (skipping any `/w/`) is **not** a recipient `/b/` (so `buzozen al bolazun` is *including*).

A hook + `/b/` right after a landmark (the `/b/` of another extra, or of a word such as a locative relation) describes that landmark, not the clause: *the village near me*.

> `zuzozen vowurel al behaham om buwon.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [in | [b-locality | [near | b-speaker]]]
>
> "Uzozen walks in the village near me."

Several extra-noun hooks on one clause are several extras. To keep the second one on the clause, put a non-`/b/` word between the first landmark and the next hook. Extra-noun **-n** makes the hook and its landmark a name, as on same-role hooks; the landmark keeps its own ending.

With an extra-noun hook, **-m** makes a **frame extra**: the landmark is the general setting, not the exact contact, source, tool, or opponent. It is not a second copy of the same extra.

<!-- cheat-sheet: joins-hooks -->
| **-l** | English | **-m** | English | Cue |
|--------|---------|--------|---------|-----|
| **`al`** | *in* | **`am`** | *amid* | **a** ≈ add (into a volume); **-m** that volume as the setting |
| **`aol`** | *on* | **`aom`** | *over* | **ao** ≈ added onto one face; **-m** that face as the setting |
| **`ol`** | *at* | **`om`** | *near* | **o** ≈ one (the one point); **-m** that point as the setting |
| **`oel`** | *toward* | **`oem`** | *in the direction of* | **o** ≈ at + **e** ≈ for: aimed at one point; **-m** that aim as the setting |
| **`ul`** | *from* | **`um`** | *away from* | **u** ≈ undo (leave); **-m** that origin as the setting |
| **`ual`** | *out of* | **`uam`** | *out from among* | **ua** ≈ leave a volume; **-m** that volume as the setting |
| **`uol`** | *through* | **`uom`** | *by way of* | **uo** ≈ leave via one path; **-m** that path as the setting |
| **`el`** | *for* | **`em`** | *used by* (*B's*) | **e** ≈ order (aim the act); **-m** what was meant for B is now in B's use |
| **`ael`** | *using* | **`aem`** | *by* | **ae** ≈ add to the aim: the means that serves it; **-m** the channel |
| **`uel`** | *against* | **`uem`** | *contrary to* | **ue** ≈ undo aimed at; **-m** the opposing frame |

> `zuzozen vuhohol aol buhohol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-sit | [on | b-chair]
>
> "Uzozen sits on a chair."

> `zuzozen danal ael babewel varayel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-book | [using | b-pen] | v-write
>
> "Uzozen writes a book with a pen."

**Compare with:** hook **`oel`** names where a path **ends** (*toward X*). A [sequence](joins.md#sequence-ae) join on one noun, **`zael`**, names where it **starts** (*from X on*).

> `zuzozen uel bagayul vavuvul.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [against | b-guard] | v-fight
>
> "Uzozen fights against a guard."

> `zuzozen vavobel aem bedavel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-go | [by | b-train]
>
> "Uzozen goes by train."

> `zuzozen vowurel uem berulal.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [contrary-to | b-rain]
>
> "Uzozen walks despite the rain."

To ask *how?* about the means (*with what?*), put the blank **`bar`** after **`ael`**, the same way as [Where?](questions.md#where).

> `yol ael bar zuzozen danal varayel.`
>
> y-question | [using | b-wh] | z-Uzozen | d-book | v-write
>
> "What does Uzozen write the book with?"

**Compare with:** some English prepositions use other forms. *So that Olazun sits* is **`hovam blarl`**.

### Abstract landmarks (*in a crisis*, *out of debt*) {#abstract-landmarks}

English uses the same prepositions for states, plans, and conditions as for places: *in a crisis*, *on a policy*, *out of debt*. Agebon does the same. Put a noun in its abstract **-m** sense right after an extra-noun hook, and the hook keeps the picture it has with a place: a state is something you are **in**, a plan is a surface you stand **on**, a tendency is somewhere you head **toward**, a condition is something you get **out of**. So a new pairing needs no new word: read the hook's picture in an abstract space.

> `zolazun vavobel ual baram.`
>
> z-Olazun | v-go | [out-of | b-debt]
>
> "Olazun gets out of debt."

> `zuzozen varayel uel bonam.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-write | [against | b-policy]
>
> "Uzozen writes against the policy."

| Hook | Abstract landmark | Example | English |
|------|-------------------|---------|---------|
| **`al`** *in* | a state or condition you are inside | `al bobowum` | *in a crisis* |
| **`aol`** *on* | a plan, rule, or footing you stand on | `aol bonam` | *on policy* |
| **`ol`** *at* | a point of concern or a status | `ol babevam` | *at issue* |
| **`oel`** *toward* | a tendency you head toward | `oel buzebum` | *toward a habit* |
| **`ul`** *from* | what something comes from or owes to | `ul buzobam` | *from heritage* |
| **`ual`** *out of* | a condition you leave | `ual baram` | *out of debt* |
| **`uol`** *through* | something you last through | `uol bobowum` | *through the crisis* |
| **`el`** *for* | what you favor | `el bonam` | *for the policy* |
| **`ael`** *using* | what you go by | `ael bugarom` | *by tradition* |
| **`uel`** *against* | what you oppose | `uel bonam` | *against the policy* |

The frames read the same way, with the landmark as the general setting: `am bobowum` *amid the crisis*, `uem bonam` *contrary to the policy*. A reading that needs a different picture is not a hook reading; say it with its own word.

**Compare with:** the landmark's **-m** picks its abstract sense (`bobowum` *catastrophe*, where `bobowul` is *bomb*); the hook's own **-m** (`am`, `uem`) makes the landmark the setting. The two choices are separate, and a word can take both.

### Whose (`em`) {#genitive}

English *Uzozen's dog* often means the dog is in Uzozen's **use**: Uzozen keeps it, walks it, feeds it. Put **`em`** right after the noun, then the user in `/b/`. `em` says B uses the thing or has access to it. It does not say who owns it. (cue: **`el`** is *for B*, a get meant to reach B; **`em`** says it already reached B and is in B's use)

> `zudozol em buzozen varuvel.`
>
> [z-dog | [used-by | b-Uzozen]] | v-run
>
> "Uzozen's dog runs." (the dog Uzozen keeps)

> `zuzozen dahehal em bolazun vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [d-house | [used-by | b-Olazun]] | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sees Olazun's house." (the house Olazun lives in)

For *my* and *your*, put the [speaker or listener](pronouns.md#special-pronouns) in `/b/`: `em buwon` is *my*, `em bawan` is *your*.

> `zudozol em buwon vezuvol.`
>
> [z-dog | [used-by | b-speaker]] | v-sleep
>
> "My dog sleeps."

To describe the thing, put its `/ɡ/` word **between** the noun and **`em`**. The adjective stays on the noun, and `em` still attaches to the noun. A `/ɡ/` word **after** the `/b/` describes B instead ([complex chaining](clause.md#complex-chaining)).

> `zudozol gelazom em buwon vezuvol.`
>
> [z-dog | g-big | [used-by | b-speaker]] | v-sleep
>
> "My big dog sleeps."

With no verb, the same order is a sentence. A noun marked with `em` is one the listener can pick out, so the `/ɡ/` word is a [property](predication.md#classification-property), not *there is …*.

> `zudozol gelazom em buwon.`
>
> [z-dog | g-big | [used-by | b-speaker]]
>
> "My dog is big."

**Compare with:** `zudozol gelazom.` is *There is a big dog* ([existence](predication.md#existence)): a new noun with no `em` introduces the thing. `zudozol em buwon gelazom` puts *big* on the speaker, not the dog. `zudozol em buwon.` alone says your dog is here (*there's my dog*).

Things B uses include places B lives or sits, tools and rides B uses, ideas B works with, and B's turn, time, or occasion: *Uzozen's birthday* is the day that is Uzozen's to have.

> `zuburel em buzozen.`
>
> [z-birthday | [used-by | b-Uzozen]]
>
> "It's Uzozen's birthday."

Other kinds of *'s* each have their own word:

| English *'s* | Use |
|--------------|-----|
| legal ownership (*the dog Uzozen owns*) | `gogunum` + `/b/` |
| something B made (*the book Uzozen wrote*) | a [role compound](roles.md#role-compounds) |

**`em`** always needs a noun on its left: it belongs to the nearest noun before it (after that noun's `/ɡ/` words). After a verb or at the start of a sentence there is no thing for B to use, so `em` + `/b/` is not a sentence there. For someone's act (*Uzozen's walk*), say the act as its own sentence, then [point back to it](dependents.md#which-noun).

**`em`** never takes a person on the left: a person is not something someone uses. **`gogunum`** never does either, because no one owns a person. **`em`** never names a feeling or trait either: a feeling or trait stays something B has, not something B uses. A trait is a `/ɡ/` word on B.

**Compare with:** *for Uzozen* is `el`. A [sake](sakes.md) on `/ɡ/` also marks the noun as yours, together with how it serves you.

### Opening a sentence with stacked hooks {#stacked-discourse}

English *In fact, …* or *What's more, …* adds a point that goes further than what came before. At the front of a sentence, with no `/b/` right after it, the two-vowel hook **`ael`** does this job. (cue: **ae** ≈ add, ranked upward)

> `ael zolazun vezuvol.`
>
> in.fact | z-Olazun | v-sleep
>
> "In fact, Olazun sleeps."

Opening with **`aol …`** gives English *For example, …*: this sentence is one case of what you just said. (cue: **ao** ≈ add one sample) With **-m**, `aom …` adds *among others* and `aem …` adds *in fact, and maybe more*. No other two-vowel hook opens a sentence: for *next*, *by the way*, and *on the contrary*, use a [linker](dependents.md#sentence-linkers).

> `zavayal vowurel. aol zolazun vowurel.`
>
> z-family | v-walk . for.example | z-Olazun | v-walk
>
> "The family walks. For example, Olazun walks."

**Compare with:** `al …` *Additionally* adds a separate point next to the last one; `aol …` gives an instance of it. Inside a clause, *such as* is `am` (*including, and maybe more*).

### Somewhere, nowhere, everywhere {#place-indefinites}

For English *somewhere*, *nowhere*, and *everywhere*, put a [standalone join](joins.md#standalone-phrase) in the `/b/` slot after a place hook. Outside a question, **`bar`** is an unspecified member (*somewhere*), the same way **`zar`** is *someone*. Under **`yol`**, it is the *where?* blank.

| Agebon | English |
|--------|---------|
| **`ol bar`** | *somewhere* (under **`yol`**: *where?*) |
| **`ol bur`** | *somewhere else* |
| **`ol bal`** | *nowhere* |
| **`ol bual`** | *everywhere* |

> `zuzozen vowurel ol bual.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [at | b-everything]
>
> "Uzozen walks everywhere."

> `zudozol vezuvol ol bal.`
>
> z-dog | v-sleep | [at | b-none]
>
> "The dog sleeps nowhere."

### Here and there, this and that {#deixis}
<a id="here-there"></a>

English *here*, *there*, *this*, and *that* point at things by their distance from the speaker. Agebon points from a **person in the conversation** instead: put a place hook before the [speaker, listener, or interlocutors](pronouns.md#special-pronouns) in `/b/`.

Names come first. When you know who is talking or listening, use their name as the landmark (`om buzozen` *near Uzozen*). Use speaker and listener when the conversation role itself matters, or when you have no name to use.

<!-- cheat-sheet: joins-hooks -->
| Agebon | Landmark | English |
|--------|----------|---------|
| `om buwon` | near the speaker | *here* / *this* |
| `om bawan` | near the listener | *there* / *that* (by you) |
| `om bayan` | near both of you | *here* (with us) |
| `um bayan` | away from both of you | *over there* / *yonder* |

Use `ol` in place of `om` for the exact spot (`ol buwon` *right where I am*).

> `zudozol om buwon vuhohol.`
>
> z-dog | [near | b-speaker] | v-sit
>
> "This dog sits here by me."

> `zuzozen danal om bawan vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-book | [near | b-listener] | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sees that book by you."

> `zolazun vezuvol um bayan.`
>
> z-Olazun | v-sleep | [away-from | b-interlocutors]
>
> "Olazun sleeps over there." (away from both you and me)

English *come* and *go* also point from a person, but they hide which one: *I'm coming* moves toward the listener, not the speaker. Agebon names the landmark with the plain motion verb **`vavobel`** (*go*) and a path hook: **`oel`** (*toward*) for *come*, **`ul`** (*from*) for *go away*. `vavobel` says nothing about how someone travels; use `vowurel` (*walk*) or `varuvel` (*run*) only when the manner matters.

> `zuzozen vavobel oel buwon.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-go | [toward | b-speaker]
>
> "Uzozen comes over to me."

> `zolazun vavobel oel bawan.`
>
> z-Olazun | v-go | [toward | b-listener]
>
> "Olazun is coming to you."

> `zuzozen vavobel ul buwon.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-go | [from | b-speaker]
>
> "Uzozen goes away from me."

With an object, `vavobel` moves something else: `zuzozen vavobel duhohol.` *Uzozen moves the chair.* When the mover goes along with the thing, use **`velobal`** (*carry*). The same hooks then give English *bring* and *take*:

> `zuzozen velobal dobogal oel buwon.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-carry | d-box | [toward | b-speaker]
>
> "Uzozen brings me the box."

> `zuzozen velobal dobogal ul buwon.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-carry | d-box | [from | b-speaker]
>
> "Uzozen takes the box away." (away from where I am)

In reported speech, the speaker is still whoever says the whole sentence. After *that* (`darl`, see [dependents](dependents.md#stand-in)), `om buwon` is near the person talking now. Inside a quoted [cite span](spans.md), the quoted person is the speaker, as in English direct quotes.

**Compare with:** *that dog* for a dog already named is [resume **-r**](pronouns.md#resume-r) (`zudozor`), not a place. *Where?* is `ol bar` ([Where?](questions.md#where)).

### Point back (`or` / `ar` / `ur` / `er`) {#hook-resume}

English *there*, *from there*, and *for it* point back to a place or thing already named. In Agebon, give the extra-noun hook **-r** and leave out the `/b/` word: after the verb, the hook points back to the landmark already in play, the way [resume **-r**](pronouns.md#resume-r) does on a noun. A hook with **-r** has no noun on its right, and no phrase on its left to pair with (it is not a same-role hook).

<!-- cheat-sheet: joins-hooks -->
| Agebon | Points back to | English |
|--------|----------------|---------|
| `ar` | inside the place already named | *in there* / *in it* |
| `or` | the place already named | *there* |
| `ur` | the place already named, as a source | *from there* |
| `er` | the one already named, as a goal | *for that* / *for it* |

> `zuzozen vuhohol ol bahehal. zolazun vuhohol or.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-sit | [at | b-house] . z-Olazun | v-sit | there
>
> "Uzozen sits at the house. Olazun sits there too."

> `zudozol vezuvol al bahehal. zudozor varuvel ur.`
>
> z-dog | v-sleep | [in | b-house] . z-←dog | v-run | from.there
>
> "The dog sleeps in the house. It runs from there."

Stacked extra-noun vowels point back the same way: the vowels keep the extra they named (`aol` *on*, `ael` *using*, `uol` *through*), and **-r** drops the landmark.

<!-- cheat-sheet: joins-hooks -->
| Agebon | Points back to | English |
|--------|----------------|---------|
| `aor` | the face already named | *on it* |
| `aer` | the means already named | *with it* |
| `uor` | the path already named | *through there* |

> `zuzozen vuhohol aol buhohol. zolazun vuhohol aor.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-sit | [on | b-chair] . z-Olazun | v-sit | on.it
>
> "Uzozen sits on a chair. Olazun sits on it too."

> `zuzozen danal ael babewel varayel. zolazun varayel aer.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-book | [using | b-pen] | v-write . z-Olazun | v-write | with.it
>
> "Uzozen writes a book with a pen. Olazun writes with it too."

> `zuzozen vowurel uol bahehal. zolazun vowurel uor.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [through | b-house] . z-Olazun | v-walk | through.there
>
> "Uzozen walks through the house. Olazun walks through there too."

At the front of a sentence, a resume hook points back to an earlier stretch of talk, not just the last sentence.

| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| `or …` | back to the main line after a side topic | *Anyway, …* | **o** ≈ one (the one main thread) |
| `er …` | say an earlier point again | *As I said, …* | **e** ≈ order (the wording again) |
| `ar …` | add to an earlier point, not the last one | *Going back to that, …* | **a** ≈ add |
| `ur …` | drop the last point | *Never mind that, …* | **u** ≈ undo |

> `or zuzozen vowurel.`
>
> anyway | z-Uzozen | v-walk
>
> "Anyway, Uzozen walks."

> `ur zolazun vezuvol.`
>
> never.mind.that | z-Olazun | v-sleep
>
> "Never mind that: Olazun sleeps."

**Compare with:** `ol …` *Instead* replaces the last claim. `or …` *Anyway* leaves the side topic standing and returns to the main one; it leaves the [topic](pronouns.md#topic) alone too, so after a side topic the full return is `or xuzozer …`. To go back to a **person or thing** rather than a line of talk, return to it as the [topic](pronouns.md#topic-return) (`xuzozer`), which also makes it the topic again.

### Parallel chains {#parallel-chains}

To say more than one thing about the **same** A (*including B but except C*, or two *including*s), keep adding hooks after B: `A HOOK₁ B HOOK₂ C`. Each hook applies to A, not to the B before it, and each has its own vowel and ending. So C is never tucked under B the way English *including (B except C)* can be.

> `zavayal am zuzozen ul zolazun.`
>
> z-family | including.open | z-Uzozen | except | z-Olazun
>
> "The family, including Uzozen and maybe more, except Olazun."

| Chain | English |
|-------|---------|
| `A am B am C` | A including B and including C, and maybe more |
| `A al B al C` | A including B and including C |
| `A am B ul C` | A including B, and maybe more, except C |
| `A ul B ul C` | A except B and except C |

**Compare with:** a [list join](joins.md) after the hook is finished first, and the whole list becomes B: `zudozol ol zagozol zubezol zam` is *a cat and a bird (and possibly more) instead of a dog*.

### Discourse placements {#discourse-placements}

Beginner put a discourse hook at the front of a plain statement. It can sit in three more places, with the same spellings and vowel meanings:

| Placement | Agebon | Use |
|-----------|--------|-----|
| Before a written turn marker | `HOOK yal BODY` | The written **`yal`** / **`yol`** / …, after any vocatives and interjections |
| Before a default-statement body | `HOOK BODY` | Implied **`yal`** ([speech moves](speech-moves.md#speech-act)) |
| Later clause under `/x/` | `… JOIN HOOK BODY` | Same as the opener |
| Before an `/x/` linker | `HOOK LINKER BODY` | Same as prior talk |

A sentence opening takes only one discourse hook; you cannot chain them the way you chain in-clause hooks. After a written turn marker, a hook inside the body is an in-clause hook.

On a later clause in a [clause `/x/` join](joins.md#clause-joins), the hook goes right after the join, before that clause’s body: `A xam al B` → *A and additionally B*. Before an **`/x/`** linker (*therefore*, *however*, …), it applies to the linker and the rest of the sentence together: `al xahem …` → *Additionally, therefore …*. When one connective is enough, prefer plain `al …` or plain `xahem …`.

> `yuzozen al zolazun vowurel.`
>
> y-Uzozen | additionally | z-Olazun | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen! Additionally, Olazun walks."

The call comes first, then the discourse hook, then the body.

**Compare with:** packaging a list uses prefixed [joins](joins.md#join-series-ending-shared) (`zal` / `zam` / `val` …).

### Detail on the hook (`/w/`) {#hook-w}

To add detail to the hook itself (*hesitantly excepting*, *never excepting*), put a `/w/` word right before the hook. A `/w/` word always describes the word **right after** it, and a hook takes one the same way a `/ɡ/` or `/h/` word does. A [restrictor](restrictors.md) works there too. The hook's right-hand piece follows as usual.

On a same-role hook, the detail describes the *except* (or *including*, *rather*, *instead*). An `/h/` word in the clause still describes the verb.

> `zavayal wozugem ul zuzozen.`
>
> z-family | [w-hesitation | except] | z-Uzozen
>
> "The family, hesitantly excepting Uzozen."

> `zavayal wal ul zuzozen.`
>
> z-family | [w-never | except] | z-Uzozen
>
> "The family, never excepting Uzozen."

A surprise word on the *including* hook gives English *even*: **`wemom al`** adds B and says openly that B is the one nobody expected.

> `zolazun wemom al zuzozen vowurel.`
>
> z-Olazun | [w-amazement | including] | z-Uzozen | v-walk
>
> "Olazun walks, and even Uzozen does."

**Compare with:** `zavayal ul zuzozen hedurem vuguyel` puts haste on the **cooking**, not on the except.

On an extra-noun hook, `/w/` goes before the hook and `/b/` still comes right after it. A `/ɡ/` word after the landmark still describes the landmark.

> `zudozol vezuvol wal al bahehal.`
>
> z-dog | v-sleep | [[w-never | in] | b-house]
>
> "A dog sleeps, never in a house."

A discourse hook takes `/w/` the same way: `welazom al zuzozen vowurel` is *Additionally, very much so, Uzozen walks.* A `/b/` right after the hook still makes it an extra-noun hook, not a discourse hook.

### Ranges (`oel` / `ual` / `uel`) {#ranges}

When the items sit on a line with an order (people in a queue, days, chapters, numbers), English *A through B* names a **range**: A, B, and everything between. Put a stacked hook between two words in the same role: `A HOOK B`. Between same-role words these hooks name only ranges; with `/b/` on the right and no `/b/` on the left, they are [extra-noun hooks](#extra-noun) (*toward*, *out of*, *against*).

> `zuzozen oel zahelan vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | through | z-Ahelan | v-walk
>
> "Everyone from Uzozen through Ahelan walks."

<!-- cheat-sheet: joins-hooks -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| **`oel`** | A, B, and everything between | *A through B* | **o** ≈ at + **e** ≈ for: start at A, head for B |
| **`ual`** | only what lies between | *strictly between A and B* | **ua** ≈ take away both ends |
| **`uel`** | only what lies outside | *outside A–B* | **ue** ≈ undo the stretch from A to B |

The range runs from A to B, in the order you say them. Endings work as on other hooks: **-m** makes the ends soft (*roughly A through B*), and **-n** names the range as a whole. **-r** stands in for one member you are not naming, and under a [question](questions.md#fill-ask-r) it asks which one:

> `zuzozen uar zahelan vezuvol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | some.strictly-between | z-Ahelan | v-sleep
>
> "Someone between Uzozen and Ahelan sleeps."

A range **-r** always has a word on each side (`oer` / `uar` / `uer`). With nothing on the right, stacked extra-noun **-r** still [points back](#hook-resume) (`aor` *on it*).

> `zuzozen ual zahelan vezuvol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | strictly-between | z-Ahelan | v-sleep
>
> "Everyone between Uzozen and Ahelan sleeps." (not Uzozen or Ahelan themselves)

Between two numbers with digits, plain **`al`** and **`ul`** also make a range, because *3, including 5* has nothing to include into.

A word right after B describes B alone, not the range. To describe the range itself, put `/w/` right before the hook:

> `zuzozen wemom oel zahelan vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [w-amazement | through] | z-Ahelan | v-walk
>
> "Everyone from Uzozen all the way through Ahelan walks." (a surprisingly wide range)

**Compare with:** `zuzozen zahelan zal` is *Uzozen and Ahelan*, the two ends only. `zuzozen zahelan zael` is *Uzozen, then Ahelan*, an order with nothing between.

## Advanced {#advanced}

### Hook compounds (cite + extra-noun hook) {#hook-compounds}

English has single verbs for moving in relation to a place: *enter*, *leave*, *oppose*. Agebon makes them by **fusing** an extra-noun hook onto the end of a citation: *walk* + *from* (`vowurel ul buyahel` *walks from a station*) becomes one word, *leave*. Every hook in the extra-noun grid can fuse this way.

Keep the left word’s own **-l** or **-m**, then write the hook, with no mid-word **`x`**. The dictionary form is the **citation** (no role letter), because the same stem can sit under `/z/` or `/ɡ/` as well as `/v/`.

The left word is always a five-letter root from the dictionary. Three-letter roots never fuse, so a word that starts with one is never a hook compound; to say *from a map*, use the two words.

```text
CITE + HOOK
owurel + ul → owurelul
```

In a clause, add a role letter to that citation (`vowurelul`). The extra participant is an ordinary object (`/d/`), not `/b/` (that would still be a recipient after a verb).

> `zuzozen duyahel vowurelul.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-station | v-leave
>
> "Uzozen leaves a station."

> `owurelul`
>
> leave
>
> "leave" (the dictionary form)

The left ending is part of the stem, so two different left roots stay distinct once the hook is attached. In speech that ending starts the hook's syllable, so no consonant closes a syllable mid-word: `owurelul` is *o-wu-re-lul*.

| Hook | Free extra-noun | Fused **-l** | Fused **-l**, abstract `/d/` | Fused **-m** | Citation (from *walk*) |
|------|-----------------|--------------|------------------------------|--------------|------------------------|
| **`al` / `am`** | *in* / *amid* | *enter* | *enter a crisis* | *mill amid* | `owurelal` / `owurelam` |
| **`aol` / `aom`** | *on* / *over* | *mount* | *take up a policy* | *cover* | `owurelaol` / `owurelaom` |
| **`ol` / `om`** | *at* / *near* | *attend* | *attend to an issue* | *adjoin* | `owurelol` / `owurelom` |
| **`oel` / `oem`** | *toward* / *in the direction of* | *head for* | *head for a habit* | *orient* | `owureloel` / `owureloem` |
| **`ul` / `um`** | *from* / *away from* | *leave* | *leave a heritage behind* | *recede* | `owurelul` / `owurelum` |
| **`ual` / `uam`** | *out of* / *out from among* | *exit* | *get out of debt* | *pick out* | `owurelual` / `owureluam` |
| **`uol` / `uom`** | *through* / *by way of* | *traverse* | *get through a crisis* | *relay* | `owureluol` / `owureluom` |
| **`el` / `em`** | *for* / *used by* | *serve* | *serve a policy* | *have in use* | `owurelel` / `owurelem` |
| **`ael` / `aem`** | *using* / *by* | *wield* | *wield a tradition* | *channel* | `owurelael` / `owurelaem` |
| **`uel` / `uem`** | *against* / *contrary to* | *oppose* | *oppose a policy* | *defy* | `owureluel` / `owureluem` |

An abstract object keeps the hook's picture, as on the [free hook](#abstract-landmarks):

> `zuzozen dobowum vowureluol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-catastrophe | v-traverse
>
> "Uzozen gets through the crisis."

> `zolazun dududal vowurelel.`
>
> z-Olazun | d-tea | v-serve
>
> "Olazun serves tea."

Other roots fuse the same way: `vezuyeluel` *contradict* is *tell* fused with *against*. The dictionary lists these words as citations (`owurelul`, not `vowurelul`).

**Compare with:** `vowurel ul buyahel` still *walks from a station* as two words. Same-role *including* / *rather* / *instead* / *except* never fuse; they are always separate hook words. A [lexical compound](compounds.md#lexical-compounds) like *doorbell* glues two content roots with a join letter; this glues a citation to an extra-noun hook.

### Hook prefixes (hook + `x` + stem) {#hook-prefixes}

English builds describing words from a relation and a thing: *anti-war*, *indoor*, *transoceanic*, *water-powered*. In Agebon, write the role letter, then the vowels of an extra-noun hook, then **`x`**, then a stem, then the ending. The stem is the hook's landmark, and the word names something that stands in that relation to it: `guexavuvul` is *against fighting* as one word. Where the free hook needs its own `/b/` word after the noun, the prefix packs the relation into a single word, so it can describe a noun, fill any noun slot, and become a dictionary word. (cue: **x** ≈ plus, the hyphen in *anti-war*)

> `zuzozen guexavuvul.`
>
> z-Uzozen | g-against-x-fight
>
> "Uzozen is anti-fighting."

> `zehaval gaexowulal.`
>
> z-ship | g-using-x-water
>
> "The ship is water-powered."

Plain **`x`** spells the hook's **-l** reading. An **`m`** right before the **`x`** picks the hook's **-m** frame, the same **`m`** that turns `uel` into `uem`: `guemxewulel` is *contrary to the wind*, headed into it. The stem keeps its own sense choice: **-l** for the concrete sense, **-m** for the abstract one, read the way an [abstract landmark](#abstract-landmarks) reads after a free hook.

> `zuzozen guemxewulel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | g-contrary-to-x-wind
>
> "Uzozen is headed into the wind."

| Hook | Prefix | Concrete stem | Abstract stem |
|------|--------|---------------|---------------|
| `al` *in* | **`…ax…`** | `gaxahehal` *indoor* | `gaxobowum` *mid-crisis* |
| `aol` *on* | **`…aox…`** | `gaoxohalal` *seagoing* | `gaoxonam` *on-policy* |
| `ol` *at* | **`…ox…`** | `goxahehal` *at the house* | `goxabevam` *at issue* |
| `oel` *toward* | **`…oex…`** | `goexahehal` *houseward* | `goexuzebum` *habit-forming* |
| `ul` *from* | **`…ux…`** | `guxahehal` *from the house* | `guxuzobam` *heritage-derived* |
| `ual` *out of* | **`…uax…`** | `guaxowulal` *out of the water* | `guaxaram` *debt-free* |
| `uol` *through* | **`…uox…`** | `guoxohalal` *transoceanic* | `guoxobowum` *crisis-spanning* |
| `el` *for* | **`…ex…`** | `gexahehal` *household* | `gexonam` *pro-policy* |
| `ael` *using* | **`…aex…`** | `gaexowulal` *water-powered* | `gaexugarom` *tradition-based* |
| `uel` *against* | **`…uex…`** | `guexohalal` *sea-facing* (a seawall) | `guexonam` *anti-policy* |

| Hook | Prefix | Concrete stem | Abstract stem |
|------|--------|---------------|---------------|
| `am` *amid* | **`…amx…`** | `gamxohalal` *mid-ocean* | `gamxobowum` *crisis-era* |
| `aom` *over* | **`…aomx…`** | `gaomxohalal` *over the ocean* (a flight) | `gaomxonam` *policy-covered* |
| `om` *near* | **`…omx…`** | `gomxahehal` *near the house* | `gomxabevam` *problem-adjacent* |
| `oem` *in the direction of* | **`…oemx…`** | `goemxahehal` *facing the house* | `goemxuzebum` *habit-leaning* |
| `um` *away from* | **`…umx…`** | `gumxahehal` *away from the house* | `gumxuzebum` *habit-shedding* |
| `uam` *out from among* | **`…uamx…`** | `guamxayayal` *picked from the group* | `guamxayayam` *community-drawn* |
| `uom` *by way of* | **`…uomx…`** | `guomxohel` *bridge-routed* | `guomxewulem` *influence-routed* |
| `em` *used by* | **`…emx…`** | `gemxayayal` *group-owned* | `gemxayayam` *community-held* |
| `aem` *by* | **`…aemx…`** | `gaemxorahel` *road-borne* | `gaemxonam` *policy-channeled* |
| `uem` *contrary to* | **`…uemx…`** | `guemxewulel` *headwind* | `guemxonam` *off-policy* |

A pair differs the way the free hooks do: `goxahehal` *at the house* is there, and `gomxahehal` *near the house* is only close to it.

The role letter is the word's slot, as on any content word:

| Role letter | The word names | Example | English |
|-------------|----------------|---------|---------|
| `/ɡ/` | a property: in that relation to the stem | `gexegobom` | *body-positive* |
| `/z/` `/d/` `/b/` | someone or something in that relation | `zuexavuvul` | *an opponent of fighting* |
| `/h/` | how the clause stands to the stem | `hoexahehal` | *houseward* (of the movement) |

> `zuexavuvul vowurel.`
>
> z-against-x-fight | v-walk
>
> "An opponent of fighting walks."

> `zuzozen vowurel hoexahehal.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | h-toward-x-house
>
> "Uzozen walks houseward."

The stem may be an ordinary compound (`guexahehaxowurel` *against walking in houses*) or a [hook compound](#hook-compounds) citation:

> `zolazun guexowurelul.`
>
> z-Olazun | g-against-x-leave
>
> "Olazun is against leaving."

[Label scope](predication.md#label-scope) goes after the stem, as on any word, and **-r** resumes the whole word:

> `zolazun guexavuvuthal.`
>
> z-Olazun | g-against-x-fight-th-episode
>
> "Olazun is against fighting this time."

> `zuexavuvul vowurel. zuexavuvur vezuvol.`
>
> z-against-x-fight | v-walk . z-←against-x-fight | v-sleep
>
> "An opponent of fighting walks. That opponent sleeps."

A common hook prefix can enter the dictionary as a citation, with no role letter (`uexavuvul`). In speech **`x`** starts the stem's first syllable (*gue-xa-vu-vul*); with **`mx`**, the **`m`** closes the hook's syllable (*guem-xe-wu-lel*).

**Compare with:** the free hook keeps the landmark as its own word: `zumon uel buduvul vowurel.` is *someone against the duel walks*, and a later `buduvur` points back to that duel. The prefix makes one word for a kind: `zuexuduvul vowurel.` is *an opponent of dueling walks*, with no landmark to point back to.

### Hooked joins (hook + `x` + join) {#hooked-joins}

English bounds *everyone*, *someone*, and *nothing* with a preposition: *everyone at the house*, *something for me*, *everyone against me*. A free hook after a join does a different job: it is an extra of the whole clause, so `zual vezuvol ol bahehal.` says where the sleeping happens, not who counts ([universals and domains](joins.md#universals-domains-generics)). To bound the join's own set, write the role letter, then the vowels of an extra-noun hook, then **`x`**, then the join, and put the hook's landmark right after the word as a `bl-` word. The join keeps its meaning and its ending, and the hook now says which members count: the ones that stand in that relation to the landmark. So *who counts* and *where it happens* get different spellings, and one clause can say both. (cue: the hook comes first, as in a [hook prefix](#hook-prefixes))

> `zoxual blahehal vezuvol.`
>
> [z-at-x-everything | bl-house] | v-sleep
>
> "Everyone at the house sleeps."

> `zual vezuvol ol bahehal.`
>
> z-everything | v-sleep | [at | b-house]
>
> "Everyone sleeps at the house."

> `zaxual bluzahal vezuvol ol bahehal.`
>
> [z-in-x-everything | bl-school] | v-sleep | [at | b-house]
>
> "Everyone in the school sleeps at the house."

| Piece | What it says | In `zoxual` |
|-------|--------------|-------------|
| role letter | the slot, as on any join: `/z/`, `/d/`, `/b/`, or `/h/` | `z` |
| hook vowels | an [extra-noun hook](#extra-noun-intermediate), with its **-l** left unwritten | `o` *at* |
| `m` or `r` | the hook's **-m** frame, or its **-r** point back | none |
| **`x`** | the seam, as in a hook prefix | `x` |
| join vowels + ending | any [standalone join](joins.md#standalone-phrase), read as usual | `ual` *everyone* |

The landmark takes `bl-` because it completes the word before it, as after an `/h/` word. Any word that can be a `bl-` word works: a name (`bluzozen`), a content word (`blahehal`), a [special pronoun](pronouns.md#special-pronouns) (`bluwon`), a resume (`blahehar`), or a standalone join. A [SHARED](joins.md#shared-after-the-join) `/ɡ/` comes after the landmark: `zoxual blahehal gagozol` is *every cat at the house*.

This lets a sweeping claim show how far it reaches. *Everyone's against me* names its bound (`zuexual bluwon`), and the join ending says whether exceptions are ruled out: closed `zuexual` is *every one of them*, open `zuexuam` is *as far as I know*. A listener can then check the bound, or ask for the exception.

In speech **`x`** starts the join's syllable (*zo-xu-al*). With `m` or `r`, that letter closes the hook's syllable (*zom-xu-al*, *zor-xu-al*). The [first-syllable stress](phonology.md#rhythm-and-stress) keeps a hooked join apart from a join followed by a hook: `zarxar` (*someone in there*) is one word with one stressed beat, and in `zarx ar` (*some people, in there*) `ar` takes a fresh beat.

#### Which hook {#hooked-joins-hooks}

Every hook in the [extra-noun grid](#extra-noun-intermediate) works, with the reading it has there. The relation holds between each member and the landmark. **-l** (no letter before the seam) is the exact relation, and `m` before the seam is the frame, as on a free hook.

| Hook | **-l** | Example | **-m** | Example |
|------|--------|---------|--------|---------|
| `a` | *in* | `daxar blahehal` *something in the house* | *amid* | `zamxar bluzahal` *someone amid the school* |
| `ao` | *on* | `daoxar bledohol` *something on the table* | *over* | `daomxar bledohol` *something over the table* |
| `o` | *at* | `zoxual blahehal` *everyone at the house* | *near* | `zomxual bluwon` *everyone here* |
| `oe` | *toward* | `zoexarx bluzahal` *some people heading to the school* | *in the direction of* | `zoemxarx bluzahal` *some people headed the school's way* |
| `u` | *from* | `duxar blahehal` *something that came from the house* | *away from* | `zumxual bluwon` *everyone away from me* |
| `ua` | *out of* | `duaxar blahehal` *something out of the house* | *out from among* | `zuamxar blayayal` *someone picked out of the group* |
| `uo` | *through* | `zuoxar blahehal` *someone passing through the house* | *by way of* | `duomxar blohel` *something that came by way of the bridge* |
| `e` | *for* | `dexar bluwon` *something for me* | *used by* | `demxar bluwon` *something I use* |
| `ae` | *using* | `zaexual blanewul` *everyone with a knife* | *by* | `zaemxual bledavel` *everyone going by train* |
| `ue` | *against* | `zuexual bluwon` *everyone against me* | *contrary to* | `zuemxual blonam` *everyone acting contrary to the policy* |

An [abstract landmark](#abstract-landmarks) reads the same way: `zaxual blobowum` *everyone in crisis*, `zuexual blonam` *everyone against the policy*. A person landmark gives [here and there](#deixis): `zomxual bluwon` *everyone here*, `zomxual blawan` *everyone there by you*, `zumxual blayan` *everyone over there*, and `zoxual bluwon` *everyone right where I am*.

**Compare with:** belonging and origin are [of-relations](relations.md#of-relations), not places. *Everyone in the family* (its members) is `zual gobem blavayal`, and *everyone from the town* (where they are from) is `zual gaham blahaham`. `zaxual blavayal` is everyone inside the family's space, and `zuxual blahaham` is everyone who came from the town, since `u` names where a path starts.

#### Endings and items {#hooked-joins-endings}

The join part keeps its readings, so every [join ending](joins.md#standalone-phrase) works, under any hook ending. The hook's ending sits before the seam and the join's at the end of the word, so the two never meet.

| Join ending | Agebon | English | Under `yol` |
|-------------|--------|---------|-------------|
| **-r** | `zoxar blahehal` | *someone at the house* | *who at the house?* |
| **-r**, other series | `zoxor blahehal` / `zoxur blahehal` / `zoxer blahehal` | *anyone at the house* / *someone else at the house* / *whoever ranks highest at the house* | *any? / who else? / which one most?* |
| **-r** + **-x** | `zoxarx blahehal` | *some people at the house* | *who all at the house?* |
| **-l** | `zoxal blahehal` | *nobody at the house* | |
| **-m** | `zoxam blahehal` | *nobody at the house, as far as I know* | |
| **-l** on `ua` | `zoxual blahehal` | *everyone at the house, no exceptions* | |
| **-m** on `ua` | `zoxuam blahehal` | *everyone at the house, as far as I know* | |

The two endings are separate choices: `zomxuam bluwon` is *everyone around here, as far as I know*. A standalone join as the landmark names the widest bound out loud: `zoxual blual` is *everyone, anywhere, no exceptions*, and `zoxuam blual` is *people anywhere, in general*.

> `yol dexar bluwon.`
>
> y-question | [d-for-x-wh | bl-speaker]
>
> "What is there for me?"

With items before it, a closed or open join lists the whole set within the bound, as any join does:

> `zuzozen zoxal blahehal vowurel.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-at-x-and | bl-house] | v-walk
>
> "Of everyone at the house, only Uzozen walks."

On **-r**, the join stands for one of the items, and the hook says which: the one in that relation.

> `yol zudozol zagozol zoxar blahehal.`
>
> y-question | [z-dog | z-cat | z-at-x-wh | bl-house]
>
> "Which of the dog and the cat is at the house?"

A hooked join sits where any join of its role letter sits. A plain `/b/` one is the recipient (`zuzozen vezuyel boxar blahehal.` *Uzozen tells someone at the house.*), and one right after an extra-noun hook is that hook's landmark:

> `zudozol vezuvol al baoxar bledohol.`
>
> z-dog | v-sleep | [in | [b-on-x-something | bl-table]]
>
> "The dog sleeps in something on the table."

#### Point back (hook **-r**) {#hooked-joins-point-back}

To bound a join by a place already named (*everyone there*), write `r` before the seam and no `bl-` word. The hook then points back to the landmark already in play, the way [`or`](#hook-resume) does on its own: `zorxual` is *everyone there* as `or` is *there*. The hooks that point back on their own take it here: `zarxual` *everyone in there*, `zorxual` *everyone there*, `zurxual` *everyone from there*, `zerxual` *everyone it was for*, `zaorxual` *everyone on it*, `zaerxual` *everyone using it*, `zuorxual` *everyone passing through there*.

> `zuzozen vuhohol ol bahehal. zorxual vezuvol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-sit | [at | b-house] . z-there-x-everything | v-sleep
>
> "Uzozen sits at the house. Everyone there sleeps."

The landmark may be a free hook's `/b/`, any hosted `bl-` word, or an earlier hooked join's landmark; the latest one counts. A hooked join with `r` needs a landmark already in play, and every other hooked join needs its `bl-` word.

#### Bounding a time (`/h/`) {#hooked-joins-time}

English *never since …* and *always, until …* bound how often something happens by a stretch of time. On `/h/`, a hooked join's join is a bare [restrictor](restrictors.md), spelled as after the `h` of `hal` *never*, `ham`, `hual` *always*, `huam`, `har` *sometimes*, `hor`, `hur`, or `her`, and a time landmark bounds which times it covers. The restrictor still says how often; the hook says over which stretch.

<!-- Exception to house-cast: *you never listen* is the complaint these forms answer, so the listener is the point. -->
> `zawan vebal huxam blenulul.`
>
> z-listener | v-hear | [h-from-x-never.open | bl-thunderstorm]
>
> "You haven't listened since the thunderstorm, as far as I know."

<!-- cheat-sheet: joins-hooks -->
| Hook | Times it bounds | Agebon | English |
|------|-----------------|--------|---------|
| `a` | within the landmark's span | `haxual blenulul` | *always, during the thunderstorm* |
| `o` | at that moment | `hoxar blenulul` | *at some point at the thunderstorm* |
| `u` | from that point on ([since](#since)) | `huxal blenulul` | *never since the thunderstorm* |
| `oe` | up to that point | `hoexual blenulul` | *always, until the thunderstorm* |
| `om` | around that moment (the frame of `o`) | `homxual blenulul` | *always, around the time of the thunderstorm* |

> `zuzozen vowurel hoexual blenulul.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [h-toward-x-always | bl-thunderstorm]
>
> "Uzozen always walked, until the thunderstorm."

> `zuzozen vezuyol homxual blenulul.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-sing | [h-near-x-always | bl-thunderstorm]
>
> "Uzozen always sings around the time of the thunderstorm."

A loose bound is often the honest one: *you always get like this at the holidays* usually means *around* them (`homxual`), and the listener can check that.

**Compare with:** [`harum`](dependents.md#time-poles) *until* places the event itself; `hoexual` bounds the *always*.

**Point back to a time.** With `r` before the seam, `ar`, `or`, and `ur` point back to the latest **time** landmark: the `bl-` word of a time pole (`herem`, `habam`, `halam`, `harum`, `hevahum`), the landmark of an earlier `/h/` hooked join, or a `blarl` in either. A place landmark does not count.

> `zuzozen vowurel halam blenulul. zawan vebal hurxal.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [h-after | bl-thunderstorm] . z-listener | v-hear | h-since.then-x-never
>
> "Uzozen walked after the thunderstorm. You haven't listened since then."

#### Bounded by a clause {#hooked-joins-clause}

To name the stretch with a sentence (*never, in all the time since we moved in*), put the stand-in **`blarl`** after an `/h/` hooked join, as [`ul barl`](#since) takes one for *since*. The sentence after it is the time that bounds the restrictor. It runs to the period, so the hooked join comes last in the main clause.

> `zawan vebal huxal blarl zayan vavobel al bahehal.`
>
> z-listener | v-hear | [h-from-x-never | bl-that-clause] | z-interlocutors | v-go | [in | b-house]
>
> "You haven't listened since we moved into the house."

**Compare with:** a [hook prefix](#hook-prefixes) also puts a hook before **`x`**, but the stem after the seam is its landmark: `guexavuvul` is *anti-fighting*. In a hooked join the join after the seam is the head, and the landmark is the `bl-` word: `zuexual bluwon` is *everyone against me*. The shape after **`x`** tells them apart: a root, or only vowels and an ending. Use the prefix for a kind of member that can enter the dictionary (`zual goxahehal` *everyone at-home*), and the hooked join for one landmark (`zoxual blahehal` *everyone at that house*), which may be a name, a special pronoun, a resume, a standalone join, or a clause, and may be pointed back to.
