# Discourse hooks {#discourse-hooks}

English *In fact*, *for example*, *here*, *somewhere*, and *anyway* attach this sentence to prior talk, or point at a place already in play. Agebon reuses the same hook spellings, stacked or with **-r**. Everyday *including*, *in*, and *Additionally* are in [hooks](hooks.md).

### Opening a sentence with stacked hooks {#stacked-discourse}

English *In fact, …* or *What's more, …* adds a point that goes further than what came before. At the front of a sentence, with no `/b/` right after it, the two-vowel hook **`ael`** does this job. (cue: **ae** ≈ add, ranked upward)

> `ael zolazun vezuvol.`
>
> in.fact | z-Olazun | v-sleep
>
> "In fact, Olazun sleeps."

Opening with **`aol …`** gives English *For example, …*: this sentence is one case of what you just said. (cue: **ao** ≈ add one sample) With **-m**, `aom …` adds *among others* and `aem …` adds *in fact, and maybe more*. No other two-vowel hook opens a sentence: for *next*, *by the way*, and *on the contrary*, use a [linker](sentence-linkers.md#sentence-linkers).

> `zavayal vowurel. aol zolazun vowurel.`
>
> z-family | v-walk . for.example | z-Olazun | v-walk
>
> "The family walks. For example, Olazun walks."

**Compare with:** `al …` *Additionally* adds a separate point next to the last one; `aol …` gives an instance of it. Inside a clause, *such as* is `am` (*including, and maybe more*).

### Somewhere, nowhere, everywhere {#place-indefinites}

For English *somewhere*, *nowhere*, and *everywhere*, put a [standalone join](joins.md#standalone-phrase) in the `/b/` slot after a place hook. Outside a question, **`bar`** is an unspecified member (*somewhere*), the same way **`zar`** is *someone*. Under **`yol`**, it is the *where?* blank.

| Agebon | English |
|--------|---------|
| **`ol bar`** | *somewhere* (under **`yol`**: *where?*) |
| **`ol bur`** | *somewhere else* |
| **`ol bal`** | *nowhere* |
| **`ol bual`** | *everywhere* |

> `zuzozen vowurel ol bual.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [at | b-everything]
>
> "Uzozen walks everywhere."

> `zudozol vezuvol ol bal.`
>
> z-dog | v-sleep | [at | b-none]
>
> "The dog sleeps nowhere."

### Here and there, this and that {#deixis}

English *here*, *there*, *this*, and *that* point at things by their distance from the speaker. Agebon points from a **person in the conversation** instead: put a place hook before the [speaker, listener, or interlocutors](pronouns.md#special-pronouns) in `/b/`.

Names come first. When you know who is talking or listening, use their name as the landmark (`om buzozen` *near Uzozen*). Use speaker and listener when the conversation role itself matters, or when you have no name to use.

<!-- cheat-sheet: joins-hooks -->
| Agebon | Landmark | English |
|--------|----------|---------|
| `om buwon` | near the speaker | *here* / *this* |
| `om bawan` | near the listener | *there* / *that* (by you) |
| `om bayan` | near both of you | *here* (with us) |
| `um bayan` | away from both of you | *over there* / *yonder* |

Use `ol` in place of `om` for the exact spot (`ol buwon` *right where I am*).

> `zudozol om buwon vuhohol.`
>
> z-dog | [near | b-speaker] | v-sit
>
> "This dog sits here by me."

> `zuzozen danal om bawan vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-book | [near | b-listener] | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sees that book by you."

> `zolazun vezuvol um bayan.`
>
> z-Olazun | v-sleep | [away-from | b-interlocutors]
>
> "Olazun sleeps over there." (away from both you and me)

English *come* and *go* also point from a person, but they hide which one: *I'm coming* moves toward the listener, not the speaker. Agebon names the landmark with the plain motion verb **`vavobel`** (*go*) and a path hook: **`oel`** (*toward*) for *come*, **`ul`** (*from*) for *go away*. `vavobel` says nothing about how someone travels; use `vowurel` (*walk*) or `varuvel` (*run*) only when the manner matters.

> `zuzozen vavobel oel buwon.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-go | [toward | b-speaker]
>
> "Uzozen comes over to me."

> `zolazun vavobel oel bawan.`
>
> z-Olazun | v-go | [toward | b-listener]
>
> "Olazun is coming to you."

> `zuzozen vavobel ul buwon.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-go | [from | b-speaker]
>
> "Uzozen goes away from me."

With an object, `vavobel` moves something else: `zuzozen vavobel duhohol.` *Uzozen moves the chair.* When the mover goes along with the thing, use **`velobal`** (*carry*). The same hooks then give English *bring* and *take*:

> `zuzozen velobal dobogal oel buwon.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-carry | d-box | [toward | b-speaker]
>
> "Uzozen brings me the box."

> `zuzozen velobal dobogal ul buwon.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-carry | d-box | [from | b-speaker]
>
> "Uzozen takes the box away." (away from where I am)

In reported speech, the speaker is still whoever says the whole sentence. After *that* (`darl`, see [dependents](sentence-linkers.md#stand-in)), `om buwon` is near the person talking now. Inside a quoted [cite span](spans.md), the quoted person is the speaker, as in English direct quotes.

**Compare with:** *that dog* for a dog already named is [resume **-r**](pronouns.md#resume-r) (`zudozor`), not a place. *Where?* is `ol bar` ([Where?](fill-ask-roles.md#where)).

### Point back (`or` / `ar` / `ur` / `er`) {#hook-resume}

English *there*, *from there*, and *for it* point back to a place or thing already named. In Agebon, give the extra-noun hook **-r** and leave out the `/b/` word: after the verb, the hook points back to the landmark already in play, the way [resume **-r**](pronouns.md#resume-r) does on a noun. A hook with **-r** has no noun on its right, and no phrase on its left to pair with (it is not a same-role hook).

<!-- cheat-sheet: joins-hooks -->
| Agebon | Points back to | English |
|--------|----------------|---------|
| `ar` | inside the place already named | *in there* / *in it* |
| `or` | the place already named | *there* |
| `ur` | the place already named, as a source | *from there* |
| `er` | the one already named, as a goal | *for that* / *for it* |

> `zuzozen vuhohol ol bahehal. zolazun vuhohol or.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-sit | [at | b-house] . z-Olazun | v-sit | there
>
> "Uzozen sits at the house. Olazun sits there too."

> `zudozol vezuvol al bahehal. zudozor varuvel ur.`
>
> z-dog | v-sleep | [in | b-house] . z-←dog | v-run | from.there
>
> "The dog sleeps in the house. It runs from there."

Stacked extra-noun vowels point back the same way: the vowels keep the extra they named (`aol` *on*, `ael` *using*, `uol` *through*), and **-r** drops the landmark.

<!-- cheat-sheet: joins-hooks -->
| Agebon | Points back to | English |
|--------|----------------|---------|
| `aor` | the face already named | *on it* |
| `aer` | the means already named | *with it* |
| `uor` | the path already named | *through there* |

> `zuzozen vuhohol aol buhohol. zolazun vuhohol aor.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-sit | [on | b-chair] . z-Olazun | v-sit | on.it
>
> "Uzozen sits on a chair. Olazun sits on it too."

> `zuzozen danal ael babewel varayel. zolazun varayel aer.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-book | [using | b-pen] | v-write . z-Olazun | v-write | with.it
>
> "Uzozen writes a book with a pen. Olazun writes with it too."

> `zuzozen vowurel uol bahehal. zolazun vowurel uor.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [through | b-house] . z-Olazun | v-walk | through.there
>
> "Uzozen walks through the house. Olazun walks through there too."

At the front of a sentence, a resume hook points back to an earlier stretch of talk, not just the last sentence.

| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| `or …` | back to the main line after a side topic | *Anyway, …* | **o** ≈ one (the one main thread) |
| `er …` | say an earlier point again | *As I said, …* | **e** ≈ order (the wording again) |
| `ar …` | add to an earlier point, not the last one | *Going back to that, …* | **a** ≈ add |
| `ur …` | drop the last point | *Never mind that, …* | **u** ≈ undo |

> `or zuzozen vowurel.`
>
> anyway | z-Uzozen | v-walk
>
> "Anyway, Uzozen walks."

> `ur zolazun vezuvol.`
>
> never.mind.that | z-Olazun | v-sleep
>
> "Never mind that: Olazun sleeps."

**Compare with:** `ol …` *Instead* replaces the last claim. `or …` *Anyway* leaves the side topic standing and returns to the main one; it leaves the [topic](topic.md#topic) alone too, so after a side topic the full return is `or xuzozer …`. To go back to a **person or thing** rather than a line of talk, return to it as the [topic](topic.md#topic-return) (`xuzozer`), which also makes it the topic again.

### Parallel chains {#parallel-chains}

To say more than one thing about the **same** A (*including B but except C*, or two *including*s), keep adding hooks after B: `A HOOK₁ B HOOK₂ C`. Each hook applies to A, not to the B before it, and each has its own vowel and ending. So C is never tucked under B the way English *including (B except C)* can be.

> `zavayal am zuzozen ul zolazun.`
>
> z-family | including.open | z-Uzozen | except | z-Olazun
>
> "The family, including Uzozen and maybe more, except Olazun."

| Chain | English |
|-------|---------|
| `A am B am C` | A including B and including C, and maybe more |
| `A al B al C` | A including B and including C |
| `A am B ul C` | A including B, and maybe more, except C |
| `A ul B ul C` | A except B and except C |

**Compare with:** a [list join](joins.md) after the hook is finished first, and the whole list becomes B: `zudozol ol zagozol zubezol zam` is *a cat and a bird (and possibly more) instead of a dog*.

### Discourse placements {#discourse-placements}

Beginner put a discourse hook at the front of a plain statement. It can sit in three more places, with the same spellings and vowel meanings:

| Placement | Agebon | Use |
|-----------|--------|-----|
| Before a written turn marker | `HOOK yal BODY` | The written **`yal`** / **`yol`** / …, after any vocatives and interjections |
| Before a default-statement body | `HOOK BODY` | Implied **`yal`** ([speech moves](speech-acts.md#speech-act)) |
| Later clause under `/x/` | `… JOIN HOOK BODY` | Same as the opener |
| Before an `/x/` linker | `HOOK LINKER BODY` | Same as prior talk |

A sentence opening takes only one discourse hook; you cannot chain them the way you chain in-clause hooks. After a written turn marker, a hook inside the body is an in-clause hook.

On a later clause in a [clause `/x/` join](joins.md#clause-joins), the hook goes right after the join, before that clause’s body: `A xam al B` → *A and additionally B*. Before an **`/x/`** linker (*therefore*, *however*, …), it applies to the linker and the rest of the sentence together: `al xahem …` → *Additionally, therefore …*. When one connective is enough, prefer plain `al …` or plain `xahem …`.

> `yuzozen al zolazun vowurel.`
>
> y-Uzozen | additionally | z-Olazun | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen! Additionally, Olazun walks."

The call comes first, then the discourse hook, then the body.

**Compare with:** packaging a list uses prefixed [joins](joins.md#join-series-ending-shared) (`zal` / `zam` / `val` …).
