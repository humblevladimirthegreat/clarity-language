# Speech moves {#speech-moves}

How to start a new speech move with `/y/`: call someone, react, or say whether you are stating, asking, commanding, or forbidding.

## Beginner {#beginner}

### Turn (`/y/`): start a new speech move {#turn-y}

A **turn** is one speech move: a statement, question, command, or other act, including talking only to yourself. A `/y/` word starts a new turn.

The `/y/` words go at the start of the turn, before its **body** (the subject, object, verb, and the rest of the clause). So a `/y/` word after a finished body never belongs to that body: it starts the next turn.

### Call someone (`/y/` + name) {#vocative}

To call someone into the turn, put `/y/` before their name, which ends in **-n**. The call can be the whole turn or come before a clause. A `/y/` word that ends in **-n** is always a call.

> `yolazun.`
>
> y-Olazun
>
> "Olazun!" (calling Olazun)

A `/ɡ/` word right after the call describes the person you call, as it would after any noun. So a full name calls with its family name (`yalen galowen.` *Alen Alowen!*), and `yolazun guzemol.` is *sleepy Olazun!*.

> `yalen galowen.`
>
> [y-Alen | g-Alowen]
>
> "Alen Alowen!" (calling Alen Alowen)

To call someone by what they are (*Waiter!*, *Doctor!*), use the kind as a title: it still ends in **-n**, so `yagayun` calls the guard.

**Compare with:** a [greeting](word-endings.md#greeting) is the speaker’s name as a citation (`SELFn.`). Calling Olazun uses `/y/`; saying Olazun walks uses `/z/` (`zolazun vowurel`).

### Interjections: reactions
<a id="interjections"></a>
<a id="interjections-conventional-calls"></a>

An **interjection** is a short reaction or exclamation that stands on its own. Write the reaction under `/y/` with an ordinary ending: **-l** for the everyday sense, **-m** for the abstract sense, just as on any [first mention](word-endings.md#beginner).

> `yemol.`
>
> y-surprise
>
> "What a surprise!"

> `yemom.`
>
> y-amazement
>
> "Amazing!"

The ending tells a reaction from a call: **-n** calls someone (`yolazun`, `yagayun`), and **-l** / **-m** give the reaction itself. So `yagayul` is *Guard!* shouted as an alarm, and `yagayun` calls the guard over.

A `/ɡ/` word right after a reaction describes it, as one after a call describes the person called.

> `yemol gelazom.`
>
> [y-surprise | g-big]
>
> "What a big surprise!"

**Compare with:** to react and then say that something is big, end the reaction with a period and start a new sentence.

### Speech act: statement, question, command
<a id="speech-act-beginner"></a>

Each turn does one thing: it states, asks, commands, or forbids. That is its **speech act**. An **act word** names it: `y`, a vowel, and **-l**. The statement word is **`yal`**, but a period already marks a statement, so you usually leave **`yal`** out.

When the turn is not a statement, put its act word immediately before the body:

> `yel vowurel.`
>
> y-command | v-walk
>
> "Walk!"

The vowel picks the act. Its cues are **a** add / hold, **o** one / pick, **e** order / instruct, **u** undo / take back.

<!-- cheat-sheet: talking -->
| Agebon | Use | Cue |
|--------|-----|-----|
| **`yal`** | statement (often omitted) | **a** ≈ add (hold the claim) |
| **`yol`** | question: yes/no and fill-in ask | **o** ≈ one (pick from a menu) |
| **`yel`** | command / instruction | **e** ≈ order (do this) |
| **`yul`** | prohibition: *don’t…* | **u** ≈ undo (take the action back) |

### Word order for each act {#act-word-order}

The first letter of each word already says its role, so any order of the body means the same thing. Each act still has a **usual order**. Use it: the act word is one short syllable, and if a listener misses it, the order still tells them which act you meant.

> `zuzozen dobogal vanuvul.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-box | v-punch
>
> "Uzozen punches the box."

> `yol zuzozen vanuvul dobogal.`
>
> y-question | z-Uzozen | v-punch | d-box
>
> "Does Uzozen punch the box?"

> `yel vanuvul dobogal.`
>
> y-command | v-punch | d-box
>
> "Punch the box!"

> `yul dobogal vanuvul.`
>
> y-prohibition | d-box | v-punch
>
> "Don't punch the box."

Only a command starts with its verb. A question or prohibition whose act word gets lost sounds like a statement, never like an order to act.

<!-- cheat-sheet: talking -->
| Act | Usual order | Cue |
|-----|-------------|-----|
| statement | verb late: subject, object, verb | the claim builds up to the action |
| question | verb second: one word, then the verb, then the rest | the verb steps forward to be checked |
| command | verb first | the action comes first: do it |
| prohibition | verb late, as in a statement | the action is held back |

The person who receives (`/b/`) stays right after the verb, wherever the verb goes: `yel varal buzozen.` *Pay Uzozen!* A question with no subject puts another word before its verb, so that it still does not start with the verb.

### Speech act: firm and soft {#speech-act}

You already mark whether a turn is a statement, question, command, or prohibition. The ending of the act word adds **how firmly** you mean it. English softens with extra words (*please*, *perhaps*, *for what it's worth*); Agebon changes only the ending.

The act word is **`y` + vowel + ending**. The vowel picks the act as before (**a** statement, **o** question, **e** command, **u** prohibition). With **-l** you stand behind the act. With **-m** you offer it softly and leave the other person free to take it up or not. **-r** marks an act you hold only for now. Write the act word whenever the turn is not a plain statement; a period still lets you omit **`yal`**.

> `yam zuzozen vowurel.`
>
> y-soft-statement | z-Uzozen | v-walk
>
> "For what it's worth, Uzozen walks."

**Compare with:** [MAY](knowing.md#may) (`thomam`) marks the scene as *could be*. **`yam`** is still a statement; you put it forward without insisting the other person take it up.

<!-- cheat-sheet: talking -->
| Agebon | Use | Cue |
|--------|-----|-----|
| **`yal`** | **statement**: claim or description | **a** add + **-l**: stand behind |
| **`yam`** | **soft statement**: offered, not insisted (*for what it's worth*) | **a** add + **-m**: hold, but leave it open |
| **`yol`** | **question**: yes/no and fill-in ask | **o** one / menu + **-l**: pick an answer |
| **`yom`** | **soft question**: wonder, gentle ask, offer-like | **o** menu + **-m**: ask without pinning |
| **`yel`** | **command**: instruction | **e** order + **-l**: do this |
| **`yem`** | **request**: soft directive (*please…*) | **e** order + **-m**: ask, don’t command |
| **`yul`** | **prohibition**: *don’t…* (firm) | **u** undo + **-l**: take the action back |
| **`yum`** | **soft prohibition**: *please don’t…* / *I’d rather you not…* | **u** undo + **-m**: prefer they don’t |

For *let's*, make a soft request with inclusive *we*, **`aya`** ([special pronouns](pronouns.md#special-pronouns)), as the subject. *Let's not* is the soft prohibition.

> `yem vowurel zayan.`
>
> y-request | v-walk | z-interlocutors
>
> "Let's walk."

> `yum zayan vezuvol.`
>
> y-soft-prohibition | z-interlocutors | v-sleep
>
> "Let's not sleep."

A [hook](hooks.md) (**`al`** / **`am`** / …) may sit among the opening `/y/` words, immediately before the first act word, or before a body that defaults to a statement.

#### For now (**-r**) {#act-r}

**-r** marks an act you hold only **for now**: provisional, and you expect to revisit it.

<!-- cheat-sheet: talking -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| **`yar`** | **provisional statement**: held until something better comes | *as things stand* / *for now, I take it that…* | **a** add + **-r**: a claim held for now |
| **`yor`** | **working question**: asked to proceed, expect to re-ask | *just checking, …?* / *for now, is it…?* | **o** menu + **-r**: an ask for now |
| **`yer`** | **command for now**: do this until told otherwise | *for now, …* / *go ahead and … for now* | **e** order + **-r**: an instruction you expect to revisit |
| **`yur`** | **hold off**: don't, for now | *hold off on …* / *don't … yet* | **u** undo + **-r**: take the action back, for now |

> `yur vowurel.`
>
> y-hold-off | v-walk
>
> "Hold off on walking for now."

> `yar zuzozen vowurel.`
>
> y-provisional-statement | z-Uzozen | v-walk
>
> "As things stand, Uzozen walks."

### Doubling an act word {#doubled-act}

Say an act word twice among the opening `/y/` words to make the act more insistent. The act stays the same; the repeat never cancels it. Use it most where a missed *don't* would be dangerous.

> `yul yul vazuyul.`
>
> y-prohibition | y-prohibition | v-sneak
>
> "Don't sneak. I mean it."

<!-- cheat-sheet: talking -->
| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| **`yal yal`** | insisted statement | *I'm telling you, …* |
| **`yol yol`** | pressing question | *I really need to know: …?* |
| **`yel yel`** | urgent command | *Do it. Now.* |
| **`yul yul`** | emphatic prohibition | *Don't. I mean it.* |

Both words take the same ending: **`yem yem`** is an insistent plea (*please, please…*), and **`yur yur`** is *really, hold off for now*. **`yal`** is usually left out, but **`yal yal`** is always written, because the repeat carries the emphasis.

Repeat only the opening act word: an act word after the body starts a new turn instead of adding emphasis.

**Compare with:** the tone mark `!` makes your voice louder and more stressed. A doubled act word makes the act itself more insistent, so it also fits a calm voice. The two can go together.

### Stacking two act words {#stacked-acts}

People often do one act while sounding like another. *Could you pass the salt?* is a request that sounds like a question. *We don't hit in this house* is a prohibition that sounds like a statement. Two different act words say both: the **first** is what you really do, and the **second** is how it sounds. The listener always knows the real act from the first word, and answers that act.

> `yel yol vanuvul dobogal zawan.`
>
> y-command | y-question | v-punch | d-box | z-listener
>
> "Could you punch the box?" (a request)

The body takes the **first** word's [usual order](#act-word-order). The second word only says how the turn sounds and never moves the verb. So a prohibition that sounds like a command still keeps its verb late, and a missed **`yul`** never leaves an order to act.

> `yul yel dobogal vanuvul.`
>
> y-prohibition | y-command | d-box | v-punch
>
> "Go ahead, punch the box." (sarcastic: don't)

> `yul yal zayan dobogal vanuvul.`
>
> y-prohibition | y-statement | z-interlocutors | d-box | v-punch
>
> "We don't punch the box." (a house rule)

<!-- cheat-sheet: talking -->
| Agebon | Really does → sounds like | English |
|--------|---------------------------|---------|
| **`yel yol`** | command → question | *Could you pass the salt?* |
| **`yel yal`** | command → statement | *You'll be home by ten.* / *Passengers remain seated.* |
| **`yel yul`** | command → prohibition | *Don't you dare finish those cookies* (teasing: do) |
| **`yul yal`** | prohibition → statement | *We don't hit in this house.* |
| **`yul yol`** | prohibition → question | *Must you?* / *Do you really have to shout?* |
| **`yul yel`** | prohibition → command | *Go ahead, try it.* (sarcastic: don't) |
| **`yal yel`** | statement → command | *Note that…* / *Mark my words.* |
| **`yal yul`** | statement → prohibition | *I won't even mention that he lied.* (said anyway) |

Each word's ending describes its own act. The first ending says how firmly you really mean the act; the second says how it sounds. So a stack can show a soft sound over a firm act, which is the pressure that is hardest to name in English:

<!-- cheat-sheet: talking -->
| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| **`yem yol`** | soft request that sounds like a question: they may say no | *Would you mind…?* |
| **`yel yom`** | firm command that sounds like a gentle ask: they may not say no | a boss's *Would you maybe…?* |
| **`yel yem`** | command with a *please* that is not optional | *Please do it.* (not a choice) |
| **`yem yel`** | soft offer that sounds like a command | *Have a seat. Go on, take another.* |
| **`yer yal`** | rule for now that sounds like a description | *For now, everyone stays inside.* |
| **`yum yal`** | soft norm | *We'd rather people didn't…* |
| **`yul yur`** | firm *no* that sounds like *not yet* | *Let's hold off for now.* (meaning never) |

> `yem yel vuhohol.`
>
> y-request | y-command | v-sit
>
> "Sit, sit!" (an offer)

A stack has two act words at most, with nothing between them. A call, a reaction, or a hook goes before the first act word. The first word is always written, even **`yal`**: without it, the second word would be the act.

### Describing a call or reaction {#describe-turn-word}

A call or a reaction can also take an adjective **before** it, with [**`gl-`**](clause.md#left-bound-adjectives), the way a noun does. Its `/w/` and its `/b/` come along as usual.

> `glolamam yolazun.`
>
> [gl-cherished | y-Olazun]
>
> "Dear Olazun!"

> `glugemam yuburel.`
>
> [gl-delight | y-birthday]
>
> "Happy birthday!"

A reaction can also take a [degree word](clause.md#degree-w) right before it, to say how strongly it holds. A call takes none: the person you call is not more or less called.

> `welazom yemom.`
>
> [w-very | y-amazement]
>
> "Truly amazing!"

The degree word grades the reaction itself. A tone mark is how you sound saying it, so the two can go together.

To say who or what you react to (*hooray for Uzozen*), add a [hook](hooks.md#extra-noun) and `/b/` after the reaction: `yohumal el buzozen.` A bare `/b/` there would start the body as the person who receives.

### Tone marks {#tone-marks}

A **tone mark** shows how you feel, or what you stress, while you say something. It changes your voice only. It never changes the speech act (that is the act word's job), and a sentence still ends in a period.

<!-- cheat-sheet: talking -->
| Mark | Feeling | Voice | Cue |
|------|---------|-------|-----|
| `!` | Strong feeling (excited, upset: the words say which) | Louder, stressed | English *!* |
| `?` | Unsure about it | Raised, tentative | English *…, I think* after the doubtful part |
| `%` | Joking, teasing (not meant literally) | Light, smiling | A wink |
| `&` | Contrast: this one, not another | Stressed and slowed | *and* not the other |
| `;` | Warm, affectionate | Soft, gentle | A soft pause |

`?` is the only mark that uses pitch: it raises the whole word it colors, and the word's [vowel pitches](phonology.md#vowel-pitch) keep their order inside it. `!` and `;` are loudness, `&` is loudness and length, and `%` is the quality of your voice.

**Doubling** a mark makes it stronger: `!!` is louder again, `??` is very unsure, `;;` is very tender, `%%` is broad teasing, `&&` is a firm contrast. **Combining** marks mixes tones: `?!` is surprised and doubtful (*Wait, what?!*), `%;` is teasing but fond, `&!` is *this* one, and I feel strongly about it. A stack has no spaces and its order does not matter (`?!` and `!?` sound the same). Each mark may appear at most twice, so `!!!` is not a mark.

Write the mark **before** what it colors, so you see the tone before you say the words:

| Written | Colors |
|---------|--------|
| Attached to a word: `!zuzozen` | that word |
| Attached to a [span](spans.md): `!d[ … ]` | the span |
| Standing alone, then a space: `! zuzozen …` | the rest of the sentence, up to the period |

> `zuzozen vawebol ?dudozol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-see | ?d-dog
>
> "Uzozen saw a dog, I think." (unsure about just the dog)

> `! zuzozen vowurel.`
>
> ! | z-Uzozen | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen walks!"

> `zuzozen vawebol &dudozol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-see | &d-dog
>
> "Uzozen saw a *dog*." (a dog, not something else)

The other marks work the same way:

> `zuzozen !!vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | !!v-walk
>
> "Uzozen *walks*!!"

> `?! zolazun vawebol dudozol.`
>
> ?! | z-Olazun | v-see | d-dog
>
> "Olazun saw a dog?!"

> `%;zolazun vowurel.`
>
> %;z-Olazun | v-walk
>
> "Good old Olazun walks." (teasing, but fond)

> `zuzozen vawebol ??dudozol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-see | ??d-dog
>
> "Uzozen saw a dog, I think, but I'm really not sure." (unsure about just the dog)

> `%% zuzozen vowurel.`
>
> %% | z-Uzozen | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen walks." (broad teasing)

> `zuzozen vawebol &&dudozol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-see | &&d-dog
>
> "Uzozen saw a *dog*." (a dog, and definitely not something else)

> `;;zolazun vawebol dudozol.`
>
> ;;z-Olazun | v-see | d-dog
>
> "Dearest Olazun saw a dog."

> `% zuzozen vowurel.`
>
> % | z-Uzozen | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen walks." (joking)

> `;zolazun vawebol dudozol.`
>
> ;z-Olazun | v-see | d-dog
>
> "Dear Olazun saw a dog."

A mark on a span colors all of it:

> `zuzozen ?d[uzozen] vezuyel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | ?d-CITE[Uzozen] | v-tell
>
> "Uzozen said “Uzozen,” I think." (unsure about just the quoted word)

**Compare with:** **`yol`** asks a question; `?` only sounds unsure. A tone mark shows the feeling.

- A mark may go on any word, including act words.
- A mark is a stack of `!`, `?`, `%`, `&`, `;`, each at most twice, in any order. A third copy (`!!!`) is not a mark, and neither are marks in a row with a space between (`! !`).
- `&` is the only way to single out one word: moving a word adds no stress.
- A mark on a word inside a colored span or sentence overrides it for that word. To mix tones on one word, stack them there.
- Before a span fence, the mark is **your** tone about the quote. Inside the fence, it is part of what is quoted (the original speaker's tone).
- Speech adds no syllables: you voice the mark over its words.

### Speech manner (*frankly* / *to be clear*) {#speech-manner}

English *frankly* and *to be clear* say in words how you are speaking. A tone mark cannot do that, because it changes only your voice. Instead, use a stance word (`/th/`, [clause](clause.md#stance-th)) built on an ordinary root. Like any stance word, it may sit anywhere in the sentence; the start is usual.

<!-- cheat-sheet: talking -->
| Agebon | English | Root |
|--------|---------|------|
| **`thagebom`** | *to be clear* / *to put it plainly* | clarity |
| **`thavewem`** | *frankly* / *honestly* / *to tell the truth* | revelation |

> `thagebom zuzozen vowurel.`
>
> th-clarity | z-Uzozen | v-walk
>
> "To be clear, Uzozen walks."

> `thavewem zolazun vezuvol.`
>
> th-revelation | z-Olazun | v-sleep
>
> "Honestly, Olazun is sleeping."

**Compare with:** the stance word describes your way of saying the claim, not the action. `/h/` on the same root (`hagebom`) would describe how the action itself is done.

### Number as interjection {#number-as-interjection}

A number word in `/y/` is a call-out (*Three more!*, a score). It can sit with the other opening `/y/` words, before the act word, or stand alone as its own turn.
