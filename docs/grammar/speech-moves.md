# Speech moves {#speech-moves}

How to start a new speech move with `/y/`: call someone, react, or say whether you are stating, asking, commanding, or forbidding.

## Beginner {#beginner}

### Turn (`/y/`): start a new speech move {#turn-y}

A **turn** is one speech move: a statement, question, command, or other act, including talking only to yourself. A `/y/` word starts a new turn.

The `/y/` words go at the start of the turn, before its **body** (the subject, object, verb, and the rest of the clause). So a `/y/` word after a finished body never belongs to that body: it starts the next turn.

### Call someone (`/y/` + name) {#vocative}

To call someone into the turn, put `/y/` before their name, which ends in **-n**. The call can be the whole turn or come before a clause. A `/y/` word that ends in **-n** is always a call.

> `yolozun.`
>
> y-Olozun
>
> "Olozun!" (calling Olozun)

A `/ɡ/` word right after the call describes the person you call, as it would after any noun. So a full name calls with its family name (`yohun gulowon.` *Ohun Ulowon!*, [multipart names](word-endings.md#multipart-names)), and `yolozun gozumol.` is *sleepy Olozun!*.

> `yohun gulowon.`
>
> [y-Ohun | g-Ulowon]
>
> "Ohun Ulowon!" (calling Ohun Ulowon)

To call someone by what they are (*Waiter!*, *Doctor!*), use the kind as a title: it still ends in **-n**, so `yayoyun` calls the guard. An occupation title is a [role compound](roles.md#role-compounds) with **-n**.

**Compare with:** a [greeting](word-endings.md#greeting) is the speaker’s name as a citation (`SELFn.`). Calling Olozun uses `/y/`; saying Olozun walks uses `/z/` (`zolozun voworel`).

### Interjections: reactions
<a id="interjections"></a>
<a id="interjections-conventional-calls"></a>

An **interjection** is a short reaction or exclamation that stands on its own. Write the reaction under `/y/` with an ordinary ending: **-l** for the everyday sense, **-m** for the abstract sense, just as on any [first mention](word-endings.md#beginner).

> `yezul.`
>
> y-surprise
>
> "What a surprise!"

> `yezum.`
>
> y-amazement
>
> "Amazing!"

The ending tells a reaction from a call: **-n** calls someone (`yolozun`, `yayoyun`), and **-l** / **-m** give the reaction itself. So `yayoyul` is *Guard!* shouted as an alarm, and `yayoyun` calls the guard over.

A `/ɡ/` word right after a reaction describes it, as one after a call describes the person called.

> `yezul geluzom.`
>
> [y-surprise | g-big]
>
> "What a big surprise!"

**Compare with:** to react and then say that something is big, end the reaction with a period and start a new sentence.

### Speech act: statement, question, command
<a id="speech-act-beginner"></a>

Each turn does one thing: it states, asks, commands, or forbids. That is its **speech act**. An **act word** names it: `y`, a vowel, and **-l**. The statement word is **`yal`**, but a period already marks a statement, so you usually leave **`yal`** out.

When the turn is not a statement, put its act word immediately before the body:

> `yel voworel.`
>
> y-command | v-walk
>
> "Walk!"

The vowel picks the act. Its cues are **a** add / hold, **o** one / pick, **e** order / instruct, **u** undo / take back. The same four vowels, with the same cues, come back in many small word families later on.

<!-- cheat-sheet: talking -->
| Egabon | Use | Cue |
|--------|-----|-----|
| **`yal`** | statement (often omitted) | **a** ≈ add (hold the claim) |
| **`yol`** | question: yes/no and fill-in ask | **o** ≈ one (pick from a menu) |
| **`yel`** | command / instruction | **e** ≈ order (do this) |
| **`yul`** | prohibition: *don’t…* | **u** ≈ undo (take the action back) |

### Word order for each act {#act-word-order}

The first letter of each word already says its role, so any order of the body means the same thing (with joined verbs, a word's place says which verb it goes with: [verb-phrase forms](join-across-roles.md#vp-clause-forms)). Each act still has a **usual order**. Use it: the act word is one short syllable, and if a listener misses it, the order still tells them which act you meant.

> `zuzazon dabagal vabavul.`
>
> z-Uzazon | d-box | v-punch
>
> "Uzazon punches the box."

> `yol zuzazon vabavul dabagal.`
>
> y-question | z-Uzazon | v-punch | d-box
>
> "Does Uzazon punch the box?"

> `yel vabavul dabagal.`
>
> y-command | v-punch | d-box
>
> "Punch the box!"

> `yul dabagal vabavul.`
>
> y-prohibition | d-box | v-punch
>
> "Don't punch the box."

Only a command starts with its verb. A question or prohibition whose act word gets lost sounds like a statement, never like an order to act.

<!-- cheat-sheet: talking -->
| Act | Usual order | Cue |
|-----|-------------|-----|
| statement | verb late: subject, object, verb | the claim builds up to the action |
| question | verb second: one word, then the verb, then the rest | the verb steps forward to be checked |
| command | verb first | the action comes first: do it |
| prohibition | verb late, as in a statement | the action is held back |

The person who receives (`/b/`) stays right after the verb, wherever the verb goes: `yel vamol buzazon.` *Pay Uzazon!* With [joined verbs](join-across-roles.md#vp-clause-forms), the whole join counts as the verb, so a `/b/` right after the join word receives from every verb. A question with no subject puts another word before its verb, so that it still does not start with the verb.

### Speech act: firm and soft {#speech-act}

You already mark whether a turn is a statement, question, command, or prohibition. The ending of the act word adds **how firmly** you mean it. English softens with extra words (*please*, *perhaps*, *for what it's worth*); Egabon changes only the ending.

The act word is **`y` + vowel + ending**. The vowel picks the act as before (**a** statement, **o** question, **e** command, **u** prohibition). With **-l** you stand behind the act. With **-m** you offer it softly and leave the other person free to take it up or not. **-r** marks an act you hold only [for now](#act-r). Write the act word whenever the turn is not a plain statement; a period still lets you omit **`yal`**. A statement word you do write also ends a [carried channel](knowing.md#carry-forward): the claim after it is in your own voice again.

> `yam zuzazon voworel.`
>
> y-soft-statement | z-Uzazon | v-walk
>
> "For what it's worth, Uzazon walks."

**Compare with:** [MAY](knowing.md#may) (`thovum`) marks the scene as *could be*. **`yam`** is still a statement; you put it forward without insisting the other person take it up.

<!-- cheat-sheet: talking -->
| Egabon | Use | Cue |
|--------|-----|-----|
| **`yal`** | **statement**: claim or description | **a** add + **-l**: stand behind |
| **`yam`** | **soft statement**: offered, not insisted (*for what it's worth*) | **a** add + **-m**: hold, but leave it open |
| **`yol`** | **question**: yes/no and fill-in ask | **o** one / menu + **-l**: pick an answer |
| **`yom`** | **soft question**: wonder, gentle ask, offer-like | **o** menu + **-m**: ask without pinning |
| **`yel`** | **command**: instruction | **e** order + **-l**: do this |
| **`yem`** | **request**: soft directive (*please…*) | **e** order + **-m**: ask, don’t command |
| **`yul`** | **prohibition**: *don’t…* (firm) | **u** undo + **-l**: take the action back |
| **`yum`** | **soft prohibition**: *please don’t…* / *I’d rather you not…* | **u** undo + **-m**: prefer they don’t |

For *let's*, make a soft request with inclusive *we*, **`aha`** ([special pronouns](pronouns.md#special-pronouns)), as the subject. *Let's not* is the soft prohibition.

> `yem voworel zahan.`
>
> y-request | v-walk | z-interlocutors
>
> "Let's walk."

> `yum zahan vuzevol.`
>
> y-soft-prohibition | z-interlocutors | v-sleep
>
> "Let's not sleep."

A [hook](hooks.md) (**`al`** / **`am`** / …) may sit among the opening `/y/` words, immediately before the act word, or before a body that defaults to a statement.

#### For now (**-r**) {#act-r}

**-r** marks an act you hold only **for now**: provisional, and you expect to revisit it.

<!-- cheat-sheet: talking -->
| Egabon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| **`yar`** | **provisional statement**: held until something better comes | *as things stand* / *for now, I take it that…* | **a** add + **-r**: a claim held for now |
| **`yor`** | **working question**: asked to proceed, expect to re-ask | *just checking, …?* / *for now, is it…?* | **o** menu + **-r**: an ask for now |
| **`yer`** | **command for now**: do this until told otherwise | *for now, …* / *go ahead and … for now* | **e** order + **-r**: an instruction you expect to revisit |
| **`yur`** | **hold off**: don't, for now | *hold off on …* / *don't … yet* | **u** undo + **-r**: take the action back, for now |

> `yur voworel.`
>
> y-hold-off | v-walk
>
> "Hold off on walking for now."

> `yar zuzazon voworel.`
>
> y-provisional-statement | z-Uzazon | v-walk
>
> "As things stand, Uzazon walks."

### Emphatic prohibition {#emphatic-prohibition}

When a missed *don't* would be dangerous, say **`yul`** twice among the opening `/y/` words. The repeat makes the prohibition louder; it never cancels it. Repeat only the opening act word: a `yul` after the body starts a new turn instead of adding emphasis.

> `yul yul vezeyul.`
>
> y-prohibition | y-prohibition | v-sneak
>
> "Don't sneak. I mean it."

### Describing a call or reaction {#describe-turn-word}

A call or a reaction can also take an adjective **before** it, with [**`gl-`**](clause.md#left-bound-adjectives), the way a noun does. Its `/w/` and its `/b/` come along as usual.

> `glelamam yolozun.`
>
> [gl-cherished | y-Olozun]
>
> "Dear Olozun!"

> `glogamam yoberol.`
>
> [gl-delight | y-birthday]
>
> "Happy birthday!"

A reaction can also take a [degree word](clause.md#degree-w) right before it, to say how strongly it holds. A call takes none: the person you call is not more or less called.

> `weluzom yezum.`
>
> [w-very | y-amazement]
>
> "Truly amazing!"

The degree word grades the reaction itself. A [tone mark](#tone-marks) is how you sound saying it, so the two can go together.

To say who or what you react to (*hooray for Uzazon*), add a [hook](hooks.md#extra-noun) and `/b/` after the reaction: `yohomal el buzazon.` A bare `/b/` there would start the body as the person who receives.

### Tone marks {#tone-marks}

A **tone mark** shows how you feel, or what you stress, while you say something. It changes your voice only. It never changes the speech act (that is the act word's job), and a sentence still ends in a period.

<!-- cheat-sheet: talking -->
| Mark | Feeling | Voice | Cue |
|------|---------|-------|-----|
| `!` | Strong feeling (excited, upset: the words say which) | Louder, stressed | English *!* |
| `?` | Unsure about it | Rising, tentative | English *…, I think* after the doubtful part |
| `%` | Joking, teasing (not meant literally) | Light, smiling | A wink |
| `&` | Contrast: this one, not another | Stressed and slowed | *and* not the other |
| `;` | Warm, affectionate | Soft, gentle | A soft pause |

**Doubling** a mark makes it stronger: `!!` is louder again, `??` is very unsure, `;;` is very tender, `%%` is broad teasing, `&&` is a firm contrast. **Combining** marks mixes tones: `?!` is surprised and doubtful (*Wait, what?!*), `%;` is teasing but fond, `&!` is *this* one, and I feel strongly about it. A stack has no spaces and its order does not matter (`?!` and `!?` sound the same). Each mark may appear at most twice, so `!!!` is not a mark.

Write the mark **before** what it colors, so you see the tone before you say the words:

| Written | Colors |
|---------|--------|
| Attached to a word: `!zuzazon` | that word |
| Attached to a [span](spans.md): `!d[ … ]` | the span |
| Standing alone, then a space: `! zuzazon …` | the rest of the sentence, up to the period |

> `zuzazon vanobol ?dodozol.`
>
> z-Uzazon | v-see | ?d-dog
>
> "Uzazon saw a dog, I think." (unsure about just the dog)

> `! zuzazon voworel.`
>
> ! | z-Uzazon | v-walk
>
> "Uzazon walks!"

> `zuzazon vanobol &dodozol.`
>
> z-Uzazon | v-see | &d-dog
>
> "Uzazon saw a *dog*." (a dog, not something else)

The other marks work the same way:

> `zuzazon !!voworel.`
>
> z-Uzazon | !!v-walk
>
> "Uzazon *walks*!!"

> `?! zolozun vanobol dodozol.`
>
> ?! | z-Olozun | v-see | d-dog
>
> "Olozun saw a dog?!"

> `%;zolozun voworel.`
>
> %;z-Olozun | v-walk
>
> "Good old Olozun walks." (teasing, but fond)

> `zuzazon vanobol ??dodozol.`
>
> z-Uzazon | v-see | ??d-dog
>
> "Uzazon saw a dog, I think, but I'm really not sure." (unsure about just the dog)

> `%% zuzazon voworel.`
>
> %% | z-Uzazon | v-walk
>
> "Uzazon walks." (broad teasing)

> `zuzazon vanobol &&dodozol.`
>
> z-Uzazon | v-see | &&d-dog
>
> "Uzazon saw a *dog*." (a dog, and definitely not something else)

> `;;zolozun vanobol dodozol.`
>
> ;;z-Olozun | v-see | d-dog
>
> "Dearest Olozun saw a dog."

> `% zuzazon voworel.`
>
> % | z-Uzazon | v-walk
>
> "Uzazon walks." (joking)

> `;zolozun vanobol dodozol.`
>
> ;z-Olozun | v-see | d-dog
>
> "Dear Olozun saw a dog."

A mark on a span colors all of it:

> `zuzazon ?d[uzazon] vezeyel.`
>
> z-Uzazon | ?d-CITE[Uzazon] | v-tell
>
> "Uzazon said “Uzazon,” I think." (unsure about just the quoted word)

**Compare with:** **`yol`** asks a question; `?` only sounds unsure. A tone mark shows the feeling; [emotion compose](sakes.md#emotion-compose) states it as a claim, and the two can share a sentence.

- A mark may go on any word, including act words and [sentence linkers](dependents.md#sentence-linkers). Attached to a [scope island](spans.md#scope-islands) (a chunk of words in braces `{ … }`), it colors the whole island.
- A mark is a stack of `!`, `?`, `%`, `&`, `;`, each at most twice, in any order. A third copy (`!!!`) is not a mark, and neither are marks in a row with a space between (`! !`).
- `&` is the only way to single out one word: moving a word adds no stress ([English emphasis](clause.md#english-emphasis)).
- A mark on a word inside a colored span or sentence overrides it for that word. To mix tones on one word, stack them there.
- Before a span fence, the mark is **your** tone about the quote. Inside the fence, it is part of what is quoted (the original speaker's tone).
- Speech adds no syllables: you voice the mark over its words.

### Speech manner (*frankly* / *to be clear*) {#speech-manner}

English *frankly* and *to be clear* say in words how you are speaking. A tone mark cannot do that, because it changes only your voice. Instead, use a stance word (`/th/`, [clause](clause.md#stance-th)) built on an ordinary root. Like any stance word, it may sit anywhere in the sentence; the start is usual.

<!-- cheat-sheet: talking -->
| Egabon | English | Root |
|--------|---------|------|
| **`thegabom`** | *to be clear* / *to put it plainly* | clarity |
| **`thavewom`** | *frankly* / *honestly* / *to tell the truth* | revelation |

> `thegabom zuzazon voworel.`
>
> th-clarity | z-Uzazon | v-walk
>
> "To be clear, Uzazon walks."

> `thavewom zolozun vuzevol.`
>
> th-revelation | z-Olozun | v-sleep
>
> "Honestly, Olozun is sleeping."

**Compare with:** the stance word describes your way of saying the claim, not the action. `/h/` on the same root (`hegabom`) would describe how the action itself is done.
