# Numbers {#numbers}

Use a number word when English would say **how many** of something (*three cats*) or **which place** something has in a series (*the second book*). Write that whole count or rank as **one word**.

## Beginner {#beginner}

### Counts {#counts}

To say **how many** of a thing there are, put a number word after the noun, in the same place as other adjectives (`/ɡ/`). Build the number word one syllable at a time:

1. the role letter **`g`**;
2. the count marker **`ra`**;
3. one syllable for each digit;
4. the ending **-l**.

Digits are spoken and written as these syllables:

| Digit | 1 | 2 | 3 | 4 | 5 | 6 | 7 | 8 | 9 | 0 |
|-------|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Syllable | ba | do | ge | hu | za | vo | re | lu | na | mo |

Two patterns build the table:

- **Consonant:** 1–3 are the stops **b d g**, each further back in the mouth; 4–6 the breathy sounds **h z v**, each further forward; and 7–0 the four [word endings](word-endings.md) **r l n m**.
- **Vowel:** the digits climb the [vowel pitches](phonology.md#vowel-pitch) **a o e u**, low to high, like the notes of a scale. Digit 1 is the lowest, 4 the highest, and 5 starts the climb again. 0 counts as the tenth digit, so it carries on the climb.

Three cues help with the endings: a **7**'s stroke runs back to the left, and **-r** is the ending that points back; an **8** is a loop (*l* for *loop*); for **0** the lips close and nothing comes out (*mmm*).

Neighbouring letters in a group, the ones easiest to mishear for each other, always have different vowels. If you mishear one consonant, the syllable you get is not a digit, so you notice.

So *three* is **g** + **ra** + **ge** + **l**: **`gragel`**.

> `zagozolx gragel.`
> z-cat-x | g-three
>
> "There are three cats."

> `zuzozen danalx gragel vawebol.`
> z-Uzozen | [d-book-x | g-three] | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sees three books."

A number with more digits is still **one word**: one syllable per digit, left to right. *Twenty* is **g** + **ra** + **do** + **mo** + **l**: **`gradomol`**.

### Ordinals {#ordinals}

To say **which place** a thing has in a series (*the first*, *the second*, …), swap the count marker **`ra`** for the rank marker **`re`**. **`gredol`** is *the second*.

> `zanal gredol.`
> z-book | g-2nd
>
> "There is a second book."

> `zolazun danal gregel varayel.`
> z-Olazun | [d-book | g-3rd] | v-write
>
> "Olazun writes the third book."

| Marker | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|--------|---------|-----|
| **`ra`** | count | *how many* | **`a`** ≈ add |
| **`re`** | rank | *which place* | **`e`** ≈ order |

The **`r`** right after the role letter tells you the word is a number: no other word puts a consonant there.

### More than one (`gral`) {#more-than-one}

You can say the count is **more than one** without naming how many. Keep the count marker and leave the digits off: **`gral`**.

This is a different tool from [**-x**](plurality.md) on the noun: **-x** says you mean a group (*cats* as a set), and **`gral`** says how much. Both may sit on the same noun (`zagozolx gral`).

> `zagozolx gral.`
> z-cat-x | g-more-than-one
>
> "There is more than one cat."

**Compare with:** English *some cats* (a set in this situation, no count) uses the noun plus [**-x**](plurality.md). Use **`gral`** when you mean the amount is more than one.

## Intermediate {#intermediate}

### Word shape

Beginner already built one word: role letter, marker, digit syllables, ending. The same four slots hold every **free number** you meet next (a number word on its own, not built onto a content root).

```text
[PoS] + r + V + ( group ( th + V₁ + group )* )? + [ending]
group = [exponent?] [mantissa digits?]
```

1. **PoS:** the role letter (part of speech), as on any other word.
2. **Number marker:** consonant **r** plus a vowel **V**. **V** is a single vowel at this stage. The cluster of role letter plus `r` is a [number-only letter-cluster exception](phonology.md#phonotactics). A marker can sound like a digit (rank **`re`**, digit 7 **`re`**), but the marker always comes straight after the role letter, so `grerel` is *the seventh*.
3. **Zero or more digit groups:** each group is an optional exponent (the *order of magnitude* piece) and optional mantissa digits (the ordinary digits). A group always has at least one of those two pieces. Digits sit next to each other with no **x** between them. A sign, when there is one, applies once to the whole word. With **no groups**, the word is digitless: it says what kind of number it is, but not its value.
4. **Group separator:** each group after the first opens with **`th`** plus **V₁**, the first vowel of the marker.
5. **Ending:** `-l` / `-m` / `-n` / `-r`.

Speech always uses the fully spelled form. Writing also has a shorthand for numbers with two or more digits (`g+12`); zero, one digit, or none stays spelled (`gragel`, `gral`).

**Related form:** naming a group of people or things uses [**-x**](plurality.md) on an ordinary noun.

### Parts of speech on numbers {#parts-of-speech-on-numbers}

Beginner put the number after a noun (*three cats*). You can also put the **number word on its own** in other slots: as the subject, as the object, or as the extra noun under a host.

Change only the first letter. The marker and digits stay the same, and on these four role letters the number still means a plain amount, rank, or code.

> `zragel vezuvol.`
> z-three | v-sleep
>
> "Three are sleeping."

| Prefix | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| `/ɡ/` | modifier after the noun | *three cats*, *the second page*, *room 12* |
| `/z/` | subject | the value, code, or rank as subject |
| `/d/` | direct object | a code being dialed or a value being stated |
| `/b/` | extra noun | *of size 12*; host *on* + a label |

### Marker vowel (referential identity) {#marker-vowel-referential-identity}

Beginner already used **`ra`** (*how many*) and **`re`** (*which place from the start*). The marker vowel **V** completes that set: it says whether the word is a count, a rank, or a string of digits, and on a count it also gives the sign of the whole number. The table shows each marker's shorthand symbol in parentheses. Forward ordinal **`e`** matches [rank join **e**](joins.md#rank-joins).

<!-- cheat-sheet: numbers -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|---|---------|---------|-----|
| **a** (`+`) | positive **scalar** (count or measure amount) | `g+3` *three cats*; `g+` *plural / more than one*; `z+3` *three* (subj); `b+12` *of size 12* | **`a`** ≈ add |
| **u** (`-`) | negative **scalar** | `d-3` *−3* (obj); `z-` *some negative amount*; `g-2` signed measure on a noun | **`u`** ≈ undo |
| **e** (`#`) | **ordinal** / rank **from the start** | `g#2` *the second page*; `g#` *some rank*; `z#2` *second* (rank as subject) | **`e`** ≈ order |
| **o** (`_`) | **digit-string** / label (phones, IDs, “read the digits”) | `d_5551234`; `d_` *some code*; `g_12` *room 12*-style; `b_…` under a host relation | **`o`** ≈ one (one string) |

Each number word is one kind of number, set by its marker. In preferred writing, the marker is **`+`** / **`-`** / **`#`** / **`_`**.

#### Sign {#sign}

| Identity | How sign works |
|----------|----------------|
| **Scalar** (`a` / `u`, written `+` / `-`) | Sign **is** **V**. Multi-group scalars share one sign for the whole word (e.g. −1 000 265 004 is one **`ru`** word). |
| **Forward ordinal** (`e`, written `#`) | Direction *from the start* |
| **Digit-string** (`o`, written `_`) | Labels are unsigned. |

### Number endings {#number-endings}

Beginner already used exact **-l**. The other endings mark *about*, a conventional title, or a repeat of a number you already gave. Speech keeps the ending letter. Shorthand writing shows the ending as a mark right after the role letter (the same marks as on [spans](spans.md)).

<!-- cheat-sheet: numbers -->
| Written | Spoken | Use | Cue |
|---------|--------|-----|-----|
| *(none)*: `g+3`, `g+` | **-l** | exact, newly stated (default) | **-l** stands behind the amount |
| **`~`** after PoS: `g~+3`, `g~+` | **-m** | approximate / non-literal (“about N”) | **-m** leaves the hold open |
| **`@`** after PoS: `g@#2` | **-n** | conventional designation (titles, official labels, *the Second…*) | **-n** names the number |
| **`=`** after PoS: `g=+3`, `g=#2`, `d=_12` | **-r** | resume of a previously stated number, code, or rank (at least one digit) | **-r** points back |

A resume needs **at least one digit**: `grager` = *that three again*; `gredor` = *that second one again*. Use the same marker as the number you repeat. Digitless **-r** (`grar`) is not a resume: like a standalone join **-r**, it is *some number* in a statement and *which number?* in a question. **`=`** is never combined with **`~`** or **`@`**.

### Digitless {#digitless}
<a id="digitless-numbers"></a>
<a id="bare-marker"></a>

Beginner already used **`gral`** for *more than one*. Any marker may drop every digit group: **role letter + marker + ending** only. The marker still says what kind of number this is. With **-l** / **-m** / **-n**, the amount, rank, or label is **unspecified**. With **-r**, the word is a blank, like a standalone join **-r**: *some number* in a statement, *which number?* in a [fill-ask](questions.md#fill-ask-r).

<!-- cheat-sheet: numbers -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|---------|---------|-----|
| **`ra`** (`…+`) | unspecified **positive scalar**, specialized to **plural count / amount `>1`** | complements `…+0` (zero), `…+1` (one), `…+N` (exact N≥2) | **`a`** ≈ add |
| **`ru`** (`…-`) | unspecified **negative scalar** | *some negative amount*; exact negatives keep digits (`…-N`) | **`u`** ≈ undo |
| **`re`** (`…#`) | unspecified **rank from the start** | *some nth* / *some place*; exact ranks keep digits (`…#N`) | **`e`** ≈ order |
| **`ro`** (`…_`) | unspecified **digit-string / label** | *some code*; *a label*; exact labels keep digits (`…_…`) | **`o`** ≈ one |

**Compare with:** noun [**-x**](plurality.md) names a **group of things**. Digitless **`ra`** names a **count or amount above one** without saying how many. Both may sit on the same noun (`zagozolx gral`).

On a [hosted](clause.md#extra-nouns) offset right after a channel, digitless **`blral`** is *later* and **`blrul`** is *earlier*, with no amount claimed. A hosted number keeps its `bl` in writing too (`bl+` for `blral`), and its `l` is a [beat of its own](phonology.md#syllabic-l).

<a id="a-few"></a>

Open **`gram`** is a soft count: *a few* / *some*. It says there is more than one without claiming a number. Closed **`gral`** still means *more than one*.

> `zagozolx gram vuhohol.`
> [z-cat-x | g-more-than-one.about] | v-sit
>
> "A few cats sit."

<a id="mass-some"></a>

A mass noun such as *tea* is not counted, so *more than one* makes no sense for it. On a mass noun, **`gral`** is *some*: an amount that is not zero.

> `zududal gral vuhohol.`
> [z-tea | g-more-than-one] | v-sit
>
> "Some tea sits there."

*Many*, *few*, *enough*, and *too much* compare the amount against a baseline, so they are comparatives, not number words.

Words such as **`zar`** / **`zor`** stand for an unnamed **member of a set**. Digitless number **-r** stands for an unnamed **value** the same way: *some amount*, with no claim that it is more than one. Digitless number **-l** (and **-m** / **-n**) brings in an unnamed amount of that kind, and **`gral`** still says *more than one*.

> `zuzozen drar vawebol.`
> z-Uzozen | d-some-amount | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sees some number of them."

In a question, the same word is the blank: *how many?*.

> `yol drar zuzozen vawebol.`
> y-question | d-how-many | z-Uzozen | v-see
>
> "How many does Uzozen see?"

To repeat a number already given, keep at least one digit: `zolazun dragel vawebol. zuzozen drager vawebol.` *Olazun sees three. Uzozen sees those three too.*

#### How many? {#how-many}

To ask for a **number**, write a [number word](numbers.md) with no digits and the **-r** ending (written **`=`** after the role letter). That empty number is the blank, the same way `zar` is the blank for *who?* ([fill-ask](questions.md#fill-ask-r)). The marker says which kind of number you want. (cue: **-r** names this member; the marker vowel says count, place, or code)

> `yol dagozolx grar zuzozen vawebol.`
> y-question | [d-cat-x | g-how-many] | z-Uzozen | v-see
>
> "How many cats does Uzozen see?"

<!-- cheat-sheet: numbers -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| `g=+` (`grar`) | fill a count or amount | *How many?* / *How much?* | **a** ≈ add |
| `g=#` (`grer`) | fill a place in order | *Which place?* (*which floor, which chapter*) | **e** ≈ order |
| `g=_` (`gror`) | fill a code or label | *What number?* (*phone, room*) | **o** ≈ one |

> `yol ol brer zolazun vuhohol.`
> y-question | [at | b-which-place] | z-Olazun | v-sit
>
> "Which place does Olazun sit in?" (first, second, and so on)

Answer with the number as a [citation](word-endings.md#citation-forms) or a full sentence.

**Compare with:** outside a question, the same word is *some number* (`zuzozen drar vawebol.` *Uzozen sees some number of them*). To repeat a number already given, keep a digit: `drager` ([above](#digitless)).

#### How big? How fast? {#how-big}

To ask **how much** of a quality, put the same blank on `/w/` immediately before the adjective or adverb: **`wrar`**.

> `yol wrar gelazom zudozol.`
> y-question | [w-how-many | g-big] | z-dog
>
> "How big is the dog?"

> `yol wrar hedurem zudozol varuvel.`
> y-question | [w-how-many | h-haste] | z-dog | v-run
>
> "How fast does the dog run?"

Other role letters take the same blank: the marker with no digits and **-r** (on `/h/`, *how many times?*). Do not confuse that blank with **`har`** (*sometimes*), which is a [restrictor](restrictors.md), not a number.

### Exponents

To write a large or tiny amount in one word (*27 million*, *5.2e-4*, *25%*), put an **exponent** (the power of ten) in the digit group, before the ordinary digits (the **mantissa**).

Speech uses **`wa`** / **`wu`** for that exponent, then **`ya`** if mantissa digits follow. You never need a separate *times ten to the …* phrase: the size is part of the number word.

> `g+27e12`
>
> 🔊 `grawabadoyadorel`
>
> g-27e12
>
> "27 × 10¹²."

| Marker | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| **wa** | positive exponent, then its digits | *e12* | **`a`** ≈ add |
| **wu** | negative exponent, then its digits | *e-6* | **`u`** ≈ undo |
| **ya** | after exponent digits when a mantissa follows | (not a digit) | |
| **ye** | decimal point | *5.2* | |
| **yo** | percent closer | *25%* | |
| **yu** | percentage-point closer | *+2 pp* | |

- The exponent comes **before** that group’s mantissa digits.
- If a mantissa follows, say **`ya`** after the last exponent digit, then the mantissa. Example: `27e12` → `wa` + `ba` + `do` + `ya` + `do` + `re`.
- Exponent only, no mantissa: say `wa` / `wu` and the exponent digits, with no **`ya`**. Example: *e9* → `wa` + `na`.
- Ordinary amounts usually omit exponent **`0`**: write `gragel`.
- By default, write the **order of magnitude** (the power of ten) in **engineering notation**: exponents are usually multiples of **3** (e.g. `27e6`).
- **Decimal point:** say **`ye`** after the digit(s) left of the point; digits after **`ye`** are the fractional part.

> `g+5.2`
>
> 🔊 `grazayedol`
>
> g-5.2
>
> "5.2."

### Bare OoM {#bare-oom}
<a id="bare-oom-bands"></a>

English *hundreds* or *thousands* names a range of sizes without a count. To say that, write a **bare order of magnitude** (OoM): exponent digits with **no mantissa**. The word then covers every amount from 10ᴷ up to, but not including, 10ᴷ⁺¹, so `g+e3` is 1000–9999, not every larger amount.

<!-- cheat-sheet: numbers -->
| Form | Band | Gloss |
|------|------|--------|
| **`g+e0`** | `[10⁰, 10¹)` = **1–9** | *ones* (some amount in the ones place) |
| **`g+e1`** | `[10¹, 10²)` = **10–99** | *tens* |
| **`g+e2`** | `[10², 10³)` = **100–999** | *hundreds* |
| **`g+e3`** | `[10³, 10⁴)` = **1000–9999** | *thousands* (`e4` / `e5` are tens/hundreds of thousands) |
| **`g+e6`** | `[10⁶, 10⁷)` | *millions* band only |
| **`g-e3`** | `(−10⁴, −10³]` polarity with **`ru`** | negative thousands band |

These work the same way on `/z/`, `/d/`, and `/b/`. Soft **`grawagem`** ≈ *about in the thousands band*. Exact count inside a band uses a mantissa (`g+4e3` = 4000), not bare OoM.

### Group separator {#group-separator}

When a number has more than one digit group, you say each comma. The spoken comma is **`th`** plus the first vowel of the marker. Written text keeps the comma.

> `g+5e3,860`
>
> 🔊 `grawageyazathaluvomol`
>
> g-5e3,860
>
> "5860."

Syllable by syllable: `g` + `ra` + wa ge ya za + **tha** + lu vo mo + `l`.

<!-- cheat-sheet: numbers -->
| Marker | Separator | Example | Cue |
|--------|-----------|---------|-----|
| **`ra`** (`+`) | **`tha`** | `g+5e3,860` | **a** ≈ add (still counting up) |
| **`ru`** (`-`) | **`thu`** | `g-1e9,265e3,4` | **u** ≈ undo (the sign is heard again at every break) |
| **`re`** (`#`) | **`the`** | `g#2,3` (*2nd within the 3rd*) | **e** ≈ order |
| **`ro`** (`_`) | **`tho`** | `d_555,123,4567` | **o** ≈ one (one label) |

Say a separator exactly where a comma is written, and nowhere else: `g+860` has none; `g+5e3,860` has one. The separator must match the marker, so a listener who missed the start of a long negative number still hears **`thu`** at each break.

<a id="nested-rank"></a>

**Nested rank.** On an ordinal, each comma opens a place *within* the place before it, innermost first, as in dates: `g#2,3` is *seat 2 in row 3*; `g#16,4` is *verse 16 of chapter 4*. A large ordinal is one group of plain digits: `g#1005` (*the 1005th*).

> `zozohol g#2,3.`
>
> 🔊 `zozohol gredothegel.`
>
> z-seat | g-#-2,3
>
> "There is seat 2 in row 3."

### Writing (preferred shorthand) {#writing-preferred-shorthand}

Beginner spelled every number out (`gragel`, `gradomol`). Writing also has a **shorthand** for the ending, marker, and body: `g+3` for `gragel`, `g+20` for `gradomol`. Both writings name the same word. Running text uses shorthand once a number has two or more digits; zero, one digit, or none stays spelled. Tables use shorthand for every row, so patterns line up.

```text
[PoS] + [~|@|=]? + [marker] + [body?]
```

#### Ending marks (second slot, after PoS)

These are the same marks, with the same meanings, as on [spans](spans.md#writing): **`~`** soft, **`@`** proper. A resume uses **`=`**.

<!-- cheat-sheet: numbers -->
| Mark | Speech ending | Job | Examples |
|------|---------------|-----|----------|
| *(none)* | **-l** | exact (default) | `g+3`, `g+`, `g#2`, `d_555,123,4567` |
| **`~`** | **-m** | approximate / soft | `g~+3`, `g~+`, `g~+27e6`, `g~#2` |
| **`@`** | **-n** | conventional | `g@#2`, `g@_12` |
| **`=`** | **-r** | resume (digit) / blank | `g=+3`, `g=#2`, `g=+` |

A hedged conventional number (both **`@`** and **`~`**) is written with **`@`** only, as on spans. **`=`** is never combined with another mark. A spelled word shows its ending as the last letter (`gragel`, `gram`) instead of a mark.

#### Marker (not written as `r`+V)

| Symbol | Speech | Meaning |
|--------|--------|---------|
| **`+`** | `ra` | Positive scalar |
| **`-`** | `ru` | Negative scalar |
| **`#`** | `re` | Ordinal (from the start) |
| **`_`** | `ro` | Digit-string |

Put the marker symbol **right after** the ending mark (or right after the role letter when there is no mark), **before the digits** (or last, when there are no digits): `g+3`, `g~+3`, `g=+`, `d_555,123,4567`, `d=_`, `g@#2`. A **`_`** label never takes a sign ([Sign](#sign)).

#### Body

| Speech | Preferred writing |
|--------|-------------------|
| *(no digit groups)* | *(empty after marker)* `g+`, `g~-`, `g=#`, `d_` |
| Digit syllables (`ba`…`mo`) | Arabic **`0`–`9`** |
| `wa` / `wu` (+ `ya` when a mantissa follows) | **`e`** / **`e-`** |
| named exp **`0`** | **`e0`**: ones band `g+e0`; assert `g+3e0` / `g#e0` |
| `th` + marker vowel ([group separator](#group-separator)) | **`,`** |
| `ye` | **`.`** |
| `yo` | **`%`** |
| `yu` | **`%*`** |

**Commas** separate digit groups. Each comma is spoken as the [group separator](#group-separator). Digitless forms have no commas.

For long values, break into digit groups of at most three mantissa digits (plus their exponents); all groups still sit in **one** word after a single marker.

### Number as verb / adverb / interjection / discourse
<a id="number-as-roles"></a>

You can use a number as the **action** (*add three*), as **how often** (*three times*), as **how likely** (*70% likely*), as a **shout** (*Three more!*), as a **list label** (*point 2:*), or as a **place on a scale** (*second biggest*). Put the same number word on `/v/`, `/h/`, `/th/`, `/y/`, `/x/`, or `/w/`. The role letter picks the use; the marker still says count, rank, or code.

On `/ɡ/`, `/z/`, `/d/`, and `/b/`, the number stays a plain amount, rank, or code ([marker vowel](#marker-vowel-referential-identity)).

#### Number as verb (by marker) {#number-as-verb-by-marker}

On `/v/`, the marker decides which action the number names.

| Marker | Verb sense |
|--------|------------|
| **`ra`** | **Add N / increase by N**: transitive *add N of (object)* / *increase (object) by N*; intransitive *grow by N* when the patient is clear from context. |
| **`ru`** | **Remove N / decrease by N**: transitive *remove N of (object)* / *decrease (object) by N*; intransitive *shrink by N*. |
| **`re`** | **Take / assign rank N (from the start)**: intransitive *place Nth* / *come in Nth*; transitive *put (object) in Nth place* / *rank as Nth*. |
| **`ro`** | **Enter / dial / input that digit-string**: type the code, dial the phone number, key the ID. Object (if any) is the channel or device; the digits are in the verb. |

To set a quantity to a value, use an ordinary verb with the number as its object or as an adverb. For *multiply* and *divide*, use an ordinary verb plus **`h+N`** / **`h-N`**.

Endings still apply (**-m** ≈ *about* that amount/code/rank, **-r** resume with a digit; digitless `vrar` / `vror` is a [blank](#digitless)).

#### Number as adverb (by marker) {#number-as-adverb-by-marker}

On `/h/`, the marker decides what kind of adverb the number is.

| Marker | Writing | Adverb sense |
|--------|---------|--------------|
| **`ra`** | `h+N` | **Multiplicative / factor**: *N times*; *×N*; *by a factor of N* (alone, or with ordinary *multiply*). |
| **`ru`** | `h-N` | **Inverse / partition**: *÷N*; *into N parts*; *1/N as often*; *every Nth* (alone, or with ordinary *divide*). |
| **`re`** | `h#N` | **Nth occurrence of the event (from the start)**: *for the Nth time*; *on the Nth try* (clause-event ordinal, not discourse list independence). |
| **`ro`** | `h_…` | **Clock**: *at 15:30* (`h_15,30`). **Date** uses a calendar ordinal: *on 22 July 2026*. Channel, frequency, and other codes use a host relation + `/b/` (or `/ɡ/` on a noun). |

> `zolazun vowurel hragel.`
> z-Olazun | v-walk | h-three
>
> "Olazun walks three times."

Worked examples: `hragel` *three times*; `hrugel` *÷3 / into 3*; `hregel` *for the third time*; `h_15,30` *at 15:30*. A frequency code goes after a relation word: *on* (on `/h/`) + `b_101.1`.

Endings still apply (**-m** ≈ *about* that many times / that clock or date, **-r** resume with a digit; digitless `hrar` *how many times?* / `hror` *when?* is a [blank](#digitless)). Relative %-change factors use **`h+…`** (e.g. ×1.5).

#### Number as interjection (by marker) {#number-as-interjection-by-marker}

On `/y/`, a number is a **shout** (*Three more!*, a score). Number cheers sit with the other opening `/y/` words (before the act word, when a clause follows) or alone as a whole turn with no act word.

| Marker | Writing | Interjection sense |
|--------|---------|-------------------|
| **`ra`** | `y+N` | **Quantity addition**: *Three more!*; *Ten more!* (add N to the relevant count / tally / order). |
| **`ru`** | `y-N` | **Deficit / shortfall call**: *Three short!*; *Three fewer!*; *Down by 2!*; *−3!* (mirror of `y+`). |
| **`re`** | `y#N` | **Place / rank cheer (from the start)**: *First!*; *Second!* (podium / place shout). |
| **`ro`** | `y_…` | **Digit-label / magnitude call-out**: *Three!*; *B-12!*; *five-five-five!*; score, bingo, code, or ID as a bare reading of the digits (not “N more”). |

English *Three!* naming a score or count is **`yrol`**. *Three more!* (increment the tally) is **`yral`**.

Worked examples: `y_3` *Three!*; `yragel` *Three more!*; `yrugel` *Three short!*; `yrebal` *First!*.

Endings still apply (**-m** fuzzy *about three more!*, **-n** conventional call name, **-r** resume a prior shout’s value, with a digit).

**Compare with:** statement / question / command words in the [speech-act section](speech-moves.md#speech-act).

Bare OoM bands keep their band under `/v/` `/h/` `/y/`: **`vrawagel`** *increase by a thousands-band amount*; **`hrawamol`** *× a ones-band factor*; **`yrawagel`** *Thousands!* (band cheer).

#### Number as discourse marker (by marker) {#number-as-discourse-marker-by-marker}

To number the points of a list (*point 2:*, *firstly*) or to cite a label (*item 12*), put the number on `/x/`, the linker that opens a sentence. The marker says how the point stands toward the other points: **`#`** is a plain numbered step, **`+`** backs up an earlier point, and **`-`** is a new, independent line of support. **`_`** cites a label instead. None of these is part of the sentence's own roles, unlike `/ɡ/` *the second page* or `/h/` *three times*.

| Marker | Writing | Discourse sense |
|--------|---------|-----------------|
| **`re`** | `x#N` | **Neutral point N**: numbered item from the start, no independence framing (*point N:*, *note N:*, bare *(N)*). |
| **`ra`** | `x+N` | **Corroborating item N**: backs, restates, or same-directions an earlier item (*corroborating N:*, *echoing N:*). Legitimate for emphasis, clarity, teaching, or a related source, not a new line of support. |
| **`ru`** | `x-N` | **Independent item N**: a new line of support or consideration (*independent N:*, *distinct N:*). Would still matter if other listed items were gone. |
| **`ro`** | `x_…` | **Cite a discourse label**: agenda item, section code, slide, ticket id (digits as label, not independence framing) (*regarding item 12; under 3.2*). |

Ordinary *firstly* / *secondly* is **`x#N`**. The marker does not say whether a point argues for or against; say that in ordinary words (or with evidentiality on the claim).

Worked examples: `xredol` *point 2:*; `xradol` *corroborating 2:*; `xrudol` *independent 2:*.

Endings still apply (**-l** newly stated item, **-r** *as in (N) above* (with a digit; digitless `xrer` is a [blank](#digitless)), **-n** titled item name, **-m** fuzzy *around item N*).

#### Number as degree (by marker) {#number-as-degree}
<a id="place-on-a-scale"></a>

On `/w/`, a number sits in the degree slot right before an adjective or adverb ([clause](clause.md#adjective-detail-w)). Only the ordinal marker gives a new reading: **`w#N`** (N from 2) is the **place on that scale**, in a superlative frame (`zuzozen zel wredol gelazom` *Uzozen is the second biggest*). The other number readings on `/w/` were already set: the blank **`wrar`** asks *how much?* ([how big](#how-big)), and the Advanced just-short forms are *barely* / *almost*. A count or a fraction on `/w/` (`w+3`) is not used: a factor goes on `/h/` (`hradol` *twice*, factor).

#### Number as stance (by marker) {#number-as-stance-by-marker}
<a id="likelihood"></a>

`/th/` + number is your [stance](clause.md#stance-th) on the whole claim: how likely you think it is, or which numbered source it rests on. Spoken, it starts **`thr…`**, as in English *three*.

> `zolazun vowurel th+70.`
>
> 🔊 `zolazun vowurel thraremol.`
>
> z-Olazun | v-walk | th-70-percent-likely
>
> "Olazun is probably walking." (I'd put it at 70%)

| Marker | Writing | Stance sense |
|--------|---------|--------------|
| **`ra`** | `th+N` | **Likelihood N percent**: `th+70` *70% likely*; `th+99.9` *99.9% likely*. The digits are already a percent, 0–100; do not add `%`. |
| **`ra`** / **`ru`** | `th+` / `th-` | **likely** / **unlikely**, with no figure |
| **`ro`** | `th_N` | **According to source N**: a footnote, reference, or exhibit label (`th_3` *per [3]*). Digitless `th_` = *per a source* you do not name. |
| **`re`** | `th#N` | **N-th hand** (N from 2): how many hands stand between the event and you, on any channel that is not first-hand (`th#3` *third-hand*, a tertiary source). |

For *30% unlikely*, give the likelihood of the claim itself (`th+70`). A minus with digits (`th-N`) and an end-relative rank (`th#-N`) are not used, and neither is `th#1`: first-hand is a channel word.

Endings still apply: **-m** *about* that likelihood (`th~+70` *roughly 70%*), **-n** a conventional level (`th@+95` *at the 95% confidence level*), **-r** *the same likelihood as before* (`th=+70`); digitless `thrar` asks *how likely?* under question, and `threr` asks *how many hands?*

### Style (numeric vs spelled)
<a id="writing-style-numeric-vs-spelled"></a>
<a id="writing-style"></a>

Both writings name the **same word**. Choose the surface by one test: **how many digits does the word have?** Count every digit, including exponent digits.

| Word | Prefer | Why |
|------|--------|-----|
| Free number with **two or more digits** | **shorthand** | `g+12`, `x#21`, `g+5.2e-4`: a value to scan and compare |
| Free number with **one digit or none** | **spelled** | `gragel` *three*, `gredol` *second*, `gramol` *zero*, `gral` *more than one*, `drar` *how many*; reads as a word, as English writes *three* |
| Digit-string label or code (`_`) | **shorthand** | `d_5`, `d_555,123,4567`: a code, not an amount (English *Room 5*) |
| Numeric derivation / kind morph | **spelled** (required) | shorthand after a root does not form a word |
| Inventory tables | **shorthand** | patterns are easy to scan |

**Side by side:** number words next to each other (a range, a list of values) share one writing. If any of them needs shorthand, write them all in shorthand: `z+3 al z+12`, not `zragel al z+12`.

Speech and writing drills that teach the shorthand itself still show one-digit shorthand.

A free number in shorthand shows its ending as a mark after the role letter (`g~+3`); spelled out, it shows the ending as its last letter (`gragem`). A number built onto a content root is always spelled out (`NUM`) and never takes the marks **`~`** / **`@`** / **`=`**.

## Advanced {#advanced}

### Digitless exponents {#digitless-exponents}
<a id="extremum-digitless"></a>
<a id="infinity"></a>
<a id="special-numeric-values"></a>
<a id="hyperbole-gazillion"></a>

To say *infinitely many*, *last place*, *a gazillion*, or *absolutely zero*, write the exponent mark **`wa`** or **`wu`** with **no** exponent digits. What the word means depends on what comes before the empty exponent: nothing, nonzero mantissa digits, or the mantissa **`0`** (table). A word has only one such group, and that group takes no percent closer.

| Shape | Reading |
|-------|---------|
| **`wa`/`wu` alone** (no mantissa) | special value: +∞ / −∞ / last place / start place / … (tables below) |
| **Mantissa `≠0` + `wa`/`wu`∅** | hyperbole (*N gazillion* / *umpteenth*) |
| **Mantissa `0` + `wa`/`wu`∅** | zero × digitless exp |

> `zagozolx grawal.`
> z-cat-x | g-plus-infinity
>
> "There are infinitely many cats."

**Writing:** **`e`** = speech **`wa`**; **`e-`** = speech **`wu`**. Digitless means no power digits after that mark: `g+e` / `g+e-` in tables, spelled `grawal` / `grawul` in running text. Bare / assert **`e0`** writes power **`0`**: `grawamol`, `g+3e0` (not digitless).

The marker still says amount, rank, or code. Kind compounds may use digitless **`_`** as infinite / landmark labels (`ROOTl_e`, `ROOTl_e-`, `ROOTl_1e`, …).

#### Special values: no mantissa

##### Referential (`/ɡ/` `/z/` `/d/` `/b/`) {#special-referential}

<!-- cheat-sheet: numbers -->
| Form | Anatomy | Reading |
|------|---------|---------|
| **`g+e`** (etc.) | **`ra`** + **`wa`∅** + **-l** | **+∞** |
| **`g~+e`** | **`ra`** + **`wa`∅** + **-m** | **arbitrarily large but finite** |
| **`g+e-`** | **`ra`** + **`wu`∅** + **-l** | **arbitrarily small but finite** |
| **`g-e`** | **`ru`** + **`wa`∅** + **-l** | **−∞** |
| **`g~-e`** | **`ru`** + **`wa`∅** + **-m** | **arbitrarily large but finite** (negative / deficit) |
| **`g#e`** | **`re`** + **`wa`∅** + **-l** | **last place** / final-place landmark |
| **`g~#e`** | **`re`** + **`wa`∅** + **-m** | **near last place** |
| **`g#e-`** | **`re`** + **`wu`∅** + **-l** | **start / beginning place** / origin landmark: first with emphasis on **onset** (*the starting one*, *at the beginning*). Numbered 1st is `g#1`. Ultimate-ancestor pole |
| **`g~#e-`** | **`re`** + **`wu`∅** + **-m** | **near first place** |
| **`g-e-`** | **`ru`** + **`wu`∅** + **-l** | **just short:** arbitrarily small but finite shortfall (*a hair under*); against a target, *almost* |
| **`g~-e-`** | **`ru`** + **`wu`∅** + **-m** | **nearly**: a small shortfall, not claimed to be tiny |

Same under `/z/` `/d/` `/b/` (`zrawal` = +∞ as subject; `drewul` = start-place as object; `brawul` = *of an arbitrarily small but finite amount*; `zruwul` = a hair-short amount as subject). Contrast **`grebal`** = numbered *first* / *1st*; **`grewul`** = beginning/onset landmark.

<a id="just-short"></a>

**Just short (`gruwul`):** negative marker (**`ru`**) + digitless negative exponent (**`wu`∅**), no mantissa. It mirrors **`grawul`** (a hair over zero): **`gruwul`** is a hair **under**: the smallest shortfall. Against a target it reads *almost*.

**Compare with:** the same form on each role letter: **`gruwul`** the amount, **`xruwul`** *just before that:*, **`thruwul`** *virtually*, **`vruwul`** *nudge down*, **`hruwul`** *almost*, **`yruwul`** *so close!*. Pretend *as if* is a different thing: the mood **`eyu`** ([Notional](knowing.md#notional), e.g. `theyum`).

**Endings** (no-mantissa specials):

| Ending | Sense |
|--------|-------|
| **-l** | Exact special value (default): +∞ / −∞ / last place / start place / arbitrarily small but finite / just short as in the form table |
| **-m** on **`wa`∅** (`…em`) | **Arbitrarily large but finite** on scalars (`g~+e` / `g~-e`); on ordinals (`g~#e`) = **near last place** |
| **-m** on **`wu`∅** (`…e-m`) | Soft / approximate small on scalars (`g~+e-`); *nearly* on **`ru`+`wu`∅** (`g~-e-`); on ordinals (`g~#e-`) = **near first place** |
| **-n** | Conventional designation of that special (named ∞ symbol, official *last place* / *starting* title, …) |
| **-r** | Resume a prior special value of that marker+exp polarity (`g=+e` = *that +∞ again*; `g=+e-` = *that arbitrarily small amount again*; `g=-e-` = *that same hair-short amount again*) |

##### Number as verb and adverb {#special-number-as-verb-and-adverb}

On `/v/` and `/h/`, these special values (∞, last, start, a tiny amount) become actions and adverbs. Soft **-m** softens them the same way it does on `/ɡ/` (*a lot but finite*, *near last*, …).

**Scalars (`ra` / `ru`):**

| Form | Reading |
|------|---------|
| **`v+e`** | **increase without bound** |
| **`v~+e`** | *increase a lot but finite* |
| **`v-e`** | **decrease without bound** |
| **`v~-e`** | *decrease a lot but finite* |
| **`v+e-`** | **nudge up**: increase by an arbitrarily small but finite amount |
| **`v-e-`** | **nudge down**: decrease by an arbitrarily small but finite amount |
| **`v~-e-`** | *ease down a little* |
| **`h+e`** | **unbounded multiplicity** (× without bound / unboundedly many times) |
| **`h~+e`** | *many times but finite* |
| **`h+e-`** | **barely**: the action clears one full time by a hair (`zuzozen h+e- vuhohol.` *Uzozen barely sits*) |
| **`h~+e-`** | *scraped by*: it happened, but only with strain |
| **`h-e-`** | **almost**: the action falls a hair short of one full time (`zuzozen h-e- vuhohol.` *Uzozen almost sits*) |
| **`h~-e-`** | *came close*: it fell short despite a real effort |

**Margin vs effort:** **-l** (`hrawul` / `hruwul`) measures the margin: over or under by a hair. **`~`** (**-m**) reports the felt effort instead: `zuzozen hrawum vuhohol.` is *Uzozen managed to sit, just*; `zuzozen hruwum vuhohol.` is *Uzozen tried hard to sit and didn't quite*. Division into parts keeps its counted form `h-N`.

**On a quality:** the same pair sits on `/w/` before an adjective or adverb. **`wrawul`** = *barely*; **`wruwul`** = *almost*.

> `zudozol wruwul gunemul vowurel.`
> [z-dog | [w---e- | g-angry]] | v-walk
>
> "An almost angry dog walks."

**Stance:** a whole claim that holds all but a hair is stance, not manner, so it sits on `/th/`: **`thruwul`** = *virtually* / *practically*; **`thruwum`** = *more or less*. **`hruwul`** says the event fell short; **`thruwul`** says your claim is a hair short of exact.

#### Hyperbole: mantissa + digitless exponent
<a id="gazillion"></a>

For a joking exaggeration (*a gazillion*, *the umpteenth*), write nonzero **mantissa digits** and then a digitless **`wa`** or **`wu`**. On an amount this is comically huge (**`wa`**) or tiny (**`wu`**); on a rank it is a comically late or early place. Mantissa **`0`** + digitless is [zero × exponent](#zero-exponent). Real powers of ten keep digitful exponents (`g+1e9`). End-relative place is marker **`#-`**.

### From the end: end-relative ordinal marker **`#-`** / **`ue`**
<a id="from-the-end"></a>
<a id="nth-from-the-end"></a>
<a id="negative-ordinal"></a>

To count **from the last place** (*penultimate*, *starting with the last one*), use marker **`ue`** (writing **`#-`**). Count back within the **same generation** (no exponent). Speech uses the marker vowels in u-first order: `#-` is spelled and pronounced **rue** before its digit syllables and ending (e.g. `gruedol`). The number’s role-letter-plus-`r` cluster distinguishes it from join **`ue`**.

> `zanal gruedol.`
> z-book | g-2nd-from-end
>
> "There is a second-to-last book."

| Form | Reading |
|------|---------|
| **`g#-1`** | *1st from the end*: same referent as last-place **`g#e`**, framed as counting from the end (English *Starting with the last one*) |
| **`g#-2`** | *2nd from the end* / *penultimate* |
| **`g#-3`** | *3rd from the end* / *antepenultimate* |

### Stacked markers {#stacked-markers}

When a number identity needs two marker vowels, write the symbols in their conventional order and pronounce the vowels with **`u` first**. The letter spelling and speech therefore use **ruo** for a negative digit-string label (`#_`) and **rua** for a symmetric error bound (`+-`). A negative label identifies a value below zero, such as a basement floor; `+-N` gives an uncertainty of plus or minus N. These stacked forms work with the ordinary number endings.

Write the role letter, symbol stack, then digits: `gruobal` labels a floor numbered −1; `gruagel` gives an uncertainty of ±3.

<!-- cheat-sheet: numbers -->
| Symbol stack | Spelled and spoken marker | Use |
|--------------|---------------------------|-----|
| **`#_`** | **r-u-o** | A digit-string label whose value is negative (for example, a below-ground floor label). |
| **`+-`** | **r-u-a** | A symmetric error bound of ±N. |
| **`#-`** | **r-u-e** | Count from the end ([from the end](#from-the-end)). |
| **`_#`** | **r-o-e** | A calendar date, day then month then year ([time](numbers-applied.md#time)). |

These four are the whole set. The other two standard stacks, **r-a-o** and **r-a-e**, are not used. Labels carry no sign (a dialing code `+44` is written `_44`), and a count and a rank are separate markers on separate words.

### Ambient magnitude (casual)
<a id="ambient-magnitude"></a>

In careful writing, an omitted exponent means ones-place (`gragel` = 3). Prefer writing the exponent when the magnitude matters.

In casual measure-heavy talk, a stretch of conversation may set an **ambient** order of magnitude (often an engineering `e3` / `e6` / …). Numbers written without an exponent are then heard at that magnitude until you cancel it. A [topic change](pronouns.md#topic-resets) starts a new stretch, so bare numbers go back to ones until an ambient decade is set again.

**Writing `e0`** says *ones* outright and cancels the ambient magnitude:

| In a stretch where ambient OoM = 3 | Reading |
|------------------------------------|---------|
| **`g+3`** (casual ambient) | *three at ambient* → often 3×10³ |
| **`g+3e3`** | *three at 10³* (exp explicit) |
| **`g+3e0`** | *three at OoM 0* → 3×10⁰ = **3** (break out of ambient) |
| **`g+e0`** | *ones-band amount* (bare OoM), not ambient thousands |

Write **`Ne0`** (any nonzero mantissa **N**) to insist on ones. Formal prose that never sets an ambient magnitude rarely needs **`e0`**: leave it out when ones are already clear.
