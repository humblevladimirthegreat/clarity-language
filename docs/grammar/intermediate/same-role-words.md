# Before, after, while, and like {#before-after-while-like}

Relate two members of one role inside one sentence: *Azozon walks ahead of Ahelan*, *walks while singing*, *Azozon, like Ahelan, walks*.

### Same-role words {#same-role-words}

English *Azozon walks ahead of Ahelan* says that two people walk and puts one ahead of the other. A [hinge](/dependents#hinges) such as `habam blarl` relates two whole events, so the walking would be said twice, once in each sentence. A **same-role word** relates two phrases that play the same role in one sentence (two subjects, two objects, or two verbs), so the part they share is said once. It is a relation word you already know with its role letter dropped: `habam` *before* becomes `abam`.

```text
A WORD B
```

- **B**, the phrase right after the word, keeps A's role letter. B is a second member of A's slot, in the same event: in *Azozon walks ahead of Ahelan*, Ahelan walks too.
- **A** is the nearest phrase before the word that has B's role letter. When B is a verb, A is the verb before it, not the subject.
- The word has no role letter, like a hook, but it is a whole root plus an ending (four letters or more), so it never looks like a two- or three-letter hook.

Four relation words have a same-role form:

<!-- cheat-sheet: to-english -->
| Open **-m** | Closed **-l** | From | English |
|-------------|---------------|------|---------|
| `abam` | `abal` | `habam` | *before*, *ahead of* |
| `alam` | `alal` | `halam` | *after*, *behind* |
| `erom` | `erol` | `herom` | *while*, *at the same time as* |
| `ewem` | `ewel` | `hewem` | *like* |

The ending says whether B is the whole of it, as on the [same-role hooks](/hooks#closed-and-open-endings). **-m** says B may not be the only one (*and maybe others*). **-l** says B is the whole of it. When you are unsure, prefer **-m**. On its own as a one-word reply, the word is just its root's citation: `abal` is *backpack*.

**Related form:** the [same-role hooks](/hooks#including) sit in the same place, between two phrases of one role (`zuvayal am zazozon` *the family, including Azozon*). A hook changes who is in the slot. A same-role word keeps both members and says how B relates to A: earlier, later, at the same time, or alike.

### Before and after (`abam` / `alam`) {#before-and-after}

Use `abam` when A comes before B, in time or in a moving line, and `alam` when A comes after B. B fills the same role: in a line of walkers, both walk.

> `zazozon abam zahelan vowurol.`
>
> z-Azozon | before.open | z-Ahelan | v-walk
>
> "Azozon walks ahead of Ahelan." (Ahelan walks too)

> `zazozon alam zahelan vowurol.`
>
> z-Azozon | after.open | z-Ahelan | v-walk
>
> "Azozon walks behind Ahelan." (Ahelan walks too)

Between two verbs, the word orders two acts of the same doer. A is `vowurol`, the nearest verb before `abam`:

> `zazozon vowurol abam vazuvol.`
>
> z-Azozon | v-walk | before.open | v-swim
>
> "Azozon walks before swimming."

To ask for B, put the word and the [fill-ask](/questions#fill-ask) blank first, as with any blank that completes a word. A is then the nearest phrase with the blank's role letter after the pair:

> `yel abam zar zazozon vowurol.`
>
> y-question | before.open | z-wh | z-Azozon | v-walk
>
> "Who does Azozon walk ahead of?"

When the blank is A, it already comes first, and nothing moves: `yel zar abam zazozon vowurol.` asks *Who walks ahead of Azozon?*

**Compare with:** a [route join](/rank-joins#route-ao) and an [ordered range](/ordered-ranges#ranges) also put members in order. `zazozon zahelan zaol` lists every member in order (*Azozon, then Ahelan*). `zazozon aol zahelan` walks from one end to the other, taking in everyone between. `zazozon abam zahelan` relates just the two, with Ahelan as the background.

**Compare with:** [the landmark's own front](/viewpoint#landmark-facing) places A against something that stays put: `gazuhothel blohehal` *behind the house*. Use `alam` for behind someone who is moving too.

### While (`erom`) {#same-role-while}

Use `erom` when A and B happen at the same time. Most often B is a second verb of the same doer:

> `zazozon vowurol erom vozuyel.`
>
> z-Azozon | v-walk | while.open | v-sing
>
> "Azozon walks while singing."

Between two subjects, it is *at the same time as*: `zazozon erom zahelan vozuyel.` *Azozon sings at the same time as Ahelan.*

With **-l**, `erol` is *only while*: A happens during B and at no other time.

> `zazozon vezuyol erol vowurol.`
>
> z-Azozon | v-tell | while.closed | v-walk
>
> "Azozon talks only while walking."

**Compare with:** a [hinge](/dependents#hinges) relates two whole events, each its own sentence after `blarl`, so the two can have different doers and different verbs: `zazozon vowurol herom blarl zahelan vozuyel.` *Azozon walks while Ahelan sings.* Use `erom` when the two share the rest of the sentence, so it is said once.

### Like (`ewem`) {#same-role-like}

English *Azozon, like Ahelan, walks* says that both walk, and holds up Ahelan as the case Azozon matches. Use `ewem` between the two: B is a separate member of the same role, in the background.

> `zazozon ewem zahelan vowurol.`
>
> z-Azozon | like.open | z-Ahelan | v-walk
>
> "Azozon, like Ahelan, walks." (Ahelan walks too)

With **-l**, `ewel` is *like B, and B alone*. It is not *exactly like*: an exact match is [`hewel`](/exactly-like#exactly-like) with a `bl-` model.

> `zazozon ewel zahelan vowurol.`
>
> z-Azozon | like.closed | z-Ahelan | v-walk
>
> "Azozon, like Ahelan, walks." (Ahelan alone, no others)

A reply to a fill-ask may use the word too. As in any [reply of more than one noun](/questions#fill-ask), both members keep the blank's role letter:

> `yel zar vowurol.`
>
> y-question | z-wh | v-walk
>
> "Who walks?"

> `zazozon ewem zahelan.`
>
> z-Azozon | like.open | z-Ahelan
>
> "Azozon, like Ahelan."

**Compare with:** when B is one example of A (*animals like dogs*, *the family, such as Azozon*), B is part of A, so use the hook `am` [inside a clause](/discourse-hooks#stacked-discourse): `zuvayal am zazozon vowurol.` *The family, such as Azozon, walks.* Use `ewem` when B stands beside A.

**Not the same job as:** a `/ɡ/` [likeness](/relations#likeness) word, which limits who counts: `zual gewem blazozon vowurol.` *Everyone like Azozon walks.* `ewem` limits nothing; it relates two members.

#### Pick one {#intermediate-pick-one}

**1.** *Azozon sings only while dancing.* `zazozon vozuyel erom vunurol.` or `zazozon vozuyel erol vunurol.`

::: details Show answer
`zazozon vozuyel erol vunurol.`

z-Azozon | v-sing | while.closed | v-dance

*Only while* says dancing is the whole of it, so the word takes **-l**; with **-m**, Azozon might also sing at other times.
:::

#### What changes {#intermediate-what-changes}

**1.** `zahelan ewem zazozon vowuyal.` / `zahelan ewel zazozon vowuyal.`

::: details Show answer
z-Ahelan | like.open | z-Azozon | v-wave

z-Ahelan | like.closed | z-Azozon | v-wave

Both say Ahelan, like Azozon, waves. The **-m** form leaves room for others besides Azozon; the **-l** form says Azozon is the only one.
:::
