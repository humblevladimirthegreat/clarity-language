# Viewpoint and landmark {#viewpoint-and-landmark}

### Scope across scenes (`e`, `a`, pairs) {#scope}
<a id="scope-across-scenes"></a>

The scope slot after **`w`** takes every [label scope](/label-seams#label-scope) vowel, not just **`u`** and **`o`**. They are the same vowels other labels write after mid-word **`th`**; on a role word they come first, and nowhere else. A **`th`** scope after a role word's stem (`zwuzuduyethel`) is not a word.

| V1 | Reach | Example | English |
|----|-------|---------|---------|
| **`u`** | this episode: one act is enough | `zwuzeduyol` | *someone who teaches* |
| **`o`** | practiced role | `zwozeduyol` | *a teacher* |
| **`e`** | this pair: toward one party, named in a `bl-` word on `/ɡ/` | `gwezeduyol blalazun` | *is a teacher to Alazun* |
| **`a`** | a type across scenes | `zwazeduyol` | *a teacher type* |
| pairs | the scope stacks: **`ae`**, **`ao`**, **`oe`**, **`ua`**, **`ue`**, **`uo`** | `gwuozeduyol`, `gwaezeduyol` | *not in that capacity*, *a teacher by circumstance* |

> `zazozon gwezeduyol blalazun.`
>
> z-Azozon | [g-pair-agent-teach | bl-Alazun]
>
> "Azozon is Alazun's teacher."

On `/z/`, `/d/`, and `/b/`, pair scope **`e`** stands alone: the party is the one the clause already relates the person to. On `/ɡ/` it takes its `bl-` party, as on any [pair-scope label](/label-seams#scope-relative).

> `zazozon gwuozeduyol.`
>
> z-Azozon | g-outside-role-agent-teach
>
> "Azozon is not acting as a teacher here."

Scope works on [role pointers](/pronouns#role-pointers) too. `zwuzer` is *the latest doer*; `zwozer` is *the latest doer, in their practiced role*: after *Azozon teaches*, `zwozer` is Azozon as a teacher. With **-m** it gives a useful contrast: `dwuzem` is *what they did (this time)*, and `dwozem` is *their practice, their craft*.

### The role you act in (`/h/`) {#capacity}

English *as a teacher, I …* names the role the act is done in, not a person standing in the clause. Write the same role compound, with a slot part (`z`, `d`, or `b`), on `/h/`: a person cannot be an adverb, so the word is the capacity the act is done in.

> `zazozon vezuyol hwozeduyol.`
>
> z-Azozon | v-tell | h-role-agent-teach
>
> "Azozon speaks as a teacher."

The scope vowel does its usual work. Pair scope **`e`** takes its `bl-` party, as on `/ɡ/`.

| Agebon | English |
|--------|---------|
| `hwuzeduyol` | *as the one teaching this time* |
| `hwozeduyol` | *as a teacher* |
| `hwezeduyol blalazun` | *as Alazun's teacher* |
| `hwuozeduyol` | *not as a teacher* (off duty) |
| `hwodebebul` | *as a patient* (the one a doctor treats) |
| `hwobezuyol` | *as a confidant* |

**Compare with:** resemblance is the [similative](/relations#similative) (`hewem blwozeduyol` *like a teacher*). An ordinary `/h/` adverb is how the event goes (`hadurom` *hastily*).

**Compare with:** `zazozon gwozeduyol.` says Azozon **is** a teacher. `zazozon vezuyol hwozeduyol.` says the telling is done in that role.

### Places, tools, and times {#places-tools-times}

A role compound names a participant: the doer, what the event acts on or makes, or the extra `/b/` party. A place, tool, or time is a landmark, brought in with an [extra-noun hook](/hooks#extra-noun) (`al bohehal` *in a house*, `aol banewul` *using a knife*), and it has no role compound. Name it with words you already have:

| Job | Agebon | English |
|-----|--------|---------|
| a kind of tool, place, or time | an ordinary [compound](/compounds#two-roots-one-word) with a head root: `daruyoxuwal` (write + tool), `dezuvexodurol` (sleep + time) | *a writing tool*, *bedtime* |
| the landmark a clause named | resume its noun: after `aol banewul`, `danewur` | *that knife* |
| that landmark as a place or means | the hook's own [pointer](discourse-hooks.md#hook-resume): `ar` *in there*, `er` *there*, `ur` *from there*, `aer` *with it* | *there*, *with it* |
| a landmark nobody named | two sentences: a place exists, and the event happened there | *wherever it happened* |
| who pays, who it is for | the hook in a clause (`uol` *against*, `ol` *for* plus `/b/`), then a resume | *the one it was stolen from* |

> `zazozon vuguyol aol banewul. zahelan danewur vayebel.`
>
> z-Azozon | v-cook | [using | b-knife] . z-Ahelan | d-←knife | v-see
>
> "Azozon cooks with a knife. Ahelan sees the knife."

> `zazozon vuguyol aol banewul. zahelan vuguyol aer.`
>
> z-Azozon | v-cook | [using | b-knife] . z-Ahelan | v-cook | on.it
>
> "Azozon cooks with a knife. Ahelan cooks with it too."

### Content of the event {#content-of-the-event}

English *the way it was done*, *what it left them*, *what came of it*, or *that remark* names a quality of an event, not a person in it. Write the same role-word shape, and put a part consonant for that quality after the scope vowel: the word is then a thing you can talk about, resume, or point to.

> `dwuhowurol vayebel.`
>
> d-episode-manner-walk | v-see
>
> "Someone sees a way of walking."

A slot part on the same stem is still a participant: `dwuzowurol` is *someone who walks*, as [role compounds](/roles#role-compounds) name a doer.

The stem is still the event. The part says which quality of it you mean. Pointers drop the stem and read the latest matching event, as [role pointers](/pronouns#role-pointers) do for people:

> `zazozon vowurol hadurom. zahelan dwuher vayebel.`
>
> z-Azozon | v-walk | h-haste . z-Ahelan | d-←episode-manner.same | v-see
>
> "Azozon walks in haste. Ahelan sees the way it was done."

If that clause named no manner word, `dwuher` is the event's own manner: how walking went, unnamed. A restrictor, a time pole, or a [say word](feelings-permission.md#say-words) is already doing its own job on `/h/`, so the pointer skips it.

| Part | Names | Compound | Pointer |
|------|-------|----------|---------|
| **`h`** | how, when, or where it happened | `dwuhowurol` *a way of walking* | `dwuher` *the way it was done* |
| **`g`** | the state it leaves its participant in | `dwuguguyol` *how cooked it is* | `dwuger` *the state it left them in* |
| **`th`** | a reason, thought, or feeling about it | `dwuthowurol` *a reason to walk* | `dwuther` *the reason for it* |
| **`w`** | how much or how intense it was | `dwuwowurol` *the amount of walking* | `dwuwer` *how much it was* |
| **`y`** | the turn that said it (question, command, remark) | `dwuyowurol` *a remark about walking* | `dwuyer` *that remark* |
| **`x`** | what followed from it | `dwuxowurol` *what comes of walking* | `dwuxer` *what came of it* |
| **`v`** | the event itself | (no compound: the event noun is the stem, `dowurol`) | `dwuver` *that event* |

Scope **`a`** / **`e`** / **`o`** / **`u`** still say how far the label reaches: `dwohowurol` is *someone's usual way of walking*, `dwahowurol` *a walking style*.

A **`g`** pointer is the `/d/` participant's state when the event has one, and the `/z/` participant's otherwise. After *Azozon punches Alazun*, `dwuger` is Alazun's state; after *Azozon runs*, it is Azozon's. **`w`** always reads the event's own extent: a `/w/` word grades the next adjective, never the event, so `dwuwer` is *that much*. `dwugar` and `dwuwar` are not words: no clause names a resulting state or an amount for *the other* to differ from.

A **`th`** pointer reads [sake](/sakes) words, feeling roots, and [causation poles](/causation) on `/th/`. A channel, MAY, the notional, or a holder is the speaker's stance on the *claim*, not a stance on the event, so the pointer skips those too. With no such word, `dwuther` is still *the reason for it*: the gap the spelling fills.

A **`y`** pointer reaches turns, not events: `dwuyer` the latest turn, `dwuyar` the one before it, `dwuyor` this very turn, `dwuyur` whatever was said. With **-m**, `dwuyem` is what the saying did, apart from what it said.

If the next sentence after an event opened with a [consequence linker](sentence-linkers.md#sentence-linkers) (`xahom` *therefore*, `xahol` *it follows that*), an **`x`** pointer is that sentence's event:

> `zazozon vabuvul. xahom zalazun veyamul. zahelan dwuxer vayebel.`
>
> z-Azozon | v-punch . x-therefore | z-Alazun | v-scream . z-Ahelan | d-←episode-outcome.same | v-see
>
> "Azozon punches. Therefore Alazun screams. Ahelan sees what came of it."

**Compare with:** a landmark is a thing (*the house*, *the knife*), reached by resuming its noun or with a hook pointer (`aer` *with it*). An **`h`** part is a quality of the event (*the haste*): *how* is `dwuher`. **`g`** is the state the event leaves its participant in; **`x`** is what happens next because of it.

### Viewpoint laterals (`DIR` × anchor) {#viewpoint-laterals}
<a id="spatial-laterals"></a>
<a id="my-left-your-left"></a>

English *left* / *right* / *ahead* / *back* can mean a compass heading (*west*) or a side of a viewpoint (*Azozon’s left*, *the car’s left*). For the viewpoint reading, write the same arrow root, then mid-word **`th`**, then the person or thing whose facing counts as north, then an ordinary [word ending](/word-endings). You can then say *walk left* without meaning *walk west*.

A person’s front is the direction they face. For an object, use the front its design or current use makes forward; if that does not settle a front, make the frame clear from context.

The [sake](/sakes#sake-inventory) roots are never a direction: after one of them, **`th`** starts a sake word, even when a root-shaped piece follows ([emotion compose](feelings-permission.md#emotion-compose)).

> `yol vowurol hewuhothazozon.`
>
> y-command | v-walk | h-west-th-Azozon
>
> "Walk left." (Azozon’s left)

Bare arrow words are compass points on a north-up map:

> `yol vowurol hewuhol.`
>
> y-command | v-walk | h-west
>
> "Walk west."

Speaker and listener roots are the facing person when you mean *my left* / *your left*:

> `yol vowurol zawan hewuhothawan.`
>
> y-command | v-walk | z-listener | h-west-th-listener
>
> "You, walk left." (your left)

For *their left*, [resume](/pronouns#resume-r) the person on **-r** after **`th`**: `hewuhothazozor` is *on Azozon's left*, said of someone already named.

> `zazozon vowurol. zalazun vehohel hewuhothazozor.`
>
> z-Azozon | v-walk . z-Alazun | v-sit | h-west-th-←Azozon
>
> "Azozon walks. Alazun sits on their left." (Azozon's left)

| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| PoS | slot as usual (`/ɡ/` property, `/h/` path / manner, `/z/` `/d/` `/b/` side-region) | same slots as other content |
| DIR | an arrow-rose root | *north* / *ahead*, … |
| **`th`** | joins DIR to the viewpoint | viewpoint after **`th`** (cue: **th** ≈ *think*, whose point of view) |
| ANCHOR | what sets the facing frame: [special](/pronouns#special-pronouns) **`uwe`** / **`awa`** / **`aya`**, a person’s name, or a content **-r** of a person or oriented object | *Azozon’s left*, *your left*, *the car’s left*, *their left* |
| Ending | ordinary ending is written at the end of the whole compound; its reference is the viewpoint anchor, not the direction | viewpoint kind / hedge / name / resume |

**Compare with:** naming a doer from an event puts **`w`**, a scope vowel, and a part consonant before the stem ([role compounds](/roles#role-compounds): `zwuzuvuvul`). Viewpoint laterals put a **direction root** left of **`th`** and a facing person on the right.

### Arrow rose (compass vs face)

The examples above used *west* / *left*. The other seven arrow roots work the same way: alone, each is a compass heading; with **`th`** and a facing person, it is that direction from the person’s body.

<!-- cheat-sheet: roles-comparing -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| **`onuhol`** | compass heading, or ahead on a face | *north* / *ahead* | ⬆️ *up-arrow*: map north, or ahead if facing is north |
| **`azuhol`** | compass heading, or back on a face | *south* / *back* | ⬇️ *down-arrow*: map south, or back if facing is north |
| **`ahol`** | compass heading, or right on a face | *east* / *right* | ➡️ *right-arrow*: the sun’s path east, or right of facing |
| **`ewuhol`** | compass heading, or left on a face | *west* / *left* | ⬅️ *left-arrow*: opposite east, or left of facing |
| **`unuhol`** | compass heading, or forward-right on a face | *northeast* / *forward-right* | ↗️ *northeast-arrow*: between north and east |
| **`anuhol`** | compass heading, or forward-left on a face | *northwest* / *forward-left* | ↖️ *northwest-arrow*: between north and west |
| **`ozuhol`** | compass heading, or back-right on a face | *southeast* / *back-right* | ↘️ *southeast-arrow*: between south and east |
| **`azohol`** | compass heading, or back-left on a face | *southwest* / *back-left* | ↙️ *southwest-arrow*: between south and west |

Indoor headings may follow a local map (building north). When you mean directions from a person’s body, name the viewpoint or use the listener (`…thazawan`, `…thehon`).

### Gravity (`abuho` / `oduho`) {#gravity}

English *up* / *down* usually follows the pull of gravity, not anyone’s facing. Write **`abuho`** (skyward) or **`oduho`** (toward the pull) as ordinary content words, with no **`th`** + facing person. Everyone shares that frame.

> `yol vowurol habuhol.`
>
> y-command | v-walk | h-up
>
> "Walk up."

> `yabuhol.`
>
> y-up
>
> "Up!"

<!-- cheat-sheet: roles-comparing -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Same root as | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|--------------|-----|
| **`abuhol`** | skyward, shared gravity frame | *up* | | ⏫: away from the pull |
| **`oduhol`** | toward the pull, shared gravity frame | *down* | | ⏬: toward the pull |
| **`evuhol`** | the vertical axis | *up–down axis* | | ↕️: the axis only |

### Viewpoint vs landmark

English *left of the tree, from Azozon’s view* needs both a facing person and a landmark. Put the facing person after **`th`**; put the tree, door, or wall in `/b/`. The `/b/` word is the landmark, not whose left it is.

> `zanal gewuhothazozon bledalol.`
>
> z-book | [g-west-th-Azozon | bl-tree]
>
> "The book is left of the tree, from Azozon’s view."

A bare compass word plus `/b/` is the region on that side of the landmark (`gewuhol bledalol` *west of the tree*).

Gravity works the same way: bare **`oduho`** + `/b/` is *under* the landmark, and bare **`abuho`** + `/b/` is *above* / *over* it.

> `zodozel vezuvel hoduhol bledalol.`
>
> z-dog | v-sleep | [h-down | bl-tree]
>
> "The dog sleeps under the tree."

> `zebezel vehohel gabuhol blodozel.`
>
> z-bird | v-sit | [g-up | bl-dog]
>
> "A bird sits above the dog."

**Compare with:** locative *at* / *on* / *inside* name how the figure sits relative to the landmark ([locative relations](of-relations.md#locative-relations)), not a compass heading.

<!-- cheat-sheet: roles-comparing -->
| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| Bare DIR | compass / gravity | `gewuhol` *west*; `gabuhol` *up* |
| **`DIR th ANCHOR`** | that point on **ANCHOR’s** facing rose | `gewuhothawan` *listener-left* |
| DIR + **`th`** + **`e`** + `/b/` | that point on the **landmark’s** own rose | `gazuhothel blohehal` *behind the house* |
| **complex `/ɡ/` + `/b/`** | region on a side **of a landmark** | `gewuhothazozon bledalol` *left of the tree, from Azozon’s view* |

### The landmark's own front (DIR + `th` + `e`) {#landmark-facing}

English *behind the house* means the house's own back, not a compass heading and not the speaker's back. Write the arrow root, then **`th`**, then **`e`** in place of a facing person, then the ending. Put the landmark in a `bl-` word right after it. **`e`** is the same vowel as pair scope on a label: here, the one `/b/` landmark is the one whose facing counts.

> `zodozel vehohel gazuhothel blohehal.`
>
> z-dog | v-sit | [g-south-th-landmark | bl-house]
>
> "The dog sits behind the house."

> `zazozon vowurol honuhothel blohel.`
>
> z-Azozon | v-walk | [h-north-th-landmark | bl-door]
>
> "Azozon walks in front of the door."

This is [label scope](/label-seams#label-scope) **`the`** on a direction: the direction relative to that landmark. Arrow roots take no other scope vowel.

The landmark's front is where its design or current use faces (a house's front door, a car's windshield). A DIR + **`th`** + **`e`** word always takes a `/b/` landmark.

<!-- cheat-sheet: roles-comparing -->
| Agebon | English |
|--------|---------|
| `gazuhothel blohehal` | *behind the house* (its own back) |
| `gonuhothel blohehal` | *in front of the house* |
| `gewuhothel blohehal` | *on the house's left* |
| `gewuhothazozon blohehal` | *left of the house, from Azozon's view* |
| `gewuhol blohehal` | *west of the house* |

### Endings and resume

Write the ordinary [word ending](/word-endings) at the end of the whole compound. In a viewpoint lateral, the ending applies to the **viewpoint anchor**, not to DIR: **-l** / **-m** describe the anchor’s reference, **-n** names the anchor, and **-r** resumes it. A bare direction word still takes its ending on the direction itself.

| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| **-l** | indefinite / kind viewpoint anchor or unmarked path | left of a viewpoint |
| **-m** | soft / hedged viewpoint anchor | left of a hedged viewpoint |
| **-n** | named viewpoint anchor | that named viewpoint’s left |
| **-r** | resume the most recent matching viewpoint anchor in a **`DIR th ANCHOR`** compound, or the anchor alone when it is the latest match: ordinary [pronoun](/pronouns) rules | that viewpoint’s left |

<!-- cheat-sheet: roles-comparing -->
| Agebon | English |
|--------|---------|
| `gewuhol` | *west* (shared map) |
| `gewuhothazozon` | *Azozon’s left* |
| `gewuhothawan` | *your left* (listener facing) |
| `gewuhothuwen` | *my left* (speaker facing) |
| `gewuhothayan` | *our left* (shared facing) |
| `gabuhol` / `habuhol` | gravity *up* |
