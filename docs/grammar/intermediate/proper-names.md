# Names and titles {#proper-names-and-titled-phrases}

This page puts the name ending from Beginner on every role letter, for titles, brands, and named events. It then shows how to mean one thing a name applies to (*an iPhone*), the four endings on a country or tradition root, names of several words (a store name or a title), and a given name with a family name.

### Proper name (`-n`) {#proper-name--n}

Beginner used `-n` for a person’s or place’s name. You can also mark a title, a brand, or another established name with `-n` on any role letter: a named race as the verb, a named style as the adjective, a named standard as the adverb. The first letter still says the word’s role in the clause; `-n` says you mean the thing that bears that name, not the kind the root describes.

A brand, a product line, a work, or a yearly event counts as one named thing, even though it has many units, copies, or runnings. To mean one of those, use `-ln`.

> `zahelan vozuyen.`
>
> z-Ahelan | v-Ozuyen
>
> "Ahelan sings Ozuyen." (that titled song)

The table shows `-n` on each role letter:

| Ageben | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| `/z/` `/d/` `/b/` + `-n` | this named entity | person, place, titled work, brand or product line (*Azozon*, *Paris*, *the Odyssey*; *book by* **[Rowling]**) |
| `/v/` + `-n` | this named event, rite, or titled action | *perform* **[Hamlet]**, *run* **[the Boston Marathon]**, *observe* **[Ramadan]** |
| `/ɡ/` + `-n` | this named style, brand, family, or category | **[Art Deco]** furniture, **[iOS]** app, *Alon* **[Olowon]** |
| `/w/` + `-n` | named scale, grade, or criterion on the following adjective | *spicy* **[Scoville]**, *large* **[King-size]** |
| `/h/` + `-n` | named standard, channel, or official frame | *according to* **[GAAP]**, *in* **[Q3]** as the named quarter  |
| `/y/` vocative + `-n` | address this named person, place, or title; on `/y/`, `-n` always calls | `yalazun`; a kind as a title (`yagayun`); titled group (*Team Alpha*) |

### One of a name (`-ln`) {#name-instance--ln}

English *an iPhone*, *a copy of the Odyssey*, and *someone named Azozon* each mean one thing the name applies to, not the named thing itself. End the word in `-ln` instead of `-n`, on `/z/`, `/d/`, or `/b/`. Like `-l`, it brings a new thing into the conversation, and the **n** says which name it falls under. So you can mention one bed of a bed brand without a separate word for *bed*. (cue: `l` first mention, then `n` name)

> `zalazun dazozoln vayebel.`
>
> z-Alazun | d-Azozon.instance | v-see
>
> "Alazun sees an Azozon." (one bed of that brand)

What counts as one depends on what the name names:

| Name (`-n`) | One of it (`-ln`) |
|---------------|---------------------|
| a brand or product line | one unit (*an Azozon*) |
| a work | one copy or one performance |
| a yearly event | one running of it |
| a person | someone with that name (*another Azozon*) |

<!-- cheat-sheet: word-shape-clause -->
| Ageben | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| `dazozon` | the named thing itself | *Azozon* (the brand) |
| `dazozoln` | one thing the name applies to | *an Azozon* |
| `dazozolnx` | some things the name applies to ([plurality](/plurality)) | *some Azozons* |
| `zaloln golowon` | the same on a full name | *someone named Alon Olowon* |

An `-ln` word is a new mention, so a lone `zazozoln.` is *there is an Azozon*, and a later `-r` resume (`dazozor`) is that same bed, not the brand. A foreign name in a span uses the fence mark `^@` for the same job ([spans](/spans#when-required)).

Two nearby forms mean something else. `gazozon` after a noun puts it in the brand (`zoyahel gazozon` *the bed is an Azozon*), and `-l` on the root is the root’s own sense: `dazozol` is *a swan*.

### Countries and traditions {#countries-traditions}

English uses separate words for a country, its people, and its flag (*Japan*, *Japanese*, *a Japanese flag*). In Ageben a country root or a tradition root takes all four endings, each with one job, so one root covers the country, its people, and its flag:

| Ending | Country (`azabana`) | Tradition (`uguge`) |
|--------|-------------------|---------------------|
| `-n` | the country as a named place: *Japan* | the tradition as a named whole: *Christianity* |
| `-m` | belonging to its people: *Japanese* | belonging to its people: *Christian* |
| `-l` | its flag: *a Japanese flag* | its symbol: *a crucifix* |
| `-ln` | one thing that bears the name (*a ship named Japan*) | one thing that bears the name |

`-n` names the whole and `-m` says someone or something belongs to it, so a nationality is not a special case. A person, a quality, or a thing from that people (*Japanese food*) takes `-m`; `-n` on `/ɡ/` is a named label instead: a style, a brand, or a family name (*Art Deco*, *iOS*, *Alon* **[Olowon]**).

> `zazozon gazabanam.`
>
> z-Azozon | g-japanese
>
> "Azozon is Japanese."

<!-- cheat-sheet: word-shape-clause -->
| Ageben | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| `azabanan` | the named country | *Japan* |
| `dazabanal` | its flag | *a Japanese flag* |
| `gazabanam` | belonging to its people | *Japanese* |

Country names are spelled from the English sound (`azabanan` from *Japan*). The [lexicon](lexicon.md#respell) shows that spelling for a name you type. A person whose English name you want to keep as written uses a [span](/spans#loans) instead.

### Titled phrases (hook, join, span)
<a id="titled-phrases"></a>

A store name, a title, or a handle can be several words. Put `-n` on the hook, join, or span that holds those words together, and that ending names the whole phrase. Each inner word keeps its own ending: a kind stays `-l`, an abstract stays `-m`, a resume stays `-r`, and a person or place that is itself a name still takes `-n`.

> `dedudal en dogudal.`
>
> NAME[d-tea | instead | d-coffee]
>
> "Coffee-Instead-of-Tea" (a cafe name)

| Package | Where `-n` sits | Inner words |
|---------|-------------------|-------------|
| hook | the hook (`en`, extra-noun `-n`, …) | sides keep `-l` / `-m` / `-r`, or `-n` if that side is a name |
| join | the join (`zen`, …) | members keep their usual endings |
| [span](/spans#when-required) | the fence (`@` / spoken `-n`) | inner words keep their usual endings |

A one-word name, a hyphenated name with mid-word `x`, and each part of a full name still end the content word in `-n`.

### Multipart names {#multipart-names}

English writes a given name and a family name as two words (*Alon Olowon*). Ageben does too. The given name is the noun, with `-n`, and the family name follows it as a `/ɡ/` word with `-n`: like a brand, a family is a named group the person falls under. Given name first is the usual order.

> `zalon golowon vowurol.`
>
> [z-Alon | g-Olowon] | v-walk
>
> "Alon Olowon walks."

The family name describes the given name, so the person is still one noun in one role. A resume repeats the given name’s stem, so `zalor` points back to Alon Olowon, and you can drop the family name once the person is known.

<!-- cheat-sheet: word-shape-clause -->
| Ageben | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| `alon golowon` | citation of a full name | *Alon Olowon* |
| `zalon golowon` | full name in a noun slot | *Alon Olowon* |
| `zaloln golowon` | [one of a name](#name-instance--ln) | *someone named Alon Olowon* |
| `zalor` | [resume](/pronouns) | *Alon* (the same person) |

Put the family name first, as an adjective before the noun, only when your culture names people family first. The same family name can describe the family itself (`zuvayal golowon` *the Olowon family*), and what the family uses takes the ordinary *whose* hook: `dohehal om buvayal golowon` is *the Olowon family’s house*.

A full name works as a [greeting](/word-endings#greeting) too: `alon golowon.` is Alon Olowon saying hello. A [call](/speech-moves#vocative) takes the family name the same way (`yalon golowon.` *Alon Olowon!*).

A family name is an adjective, but the person who receives is a plain `/b/` word, so it may stand right after the family name: `zalon golowon balazun vezuyol.` is *Alon Olowon tells Alazun*, the same as `zalon golowon vezuyol balazun.` Only a [`bl-` word](/clause#extra-nouns) would complete it.

A **hyphenated name** (*Mary-Kate*, *Coffee-Tea*) is one word: roots join left to right with mid-word `x`, and `-n` names the whole.

> `zaloxolowon vowurol.`
>
> z-Alo-x-Olowon | v-walk
>
> "Aloxolowon walks."

| Ageben | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| `zaloxolowon` | hyphenated given name | *Aloxolowon* |
| `zaloxolowon golowon` | hyphenated given name and a family name | *Aloxolowon Olowon* |
| `zogudaxedudan`, `zogudaxedudaxowulan` | shop label from lexicon roots | *Coffee-Tea*; *Coffee-Tea-Water* |

A business or product name uses the same pieces. A compact label is one `x` word. A product under a maker’s name takes the maker as its `/ɡ/` name, as a person takes a family name (*a Mustang by Ford*). A name with grammar inside it, such as a verb or *instead of*, is a [titled phrase](#titled-phrases) or a [span](/spans#when-required), where any role letter can appear.

A nativized loan (a foreign word adapted into an Ageben root, plus an ordinary ending) takes the same four endings as any content word. A **dictionary compound** also fills one role: it is one stem, written without mid-word `x` (`zoyahehal` *bedroom*).

Do not confuse a full name with a sentence. `zalon golowon.` alone is a sentence: *Alon is an Olowon* ([classification](/predication#classification)). The citation `alon golowon` has no role letter, so it only names. Two people in one role take a [join](/joins#right-close) after them (`zalon zolowon zal`).
