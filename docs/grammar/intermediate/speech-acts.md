### Doubling an act word {#doubled-act}

Say an act word twice among the opening `/y/` words to make the act more insistent. The act stays the same; the repeat never cancels it. Use it most where a missed *don't* would be dangerous.

> `yul yul vozuyul.`
>
> y-prohibition | y-prohibition | v-sneak
>
> "Don't sneak. I mean it."

<!-- cheat-sheet: talking -->
| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| **`yal yal`** | insisted statement | *I'm telling you, …* |
| **`yel yel`** | pressing question | *I really need to know: …?* |
| **`yol yol`** | urgent command | *Do it. Now.* |
| **`yul yul`** | emphatic prohibition | *Don't. I mean it.* |

Both words take the same ending: **`yom yom`** is an insistent plea (*please, please…*), and **`yur yur`** is *really, hold off for now*. **`yal`** is usually left out, but **`yal yal`** is always written, because the repeat carries the emphasis.

Repeat only the opening act word: an act word after the body starts a new turn instead of adding emphasis.

**Compare with:** the tone mark `!` makes your voice louder and more stressed. A doubled act word makes the act itself more insistent, so it also fits a calm voice. The two can go together.

### Stacking two act words {#stacked-acts}

People often do one act while sounding like another. *Could you pass the salt?* is a request that sounds like a question. *We don't hit in this house* is a prohibition that sounds like a statement. Two different act words say both: the **first** is what you really do, and the **second** is how it sounds. The listener always knows the real act from the first word, and answers that act.

> `yol yel vabuvul dubogal zawan.`
>
> y-command | y-question | v-punch | d-box | z-listener
>
> "Could you punch the box?" (a request)

The body takes the **first** word's [usual order](/speech-moves#act-word-order). The second word only says how the turn sounds and never moves the verb. So a prohibition that sounds like a command still keeps its verb late, and a missed **`yul`** never leaves an order to act.

> `yul yol dubogal vabuvul.`
>
> y-prohibition | y-command | d-box | v-punch
>
> "Go ahead, punch the box." (sarcastic: don't)

> `yul yal zayan dubogal vabuvul.`
>
> y-prohibition | y-statement | z-interlocutors | d-box | v-punch
>
> "We don't punch the box." (a house rule)

<!-- cheat-sheet: talking -->
| Agebon | Really does → sounds like | English |
|--------|---------------------------|---------|
| **`yol yel`** | command → question | *Could you pass the salt?* |
| **`yol yal`** | command → statement | *You'll be home by ten.* / *Passengers remain seated.* |
| **`yol yul`** | command → prohibition | *Don't you dare finish those cookies* (teasing: do) |
| **`yul yal`** | prohibition → statement | *We don't hit in this house.* |
| **`yul yel`** | prohibition → question | *Must you?* / *Do you really have to shout?* |
| **`yul yol`** | prohibition → command | *Go ahead, try it.* (sarcastic: don't) |
| **`yal yol`** | statement → command | *Note that…* / *Mark my words.* |
| **`yal yul`** | statement → prohibition | *I won't even mention that he lied.* (said anyway) |

Each word's ending describes its own act. The first ending says how firmly you really mean the act; the second says how it sounds. So a stack can show a soft sound over a firm act, which is the pressure that is hardest to name in English:

<!-- cheat-sheet: talking -->
| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| **`yom yel`** | soft request that sounds like a question: they may say no | *Would you mind…?* |
| **`yol yem`** | firm command that sounds like a gentle ask: they may not say no | a boss's *Would you maybe…?* |
| **`yol yom`** | command with a *please* that is not optional | *Please do it.* (not a choice) |
| **`yom yol`** | soft offer that sounds like a command | *Have a seat. Go on, take another.* |
| **`yor yal`** | rule for now that sounds like a description | *For now, everyone stays inside.* |
| **`yum yal`** | soft norm | *We'd rather people didn't…* |
| **`yul yur`** | firm *no* that sounds like *not yet* | *Let's hold off for now.* (meaning never) |

> `yom yol vehohel.`
>
> y-request | y-command | v-sit
>
> "Sit, sit!" (an offer)

A stack has two act words at most, with nothing between them. A call, a reaction, or a hook goes before the first act word. The first word is always written, even **`yal`**: without it, the second word would be the act.

### Describing a call or reaction {#describe-turn-word}

A call or a reaction can also take an adjective **before** it, with [**`gl-`**](/left-bound-adjectives#left-bound-adjectives), the way a noun does. Its `/w/` and its `/b/` come along as usual.

> `glolamam yalazun.`
>
> [gl-cherished | y-Alazun]
>
> "Dear Alazun!"

> `glugomam yuburol.`
>
> [gl-delight | y-birthday]
>
> "Happy birthday!"

A reaction can also take a [degree word](left-bound.md#degree-w) right before it, to say how strongly it holds. A call takes none: the person you call is not more or less called.

> `welazem yemem.`
>
> [w-very | y-amazement]
>
> "Truly amazing!"

The degree word grades the reaction itself. A tone mark is how you sound saying it, so the two can go together.

To say who or what you react to (*hooray for Azozon*), add a [hook](/hooks#extra-noun) and `/b/` after the reaction: `yahumal ol bazozon.` A bare `/b/` there would start the body as the person who receives.

### Speech manner (*frankly* / *to be clear*) {#speech-manner}

English *frankly* and *to be clear* say in words how you are speaking. A tone mark cannot do that, because it changes only your voice. Instead, use a stance word (`/th/`, [clause](/clause#stance-th)) built on an ordinary root. Like any stance word, it may sit anywhere in the sentence; the start is usual.

<!-- cheat-sheet: talking -->
| Agebon | English | Root |
|--------|---------|------|
| **`thagebem`** | *to be clear* / *to put it plainly* | clarity |
| **`thavewom`** | *frankly* / *honestly* / *to tell the truth* | revelation |

> `thagebem zazozon vowurol.`
>
> th-clarity | z-Azozon | v-walk
>
> "To be clear, Azozon walks."

> `thavewom zalazun vezuvel.`
>
> th-revelation | z-Alazun | v-sleep
>
> "Honestly, Alazun is sleeping."

**Compare with:** the stance word describes your way of saying the claim, not the action. `/h/` on the same root (`hagebem`) would describe how the action itself is done.

### Number as interjection {#number-as-interjection}

A number word in `/y/` is a call-out (*Three more!*, a score). It can sit with the other opening `/y/` words, before the act word, or stand alone as its own turn.
