# Yes, no, and not that {#yes-no-and-not-that}

### Polar particle: fuller inventory

Beginner already used *yes* / *true* (**`yaol`**), *no* / *false* (**`yuol`**), and *sure* (**`yael`**). The rest of the map is *reject this option*, *reject the question itself*, and *decline to answer*. The first vowel names the family: **`u`** not, **`a`** add, **`o`** pass. The second vowel says what you are answering: **`o`** the claim or the order, **`e`** this option, **`a`** the question's own premise. You can also hear the family: the three undo answers [fall](phonotactics.md#phonotactics) and the other three rise.

| Ageben | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| **`ao`** (`yaol`) | the claim matches, or *got it*; comply with an order | *true* · *will do* | **a** ≈ add + **o** ≈ command (judged in line) |
| **`uo`** (`yuol`) | flip / mismatch; refuse an order | *false* · *I won't* | **u** ≈ not + **o** ≈ command (judged out of line) |
| **`ae`** (`yael`) | take up an offered option | *sure* | **a** ≈ add + **e** ≈ question (this option) |
| **`ue`** (`yuel`) | reject this option | *not that* | **u** ≈ not + **e** ≈ question |
| **`ua`** (`yual`) | reject the question's premise | *neither: the question doesn't apply* | **u** ≈ not + **a** ≈ add (undo what the question added) |
| **`oe`** (`yoel`) | decline to answer | *I won't answer* | **o** ≈ rank + **e** ≈ choice (pass to the next one in line) |

**`yual`** answers a question that assumes something false. *Have you stopped lying?* has no honest *yes* or *no* if you never lied. **`yual.`** says so in one word, without accepting the frame. It judges the question, not the speaker's willingness: for a refusal to answer, use **`yoel`** (below).

> `yel zazozon vowurol ul bohehal. yual.`
>
> y-question | z-Azozon | v-walk | [from | b-house] . y-reject-frame
>
> "Did Azozon walk away from the house?" "The question doesn't apply." (Azozon was never at the house)

#### Three endings {#polar-endings}

Each stance word takes one of three endings, on the same settled-to-passing scale as [time horizon](/sakes#settled-to-passing). **-l** holds the stance firmly. **-m** is the soft twin. **-r** marks a stance you hold only *for now*: provisional, and you expect to revisit it.

<!-- cheat-sheet: talking -->
| | **-l** firm | **-m** soft | **-r** for now |
|--|-------------|-------------|--------------------|
| **`ao`** | `yaol` *true* | `yaom` *I think so* | `yaor` *true, for now* / *I'll take that on board for now* |
| **`uo`** | `yuol` *false* | `yuom` *I don't think so* | `yuor` *not so, for now* (I'm holding off, and may reconsider) |
| **`ae`** | `yael` *sure* | `yaem` *okay* | `yaer` *fine, for now* / *let's try it* |
| **`ue`** | `yuel` *not that* | `yuem` *nah* | `yuer` *not that, for now* / *not right now* |
| **`ua`** | `yual` *the question doesn't apply* | `yuam` *it's more complicated than that* | `yuar` *it doesn't apply, for now* (the premise fails at the moment) |
| **`oe`** | `yoel` *I won't answer* | `yoem` *I'd rather not say* | `yoer` *pass, for now* |

**`yaor`** takes a claim on board without committing to it. English *oh, I see* often sounds like agreement; **`yaor`** says only that you hold the claim for now. You can restate the claim after the stance word:

> `yaor zazozon vowurol.`
>
> y-yes-for-now | z-Azozon | v-walk
>
> "All right, so Azozon walks, for now."

**`yuar`** says the question's premise fails at the moment, though it may hold later (cue: a door that is not there yet). **`yuor`** holds off on the claim; **`yuar`** holds off on what the question took for granted.

> `yel zazozon vowurol ul bohehal. yuar.`
>
> y-question | z-Azozon | v-walk | [from | b-house] . y-reject-frame-for-now
>
> "Is Azozon walking away from the house?" "The question doesn't apply, for now." (Azozon isn't at the house yet)

**`yoel`**, **`yoem`** and **`yoer`** decline to answer without saying *no* and without judging the question. Nobody should hear them as a denial or as a rejected frame.

> `yel zalazun vezuvel. yoer.`
>
> y-question | z-Alazun | v-sleep . y-decline-for-now
>
> "Is Alazun asleep?" "I'll pass for now."

#### Answering a command or request {#answer-command}

A command (**`yol`**) or request (**`yom`**) is not a claim, so *true* and *false* do not fit it. The same stance words answer it, but now they say whether you will do what was asked. The **o** in **`yaol`** / **`yuol`** is the command vowel of **`yol`** itself.

| Stance | **-l** firm | **-m** soft | **-r** for now |
|--------|-------------|-------------|----------------|
| **`ao`** accept the order | `yaol` *will do* | `yaom` *okay, I'll try* | `yaor` *will do, for now* |
| **`uo`** refuse the order | `yuol` *I won't* | `yuom` *I'd rather not* | `yuor` *I won't, for now* |
| **`ae`** take it up as an offer | `yael` *sure, I'll take that on* | `yaem` *okay, fine* | `yaer` *fine, for now* |
| **`ue`** reject this option | `yuel` *not that* | `yuem` *nah* | `yuer` *not that, for now* |
| **`ua`** reject the premise | `yual` *that can't be done* | `yuam` *it's more complicated than that* | `yuar` *it doesn't apply, for now* |
| **`oe`** decline to answer | `yoel` *I won't say* | `yoem` *I'd rather not say* | `yoer` *pass, for now* |

After a flat command, `yaol` / `yuol` are the plain comply / refuse. After a request (**`yom`**), the **e** words fit better, because you are choosing whether to take it on.

After [two stacked act words](speech-acts.md#stacked-acts), answer the first one, the act the speaker really does. A request that sounds like a question (`yol yel …`) takes **`yael`** / **`yuel`**, not a *yes* or *no* about whether you could.

> `yol vowurol zawan. yaol.`
>
> y-command | v-walk | z-listener . y-yes
>
> "Walk." "Will do."

> `yom vowurol zawan. yuer.`
>
> y-request | v-walk | z-listener . y-refuse-option-for-now
>
> "Please walk." "Not right now."

For a stronger stance, use the [tone mark](tone-marks.md#tone-marks) **`!!`**, not a different word. **`!!yael`** is *Absolutely!* / *Yes, please!*, and **`!!yuel`** is *Hell no!*

> `!!yael.`
>
> !!y-sure
>
> "Absolutely!"

> `!!yuel.`
>
> !!y-refuse-option
>
> "Hell no!"

| Ask flavor | Firm (**-l**) | Soft (**-m**) |
|------------|----------------|----------------|
| *Is it so?* / confirm the claim | **`yaol`**, deny with **`yuol`** | **`yaom`**, deny with **`yuom`** |
| *Want this?* / offer | **`yael`**, reject-this with **`yuel`** | **`yaem`**, reject-this with **`yuem`** |
| Command (**`yol`**) | **`yaol`**, refuse with **`yuol`** | **`yaom`**, refuse with **`yuom`** |
| Loaded question | **`yual`** | **`yuam`** |
| Declined question | **`yoel`** | **`yoem`** |

A stance word can stand alone (`yaol.`), sit before a body (`yaol zazozon vowurol.`), or come as a confirming check-back in the next turn after `.`. After a stance word plus a body, continue with **`/x/`**; write a second polar word only in a new turn. When you think aloud on your own, **`yuol`** can mark a correction, **`yael`** the next step, and **`yaol`** a point you settle.

**Compare with:** *not* inside the clause uses **`zul`** / **`vul`**. Polar **`yuol`** / **`yuel`** answer a *yes* / *no* or an offer.

### Check-backs: asking for an answer {#check-backs}

Beginner closed a claim with the check-back **`yel yaol.`** (*…, right?*). Every polar word works as a check-back the same way: after your sentence, write **`yel`** and the polar word as a turn of their own. The check-back asks the listener for that answer. Naming an answer makes it easy to give, so pick the one you want to make easy.

After a request or a suggestion, **`yel yael.`** asks *okay?*: will they take it up? **`yel yuel.`** asks *or would you rather not?*, so they can turn it down without having to push back. After a claim, **`yel yuol.`** asks *or am I wrong?*: it invites a correction, where **`yel yaol.`** leans toward agreement.

> `yom vowurol zayan. yel yael.`
>
> y-request | v-walk | z-interlocutors . y-question | y-sure
>
> "Let's walk, okay?"

> `yom vowurol zawan. yel yuel.`
>
> y-request | v-walk | z-listener . y-question | y-refuse-option
>
> "Please walk, or would you rather not?"

> `zazozon vowurol. yel yuol.`
>
> z-Azozon | v-walk . y-question | y-no
>
> "Azozon walked, or am I wrong?"

| Ageben | Use | English |
<!-- cheat-sheet: talking -->
|--------|-----|---------|
| `yel yaol.` | ask them to confirm a claim | *…, right?* |
| `yel yuol.` | invite a correction | *…, or am I wrong?* |
| `yel yael.` | ask them to take up a request or an offer | *…, okay?* / *…, deal?* |
| `yel yuel.` | offer them the refusal | *…, or would you rather not?* |
| `yel yaor.` | ask them to accept it for now | *…, can we go with that for now?* |
| `yel yoem.` | after a question, offer them a pass | *…, or would you rather not say?* |

Soft **`yem`** asks more gently (`yem yael.` *…, if that's okay?*), and the other polar words and endings make check-backs the same way.

After [two stacked act words](speech-acts.md#stacked-acts), a check-back asks about the first one, the real act. So *okay?* after a command that sounds gentle shows that the gentle sound was only a surface:

> `yol yem vowurol zawan. yel yael.`
>
> y-command | y-soft-question | v-walk | z-listener . y-question | y-sure
>
> "Would you maybe walk? Okay?" (it is an order)

**Compare with:** `yom vowurol zawan. yel yael.` is a real request, which the listener is free to turn down.

**Compare with:** the answer itself is the polar word alone (`yael.` *sure*). The check-back puts **`yel`** first, so it asks for that answer instead of giving it.

### Confirming a negative {#confirming-a-negative}

When the question already contains *not* (*Didn’t Azozon run?*), *yes* and *no* still say whether **that claim** matches. **`yaol`** confirms the denial (*true: they didn’t*). **`yuol`** says the denial is false (*they did*). Restate the body after the stance word to make the answer unmistakable. Offer words (**`yael`** / **`yuel`**) still answer *take this?*, not true/false.

> `yel zazozon veruvol vul. yaol.`
>
> y-question | z-Azozon | [v-run | v-not] . y-yes
>
> "Didn’t Azozon run?" "True, Azozon didn’t."

> `yaol veruvol vul.`
>
> y-yes | [v-run | v-not]
>
> "True, they didn’t run."

### Yes/no with single-item / standalone
<a id="yes-no-single-item-standalone"></a>
<a id="under-question"></a>

A *yes/no* question can also check a **named** item: put a single-item join after it instead of a fill-ask blank. Single-item **-l** (or usual **-n**) asks the listener to confirm that this one item alone is meant (*Just Azozon?*, *Has to be Azozon?*). Single-item **-m** offers it (*How about Ahelan?*). **`…ul`** / **`…um`** / **`…un`** confirm or offer a denial (*Not Azozon?*). Standalone **-l** / **-m** check emptiness (*Nothing?*, *Got nothing?*). Fill-ask still uses join **-r**.

> `yel zazozon zal.`
>
> y-question | [z-Azozon | z-and]
>
> "Just Azozon?"

> `yel zahelan zam.`
>
> y-question | [z-Ahelan | z-and.open]
>
> "How about Ahelan?"

**Compare with:** *who* / *which* still uses join **-r** ([fill-ask](/questions#fill-ask-r)).
