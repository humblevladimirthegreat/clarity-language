# Prescription and motive {#prescription-and-motive}

### Motive (`tho`): time horizon
<a id="sake-preference"></a>

English *doing this for …* and *for the sake of …* name a **motive**: why the action is happening, as a description, not a *should*. Write `/th/` on the clause with the sake root, mid-word **`th`**, **`o`**, then an ending. That ending is the **time horizon**, on the same settled-to-passing scale as [met](/sakes#time-horizon-endings-on-met): whether the act serves the sake over time or right away. If you do not know, use **-m**. To say this is the usual reason, add **`hual`** after the motive word (**`…thom`** plus **`hual`**).

> `zuwen vezuyol thawethom.`
>
> z-speaker | v-tell | th-relatedness-motive-any-term
>
> "I talk to feel connected."

The reason is yours. On someone else's act, a motive word still names what the act serves **for you**: `zazozon vezuyol thawethom` is *Azozon talks, and to me it is about feeling connected*. It does not say why Azozon talks; their reasons are in their mind, and Agebon has no way to say a reason belongs to whoever is acting.

**Not the same job as:** prescription **`the`**, which says the act *ought* to serve this sake. **`tho`** describes why the act is happening: *doing it for this sake*.

On `/ɡ/`, the same stance gives **your** noun’s purpose (*my gift for relatedness*).

**For other English *for*, use:**

- an intended **event** (*walks so that Alazun sits*): [so that](/dependents#so-that) (**`hovam`**), not a sake
- the other side of a swap (*a book for a hammer*): [exchange](/relations#exchange) (`heham`)
- acting for someone (*tells on behalf of Alazun*): [proxy](/relations#proxy) (`hanem`)

A reason held **to keep a cost off** is a *so that … not* dependent ([so that](/dependents#so-that), **`hovam blurl`**), as with prescription.

<!-- cheat-sheet: why-allowed -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| `…thol` | does this for the sake over time | *lasting* | **-l** ≈ lasting |
| `…thom` | does this for the sake; time horizon unstated (soft default if unknown) | *for* | **-m** ≈ open |
| `…thor` | does this for the sake right away | *immediate* | **-r** ≈ right now |

Neither end is better; a good day needs both, and doing something for a payoff right now is often exactly what is needed. When the **situation** is what pulls the reason, say so with the [because pole](/causation) and the situation as `/b/`.

### Which ending table?

Beginner already used *serves* and *detracts from*. Intermediate adds *ought this act for* and *doing for*. Pick the ending table that matches the stance vowel.

<!-- cheat-sheet: why-allowed -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| **`tha`** | [time horizon](/sakes#time-horizon-endings-on-met) endings | *serves* / *met* | **a** ≈ add |
| **`the`** | [invited / offered / trial](/ought-for-a-sake#sake-force) endings | *ought this act for this sake* | **e** ≈ order (instruct / ought) |
| **`tho`** | [time horizon](#sake-preference) endings | *doing for this sake* | **o** ≈ one (one sake as the reason) |
| **`thu`** | [changeability](/sakes#sake-changeability) endings | *detracts from* / *unmet* | **u** ≈ undo |

### Attachment sites

Beginner already attached `/ɡ/` after a noun and `/th/` on the clause for met and unmet. The rest of the map:

| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| `g…tha…` after a noun | praise: **your** noun serves the sake | *my gift (serves relatedness)* (`gawetha…`) |
| `g…thu…` after a noun | criticism: **your** noun detracts from the sake | *my book (detracts from competence)* (`guwathu…`) |
| `g…tho…` after a noun | **your** noun’s purpose is this sake | *my gift for relatedness* (`gawetho…`) |
| `/w/` sake + `goyom` | the same stances on a noun that is **not** yours | *the gathering (detracts from autonomy)* (`wunathu… goyom`) |
| `/w/` sake + another adjective | the stance on that one property | *pleasantly hot* (`wodetham gohamul`) |
| `gl-` + sake word | the same adjective before the **belonging** | `glawethal zewal` |
| `th…tha…` on the clause | the event serves the sake | *tells: competence is met* (`thuwatha…`) |
| `th…the…` on the clause | prescription: the act ought to serve the sake | *ought … for this sake* (`thawethe…`); ending = [invited / offered / trial](/ought-for-a-sake#sake-force) |
| `th…tho…` on the clause | motive | *I talk (to feel connected)* (`thawetho…`) |
| `/w/` before a sake `/ɡ/` | extra detail on that adjective | *very relatedness-serving* |

Prescription **`the`** goes on `/th/` only: it is about an act, and a noun is not an act. For what a noun is **for**, use **`tho`**. On **`the`**, use **-l** for an invitation, **-m** for an offer, and **-r** for a trial. On **`thu`** and **`tho`**, prefer **-m** when you cannot tell which ending fits.

For several sakes, write several `/ɡ/` or `/th/` words (`guwathal gawethal`), one sake each. The noun or verb the sake word describes keeps its own ending, which says whether it is concrete or abstract.

A `/w/` word before a sake word on `/ɡ/` grades that adjective. A `/w/` sake word immediately before **`goyom`** is the sake itself.

> `zuwen vezuyol thuwathal thawethom.`
>
> z-speaker | v-tell | th-competence-met-lasting | th-relatedness-motive-any-term
>
> "I talk to feel connected, and it builds my confidence for the long run."

### Whose stake {#whose-stake}

A sake word with no `/b/` is about **your** sake. To say it is someone else's, put that person in a `bl-` word right after the sake word. This works on every stance and every placement: met, unmet and motive on a noun, and met, unmet, prescription and motive on the clause.

This holds on someone else's act too. `zazozon vezuyol thawetham` is *Azozon talks, and that helps me feel connected*: what the act serves is your stake, as you see it. A `/b/` names whose stake the act serves, not whose reason it is.

> `zazozon vezuyol thawethom blalazun.`
>
> z-Azozon | v-tell | [th-relatedness-motive-any-term | bl-Alazun]
>
> "Azozon talks for Alazun's sake." (Alazun's relatedness)

> `zalazun vabuyol thawethem blazozon.`
>
> z-Alazun | v-bow | [th-relatedness-ought-offered | bl-Azozon]
>
> "Alazun ought to bow, for Azozon's relatedness." (unasked advice)

> `zewal gawethal blalazun.`
>
> z-present | [g-relatedness-met-lasting | bl-Alazun]
>
> "My gift serves Alazun's relatedness in the long term."

> `zabowam wawethul goyom blalazun.`
>
> z-gathering | [[w-relatedness-unmet-irreversible | g-stimulus] | bl-Alazun]
>
> "The gathering does lasting harm to Alazun's relatedness."

The `/b/` names whose **stake** it is, not who **owns** the noun. On `/ɡ/` the noun is still yours: `zewal gawethal blalazun` is *my gift, good for Alazun*. Someone else's thing is `gegunum` plus `/b/`, or **`goyom`**.

**Compare with:** the *for* hook (`ol balazun`) names who the act is for without naming which sake.

### Contrary to a stance (`uom`) {#contrary-to-stance}

English *contrary to the plan* says that the event happens even though a plan says otherwise. Put the hook **`uom`** *contrary to* right before a `/th/` stance word or an [intention adverb](/clause#intention-adverbs). That word then says what the event goes against, and it no longer applies to the claim. `zazozon vowurol thagom.` says you plan for Azozon to walk; `zazozon vowurol uom thagom.` says Azozon walks anyway. (cue: **ue** ≈ undo aimed at; **-m** the opposing frame, which the stance spells out)

> `zazozon vowurol uom thagom.`
>
> z-Azozon | v-walk | [contrary-to | th-plan-itinerary]
>
> "Azozon walks, contrary to my plan."

The stance keeps its endings. A [plan](/intention#plan) after **`uom`** is your own, like any stance.

Only the stance right after **`uom`** is what the event goes against. A stance anywhere else in the clause still applies to the claim:

> `zazozon vowurol uom thagom thomam.`
>
> z-Azozon | v-walk | [contrary-to | th-plan-itinerary] | th-MAY
>
> "Azozon may be walking, contrary to my plan."

The word after **`uom`** has to say something the event can go against: a plan.

| After **`uom`** | English |
|-----------------|---------|
| plan **`thagor`** / **`thagom`** / **`thagol`** | *contrary to my plan* |

Sake words cannot follow **`uom`**: to say an act goes against someone's interest, put an [unmet](/sakes#sake-changeability) sake word on the clause (`zazozon vowurol thudathum blalazun.` *Azozon's walking harms Alazun's interests*).

**Compare with:** *despite the rain* is **`uom`** or **`hezem`** with a noun in `/b/` (`zazozon vowurol uom berulal.`). *Although Alazun said no* as a full sentence is **`hezem blarl`** plus that sentence. The sentence after **`blarl`** needs an event or a thing, so a stance word alone cannot fill it; use **`uom`** with the stance instead.

### Enough and too (sake bars) {#sake-bars}

English *enough* and *too much* compare an amount with **what a need requires**. Write a met sake word on `/th/` as the [bar](comparative-arity.md#bars) right before a rank join: it stands for what that sake needs, and the rank join compares the amount with it. The equal-rank join **`zoel`** is *enough*. Rank **`zol`** is *too much*, and **`zuol`** is *not enough*. Use the unspecified sake **`uda`** when you do not name which sake.

> `zedudal thudatham zoel gral.`
>
> [z-tea | th-sake-met-any-term | z-equal-rank | g-amount]
>
> "There is enough tea."

> `zedudal thudatham zol gral.`
>
> [z-tea | th-sake-met-any-term | z-rank/more | g-amount]
>
> "There is too much tea."

> `zedudal thudatham zuol gral.`
>
> [z-tea | th-sake-met-any-term | z-rank/less | g-amount]
>
> "There is not enough tea."

The bar keeps its [time-horizon ending](/sakes#time-horizon-endings-on-met), which now says **how long the payoff has to last**: **`thudathal`** is *enough to last*, and **`thudathar`** is *enough for now*.

> `zedudal thudathar zoel gral.`
>
> [z-tea | th-sake-met-immediate | z-equal-rank | g-amount]
>
> "There is enough tea for now."

Name a sake to say which need sets the limit. With a quality as the scale, the same pattern gives *too ADJ* and *ADJ enough*. To say **whose** stake it is, put that person in a `bl-` word right after the bar.

> `zedudal thobatham zol gohamul.`
>
> [z-tea | th-physical-met-any-term | z-rank/more | g-hot]
>
> "The tea is too hot to be safe."

> `zedudal thobatham blalazun zol gohamul.`
>
> [z-tea | [th-physical-met-any-term | bl-Alazun] | z-rank/more | g-hot]
>
> "The tea is too hot for Alazun's health."

On the [time scale](comparative-arity.md#time-scale), a sake bar is *too late* and *too early*:

> `zazozon thudatham zuol bral vevorol.`
>
> [z-Azozon | th-sake-met-any-term | z-rank/less | b-later] | v-arrival
>
> "Azozon arrives too early."

| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| met sake bar + **`zoel`** | reaches what the sake needs | *enough* / *ADJ enough* |
| met sake bar + **`zol`** | past what the sake needs | *too much* / *too ADJ* / *too late* |
| met sake bar + **`zuol`** | short of what the sake needs | *not enough* / *too early* |
| bar ending **-l** / **-m** / **-r** | how long the payoff has to last | *enough to last* / *enough* / *enough for now* |
| `bl-` word right after the bar | whose stake | *too hot for Alazun* |

**Compare with:** the same met word after the fence is about the whole claim: `zedudal zol gohamul thobatham` is *the tea is the hottest, and that serves my health*.
