# Topic {#discourse-topic}

English *now, about Azozon…*, *speaking of dogs…*, and *so, back to Azozon…* say what the talk is about from here on, and every later *he* or *it* follows. Ageben makes that overt as a **topic**. Pointing back at someone already named, without setting what the talk is about, is in [pronouns](/pronouns).

### Topic {#topic}

A topic is set only by an `/x/` word at the start of a sentence: `/x/`, then the thing the talk is about, then its ending. Nothing else sets one. Being named first, being the subject, and [*as for*](/clause#adverbs-h) do not, so a listener or a tool can tell at every point what the topic is.

<!-- cheat-sheet: people-pointing -->
| Ageben | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| `xazozon` | introduce a person | *now, about Azozon* |
| `xodozel` | introduce a kind | *now, about a dog* |
| `xazozor` | return to an earlier topic: whole stem + **-r** | *back to Azozon* |
| `xevewom` / `xavedam` | clear the topic ([linkers](sentence-linkers.md#sentence-linkers)) | *next* / *by the way* |

1. **No topic at the start.** A conversation opens with none.
2. **Introduce.** An `/x/` word on **-l**, **-m**, or **-n** that is not one of the six sentence linkers makes its noun the topic. The noun keeps its ordinary ending, so the topic need not be a person or a name (`xodozel` *now, about a dog*). Any [compound](/compounds) works the same way: a hyphenated name (`xazozoxalazun`), a noun pair (`xuwedaxubudal` *now, about peanut butter*), or a [role compound](/roles#role-compounds) (`xwuzeduyol` *now, about people who teach*; `xwuzeduyor` returns to it). It takes the same adjectives and hooks as any noun (`xodozel om bazozon` *now, about Azozon's dog*), and it may stand alone as a whole sentence to announce a topic before saying anything about it.
3. **Return.** `/x/` + the whole stem of an earlier word + **-r** makes that thing the topic again.
4. **Clear.** `xevewom` and `xavedam` each open a new frame. After either there is no topic until the next introduce or return.
5. **Persist.** The topic holds until rule 2, 3, or 4 changes it. Clause joins, other linkers, and agenda numbering leave it alone. A goodbye ends the conversation and clears it.
6. **Where.** A topic word opens a sentence in your own talk, or in a quote. It never follows a clause join and never sits inside a dependent or an aside.

> `xazozon.`
>
> x-Azozon
>
> "Now, about Azozon."

`/x/` takes the speaker, listener, interlocutors, or people in general like any noun: `xuwen` *now, about me*, `xawan` *now, about you*, `xayan` *now, about the two of us*, `xeyen` *now, about people in general*. A nonspecific `umen` cannot be a topic, because a topic is someone in particular. The topic is fixed to that person (or the address set) at the moment it is set, so after `xawan`, both speakers say `zalen` for the same person.

**Every return is explicit.** There is one topic, not a stack. Coming back from a side topic, or from a new frame, means naming the old topic again with `xazozor`.

A topic-setting `/x/` word needs no voice of its own: the `/x/` word itself tells the listener that the subject changed. The speech move is unchanged, so the listener hears a new subject, not a new act ([periods](sentence-linkers.md#orthography-and-prosody-periods)).

### The topic pronoun {#topic-pronoun}

The topic is referred to with one pronoun: **`ale`** *star* with **-n**, in `/z/`, `/d/`, or `/b/`. It stays the same in every role: `zalen` is the topic as subject, `dalen` as object, `balen` as extra noun. (cue: ⭐ the star of the talk, what everyone is looking at)

> `xazozon zalen dalazun vayebel. zalazur vezuyol balen. zalen veruvol.`
>
> x-Azozon | z-TOPIC | d-Alazun | v-see . z-←Alazun | v-tell | b-TOPIC . z-TOPIC | v-run
>
> "Now, about Azozon: Azozon sees Alazun. Alazun tells Azozon. Azozon runs."

A thing works as a topic the same way:

> `xodozel zazozon dalen vayebel. zalen veruvol. zazozor vehohel.`
>
> x-dog | z-Azozon | d-TOPIC | v-see . z-TOPIC | v-run . z-←Azozon | v-sit
>
> "Now, about a dog: Azozon sees it. It runs. Azozon sits."

With Azozon as the topic, *Azozon sees themself* is `xazozon zalen vayebel dalen.` (`dwuzor` still works), and *Azozon's dog walks* is `xazozon zodozel om balen vowurol.`: *the topic's* is the same in every role.

A dropped subject is never read as the topic. `xazozon dalazun vayebel.` leaves out who sees; it does not say Azozon sees. Write `zalen` whenever the topic is the doer (and `dalen` or `balen` in the other roles).

The topic pronoun is the topic itself, so it is never a topic word or a resume: `xalen`, `xaler` and `zaler` are not sentences. To start a new stretch on the same topic, name it again with `xazozor`.

A topic pronoun with no topic set is not a sentence. On **-l** and **-m** the star root keeps its ordinary senses: `zalel` *a star*, `zalem` *visibility*. **-x** adds associates: `zalenx` *the topic and associates*.

*As for* frames one sentence and leaves the topic alone:

> `xazozon zalen vowurol. hehugom blalazun zodozel veruvol. zalen vehohel.`
>
> x-Azozon | z-TOPIC | v-walk . [h-as-for | bl-Alazun] | z-dog | v-run . z-TOPIC | v-sit
>
> "Now, about Azozon: Azozon walks. As for Alazun, a dog runs. Azozon sits."

A side topic, then an explicit return:

> `xazozon zalen vowurol. xavedam zodozel veruvol. xazozor zalen vehohel.`
>
> x-Azozon | z-TOPIC | v-walk . x-by-the-way | z-dog | v-run . x-←Azozon | z-TOPIC | v-sit
>
> "Now, about Azozon: Azozon walks. By the way, a dog runs. Back to Azozon: Azozon sits."

Without `xazozor`, the last sentence would have no topic and `zalen` would not be a sentence.

### What a topic change resets {#topic-resets}

Introduce, return, and clear each start a new **topic stretch**.

| State | Resets? | Why |
|-------|---------|-----|
| [Role pointer](/pronouns#role-pointers) anchors | yes | `zwuzer` *the latest doer* and `zwuzar` *the other one* never reach into an earlier stretch. Someone from before is picked up by name or whole-stem **-r**. |
| Whole-stem **-r** | no | It is the explicit way back (`xazozor`), so it reaches across stretches. |
| [Tags](tag-pronouns.md#tag-pronouns) | no | A tag is assigned on purpose, so distance does not blur it, and placeholder reasoning often runs through a side topic. |
| Agenda numbering (`x#N`, outline depth) | no | Agenda items sit above topics; one item may hold several. |
| Speech move, line of talk | no | They track the move and the line of talk, not people. |

`xazozor` while Azozon is already the topic is still a topic change: it starts a new stretch with the same topic. That lets you clear the role pointers' anchors (*so, Azozon again*) without leaving the topic.

### Return versus introduce {#topic-return}

Both set the topic. The difference is the ordinary difference between **-r** and a first mention:

<!-- cheat-sheet: people-pointing -->
| Ageben | Topic | English |
|--------|-------|---------|
| `xazozon` | Azozon | *now, about Azozon* (a name picks out the same person either way) |
| `xazozor` | Azozon, the one mentioned before | *back to Azozon* |
| `xodozel` | **a** dog, new to the talk | *now, about a dog* |
| `xodozer` | **the** dog from before | *back to the dog* |

For names, the two pick the same person: choose by what the listener should hear, something new or a way back. For kinds, only the return reaches the earlier thing. A return that resumes a [sentence linker](sentence-linkers.md#sentence-linkers) (`xahor` after `xahom`) is only that linker again, and sets no topic.

A return makes no claim by itself, but it does set the topic, so leaving it out changes who `zalen` is, or leaves `zalen` with no topic, which is not a sentence.

> `zazozon vowurol. zalazun veruvol. xazozor zalen vehohel.`
>
> z-Azozon | v-walk . z-Alazun | v-run . x-←Azozon | z-TOPIC | v-sit
>
> "Azozon walks. Alazun runs. Back to Azozon: Azozon sits."

### Groups as the topic {#topic-groups}

**-x** goes on a topic word like on any noun: `xazozonx` makes *Azozon and associates* the topic, `xodozelx` makes *some dogs* the topic, and `xazozorx` returns to the group. `zalen` then means the group. **-x** on the pronoun (`zalenx`) still adds associates to whatever the topic is.

> `xodozelx zazozon dalen vayebel.`
>
> x-dog-x | z-Azozon | d-TOPIC | v-see
>
> "Now, about some dogs: Azozon sees them."

### Topic words and dependents {#topic-dependents}

A [dependent](/dependents#dependent-clauses) is the sentence after a stand-in (`darl`, `derl`, `dorl`, `durl`, `barl`), and it belongs to the outer sentence. A topic word cannot sit inside one: a topic change is something you yourself do to the conversation, but the content of a dependent is not something you assert yourself. Change the topic before the outer sentence, or frame one clause with *as for*:

| Want | Use |
|------|-----|
| Content about X, in one sentence | `hehugom` + `/b/` *as for* inside the dependent: a frame for that clause only, no reset |
| Change the topic, then report | the `/x/` word first: `xalazun zazozon vezuyol darl zalen vowurol.` *Now, about Alazun: Azozon says that Alazun walks.* |
| Report someone's own topic change | quote their wording |

A quote is someone else's talk, so it keeps its own topic and its own tags, and neither carries over into yours. An aside uses your stretch and never changes the topic. For a real side topic, end the sentence and use `xavedam`.

Reading the topic from inside a dependent is fine: `zalen`, tags, and role pointers inside the content resolve to your current stretch. *Azozon says that the main one runs* means your topic, not Azozon's. A clause join is not a dependent, but it keeps the clauses in one written sentence, so a topic word cannot follow one either: change the topic after a period.

### Asking about the topic {#topic-question}

To ask what the talk is about, ask *which topic?*: the fill-ask blank `zar`, then `gehugom` *topic*. The natural answer is a lone `/x/` introduce or return, which sets the topic for both speakers.

> `yel zar gehugom.`
>
> y-question | [z-wh | g-topic]
>
> "Which topic?" / "What are we talking about?"

**Compare with:** English *topic* covers several jobs, and Ageben keeps them apart. The discourse topic is `/x/`. A sentence frame is `hehugom` *as for*. `&` contrasts one phrase ([tone marks](tone-marks.md#tone-marks)). `zal` / `zom` / `zol` rank among others. Only `/x/` sets the topic.
