# Join across roles {#join-across-roles}

The join vowels you use on noun lists also join other things: verb phrases under `/v/` (*walks and runs*), whole clauses under `/x/` (*Uzozen walks, then Olazun runs*), and stance words under `/th/` (*not because of the rain*). With ending **-n** under `/v/`, `/ɡ/`, or `/h/`, a join word becomes a verb, adjective, or adverb that names a list move (*includes*, *without*). Phrase fences and their set, rank, arity, and scope patterns are in [joins](/joins).
### Verb-phrase and clause forms {#vp-clause-forms}
<a id="vowels--endings"></a>
<a id="ending-senses-clause-joins"></a>

[Joins](/joins) already used `vam` and `xam`. The same vowels and **-l** / **-m** / **-r** endings work under `/v/`, `/x/`, and stance `/th/`. Clause **-n** forms are in [Sequence](/clause-sequence#sequence); `/v/` **-n** forms are join-act verbs.

| | **-l** | **-m** | **-r** |
|---|--------|--------|--------|
| **a** | `val` / `xal` / `thal` | `vam` / `xam` / `tham` | `var` / `xar` / `thar` |
| **o** | `vol` / `xol` / `thol` | `vom` / `xom` / `thom` | `vor` / `xor` / `thor` |
| **u** | `vul` / `xul` / `thul` | `vum` / `xum` / `thum` | `vur` / `xur` / `thur` |
| **ao** | `vaol` / `xaol` / `thaol` | `vaom` / `xaom` / `thaom` | none |
| **ua** | `vual` / `xual` / `thual` | `vuam` / `xuam` / `thuam` | none |
| **uo** | `vuol` / `xuol` / `thuol` | `vuom` / `xuom` / `thuom` | none |
| **e** | `vel` / `xel` / `thel` | `vem` / `xem` / `them` | `ver` / `xer` / `ther` |
| **ae** | `vael` / `xael` / `thael` | `vaem` / `xaem` / `thaem` | none |
| **oe** | `voel` / `xoel` / `thoel` | `voem` / `xoem` / `thoem` | none |
| **ue** | `vuel` / `xuel` / `thuel` | `vuem` / `xuem` / `thuem` | none |

Stacked vowels take no **-r** under `/v/` or `/x/` (the *none* cells); only a stance join has stacked **-r**, as a fill-ask.

Each item in a verb-phrase join is a verb plus the words that go with it, and an item ends at its verb. One rule places every word: **a word before a verb belongs to that verb.** An `/h/`, `/d/`, or `/b/` word, or a hook with its `/b/`, between two joined verbs belongs to the verb after it. In `vowurel hedurem varuvel val`, *hastily* is on *run*, not *walk*. In the same way, an object or a `/b/` party between two verbs goes only with the second verb:

> `zuzozen vowurel dahelan vawebol val.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [v-walk | [d-Ahelan | v-see] | v-and]
>
> "Uzozen walks and sees Ahelan."

> `zuzozen vowurel bahelan vezuyel val.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [v-walk | [b-Ahelan | v-tell] | v-and]
>
> "Uzozen walks and talks to Ahelan."

The first item starts at the subject: the words between the subject and the first verb belong to the first verb. With no subject in the clause, the first item reaches back to the start of the clause.

> `zuzozen duredal vugudel vuhohol val.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [[d-popcorn | v-cooking] | v-sit | v-and]
>
> "Uzozen cooked popcorn and sat."

> `zuzozen hedurem vugudel varuvel val.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [[h-haste | v-cooking] | v-run | v-and]
>
> "Uzozen cooks hastily and runs."

Each verb takes its own recipient the same way, so one clause can name two:

> `zolazun bahelan vezuyel buzozen varal val.`
>
> z-Olazun | [[b-Ahelan | v-tell] | [b-Uzozen | v-pay] | v-and]
>
> "Olazun talks to Ahelan and pays Uzozen."

A hook with its `/b/` is an item word too:

> `zuzozen vowurel al behaham vezuvol val.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [v-walk | [[in | b-locality] | v-sleep] | v-and]
>
> "Uzozen walks, and sleeps in the village."

Words that every verb shares go in one of two places. **After the join**, a `/d/` object, a recipient `/b/`, or an `/h/` adverb is SHARED: every verb takes it. The join counts as *the verb*, so a recipient right after it is in its [usual place](/speech-moves#act-word-order).

> `zuzozen vugudel vagedal val duredal.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [v-cooking | v-eat | v-and | d-popcorn]
>
> "Uzozen cooks and eats popcorn."

> `zuzozen vowurel varuvel val huhunem.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [v-walk | v-run | v-and | h-intensity]
>
> "Uzozen walks and runs, both intensely."

> `zolazun vezuyel varal val buzozen huhunem.`
>
> z-Olazun | [v-tell | v-pay | v-and | b-Uzozen | h-intensity]
>
> "Olazun talks to Uzozen and pays them, both intensely."

**Before the subject**, a word covers every verb. That is the place for scene-setting words (`h_15,30 zuzozen vowurel varuvel val` *at 15:30 Uzozen walks and runs*), and for a shared object when you want the verbs last. English works the same way: a fronted object is shared, and *cooked popcorn and ate* is not.

> `duredal zuzozen vugudel vagedal val.`
>
> d-popcorn | z-Uzozen | [v-cooking | v-eat | v-and]
>
> "Popcorn, Uzozen cooked and ate."

A verb that has its own object takes no shared object, and a verb that has its own recipient takes no shared recipient. After `duredal vugudel vagedal val`, a shared object would give *cook* two objects, so it is not a sentence. Put the second object in its own verb's item instead (`duredal vugudel doledol vagedal val`).

A [stance](/clause#stance-th) `/th/` word and an [intention adverb](/clause#intention-adverbs) are never item words, wherever they sit: like any stance word, each is about the whole claim. A `bl-` word right after an `/h/`, `/ɡ/`, or `/th/` word still completes that word.

With no subject, a statement with a shared object starts with its verbs (`vugudel vagedal val duredal`), which is the usual order for a command. Name the subject when the act could be misheard.

A rank join on verb phrases puts the acts in order of priority, so the earlier act is the one preferred: *would rather walk than run*.

> `zuzozen vowurel varuvel vel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [v-walk | v-run | v-rank/more]
>
> "Uzozen would rather walk than run."

Nothing is SHARED after a clause (`/x/`) join; each clause carries its own words.

Sequence **`ae`** keeps its [phrase meaning](rank-joins.md#sequence-ae) in both roles: the first item is where you start. Under `/v/` it is one subject doing steps in order; under `/x/` it fences a list of steps whose order is part of the message (a recipe, directions). Standalone **`xael`** says the steps can go in any order.

> `zuzozen vowurel varuvel vael.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [v-walk | v-run | v-in-order]
>
> "Uzozen walks, then runs."

**Compare with:** clause **`xan`** ([sequence](/clause-sequence#sequence)) tells what happened next, one step at a time. **`xael`** makes the order part of the claim (a recipe, directions): `A xael B xael C` says these steps go in this order. With rank **ue**, **`xuen`** steps backward: `A xuen B` says B happened before A.

A clause join with no clause before it is standalone, like `zal` *nothing*: `xal` means *nothing happened*, and open `xam` adds *as far as I know*.

> `xam.`
>
> x-and.open
>
> "Nothing happened, as far as I know."

All the clauses in an `/x/` join share one speech act. Any `/y/` word goes before the first clause only; the later clauses take the same act and leave `/y/` out.

> `zuzozen vowurel xan zolazun varuvel.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | v-walk | x-and-then | z-Olazun | v-run]
>
> "Uzozen walks, and then Olazun runs."

> `zuzozen vowurel xol zolazun varuvel xal zahelan vezuvol.`
>
> [[z-Uzozen | v-walk | x-or-exactly-one | z-Olazun | v-run] | x-and | z-Ahelan | v-sleep]
>
> "Either Uzozen walks or Olazun runs, and Ahelan sleeps." (only one of the first two)

### Stance joins {#stance-joins}

When English would deny or weigh **how you stand** toward a claim (*not because…*), put a `/th/` join word after the [stance](/clause#stance-th) words. The claim itself is untouched; the join works only on the stance words before it. (cue: same vowels as every other join.)

> `zuzozen vowurel thahum blerulal thul.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [th-because | bl-rain] | th-not
>
> "Uzozen walks, but not because of the rain."

The other join vowels work on stance words the same way they work on a phrase list ([recap](/joins#join-type-vowel-series)). A rank join says which ground matters most: the first stance word is the main one.

> `zuzozen vowurel thahum blerulal thohom blolazun thel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [th-because | bl-rain] | [th-if | bl-Olazun] | th-rank/more
>
> "Uzozen walks mainly because of the rain, and also if Olazun is involved."

| Vowel | After stance words | Example reading |
|---|---|---|
| **ao** | `thaol` open list of grounds | *because of the rain, if Olazun, and maybe more* |
| **ua** | `thual` every ground but these | *on every ground but these two* |
| **uo** | `thuol` any ground but these | *any ground but these two will do* |
| **e** | `thel` ranked, first is the main ground | *mainly because of the rain, also if Olazun* |
| **ae** | `thael` in the order the grounds arose | *first because of the rain, then if Olazun* |
| **oe** | `thoel` equal weight | *the rain and the if-clause count the same* |
| **ue** | `thuel` ranked, first is the weakest | *chiefly if Olazun, a little because of the rain* |

Nothing is SHARED after an `/h/` or `/th/` join. To add a detail that covers the whole list, put a `/w/` word right before the join word.

**Compare with:** *Uzozen does not walk* denies the event, so the join goes on the verb (`vowurel vul`), not on `/th/`.

#### Stance join before `blarl` {#stance-join-before-barl}

When the grounds are a whole clause (*not because Olazun runs*), the pole takes **`blarl`** and the next sentence is the grounds ([poles](/dependents#poles)). That sentence comes last, so the join cannot go after it. Put the join word **between the pole and `blarl`** instead. It closes the stance words before it as usual, and **`blarl`** still belongs to the pole.

> `zuzozen vowurel thahum thul blarl zolazun varuvel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | th-because | th-not | bl-that-clause | z-Olazun | v-run
>
> "Uzozen walks, but not because Olazun runs."

Only one join fits there, and only when the last stance word before it is a pole with no `/b/` yet. The same works for every join vowel and every pole.

#### Standalone stance joins {#standalone-stance-joins}

When English would say how to take a claim without naming any grounds (*at face value*, *no judgment*, *all things considered*), put a `/th/` join with no stance words before it. It works like standalone `zal` *nothing*: the list of stance words is empty, and the join says what that empty stance means. The claim itself is untouched. (cue: same vowels as every other join.)

> `zuzozen vowurel thul.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | th-not
>
> "Uzozen walks. No judgment."

In the table, *reason* means any grounds a stance word can give: a cause, a source, a likelihood, or a value.

| Vowel | **-l** | **-m** | **-r** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **a** | `thal` *at face value* | `tham` *I don't think there's more to it* | `thar` *for reasons I'm not stating* |
| **o** | `thol` *no single reason to pick* | `thom` *I'm not sure which reason it is* | `thor` *for some reason or other* |
| **u** | `thul` *no judgment* | `thum` *not weighing in* | `thur` *not for the reason you'd think* |
| **ao** | `thaol` *take it however you like* | `thaom` *any framing is fine, I think* | question only |
| **ua** | `thual` *all things considered* | `thuam` *all known things considered* | question only |
| **uo** | `thuol` *anything goes* | `thuom` *anything goes, I think* | question only |
| **e** | `thel` *impartially* / *no decisive grounds* | `them` *I don't lean either way* | `ther` *for one main reason, not named* |
| **ae** | `thael` *in no particular order* | `thaem` *not sure where to start* | question only |
| **oe** | `thoel` *equally balanced* | `thoem` *equally balanced, I think* | question only |
| **ue** | `thuel` *no reason to object* | `thuem` *no objection that I know of* | question only |

Outside a question, only the single-vowel **-r** cells are words. Under a question, a standalone **-r** stance join is a [fill-ask](/questions#fill-ask-r) for the grounds. Every vowel takes **-r** there.

> `yol thar zuzozen vowurel.`
>
> y-question | th-why | z-Uzozen | v-walk
>
> "On what grounds do you say Uzozen walks?"

| Word | Question |
|---|---|
| `thar` | *Why do you say so?* / *On what grounds?* |
| `thor` | *For any reason?* |
| `thur` | *On what other grounds?* |
| `thaor` | *How do you want me to take this?* |
| `thuar` | *What's your overall take?* |
| `thuor` | *What reasons would you rule out?* |
| `ther` | *What's the main reason?* |
| `thaer` | *What would settle it?* |
| `thoer` | *What are you torn between?* |
| `thuer` | *What's your objection?* |

**Compare with:** `thul` *no judgment* withholds any stance; a *no chance* stance number is a stance that the claim is false. `thar` asks for the speaker's grounds, not what caused the event; for *Why does it happen?* use [`thahum blar`](/fill-ask-jobs#why).

### Join-act verbs {#join-act-verbs}

When someone *does* a list move to a thing (*includes*, *chooses*, *leaves out*), write that move as a verb: first letter **`v`**, the join vowel for that move, and ending **-n** (`van`, `von`, …). The object is one `/d/` phrase: one thing, or a group joined with a [phrase join](/joins). You can now name the act itself instead of only joining a list. On these verbs, **-n** does not mark a [name](proper-names.md#proper-name--n).

> `zuzozen doledol van.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-apple | v-includes
>
> "Uzozen adds an apple."

For an associative group (*Olazun and company*), put noun **-x** on the object as usual. **-x** on the verb itself is [collective](collectives.md#verbs-v).

**Related form:** joining a list uses [joins](/joins) (`zal` / `val` / `xal`, …).

**Compare with:** *and then* between sentences uses clause [sequence](/clause-sequence#sequence) (`xan`, …), not `van`.

<!-- cheat-sheet: joins-hooks -->
| Clause sequence | Join-act verb | Shared vowel move |
|-------------|---------------|-------------------|
| `xan` | `van` | add / include |
| `xon` | `von` | one / choose |
| `xun` | `vun` | undo / refuse |

The `/x/` forms connect clauses and keep one speech act. Their `/v/` counterparts take an object and name an act on it. Prioritizing (`ven`) has no clause sequence, and of the stacked vowels only `xaon` and `xuen` have one; the `/v/` forms follow the same series in the inventory below.

These are ordinary content verbs. To refer back to a join-act you already named, use a whole-stem **-r** resume or say it again in full.

Stacked vowels combine the same four moves (**a** / **o** / **e** / **u**):

| Agebon | Use | English | Cue | Example |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|---------|
| **`van`** | hold the object in the set | *includes / adds* | **a** ≈ add (hold / inventory) | `zuzozen doledol van` |
| **`von`** | lock the object as the sole pick | *chooses (as the one choice)* | **o** ≈ one (single pick) | `zuzozen dududal von` |
| **`vaon`** | admit the object; more may follow | *picks (more picks may follow)* | **ao** ≈ add one, more may follow (open pick) | `zuzozen dugedam vaon` |
| **`vun`** | reject the object | *denies / refuses* | **u** ≈ undo (take that member back) | `zuzozen dalamul vun` |
| **`vuan`** | leave the object out of the set | *excludes* | **ua** ≈ undo + add (cut that member) | `zuzozen dubedal vuan` |
| **`vuon`** | bar that one; the rest stay free | *bars (anything but)* | **uo** ≈ undo + one (forbid that pick) | `zuzozen dugudal vuon` |
| **`ven`** | rank the object first | *prioritizes* | **e** ≈ order (put this first) | `zuzozen dabezul ven` |
| **`vaen`** | put the object first in order | *starts with* | **ae** ≈ add + order (begin here) | `zuzozen derewom vaen` |
| **`voen`** | put the objects at the same rank | *equates / ties* | **oe** ≈ one + order (same height) | `zuzozen dolazun dahelan dal voen` |
| **`vuen`** | rank the object lower | *deprioritizes* | **ue** ≈ undo + order (drop this rank) | `zuzozen dugedam vuen` |

English *refuse* vs *leave out*: `zuzozen domudol vun` (*Uzozen refuses meat*) vs `zuzozen domudol vuan` (*Uzozen excludes meat*). One locked choice vs an open pick: `zuzozen dubedal von` vs `zuzozen dubedal vaon`. Rank vs sequence: `zuzozen derewom ven` (*prioritizes the workaround*: first by importance) vs `zuzozen derewom vaen` (*starts with the workaround*: first in order).

### Join-relations {#join-relations}

When English ties one extra thing to a noun or an action by a list move (*a cupcake with peanuts*, *walk without meat*, *work prioritizing the bug*), write the move as an adjective (first letter **`g`**) or an adverb (first letter **`h`**), with the same join vowel as [join-act verbs](#join-act-verbs) and ending **-n** (`gan` / `han`, …). Right after it comes one `/b/` word that names the extra thing. Keep that pair together: `gan` + `/b/` describes a noun, and `han` + `/b/` works as one adverb. You can now say *including*, *without*, or *prioritizing* without building a new list. Here too, **-n** does not mark a name, and an associative group on the `/b/` word takes noun **-x** as usual.

> `zugedal gan blubedal.`
>
> z-cupcake | [g-including | bl-peanut]
>
> "A cupcake with peanuts."

**`gan`** always needs its `/b/` word (`gan b…`).

**Compare with:** *writes with a hammer* is an extra-noun [hook](/hooks#extra-noun) (`ael` + `/b/`), not **`han`**. **`han`** is company / including, not the tool. *Tells on behalf of Olazun* is [proxy](/relations#proxy) (`hanom`), not **`hon`** *exclusively for*. **`uel`** is *against* that party, not **`hun`** *refusing*.

**Not the same job as:** adjective [joins](/joins) (`gal` / `gam` / `gar`). Join-relations take **-n** toward one `/b/`.

**For *including* as an afterthought, use:** a [hook](/hooks) (`al`, …). `zahunam gan bluzozen` is *a team including Uzozen*; `zahunam al zuzozen` is *the team, including Uzozen*.

For several members, put a [phrase join](/joins) **inside** the `/b/` phrase (`gan blubedal blugedam bal`; `zuzozen han blolazun blahelan bal vowurel`). For an unnamed member or none at all, use a [standalone](/joins#standalone-phrase) `/b/` join as the only `/b/` word (`gan blar` = *including something*; `guan blal` = *excluding nothing`).

| Agebon | Use | English | Cue | Example |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|---------|
| **`gan` / `han`** | `/ɡ/` noun or `/h/` event toward `/b/` | *including / with* | **a** ≈ add (hold the extra member) | `zugedal gan blubedal`; `zuzozen han blolazun vowurel` |
| **`gon` / `hon`** | `/ɡ/` or `/h/` toward `/b/` | `/ɡ/` *exclusive for* (sole pick); `/h/` *exclusively for / as the one* | **o** ≈ one (lock that pick) | `zahunam gon bluzozen`; `… hon bluzozen vowurel` |
| **`gaon` / `haon`** | `/ɡ/` or `/h/` toward `/b/` | `/ɡ/` *open to* (more may follow); `/h/` *among options* | **ao** ≈ add one, and more may follow | `zugedal gaon blubedal`; `… haon blubedal vowurel` |
| **`gun` / `hun`** | `/ɡ/` or `/h/` toward `/b/` | `/ɡ/` *against / anti-*; `/h/` *refusing* | **u** ≈ undo (reject that member) | `zugedal gun blubedal`; `… hun blomudol vowurel` |
| **`guan` / `huan`** | `/ɡ/` or `/h/` toward `/b/` | *without / excluding* | **ua** ≈ undo + add (leave that member out) | `zugedal guan blubedal`; `… huan blubedal vowurel` |
| **`guon` / `huon`** | `/ɡ/` or `/h/` toward `/b/` | `/ɡ/` *open to anything but*; `/h/` *barring / anything but* | **uo** ≈ undo + one (bar that one; rest free) | `zowulal guon blubedal`; `… huon blubedal vowurel` |
| **`gen` / `hen`** | `/ɡ/` or `/h/` toward `/b/` | `/ɡ/` *prioritizing / headed by*; `/h/` *prioritizing* | **e** ≈ order (put this first) | `zabeval gen blabezul`; `… hen blabezul verewom` |
| **`gaen` / `haen`** | `/ɡ/` or `/h/` toward `/b/` | `/ɡ/` *starting with*; `/h/` *starting with* | **ae** ≈ add + order (begin here) | `zabeval gaen blerewom`; `… haen blerewom vowurel` |
| **`goen` / `hoen`** | `/ɡ/` or `/h/` toward `/b/` | `/ɡ/` *on a par with*; `/h/` *equating / tying* | **oe** ≈ one + order (same height) | `zududal goen blugudal`; `… hoen blolazun vowurel` |
| **`guen` / `huen`** | `/ɡ/` or `/h/` toward `/b/` | `/ɡ/` *deprioritizing / trailing*; `/h/` *deprioritizing* | **ue** ≈ undo + order (drop this rank) | `zugedal guen blugedam`; `… huen blugedam vowurel` |

*Anti-* vs *without*: `gun blomudol` vs `guan blomudol`. *Without X* vs *anything but X*: `guan blubedal` vs `guon blubedal`. Sole pick vs among options: `gon bluzozen` vs `gaon bluzozen`. Workaround as top priority vs workaround as first step: `gen blerewom` vs `gaen blerewom`.

**Compare with:** *a house like Uzozen’s* / *walks like a duck* is resemblance ([like](/relations#like-resembles) `gewom` / `hewom`), not **`goen` / `hoen`** *on a par with* / *equating*.
