# Comparative scales {#comparative-arity-and-scales}

### Full comparative arity
<a id="comparative-arity"></a>

Beginner already used two names plus **`zol`** (*more … than*) and one name (*the …-est*). The same shared-scale pattern also uses reverse rank **`uo`** (*less … than* / *the least*) and **zero** names.

<!-- cheat-sheet: roles-comparing -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|---------|-----|---------|-----|
| **Multi (2+)** + shared scale under **`o` / `uo`** | comparative ladder | earlier above later (`o`); later above earlier under **`uo`** | **`o`** ≈ order; **`u`** ≈ undo |
| **Single-item (1)** + shared scale under **`o` / `uo`** | superlative | *the …-est* / *the least …* under **`uo`** | one name is the extreme |
| **Standalone (0)** + shared scale under **`o` / `uo`** | empty superlative | *there is no biggest* / *there is no smallest* under **`uo`**; open **-m** = none comes to mind | empty list: nobody is on top |

A standalone ladder says only that nothing is on top. It does not say why (a tie, no end to the scale, or items that do not compare). To assert a tie, use [equative](/comparatives#equatives) **`zoel`**. To say someone is on top without naming them, use **`zor`**.

<!-- cheat-sheet: roles-comparing -->
| Example | Reading |
|---------|---------|
| `zazozon zalazun zahelan zom gamahum` | *Azozon is more challenging than Alazun, who is more challenging than Ahelan* (open: others may fit in the ranking too) |
| `zazozon zalazun zuol gamahum` | *Azozon is less challenging than Alazun* |
| `zol gamahum` | *there is no most challenging* |
| `zom gamahum` | *no most challenging comes to mind* |
| `zuol gamahum` | *there is no least challenging* |
| `zazozon zuol gamahum` | *Azozon is the least challenging (of all)* |
| `zor gamahum` | *whatever ranks highest on challenging* (someone is on top; not saying who) |
| `zazozon zalazun zor gamahum` | *whichever of Azozon or Alazun ranks higher on challenging* |

**Compare with:** a rank join with no shared adjective is an ordinary [rank join](rank-joins.md#rank-joins) (*A matters more than B*). Add the shared `/ɡ/` when you mean *more ADJ than*.

English *not as challenging as* says the first name is below the second, so it is **`zuol`**: `zalazun zazozon zuol gamahum` is *Alazun is not as challenging as Azozon*. For a firm tie, use [`zoel`](/comparatives#equatives).

English *no more challenging than* says less: the first name is below the second or tied with them. Write the whole comparison as usual, then deny it with **`zul`**, the way you [deny a whole list](rank-joins.md#exclusivity-and-denying-a-whole-list). What English often only hints (that neither is very challenging) is not said.

> `zalazun zazozon zol gamahum zul.`
>
> [[z-Alazun | z-Azozon | z-rank/more | g-challenge] | z-not]
>
> "Alazun is no more challenging than Azozon."

### Place on a scale (`w#N`) {#place-on-a-scale}

English *the second biggest* names a place just below the winner. Keep the superlative frame (one name before `zol`) and put an **ordinal** in the degree slot, `/w/` right before the adjective: **`wrodol`** (`w#2`) is *second*. The place is on the scale the adjective names. (cue: **`o`** ≈ order, as in `grodol` *the second*)

> `zazozon zol wrodol gelazem.`
>
> [z-Azozon | z-rank/more | [w-2nd | g-big]]
>
> "Azozon is the second biggest."

Under **`zuol`** the same place counts from the bottom, so `zazozon zuol wrodol gelazem` is *the second smallest*. Use it from *second* on, because first place is the plain superlative. Write it only with one name before `zol` or `zuol`. With two names, the ordinal has no group to rank within, and outside a rank fence an ordinal on `/w/` has no reading.

**Compare with:** an ordinal after a noun picks the Nth *thing* (`zodozel grodol` *the second dog*), not the Nth on a scale. A factor (*twice as big*) goes on `/h/`, not `/w/`.

### Manner scale (`/h/`) {#manner-scale}

When English would say *Alazun walks more intensely than Azozon*, you rank **how** they walk, not a quality of the people. Keep the same rank join. Write a manner adverb (`/h/`) **immediately after** it, then the verb. The first name sits higher on that manner.

> `zalazun zazozon zol hahunom vowurol.`
>
> [z-Alazun | z-Azozon | z-rank/more | h-intensity] | v-walk
>
> "Alazun walks more intensely than Azozon."

> `zalazun zol hahunom vowurol.`
>
> [z-Alazun | z-rank/more | h-intensity] | v-walk
>
> "Alazun walks the most intensely."

> `zalazun zazozon zoel hahunom vowurol.`
>
> [z-Alazun | z-Azozon | z-equal-rank | h-intensity] | v-walk
>
> "Alazun walks as intensely as Azozon."

**Compare with:** `gahunom` after `zol` (no verb) is *more intense* as a quality of the people. After the verb, `hahunom` is an ordinary *how* they walk and ranks nothing.

### Amount scale (`gral`) {#amount-scale}

When English would say *more cats than dogs*, you rank **how many**, not a quality. Keep the rank join. For the shared word, write the [digitless number](digitless-exponents.md#digitless) **`gral`**: after a rank join it means *by amount*.

> `zogozelx zodozelx zol gral.`
>
> [z-cat-x | z-dog-x | z-rank/more | g-amount]
>
> "There are more cats than dogs."

> `zogozelx zodozelx zuol gral.`
>
> [z-cat-x | z-dog-x | z-rank/less | g-amount]
>
> "There are fewer cats than dogs."

> `zogozelx zodozelx zoel gral.`
>
> [z-cat-x | z-dog-x | z-equal-rank | g-amount]
>
> "There are as many cats as dogs."

The noun decides whether English says *many* or *much*: cats are counted, tea is measured. Agebon uses the same **`gral`** for both.

Open **`gram`** ranks by a **rough impression** instead of a count: *it looks like more cats than dogs*. Use **`gral`** when you counted or measured, and **`gram`** when you are going by how it seems.

> `zogozelx zodozelx zol gram.`
>
> [z-cat-x | z-dog-x | z-rank/more | g-amount.about]
>
> "It looks like there are more cats than dogs."

To say **how many more**, put the difference in a `bl-` word right after **`gral`**. A count needs no unit noun: the unit is the thing counted.

> `zogozelx zodozelx zol gral blragel.`
>
> [z-cat-x | z-dog-x | z-rank/more | [g-amount | bl-three]]
>
> "There are three more cats than dogs."

**Compare with:** after set join **`a`**, a shared word describes each item, so `zogozelx zodozelx zal gral` is *several cats and several dogs*. Only a **rank** join turns **`gral`** into the scale. For a named quantity such as weight or time, use its own word (`garolem`, `godurom`), not **`gral`**.

### Frequency scale (`hral`) {#frequency-scale}

English *walks more often than* ranks how many **times** something happens. Write digitless **`hral`** (*multiple times*) in the manner slot, right after the rank join. Only digitless **`hral`** sets this scale; other number words do not rank how often.

> `zazozon zalazun zol hral vowurol.`
>
> [z-Azozon | z-Alazun | z-rank/more | h-how-often] | v-walk
>
> "Azozon walks more often than Alazun."

### Time scale (`bral`) {#time-scale}

English *arrives earlier than* ranks **when** something happens. Write digitless **`bral`** (*later*) in the same place, right after the rank join. Rank **`o`** is *later than*, and **`uo`** is *earlier than*.

> `zazozon zalazun zol bral vevorol.`
>
> [z-Azozon | z-Alazun | z-rank/more | b-later] | v-arrival
>
> "Azozon arrives later than Alazun."

> `zazozon zalazun zuol bral vevorol.`
>
> [z-Azozon | z-Alazun | z-rank/less | b-later] | v-arrival
>
> "Azozon arrives earlier than Alazun."

### Covarying clauses (*the more …, the more …*) {#covarying}

English *the more Azozon walks, the more Alazun sleeps* says two amounts rise together. Put digitless **`hral`** (*multiple times*) or another amount word in each clause, and join the clauses with **`xoel`**. As with the [frequency scale](#frequency-scale), the equal-rank join turns each amount into a scale and says the two scales move in step.

> `zazozon hral vowurol xoel zalazun hral vezuvel.`
>
> [z-Azozon | h-more-than-one | v-walk | x-equal-rank | z-Alazun | h-more-than-one | v-sleep]
>
> "The more Azozon walks, the more Alazun sleeps."

For *the more …, the less …*, use **`xuol`**: when the first scale rises, the second falls.

> `zazozon hral vowurol xuol zalazun hral vezuvel.`
>
> [z-Azozon | h-more-than-one | v-walk | x-rank/less | z-Alazun | h-more-than-one | v-sleep]
>
> "The more Azozon walks, the less Alazun sleeps."

**Compare with:** **`xoel`** / **`xuol`** state only that the two go together, not which one causes the other. To say the first causes the second, use a [causation](/causation) pole.

### Factor (*twice as … as*) {#factor}

English *twice as big as* gives the **ratio** between the two heights. Use the [equative](/comparatives#equatives) **`zoel`** and its shared adjective, then a factor number (`/h/` + marker **`ra`**, [numbers](number-markers.md#number-as-adverb-by-marker)) **right after** the adjective. The first name sits at that many times the second's height.

> `zazozon zalazun zoel gelazem hradol.`
>
> [z-Azozon | z-Alazun | z-equal-rank | g-big | h-two]
>
> "Azozon is twice as big as Alazun."

Inverse marker **`ru`** divides: `hrudol` is *half as*. The factor hosts nothing, so no `bl-` word comes after it.

> `zalazun zazozon zoel gelazem hrudol.`
>
> [z-Alazun | z-Azozon | z-equal-rank | g-big | h-divided-by-two]
>
> "Alazun is half as big as Azozon."

English *three times bigger* usually means the same ratio: use **`zoel`** with `hragel`, not rank **`zol`**. After **`zol`**, a number `/h/` is not a factor; it is an ordinary *N times* on the clause. For a gap in units (*two meters taller*), put a measure noun on the scale instead of a factor.

### Comparing against a stance (bars) {#bars}

English *more than planned* and *later than planned* rank one thing against a **value a stance sets**, not against another person: the plan sets how much and when. Write that `/th/` stance word, or that [intention adverb](/clause#intention-adverbs) on `/h/` (FORMER, ability, a say word), where the second name would go, right before the rank join. That word is the **bar**. The join closes everything before it, so the bar is part of the comparison: it says what you rank against. It is not your stance on the whole sentence.

> `zazozon thagom zol bral vevorol.`
>
> [z-Azozon | th-plan-itinerary | z-rank/more | b-later] | v-arrival
>
> "Azozon arrives later than planned."

The same stance word **after** the fence has its ordinary reading, a stance on the whole sentence. With one ranked name, the fence is then a [superlative](/comparatives#superlatives):

> `zazozon zol bral vevorol thagom.`
>
> [z-Azozon | z-rank/more | b-later] | v-arrival | th-plan-itinerary
>
> "I plan for Azozon to arrive last."

A bar fence ranks **one** item against **one** bar: with a bar, there is no second name and no second bar. Any of the rank joins works (**`zol`**, **`zuol`**, **`zoel`**, and their open **-m** forms), and the bar keeps its own ending. The fence can rank an object or a recipient the same way:

> `zazozon dodelx thagom dol gral vagedal.`
>
> z-Azozon | [d-strawberry-x | th-plan-itinerary | d-rank/more | g-amount] | v-eat
>
> "Azozon eats more strawberries than planned."

> `zazozon vezuyol balazun thagom bol hral.`
>
> z-Azozon | v-tell | [b-Alazun | th-plan-itinerary | b-rank/more | h-how-often]
>
> "Azozon tells Alazun more often than planned."

A [hook](/hooks) and its `/b/` on the ranked item go right before the bar, the way they go right before a join word:

> `zanal em buwen thagom zol garolem.`
>
> [[z-book | [near | b-speaker]] | th-plan-itinerary | z-rank/more | g-heavy]
>
> "This book is heavier than planned."

<!-- cheat-sheet: roles-comparing -->
| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| name + bar + **`zol`** | above the bar | *more than planned* / *later than planned* |
| name + bar + **`zuol`** | below the bar | *less than planned* / *earlier than planned* |
| name + bar + **`zoel`** | at the bar | *as planned* |
| bar after the fence | a stance on the whole claim | *plans to be the most …* |

**Compare with:** a name before the rank join is someone you rank against (`zazozon zalazun zol guzemel` *Azozon is sleepier than Alazun*). A stance word in that place is the value that stance sets.

#### Pick one {#intermediate-pick-one}

**1.** *Alazun is not as heavy as Azozon.* `zalazun zazozon zuol garolem.` or `zalazun zazozon zol garolem zul.`

::: details Show answer
`zalazun zazozon zuol garolem.`

[z-Alazun | z-Azozon | z-rank/less | g-heavy]
