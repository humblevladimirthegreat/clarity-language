::: tip Reminder:
Intermediate sections assume you have read the beginner sections of every page.
:::

### Writing and speech rhythm (periods) {#orthography-and-prosody-periods}

A **period** closes the sentence body in writing, whatever the speech act. Speech matches that edge with a **rest**: one silent beat. The act word, not the punctuation, sets question or command. `?` and `!` are [tone marks](tone-marks.md#tone-marks) that go before the words they color.

| Writing | Speech | Use | Cue |
|---------|--------|-----|-----|
| `.` | Rest: one silent beat | Closes the prior **body**. Next stretch still defaults to **`yal`** unless a new `/y/` turn, a written act word, or *yes* / *no* particle sets otherwise | Rest = the claim is done |
| Next `/y/` turn (*yes* / *no*, a call, written act word) | The period's rest, then the `/y/` word | New turn; firm **-l** / soft **-m** imply **`yal`** / **`yam`** | The `/y/` word = a new act |
| Bare body or hook + body after `.` | The period's rest only | Implied **`yal`** (or **`yam`** only if a soft particle / written **`yam`** said so) | No `/y/` word, so same statement |
| Soft statement / soft *yes* / *no* **-m** | Nothing extra: the **-m** is heard | **`yam`** (implied or written) | **-m** leaves the hold open |
| `/x/` continue (clause join) | **No** rest: the sentence goes on | Keep opener speech act; period after the **whole** joined stretch | No rest = still going |
| `/x/` linker after `.` (`xahom …`) | The period's rest, then the linker | Keep prior speech act; may start a new written sentence under that speech act | Linker glues; still the same act |
| `/x/` topic word after `.` (`xazozon …`) | The period's rest, then the topic word | Keep prior speech act; a new topic starts | The topic word = the subject changed, not the act |
| **Stand-in** hang | **No** rest until the last dependent | Next sentence fills the slot; period after the **whole** stretch | Same hang as `/x/` continue |

**-l** vs **-m** on the act word / *yes* / *no* is how firmly you stand behind it (closed vs soft / open).

### Sentence linkers {#sentence-linkers}

Beginner already used *therefore*. It is one of six **sentence linkers**: `/x/` words that connect a finished sentence to the one after it. Each one uses its root's **abstract** sense, so it ends in **-m**.

<!-- cheat-sheet: linking-clauses -->
| English | Agebon | Abstract of | Cue | Example |
|---------|--------|-------------|-----|---------|
| *therefore* | **`xahom`** | `ahom` *progress* | ➡️: the sun’s path, so the talk moves on | `xahom` |
| *however* | **`xezem`** | `ezem` *contrast* | 🦓: two stripes, still one animal | `xezem` |
| *meanwhile* | **`xugorom`** | `ugorom` *passage* | 🕰️: time passing beside the last claim | `xugorom` |
| *next* | **`xevewom`** | `evewom` *sequence* | 🎞️: the following frame | `xevewom` |
| *but* | **`xugohum`** | `ugohum` *blockage* | 🚧: the expected path is blocked | `xugohum` |
| *by the way* | **`xavedam`** | `avedam` *accessory* | 🍟: a side dish, not the main course | `xavedam` |

*therefore* moves forward from the prior claim; *however* marks contrast; *meanwhile* puts the next claim at the same time; *next* is the next frame; *but* blocks the expected continuation (harder push-back than *however*). Those same roots keep their ordinary content readings under other role letters.

A linker reaches back to **one** sentence: the one that ends at the period before it. To link back to several claims at once, join them into that sentence with a [clause join](/joins#clause-joins).

Three linkers also take **-l** for a **firm** link: the link holds with no room for doubt, on the same settled-to-passing scale as [time horizon](/sakes#settled-to-passing). (cue: **-l** ≈ lasting: the link holds)

<!-- cheat-sheet: linking-clauses -->
| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| **`xahol`** | the next claim must hold if the last sentence does: a deduction | *it follows that* / *necessarily, then* |
| **`xezel`** | the contrast stands whatever the last claim said | *nevertheless* / *even so* |
| **`xugohul`** | the last claim is blocked outright; the next claim replaces it | *on the contrary* |

> `zalazun vezuvel vul. xugohul zalazun veruvol.`
>
> z-Alazun | [v-sleep | v-not] . x-on-the-contrary | z-Alazun | v-run
>
> "Alazun isn't sleeping. On the contrary, Alazun is running."

No other root makes a linker. Any other `/x/` content word sets the topic: **-l** / **-m** / **-n** introduce it (`xazozon` *now, about Azozon*) and **-r** returns to it. **-r** on a linker is still a [resume](/pronouns#resume-r) (the same linker again, *likewise*).

All of these `/x/` words, and the topic words, come only at the **start of a sentence**: only `/y/` turn words or a [fronted discourse hook](/hooks#discourse-hooks) may come before them. They never follow a [clause join](/joins#clause-joins), and they never open the sentence after a [stand-in](/dependents#dependent-clauses), because that sentence belongs to the dependent.

A linker and a topic word do not share a sentence. To change the topic and also use a linker, write the topic word as its own sentence, then start the next sentence with the linker (*Now, about Azozon. However, Alazun runs.*):

> `xazozon. xezem zalazun veruvol.`
>
> x-Azozon . x-however | z-Alazun | v-run
>
> "Now, about Azozon. However, Alazun runs."

**`xevewom`** also opens a new frame, like English *so, …* at the start of a turn: the talk moves on and the topic is cleared.

> `zodozel vowurol. xevewom zazozon veruvol.`
>
> z-dog | v-walk . x-next | z-Azozon | v-run
>
> "The dog walks. So, Azozon runs."

**`xavedam`** opens a side topic, like English *by the way, …*, and clears the topic too.

In a new turn with its own act word, the act word comes first and the linker starts the body:

> `yem xevewom zawan vowurol.`
>
> y-soft-question | x-next | z-listener | v-walk
>
> "So, do you want to walk?"

> `yel xavedam zawan vebal.`
>
> y-question | x-by-the-way | z-listener | v-hear
>
> "By the way, did you hear?"

### Result (*so … that*) {#result-pole}

English *so … that* ties a result to how much of something there is (*so tired that they sleep*), all in one sentence. Put the pole **`hahom`** after the word that carries the degree, then **`blarl`**, then the result as the next sentence. **`hahom`** uses the same root as *therefore*, which needs two sentences. Unlike **`hovam`** *so that*, the result really happened.

> `zazozon welazem gadamul hahom blarl zazozor vezuvel.`
>
> [z-Azozon | [w-very | g-tired]] | [h-so-result | bl-that-clause] | z-←Azozon | v-sleep
>
> "Azozon is so tired that they sleep."

> `zazozon welazem hadurom vowurol hahom blarl zalazun veruvol.`
>
> z-Azozon | [w-very | h-haste] | v-walk | [h-so-result | bl-that-clause] | z-Alazun | v-run
>
> "Azozon walks so hastily that Alazun runs."

**Compare with:** *although* / *while* attach a dependent after **`blarl`** (`hezem blarl`, `herom blarl`; see [dependent clauses](/dependents#dependent-clauses)). *Despite Alazun* is the same pole with a `bl-` noun (`hezem blalazun`). *So that Alazun sits* is **`hovam blarl`**. Discourse **`xezem`** / **`xugorom`** / **`xahom`** glue a finished sentence to the next one.

### Stand-in vowels (`-rl` / `-rm`) {#stand-in}
<a id="clause-member"></a>

Beginner used **`darl`** / **`barl`** for *that …* and **`derl`** for *whether …*. A stand-in's vowel says what kind of content the next sentence is, with the same four vowels as the [act words](/speech-moves#speech-act-beginner): **`a`** a statement, **`o`** an instruction, **`e`** a question, **`u`** something to avoid. So a dependent clause can carry an instruction (*tells Alazun **to sit***) or a warning (*tells Alazun **not to sit***).

Unlike a `/y/` act word, a stand-in does not itself state, ask, or command: it only lets that kind of content fill a role in the outer sentence. Stand-ins are not joins. The ending says how much the next sentence covers: **`-rl`** means it is the whole content (locked); **`-rm`** leaves it open (the gist, or not the only thing).

<!-- cheat-sheet: linking-clauses -->
| Stand-in | Content type | **`-rl`** locked | **`-rm`** open |
|----------|--------------|------------------|----------------|
| **`a`** add | statement-like (compare **`yal`**) | `darl` *that …* | `darm` *that …* (gist) |
| **`o`** rank | instruction-like (compare **`yol`**) | `dorl` *to …* | `dorm` *to …* (open) |
| **`e`** choice | question-like (compare **`yel`**) | `derl` *whether …* | `derm` *whether …* (open) |
| **`u`** not | avoidance-like (compare **`yul`**) | `durl` *that not / lest …* | `durm` *lest …* (open) |

You may leave out the dependent clause's `/z/` only when all of these hold:

1. The stand-in is **`dorl`** / **`dorm`**, or **`durl`** / **`durm`** telling someone *not to*.
2. It fills the outer sentence's `/d/` slot.
3. The outer sentence has one plain `/b/` (the person told).

The missing subject is then that `/b/`. It is never the listener and never the outer `/z/`. In every other case, write the inner `/z/`: after **`darl`** / **`derl`**, after any pole (**`blarl`** / **`blurl`**), and when the outer sentence has no plain `/b/` (*wants to sit*, *tries to sit*). When the doer is someone other than that `/b/`, write them too, using resume **-r** if the name would repeat.

After a pole, the dependent is **`blarl`** (or **`blarm`** for gist): *because* / *if* / *although* / *so that* all use it. There are two exceptions, both with **`blurl`**: purpose-not **`hovam blurl`** (*so that … not*) and **`thohem blurl`** (*unless*). Stand-ins never take `/x/`; clause *and* is `xal` / `xan`.

> `zazozon vezuyol balazun dorl vehohel.`
>
> z-Azozon | v-tell | b-Alazun | d-to-clause | v-sit
>
> "Azozon tells Alazun to sit."

The person told is plain `/b/`, so **`dorl vehohel`** can leave out its subject: the sitter is Alazun. The outer sentence stays a statement. With no plain `/b/`, write the sitter:

> `zazozon valom dorl zazozon vehohel.`
>
> z-Azozon | v-wish | d-to-clause | z-Azozon | v-sit
>
> "Azozon wishes to sit."

To quote someone's exact words instead, use a [span](/spans).

English *lest* / *not to* (keep this from happening) uses vowel **`u`**: **`durl`**. When it tells someone not to act, the person told (`/b/`) can be the missing subject, as with **`dorl`**. When it only keeps an outcome off, with no one told, write the inner `/z/`. The outer sentence stays a statement. *Don't sit!* as the whole move is still a [prohibition](/speech-moves#speech-act-beginner) (**`yul`**), not this stand-in.

> `zazozon vezuyol balazun durl vehohel.`
>
> z-Azozon | v-tell | b-Alazun | d-lest-clause | v-sit
>
> "Azozon tells Alazun not to sit."

When *so that* aims at keeping an outcome off, keep **`hovam`** and type `/b/` with **`blurl`**:

> `zazozon vowurol hovam blurl zazozon vehohel.`
>
> z-Azozon | v-walk | [h-so-that | bl-lest-clause] | z-Azozon | v-sit
>
> "Azozon walks so as not to sit."

English *unless* is *if … not*: keep the *if* pole **`thohem`** and type `/b/` with **`blurl`**. The following sentence is the condition that must **not** hold.

> `zazozon vowurol thohem blurl zalazun vezuvel.`
>
> z-Azozon | v-walk | [th-if | bl-lest-clause] | z-Alazun | v-sleep
>
> "Azozon walks unless Alazun sleeps."

The sentence after a stand-in runs to the end of the written sentence, so a [clause join](/joins#clause-joins) inside it stays in the dependent. *Whether Alazun walks or Ahelan runs* is **`derl`**, then both clauses with **`xel`** between them.

> `zazozon vayebel derl zalazun vowurol xel zahelan veruvol.`
>
> z-Azozon | v-see | d-whether-clause | [z-Alazun | v-walk | x-or-exactly-one | z-Ahelan | v-run]
>
> "Azozon sees whether Alazun walks or Ahelan runs."

To join another clause to the main sentence instead, end the sentence and start the next one with the join.

**Compare with:** *tells that Alazun sits* is **`darl`**. *Tells Alazun to sit* is **`dorl`**. *Walks so that Azozon sits* is **`hovam blarl`**.

### Pointing back (`-rth`) {#stand-in-back}

A stand-in on **`-rl`** / **`-rm`** holds a slot for the sentence that comes **next**. To point **back** at content already said, end the stand-in in **`-rth`**: English *so* in *Azozon says so*, or *that* in *Alazun doubts that*. Nothing follows it; the sentence ends as usual.

The vowel still says what kind of content it is, and the word stands for the most recent content of that kind:

<!-- cheat-sheet: linking-clauses -->
| Stand-in | Points back to | English |
|----------|----------------|---------|
| **`darth`** | the last statement | *so* / *that* |
| **`derth`** | the last question | *that question* |
| **`dorth`** | the last instruction | *that (instruction)* |
| **`durth`** | the last *not* / *lest* content | *not* (*I hope not*) |

> `zalazun vehohel. zazozon vezuyol darth.`
>
> z-Alazun | v-sit . z-Azozon | v-tell | d-that-same-claim
>
> "Alazun sits. Azozon says so."

> `zazozon vezuyol balazun durl vehohel. zalazun valom durth.`
>
> z-Azozon | v-tell | b-Alazun | d-lest-clause | v-sit . z-Alazun | v-wish | d-that-same-prohibition
>
> "Azozon tells Alazun not to sit. Alazun wishes not to." (not to sit)

The content can be anything a sentence says: a verbless sentence, a denied event, or several sentences chained with `/x/`. A [resume](/pronouns#resume-r) **-r** picks up one word; **`-rth`** picks up what was said.

Other role letters work the same way as the forward stand-in: **`blarth`** after a pole (*because of that*) or after [*like*](/relations#similative) **`hewem`** (*like that*), **`zarth`** as the subject. Stand-ins fill `/z/` `/d/` `/b/` only; there is no stand-in on `/ɡ/` `/h/` `/w/` `/th/`. *Such* is [whole-stem **-r**](/pronouns#resume-r), and *then* or *so* is a pole + **`blarth`**.

### Time poles on a noun {#time-pole-on-noun}

To place a **thing** in time rather than the event (*the walk after the thunderstorm*), put the [time pole](/dependents#dependent-clauses) on `/ɡ/` right after that noun: **`gerom`** *during*, **`gabam`** *before*, **`galam`** *after*, **`garum`** *until*, **`guvahum`** *by*. The `/b/` after it is the event or period, as with the `/h/` pole.

> `zalazun dowurol galam blanulul vayebel.`
>
> z-Alazun | [d-walk | [g-after | bl-thunderstorm]] | v-see
>
> "Alazun sees the walk after the thunderstorm." (*after the thunderstorm* says which walk)

Ordinary `zabal` is still *a backpack*.
