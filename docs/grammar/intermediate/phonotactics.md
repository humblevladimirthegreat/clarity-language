# Word shape {#word-shape}

### Phonotactics (word shape)
<a id="phonotactics"></a>

Beginner already used word edges: a content word ends in `-l` / `-m` / `-n` / `-r`. Here is the full shape of that word, in order, written as one lowercase token (`zazozon`).

<!-- cheat-sheet: sounds-spelling -->
| Ageben | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| Role letter | first letter | role in the clause (subject, verb, …) |
| Optional `l` after `/ɡ/` | adjective before the noun | `gl-` looks ahead to the next noun |
| Optional `l` after `/b/` | [hosted extra noun](/clause#extra-nouns) | `bl-` completes the word right before it |
| Root(s) **V(CV)+** | content | start with a vowel; each later consonant starts a new syllable |
| Mid-word `x` | productive compound seam | joins two roots inside one word |
| Mid-word `th` | stance seam | joins a sake to its stance vowel ([sakes](/sakes)) |
| Dictionary stem (no `x`) | dictionary compound | one long simple-looking root (`oyaheha` *bedroom*) |
| `-l` / `-m` / `-n` / `-r` | [word ending](/word-endings) | audible end of the content word |
| Optional `-x` | [plural](/plurality) after the ending | word-final `-lx` / `-mx` / `-nx` / `-rx` / `-lnx` (letter `x`) |
| Stand-in `-rl` / `-rm` | [dependent clauses](/dependents#dependent-clauses) | word-final `rl` / `rm` |

A syllable ends with a consonant only at the **end of the word**. Inside a root, `l` and `r` always have a vowel after them, so they start a syllable rather than sounding like a suffix (`zobulol`: prefix `z`, root `obulo`, ending `-l`). Each spelling has only one pronunciation.

Some short grammar words put two vowels side by side, a **vowel stack** (a join such as `ua`). A stack always puts its vowels in alphabetical order (`u`, `a`, `o`, `e`): `u` comes first and `e` comes last. That gives six stacks: `ua`, `uo`, `ue`, `ao`, `ae`, and `oe`. The two vowels stay two separate syllables.

Each vowel keeps its [pitch](/phonology#vowel-pitch) in a stack, so every stack is a small melody. The alphabet's vowel order drops from `u` to the lowest vowel and then climbs back, so the stacks mix falls and rises:

| Stack | Pitch | Contour |
|-------|-------|------|
| `ua` | high → low | big fall |
| `uo` | high → mid-low | medium fall |
| `ue` | high → mid-high | small fall, high |
| `ao` | low → mid-low | small rise, low |
| `ae` | low → mid-high | medium rise |
| `oe` | mid-low → mid-high | small rise, mid |

**A fall means `u`.** Every stack that starts with `u` falls, and every other stack rises. In grammar words a `u` stack is usually the *undo* or *set aside* member of its family, so you can hear which side a stack is on from its direction alone, before the second vowel is clear. The direction backs up the vowels; when the two disagree, trust the vowels.

**Related form:** word-initial `x` is the [continue](/dependents#continue-x) prefix (discourse), not a compound seam.

### Number-word exception {#number-word-exception}

When English says *how many* or *which place*, Ageben uses a [number word](/numbers). A number word puts `r` right after the role letter. Ordinary words never have that cluster, because their roots start with a vowel, so a role letter followed directly by `r` tells you a number is coming. The marker is `ra`, `ru`, `re`, or `ro`. To count from the end, write `#-` before the digits; it is spelled and spoken `rue`. Two vowels in a row stay two separate syllables.

> `zogozelx gragel.`
>
> z-cat-x | g-three
>
> "There are three cats."

`gragel` is `g` + `ra` + digit `ge` + `-l`.

A code (marker `re`) may hold letters as well as digits, and is spelled aloud one letter name at a time. Its body may break the ordinary word shape in two ways: a vowel letter comes after a short break, written **`'`** (`zre'ebal`), and two vowels may then sit side by side, even the same vowel twice (`zrebu'ul`). The role letter plus `r` already tells you a number is coming, so a code never sounds like a root. The `'` appears only inside a code.

### Syllabic `l` {#syllabic-l}

A [hosted extra noun](/clause#extra-nouns) writes `l` after its `b`. When that word is a number (`r` next), say the `l` as a beat of its own, like the end of English *bottle* <IpaPlay file="En-us-bottle.mp3" label="bottle" />. The `r` then starts the next beat as usual.

| Ageben | IPA |
|--------|-----|
| `bral` | /bɹäl/ |
| `blral` | /bl̩.ɹäl/ |

This is the only beat with no vowel letter. Like a vowel, you can hold it. Before a vowel, `bl` is one ordinary start of a beat (`blazozon` /blʉ.zo̞.ze̞n/).
