# Residue and former {#residue-and-former}

English *has left* and *used to tell* mix when the event happened and how it stands now. Ageben keeps those apart: **RESIDUE** and **FORMER** for how an outcome or a usual pattern stands, and *already* / *still* / *not yet* / *no longer* for a change compared with now. How you know a world-claim is in [knowing](/knowing).

English *has left* and *used to tell* mix two things: when the event happened, and how it stands now. Ageben places events in time with other tools: [*before* / *after* / *while*](/dependents#time-hinges), a clock or date, [LIVE](/live-and-memory#live-vs-memory) / [MEMORY](/live-and-memory#live-vs-memory), or a [forecast](evidentiality.md#forecast). Two `/h/` words keep the books on how it stands. **RESIDUE** says the outcome of an event is still in effect now. **FORMER** says a usual pattern is not one you are claiming for now. Neither says when anything happened, so use them alongside a channel or a when-frame, never as a past or present tense.

**RESIDUE** (`ara`) says the result still counts: an unpaid tab, a door that is still shut. Without it, you make no claim that anything is left over (the event is over and done, or nothing remains from it). As a plain noun, `zaram` is *debt*. (cue: 💰 a debt still on the books)

> `zalazun haram vudurul.`
>
> z-Alazun | h-RESIDUE | v-departure
>
> "Alazun has left and is still gone."

> `zalazun thevum haram vudurul.`
>
> z-Alazun | th-MEMORY | h-RESIDUE | v-departure
>
> "I remember Alazun leaving, and Alazun is still gone."

> `zalazun thugem haram vudurul.`
>
> z-Alazun | th-LIVE | h-RESIDUE | v-departure
>
> "Alazun has left and is still gone, I can see it." (what is left over is in view)

**Compare with:** [*after*](/dependents#time-hinges) (`halam blohel`) orders two events. **`haram`** does not say the leaving was earlier; it says the **outcome is still in effect**. [LIVE](/live-and-memory#live-vs-memory) is how you know, not what is left over. A result as a property can stay ordinary `/ɡ/` (*the door is shut*) with no residue word.

**FORMER** (`ela`) says a usual pattern held, but you are **not** claiming it holds now: *Azozon always sings* as a past habit. Without it, [always](/restrictors) **`hual`** describes the pattern as it stands now. As a plain noun, `zelam` is *emptiness*. (cue: 🪹 the nesting season is over; a former climate, not today's weather)

> `zazozon hual vozuyel helam.`
>
> z-Azozon | h-always | v-sing | h-FORMER
>
> "Azozon always used to sing." (no claim about now)

> `zazozon hual vozuyel helam thevum.`
>
> z-Azozon | h-always | v-sing | h-FORMER | th-MEMORY
>
> "Azozon always used to sing, as I remember."

> `zazozon hual vozuyel helam thubom blral.`
>
> z-Azozon | h-always | v-sing | h-FORMER | [th-PATTERN | bl-later]
>
> "Going by the pattern, always singing will be something Azozon used to do."

**Compare with:** PATTERN **`thubom`** says how you know (from repeated cases); it does not say the pattern is over. **`helam`** is not a past tense either: it works with LIVE and in a forecast.

Do not write **`helam`** right before **`hual`**: that spot holds the restrictor's list of occasions (*always except when …*). Put **`hual`** first (bare *always*) and FORMER after the verb, where a channel goes on a general claim.

| Ageben | Use | English | Same root as | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|--------------|-----|
| **`haram`** | outcome still in effect | *still counts* / residue | `aram` *debt* | 💰: unpaid balance still on the books |
| **`helam`** | usual pattern not claimed for now | *former climate* | `elam` *emptiness* | 🪹: the nesting season is over |

Put RESIDUE and FORMER on `/h/`, anywhere in the sentence: like an [intention adverb](/clause#intention-adverbs), each covers the whole clause. Use `/w/` only when the word applies to the one `/ɡ/` adjective right after it. Both always end in **-m**.

### Channels after `uom` {#channel-zone}

English *contrary to what I was told* and *against my gut* say the event went against what a source led you to expect. Put the channel after the hook **`uom`** (*contrary to*), the same way as a ban or a plan ([contrary to a stance](prescription.md#contrary-to-stance)). The pair names what the event goes **against**; the channel then says nothing about how you know the claim.

> `zazozon vowurol uom thebam.`
>
> z-Azozon | v-walk | [contrary-to | th-REPORTED]
>
> "Azozon walks, contrary to what I was told."

> `zazozon vowurol uom thabem.`
>
> z-Azozon | v-walk | [contrary-to | th-INTUITION]
>
> "Azozon walks, against my gut."

| After **`uom`** | Goes against | English |
|-----------------|--------------|---------|
| LIVE `thugem` | how it looks | *contrary to appearances* |
| MEMORY `thevum` | how you remember it | *contrary to what I remember* |
| PATTERN `thubom` | the trail of cases | *contrary to habit* / *unusually* |
| CLUES `theyom` | what was worked out | *contrary to my calculations* |
| REPORTED `thebam` | what was said or written | *contrary to what I was told* / *contrary to legend* |
| INTUITION `thabem` | your gut expectation | *against my gut* |

*Off schedule* goes against a plan: **`uom thagom`** ([contrary to a stance](prescription.md#contrary-to-stance)). MAY, RESIDUE, and FORMER do not follow **`uom`**: none of them is something the event could go against.

A [holder](holder.md#holder) on a channel after **`uom`** names **whose view** the event goes against. The seam covers only that pair: the event is still your claim.

> `zazozon vowurol uom thebamalazun.`
>
> z-Azozon | v-walk | [contrary-to | th-REPORTED-Alazun]
>
> "Azozon walks, contrary to what I hear Alazun thinks."
