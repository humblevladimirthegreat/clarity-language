# Left-bound chaining {#left-bound-adjectives-and-chaining}

::: tip Reminder:
Intermediate sections assume you have read the beginner sections of every page.
:::
### Complex chaining {#complex-chaining}

A relation adjective and its `bl-` word (*the same as Azozon*: **`gorel`** + `bl-`) work as one unit after a noun. A `/w/` word that grades the relation goes immediately before the `/ɡ/` word, as usual. You can keep chaining: a plain adjective after that pair describes the **extra noun**, not the first noun. (cue: 🪙 *coin*: two faces of one thing.)

> `zagayul welazem gorel blazozon.`
>
> z-guard | [[w-very | g-SAME] | bl-Azozon]
>
> "a guard who is the very same person as Azozon"

> `zagayul gorel blazozon guzemel.`
>
> z-guard | [g-SAME | [bl-Azozon | g-sleepy]]
>
> "a guard who is the same person as sleepy Azozon"

The same holds after an `/h/` or `/th/` host: an adjective after its `/b/` describes that extra noun.

> `zazozon vezuvel hewem blodozel gadamul.`
>
> z-Azozon | v-sleep | [h-like | [bl-dog | g-tired]]
>
> "Azozon sleeps like a tired dog."

Because a second relation after the pair would attach to the extra noun, you cannot put two relation adjectives **after** the same first noun. To keep both on that noun in one clause, put one [before the noun](/left-bound-adjectives#left-bound-adjectives) with **`gl-`**; its `/b/` still follows it. Otherwise use [another sentence](/dependents#which-noun).

> `glorel blazozon zagayul gorel blebayol.`
>
> [[gl-SAME | bl-Azozon] | z-guard] | [g-SAME | bl-police]
>
> "a guard who is both Azozon and the police officer"

A verb can take several `/h/` or `/th/` units. Each plain word counts as one unit, and so does a host with its `/b/` (plus any `/w/` before it).

**Compare with:** a [hook](/hooks#extra-noun) followed by `/b/` means *in* / *at* / *for*, not the person who receives.

### How much: degree words on `/w/` {#degree-w}

Beginner used **`welazem`** *very* before an adjective. The same `/w/` slot takes a few stock degree words, and each one works before an adverb as well as an adjective.

<!-- cheat-sheet: word-shape-clause -->
| Ageben | English | Cue |
|--------|---------|-----|
| `welazem` | *very* | 🐘: big, as a degree |
| `wohalal` | *extremely* / *overwhelmingly* | 🌊: an ocean of it |
| `wamezem` | *slightly* / *a bit* | 🐁: small, as a degree |
| `wadowem` | *quite* / *fairly* | 🔉: medium volume, not loud or soft |
| `wahuram` | *completely* / *totally* / *entirely* / *fully* | 💯: a hundred is the whole |

> `zodozel welazem hadurom vowurol.`
>
> z-dog | [w-very | h-haste] | v-walk
>
> "A dog walks very hastily."

> `zodozel wohalal gonumul vowurol.`
>
> [z-dog | [w-ocean | g-angry]] | v-walk
>
> "An extremely angry dog walks."

> `zodozel wamezem gonumul vowurol.`
>
> [z-dog | [w-small | g-angry]] | v-walk
>
> "A slightly angry dog walks."

> `zodozel wadowem gonumul vowurol.`
>
> [z-dog | [w-quite | g-angry]] | v-walk
>
> "A fairly angry dog walks."

Two stock words go in `/h/` and describe the verb itself. Use **`hayurom`** *kind of* / *sort of* to hedge the action, and **`herugom`** for *again*.

<!-- cheat-sheet: word-shape-clause -->
| Ageben | English | Cue |
|--------|---------|-----|
| `hayurom` | *kind of* / *sort of* | 📈: some degree, not a full one |
| `herugom` | *again* | 🔁: the same thing once more |

> `zazozon hayurom vowurol.`
>
> z-Azozon | h-kind-of | v-walk
>
> "Azozon kind of walks."

> `zodozel herugom veruvol.`
>
> z-dog | h-again | v-run
>
> "The dog runs again."

### Leaving out who acts {#no-subject}

English uses the passive (*The cat was seen*, *Mistakes were made*) to avoid naming who acted. Ageben simply leaves out the `/z/` word. The verb and its object stay the same, and the sentence says nothing about who did it.

> `dazozon vayebel.`
>
> d-Azozon | v-see
>
> "Azozon was seen."

**Compare with:** [`zumen`](/pronouns#special-pronouns) *someone* says that somebody did it, without saying who. With no subject at all, the sentence does not mention a doer. A missing subject never means the topic: to make the topic the doer, write `zalen`.

#### Pick one {#intermediate-pick-one}

**1.** *Azozon sees a police officer who is the same person as sleepy Ahelan.* `zazozon debayol gorel blahelan guzemel vayebel.` or `zazozon debayol guzemel gorel blahelan vayebel.`

::: details Show answer
`zazozon debayol gorel blahelan guzemel vayebel.`

z-Azozon | [d-police | [g-SAME | [bl-Ahelan | g-sleepy]]] | v-see

After the **`gorel`** pair, a plain adjective describes the extra noun; before it, *sleepy* describes the police officer.
:::

**2.** *Alazun was seen.* `dalazun vayebel.` or `zalazun vayebel.`

::: details Show answer
`dalazun vayebel.`

d-Alazun | v-see

Leave out the subject and keep Alazun as the object; `zalazun vayebel` is *Alazun sees*.
:::
