# Tone marks {#tone-marks-sitting}


### Tone marks {#tone-marks}

When you speak, your voice shows how you feel and which word you stress; English writing has only *!* and italics for that. In Ageben, a **tone mark** written in front of a word or a sentence shows that voice. It changes your voice only. It never changes the speech act (that is the act word's job), and a sentence still ends in a period, so you can add a feeling to any sentence without changing what the sentence does.

<!-- cheat-sheet: talking -->
| Mark | Feeling | Voice | Cue |
|------|---------|-------|-----|
| `!` | Strong feeling (excited, upset: the words say which) | Louder, stressed | English *!* |
| `?` | Unsure about it | Raised, tentative | English *…, I think* after the doubtful part |
| `%` | Joking, teasing (not meant literally) | Light, smiling | A wink |
| `&` | Contrast: this one, not another | Stressed and slowed | *and* not the other |
| `;` | Warm, affectionate | Soft, gentle | A soft pause |

`?` is the only mark that uses pitch: it raises the whole word it colors, and the word's [vowel pitches](/phonology#vowel-pitch) keep their order inside it. `!` and `;` are loudness, `&` is loudness and length, and `%` is the quality of your voice.

Doubling a mark makes it stronger, and combining two marks mixes their tones:

| Mark | Tone |
|------|------|
| `!!` | louder again |
| `??` | very unsure |
| `;;` | very tender |
| `%%` | broad teasing |
| `&&` | a firm contrast |
| `?!` | surprised and doubtful (*Wait, what?!*) |
| `%;` | teasing but fond |
| `&!` | *this* one, and you feel strongly about it |

A stack has no spaces and its order does not matter (`?!` and `!?` sound the same). Each mark may appear at most twice, so `!!!` is not a mark.

Write the mark *before* what it colors, so you see the tone before you say the words:

| Written | Colors |
|---------|--------|
| Attached to a word: `!zazozon` | that word |
| Attached to a [span](/spans): `!d[ … ]` | the span |
| Standing alone, then a space: `! zazozon …` | the rest of the sentence, up to the period |

> `zazozon ?dodozel vayebel.`
>
> z-Azozon | ?d-dog | v-see
>
> "Azozon saw a dog, I think." (unsure about just the dog)

> `! zazozon vowurol.`
>
> ! | z-Azozon | v-walk
>
> "Azozon walks!"

> `zazozon &dodozel vayebel.`
>
> z-Azozon | &d-dog | v-see
>
> "Azozon saw a *dog*." (a dog, not something else)

The other marks work the same way:

> `zazozon !!vowurol.`
>
> z-Azozon | !!v-walk
>
> "Azozon *walks*!!"

> `?! zalazun dodozel vayebel.`
>
> ?! | z-Alazun | d-dog | v-see
>
> "Alazun saw a dog?!"

> `%;zalazun vowurol.`
>
> %;z-Alazun | v-walk
>
> "Good old Alazun walks." (teasing, but fond)

> `zazozon ??dodozel vayebel.`
>
> z-Azozon | ??d-dog | v-see
>
> "Azozon saw a dog, I think, but I'm really not sure." (unsure about just the dog)

> `%% zazozon vowurol.`
>
> %% | z-Azozon | v-walk
>
> "Azozon walks." (broad teasing)

> `zazozon &&dodozel vayebel.`
>
> z-Azozon | &&d-dog | v-see
>
> "Azozon saw a *dog*." (a dog, and definitely not something else)

> `;;zalazun dodozel vayebel.`
>
> ;;z-Alazun | d-dog | v-see
>
> "Dearest Alazun saw a dog."

> `% zazozon vowurol.`
>
> % | z-Azozon | v-walk
>
> "Azozon walks." (joking)

> `;zalazun dodozel vayebel.`
>
> ;z-Alazun | d-dog | v-see
>
> "Dear Alazun saw a dog."

A mark on a span colors all of it:

> `zazozon ?d[azozon] vezuyol.`
>
> z-Azozon | ?d-CITE[Azozon] | v-tell
>
> "Azozon said “Azozon,” I think." (unsure about just the quoted word)

**Compare with:** `yel` asks a question; `?` only sounds unsure. A tone mark shows the feeling.

- A mark may go on any word, including act words.
- A mark is a stack of `!`, `?`, `%`, `&`, `;`, each at most twice, in any order. A third copy (`!!!`) is not a mark, and neither are marks in a row with a space between (`! !`).
- `&` is the only way to single out one word: moving a word adds no stress.
- A mark on a word inside a colored span or sentence overrides it for that word. To mix tones on one word, stack them there.
- Before a span fence, the mark is *your* tone about the quote. Inside the fence, it is part of what is quoted (the original speaker's tone).
- Speech adds no syllables: you voice the mark over its words.

#### Emphasis and clefts {#emphasis-and-clefts}

English moves or stresses a word to single it out: *It's a cat that Azozon sees*, *It was Azozon who saw the cat*, or a stressed *Azozon saw the cat*. Ageben word order carries no emphasis: the default order is set by the [speech act](/speech-moves#act-word-order), and any other order means the same. So ask what the English emphasis is doing:

- If it sets one person or thing against others (*Azozon, not someone else*), put `&` on that word.
- If it only puts the new part first, with nothing to set it against (answering *What does Azozon see?* with *It's a cat that Azozon sees*), write the plain sentence. What came before already shows which part is new.

Going the other way, an Ageben sentence in an unusual order (a line fitted to a tune, say) becomes a plain English sentence.

<!-- cheat-sheet: word-shape-clause -->
| Ageben | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| `zazozon dogozel vayebel.` | plain | *Azozon sees a cat.* / *It's a cat that Azozon sees.* (answering *what?*) |
| `&zazozon dogozel vayebel.` | subject contrasted | *It was Azozon who saw the cat.* |
| `zazozon &dogozel vayebel.` | object contrasted | *It's a cat that Azozon sees.* (not something else) |
| `zazozon dogozel &vayebel.` | act contrasted | *Azozon sees the cat.* (rather than doing something else to it) |

**2.** *Alazun sees a trumpet, not something else.*

`zalazun &dadorol vayebel.`

z-Alazun | &d-trumpet | v-see
:::

**3.** *Alazun finds the trumpet, I think.* (unsure about the trumpet)

::: details Show answer

z-Alazun | v-find | ?d-trumpet
:::

#### Ageben → English {#intermediate-ageben-to-english}

**1.** `! zalazun dadorol vumewel.`

::: details Show answer

*Alazun finds a trumpet!*
:::

**2.** `zazozon vayebel ;dadorol.`

::: details Show answer
z-Azozon | v-see | ;d-trumpet

*Azozon sees a trumpet.* (fondly, about the trumpet)

::: details Show answer

*Alazun finds a trumpet?* (surprised and doubtful about the whole claim)
:::
