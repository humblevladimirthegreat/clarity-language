# Evidentiality {#evidentiality-sitting}

### Evidentiality {#evidentiality}
<a id="evidential"></a>
<a id="content-readings"></a>

You already pick [LIVE or MEMORY](/live-and-memory#live-vs-memory) when the claim is in your senses now, or you remember sensing it. Five more channels finish the map: similar cases, clues about this case, a proof from premises, a report, or a gut sense. The same walk can then be reported seven ways, and the listener always knows what your claim rests on. Under other role letters the channel roots are ordinary words (`vugol` *to attest*, `debal` *an ear`).

The seven channels come in four kinds. Two are **direct** (already taught): you sensed it yourself, now (LIVE) or earlier (MEMORY). Three are **reasoned**: you worked it out from similar cases (PATTERN), from clues about this case (CLUES), or from premises it must follow from (DEDUCED). One is **received**: someone else said or wrote it (REPORTED). One is **internal**: a sense you cannot fully spell out (INTUITION).

The other channels do not place the event in time. When a reasoned or reported event happened at another time, add a when-frame: [*before* / *after* / *while*](/dependents#time-poles), a clock or date, or a signed offset on the channel. A claim that something **will** happen is a forecast: a channel plus a later offset, so a forecast always says how you know.

**For *could be* with nothing behind it, use:** [MAY](/knowing#may). When English *I think* means you have reasons (you worked it out, you heard it, you sense it), pick the channel for those reasons instead.

| Agebon | Use | English | Same root as | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|--------------|-----|
| **`thugom`** | LIVE: firsthand, sensed now | *live* / *from the scene* | `ugol` *attest* | 👁️‍🗨️: sensing-and-saying while it is in view, in earshot, or on your skin |
| **`thevum`** | MEMORY: firsthand, remembered | *from memory* | `evul` *fishing* | 🎣: you pull something back |
| **`thubem`** | PATTERN: similar cases | *from the pattern* | `ubel` *paw-prints* | 🐾: cases left behind |
| **`theyem`** | CLUES: reasoned from clues about this case | *from the clues* / *apparently* | `eyel` *investigate* | 🕵️: you work the clues |
| **`thanam`** | DEDUCED: it must hold if the premises hold | *it follows* / *by deduction* | `anal` *book* | 📖: a book already holds what it says; a deduction reads out only what the premises hold |
| **`thebam`** | REPORTED: someone else said or wrote it | *so they say* / *according to…* | `ebal` *ear* | 👂: you take in what others pass on |
| **`thabom`** | INTUITION: a gut sense | *my gut says* | `abol` *heart* | ❤️: knowing you cannot fully spell out |

Pick one channel for how you know.

> `zolazun thebam varayel.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-REPORTED | v-write
>
> "Olazun writes, so they say."

> `zolazun thabom vezuvol.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-INTUITION | v-sleep
>
> "Olazun is asleep, my gut says."

A document is REPORTED: someone wrote it, so it reached you from someone else.

**Compare with:** hearing a door is an ordinary `/v/` verb, not REPORTED. A feeling such as fear or hope is an [emotion compose](feelings-permission.md#emotion-compose); INTUITION is a way of knowing, not a feeling about the event. Something a schedule says will happen is someone's [PLAN](/intention#plan-predict). A tale you tell for its own sake is an imagined scene, not a report of fact.

### Evidence strength {#evidence-strength}

The ending on a channel says **how strong the evidence is**. Use **-m** by default. The test is what you could offer if someone asked *how do you know?*:

- **-l**: you could **show** it. You can hand over something checkable apart from you (the document, the clues, the source), and the hearer can weigh it themselves.
- **-m**: you could **describe** it. You can say what you saw, were told, or noticed, and the hearer takes your account on trust.
- **-r**: you could only **gesture** at it. You can point toward it but cannot state it precisely, so a follow-up question would find little behind it.

What counts as showing depends on the channel, so the table names what each channel grades.

Strength grades your evidence, not how sure you feel and not how likely the event is. Likelihood is a separate [stance number](number-markers.md#number-as-stance-by-marker) (`th+70`), so a strong pattern can still give middling odds, and a rumor can claim certainty. Strength is also not distance from the event: that is `th#N`, so a compiled report that is several hands removed can still be **-l**.

<!-- cheat-sheet: knowing-intending -->
| Channel | Graded on | **-l** show | **-m** (default) describe | **-r** gesture |
|---------|-----------|-------------|---------------------------|----------------|
| LIVE | clarity of view | `thugol` a clear, full view | `thugom` | `thugor` a glimpse |
| MEMORY | vividness of memory | `thevul` a vivid memory | `thevum` | `thevur` a hazy memory |
| PATTERN | number and consistency of cases | `thubel` a well-established pattern | `thubem` | `thuber` a few cases |
| CLUES | strength of clues | `theyel` strong clues | `theyem` | `theyer` a weak clue |
| DEDUCED | how far you checked the steps | `thanal` every step could be shown | `thanam` | `thanar` it seems to follow, steps unchecked |
| REPORTED | reliability of the source | `thebal` a reliable source or an authoritative document | `thebam` | `thebar` a rumor or an unofficial note |
| INTUITION | how well you can state the basis | `thabol` a strong gut sense | `thabom` | `thabor` a faint hunch |

Two rows stretch the word *show*. A live view is gone once the moment passes, so for LIVE and MEMORY **-l** means you could narrate it in fine detail. INTUITION is private, so **-l** means you can say what the sense rests on, not that you can hand it over.

DEDUCED grades how far you checked the steps, not the premises. A step can be airtight and still start from a false premise, so a deduction is only as good as what it was deduced from.

> `zolazun thebar velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-REPORTED.weak | v-departure
>
> "Rumor has it Olazun left."

> `zuzozen thevul vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | th-MEMORY.strong | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen walked, I remember it clearly."

### Second-hand and further (`th#N`) {#hand-depth}

English *second-hand*, *third-hand*, *a secondary source* and *a tertiary source* count the hands between the person who was there and you. A channel does not say that. Add a [stance number](number-markers.md#number-as-stance-by-marker) with the rank marker after the channel: **`th#N`** (from 2) is *N-th hand*. The channel says what form the claim reached you in, and the number says how far that is from the event. So `thredol` (`th#2`) is one step removed: told by someone who was there, or written by someone who asked them. `thregel` (`th#3`) is two steps removed: told by someone who was told, or compiled from reports. (cue: **`e`** ≈ order, the place in the chain)

> `zolazun thebam thregel velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-REPORTED | th-3rd-hand | v-departure
>
> "I heard third-hand that Olazun left."

> `zolazun thebal blunonal thredol velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | [th-REPORTED.strong | bl-newspaper] | th-2nd-hand | v-departure
>
> "Olazun left, according to a newspaper article by someone who interviewed people who were there."

Alone, `th#N` says the claim was passed on to you, without saying in what form. After a channel it adds the form: REPORTED (a retelling, a secondary source, or lore passed down), PATTERN (cases you know only from reports), or CLUES (clues you know only from reports). The number counts the hands on the evidence, never the reasoning steps: a conclusion drawn from your own clues is plain CLUES. First-hand has no number, because that is a channel: `th#N` with LIVE or MEMORY (`thugom`, `thevum`) is not used. The usual number endings apply: **-m** *about N-th hand* (`thregem`), **-r** with a digit *the same depth as before* (`threger`). Digitless `threr` under a question asks *how many hands?*

**Compare with:** `th_N` names *which* numbered source the claim rests on; `th#N` counts *how many hands* it passed through. Reliability of the one source you heard from is the ending on the channel (`thebal` / `thebar`).

### Source (`bl-` after the channel) {#source}

English *according to Uzozen* and *going by the camera* name **who or what you got the claim from**. Put that noun in a `bl-` word right after the channel. The channel is its [host](/clause#extra-nouns), so the `bl-` word is the source, not someone who receives anything.

> `zolazun thebam bluzozen velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | [th-REPORTED | bl-Uzozen] | v-departure
>
> "Olazun left, according to Uzozen."

> `zolazun thugom blogewol vezuvol.`
>
> z-Olazun | [th-LIVE | bl-camera] | v-sleep
>
> "Olazun is asleep, going by the camera." (watching the feed now)

What you put in that `/b/` decides what it does. A person or other noun is the source: a speaker or a document on REPORTED, a screen or a recording on LIVE and MEMORY.

**Compare with:** the source is where the claim came to you from; the claim is still yours to pass on. A numbered footnote source is `th_N`.

When you don't name the source, `blumon` (*someone*) leaves it open. Whether you are holding back who said it or have lost track changes how far to trust the report, and the channel alone does not say it. Say which in a sentence of its own:

| Agebon | English |
|--------|---------|
| `zolazun thebam blumon velurul.` | *Olazun left, according to someone.* (says neither) |
| `zolazun thebam blumon velurul. zuwon vanam dorl zar.` | *Olazun left, according to someone. I know who.* |
| `zolazun thebam blumon velurul. zuwon vanam vul dorl zar.` | *Olazun left, according to someone. I don't know who.* (a rumor) |

> `zolazun thebam blumon velurul. zuwon vanam dorl zar.`
>
> z-Olazun | [th-REPORTED | bl-someone] | v-departure . z-speaker | v-knowledge | d-whether-clause | z-something
>
> "Olazun left, according to someone. I know who."

### Forecasts (`blral`) {#forecast}

English *will* can make a forecast: a claim that something will happen. Agebon has no *will* word for this. Use a channel and put **`blral`** right after it in `/b/`: a number with a plus sign and no digits, meaning *some time after now*. Because a forecast needs a channel, it always says what it rests on; for a hunch, that channel is INTUITION. The mirror, **`blrul`**, is *some time before now*, for a past claim with no measured amount.

> `thubem blral verulal.`
>
> [th-PATTERN | bl-later] | v-rain
>
> "It will rain, going by the pattern."

> `zolazun thabor blral velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | [th-INTUITION.weak | bl-later] | v-departure
>
> "I have a faint hunch Olazun will leave."

<a id="forecast-question"></a>

In a [question](/questions#question), the channel asks what the **listener's** answer rests on: *going by the pattern you see, will it rain?* The listener answers with that channel or names a different one. Pick the channel you expect them to have: CLUES (`theyem`) when they would work it out from clues, REPORTED (`thebam`) when they would go by what they heard or read.

> `yol thubem blral verulal.`
>
> y-question | [th-PATTERN | bl-later] | v-rain
>
> "Will it rain, going by the pattern you see?"

On a [feeling](feelings-permission.md#emotion-compose) (a sake word with a locus and a motion ending, before **`goyem`**), the channel and offset date the **stimulus**. On a [feeling with no object](feelings-permission.md#feeling-no-object), they date a stimulus you leave unnamed. The feeling itself stays now.

**Compare with:** [PLAN](/intention#plan-predict) (`thagem`) says what you **intend** to do. Your own intention needs no channel. A forecast is a claim about the world. *Could be* with nothing behind it is [MAY](/knowing#may), not a forecast.

### Evidence clause (`blarl`) {#evidence-clause}

English *judging by*, *going by*, and *given that* name the **grounds** for a claim: the fact that tells you it holds. The three reasoned channels take grounds: CLUES (`theyem`, from clues), PATTERN (`thubem`, from cases that repeat) and DEDUCED (`thanam`, from premises). Each one takes **`blarl`** as its `/b/`. The main sentence stops after `blarl`, and the next sentence is the grounds ([dependent clauses](/dependents#dependent-clauses)). You state the claim, the strength of your grounds, and the grounds themselves in one sentence.

> `zolazun vanuvul theyem blarl zuzozen varemul.`
>
> z-Olazun | v-punch | [th-CLUES | bl-that-clause] | z-Uzozen | v-scream
>
> "Olazun punched, judging by the fact that Uzozen is screaming."

Grounds are not a cause. With [`thahum blarl`](iff-fault.md#only-because), the clause after `blarl` is what made the event happen. With a reasoned channel, it is only how you know the event happened: the screaming did not make Olazun punch.

> `zuzozen varemul thahum blarl zolazun vanuvul.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-scream | [th-because | bl-that-clause] | z-Olazun | v-punch
>
> "Uzozen screams because Olazun punches."

<!-- cheat-sheet: knowing-intending -->
| You say | The clause after `blarl` is | Form |
|---------|----------------------------|------|
| what made it happen | the cause | `thahum blarl` |
| what has to hold for it to happen | the condition | `thohom blarl` / `thebom blarl` / `thehem blarl` |
| how you know it happened | your grounds | `theyem blarl` / `thubem blarl` / `thanam blarl` |

The ending on the channel grades the grounds ([evidence strength](#evidence-strength)): **-l** for strong clues or a well-established pattern, **-m** by default, **-r** for a weak clue or a few cases. Strong clues still fall short of a proof: when the claim **must** hold if the grounds do, use DEDUCED.

> `verulal theyel blarl zugalal gegulol.`
>
> v-rain | [th-CLUES.strong | bl-that-clause] | [z-cloud | g-gray]
>
> "It is raining, judging by the gray clouds." (strong clues)

For a **forecast**, keep the [offset](#forecast) and put `blarl` after it. The pattern is what you go by, and the grounds clause says what you see now.

> `verulal thubel blral blarl zugalal gegulol.`
>
> v-rain | [th-PATTERN.strong | [bl-later | bl-that-clause]] | [z-cloud | g-gray]
>
> "It will rain, going by the well-established pattern, given the gray clouds."

You assert the grounds, the way you assert a cause. If you are not sure of them, say so inside the grounds clause with its own channel:

> `zolazun vanuvul theyem blarl zuzozen thugom varemul.`
>
> z-Olazun | v-punch | [th-CLUES | bl-that-clause] | z-Uzozen | th-LIVE | v-scream
>
> "Olazun punched, judging by the fact that Uzozen is screaming, which I can hear."

**Compare with:** a `bl-` noun after any channel is its [source](#source) (`thebam bluzozen` *according to Uzozen*), and no other channel takes `blarl`. A cause or condition word may not come before the channel. Put the channel first (`theyem thahum blarl`) to say both that you inferred it and that it was caused. *Could be* with no grounds is [MAY](/knowing#may).

### Carrying a channel forward {#carry-forward}
<a id="channel-frame-carry"></a>

English tells a story with one *they say* or *I remember* at the start, and every sentence after it rests on the same ground. Agebon does the same. Once you put a channel on a claim, your later claims with no channel keep it: the channel is your **frame**. You name the channel once, then keep talking.

> `zolazun thebam velurul. zolazun varuvel. zolazun vezuyol.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-REPORTED | v-departure . z-Olazun | v-run . z-Olazun | v-sing
>
> "Olazun left, so they say. Olazun ran. Olazun sang." (all three are what you heard)

The frame is **yours**. It covers only your own claims, and someone else who replies never inherits it. Their reply does not end it either: when you go on telling, your frame is still in force.

**Which sentences take the frame:** your statements with no channel of their own, including a statement after a call, an interjection, or an answer word (`yael zolazun varuvel.` *yes, Olazun ran* keeps your frame). A [question](/questions#question) does not take it, because a channel in a question asks about the listener's grounds ([forecasts](#forecast-question)). A command, request, or prohibition does not take it, because it is not a claim. Neither ends the frame, so your next statement picks it up again.

> `zolazun thebam velurul. yol zolazun varuvel. zolazun vezuyol.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-REPORTED | v-departure . y-question | z-Olazun | v-run . z-Olazun | v-sing
>
> "Olazun left, so they say. Did Olazun run? Olazun sang." (the singing is hearsay too)

**What does not end it:** a new [topic](topic.md#topic) or a return to one, the [sentence linkers](sentence-linkers.md#sentence-linkers) (*therefore*, *however*, *meanwhile*, *next*, *but*), clause joins, asides, or MAY. A story moves from one person to the next.

> `zuzozen thebam vowurel. xolazun zalon vuhohol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | th-REPORTED | v-walk . x-Olazun | z-TOPIC | v-sit
>
> "Uzozen walked, so they say. Now, about Olazun: Olazun sat." (the sitting is hearsay too)

**What ends it:**

- **A new frame.** Any channel replaces it.
- **The statement word, written out.** **`yal`** (or **`yam`** / **`yar`**) before a body says you are back in your own voice, with no frame. It is the one place where writing the usually silent [statement word](/firm-soft#speech-act) changes the meaning. In [two stacked act words](speech-acts.md#stacked-acts), it counts only as the first word, the act you really do: a rhetorical **`yal yol`** is your own claim and ends the frame, but a quiz **`yol yal`** does not.
- **`xuvedam`** *by the way*: a digression leaves what you were telling. To go back, name the channel again.
- **A goodbye**, which ends the conversation.

> `zolazun thebam velurul. yal zolazun vezuvol.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-REPORTED | v-departure . y-statement | z-Olazun | v-sleep
>
> "Olazun left, so they say. Olazun is asleep." (the second claim no longer rests on the report)

> `zolazun thebam velurul. yal yol zar vegemal.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-REPORTED | v-departure . y-statement | y-question | z-wh | v-care
>
> "Olazun left, so they say. Who cares?" (your own claim; the report no longer carries)

A [quote](/spans#writing) keeps its speaker's frame: nothing carries into it from outside, and your own frame resumes after it.

### Deduction (`thanam`) {#deduction}
<a id="deduced"></a>

English *it follows that*, *P, so Q* and *by deduction* claim more than good evidence: if the premises hold, the conclusion **must** hold. Put DEDUCED (**`thanam`**) on the conclusion and the premises after **`blarl`**, as in any [evidence clause](#evidence-clause). Join several premises with a [clause join](/joins#clause-joins); a join after a stand-in stays in the dependent, so every premise is grounds. You assert the premises and the conclusion.

> `zuzozen vuduyel thanam blarl zual gwezuduyel vuduyel xam zuzozen gwezuduyel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-teach | [th-DEDUCED | bl-that-clause] | [[z-everything | g-role-agent-teach] | v-teach | x-and.open | z-Uzozen | g-role-agent-teach]
>
> "Uzozen teaches, since every teacher teaches and Uzozen is a teacher."

To claim only that the conclusion follows, put a [pole](iff-fault.md#only-because) between the channel and **`blarl`**. The pole is the ordinary *if*: the premises are enough for the conclusion, and you do not claim them. DEDUCED says you know that link by deduction. Now you assert nothing but the link.

> `zuzozen vuduyel thanam thohom blarl zual gwezuduyel vuduyel xam zuzozen gwezuduyel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-teach | th-DEDUCED | [th-if | bl-that-clause] | [[z-everything | g-role-agent-teach] | v-teach | x-and.open | z-Uzozen | g-role-agent-teach]
>
> "If every teacher teaches and Uzozen is a teacher, it follows that Uzozen teaches."

<!-- cheat-sheet: knowing-intending -->
| You claim | Form | You assert |
|-----------|------|------------|
| *P, so Q* | Q `thanam blarl` P | P and Q |
| *Q follows from P* | Q `thanam thohom blarl` P | only the link |
| *P and Q come to the same thing* | Q `thanam thehem blarl` P | only the link, both ways |
| *P rules out Q* | Q denied with `vul`, then `thanam thohom blarl` P | only that P and Q cannot both hold |

The ending grades how far you checked the steps ([evidence strength](#evidence-strength)): **`thanal`** when you could write out every step, **`thanam`** when you could name the premises and the rule, **`thanar`** when it only seems to follow.

When the premises come first, open the conclusion with the linker **`xahel`** *it follows that* ([sentence linkers](sentence-linkers.md#sentence-linkers)). It reaches back to the one sentence before it, so join every premise into that sentence.

> `zual gwezuduyel vuduyel xam zuzozen gwezuduyel. xahel zuzozen vuduyel.`
>
> [[z-everything | g-role-agent-teach] | v-teach | x-and.open | z-Uzozen | g-role-agent-teach] . x-it-follows | z-Uzozen | v-teach
>
> "Every teacher teaches, and Uzozen is a teacher. It follows that Uzozen teaches."

A linker is not a channel, so the conclusion keeps your [frame](#carry-forward): a deduction from a report is still REPORTED.

**Compare with:** strong clues (`theyel`) make a claim likely, but it can still fail with every clue true. A deduction cannot fail unless a premise does. *Because* (`thahum`) says what made it happen; a deduction says only what must be true.

### Whose view (holder) {#holder}
<a id="holder-seam"></a>

English *Uzozen thinks…*, *she's upset*, and *he doubts it* report what is in someone else's mind as if you could see it. In Agebon, you name whose view a sentence gives (the **holder**) by joining their name onto a channel or [MAY](/knowing#may). Keep that word's own last letter (**-l**, **-m**, or **-r**) and write the name straight after it: `theyem` plus `uzozen` is `theyemuzozen`. The joining letter is the **seam**. This is the only way to name a holder, so you can never report someone's view without saying how you know it. (cue: the name hangs off the way you know it)

> `zolazun theyemuzozen velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-CLUES-Uzozen | v-departure
>
> "I gather Uzozen thinks Olazun left."

> `zolazun thebamuzozen velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-REPORTED-Uzozen | v-departure
>
> "I hear Uzozen thinks Olazun left."

> `zolazun thomamuzozen velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-MAY-Uzozen | v-departure
>
> "Maybe Uzozen thinks Olazun left."

The seam letter keeps its meaning: [evidence strength](#evidence-strength) on a channel, the [hold](/knowing#may-hold) on MAY. The name keeps its own **-n** at the end. **-n** is never a seam.

> `zolazun theyeluzozen velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-CLUES.strong-Uzozen | v-departure
>
> "I have strong clues that Uzozen thinks Olazun left."

> `zolazun thebaruzozen velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-REPORTED.weak-Uzozen | v-departure
>
> "Rumor has it Uzozen thinks Olazun left."

A holder [carries forward](#carry-forward) like a channel. After it, your bare claims go on giving that person's view, until a new frame, so you can tell a stretch of the story as they see it.

> `zolazun theyemuzozen velurul. zolazun vezuvol.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-CLUES-Uzozen | v-departure . z-Olazun | v-sleep
>
> "I gather Uzozen thinks Olazun left. Olazun is asleep." (the sleeping is Uzozen's view too)

The holder word ends the way a noun for that person would. **-n** is a name. **-r** [resumes](/pronouns#resume-r) someone already named, which is English *he* / *she* / *they*. Add [associative **-x**](/plurality#associative) after the ending for the person and their associates. A kind of person on **-lx** is some people of that kind. A [tag](tag-pronouns.md#tag-pronouns) never goes in the seam, since the seam letter before its `w` would end a syllable mid-word. For a holder you don't name, name the kind of person: `zolazun theyemeyol velurul.` is *I gather someone thinks Olazun left*.

> `zuzozen vowurel. zolazun theyemuzozer velurul.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk . z-Olazun | th-CLUES-←Uzozen | v-departure
>
> "Uzozen walks. I gather they think Olazun left."

> `zolazun theyemuzozenx velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-CLUES-Uzozen-x | v-departure
>
> "I gather Uzozen and co. think Olazun left."

> `zolazun theyemeyolx velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-CLUES-person-x | v-departure
>
> "I gather some people think Olazun left."

The holder may be the [topic pronoun](topic.md#topic-pronoun), whoever the conversation is about now, or the [generic pronoun](/pronouns#generic-pronoun), the generic view. The generic pronoun takes no **-x**.

> `xuzozen theyemalon zolazun velurul.`
>
> x-Uzozen | th-CLUES-TOPIC | z-Olazun | v-departure
>
> "Now, about Uzozen: I gather Uzozen thinks Olazun left."

> `theyemeyon zolazun velurul.`
>
> th-CLUES-ONE | z-Olazun | v-departure
>
> "I gather one would think Olazun left."

**Someone else's plan** goes the same way. [PLAN](/intention#plan-predict) alone is yours; after a holder it is theirs, and the seam word says how you know what they plan. After **`uem`** ([contrary to a stance](prescription.md#contrary-to-stance)), write the holder word, then the plan, for *against their plan*.

> `zuzozen thebamuzozen thagem vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | th-REPORTED-Uzozen | th-plan-itinerary | v-walk
>
> "I hear Uzozen plans to walk."

> `zuzozen theyemolazun thagem vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | th-CLUES-Olazun | th-plan-itinerary | v-walk
>
> "I gather Olazun plans for Uzozen to walk."

The seam word keeps its `/b/` source like any channel. A schedule is not a forecast: it is a plan you know from a record, so put the record in `/b/`:

> `zuzozen thebaluzozen blagorel thagem vezuyel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [th-REPORTED.strong-Uzozen | bl-calendar] | th-plan-itinerary | v-tell
>
> "Going by the official calendar, Uzozen plans to speak."

> `zuzozen vowurel uem theyemolazun thagem.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [contrary-to | th-CLUES-Olazun | th-plan-itinerary]
>
> "Uzozen walks, against Olazun's plan, as far as I can tell."

**Someone else's motive** goes the same way. A [motive](prescription.md#sake-preference) word alone names what the act serves for you; it does not say why the actor does it. After a holder, the motive is the holder's, and the seam word says how you know their reason.

> `zuzozen vezuyel thawothom theyemuzozen.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-tell | th-relatedness-motive-any-term | th-CLUES-Uzozen
>
> "I gather Uzozen talks to feel connected."

**The seam word is yours; the rest of the clause is the holder's.** The word that carries the name says how **you** know about the holder's view: you inferred it, heard it, or guess it. You claim nothing about whether the holder is right, and nothing about your own view. Everything else in the clause, wherever it sits, belongs to the holder:

| In the clause | With no holder | With a holder |
|---------------|----------------|---------------|
| The event | you assert it | what the holder takes to be so; you do not assert it |
| MAY, CAUSE poles, PLAN, prescription | yours | the holder's |
| [Sake](/sakes) words and [emotion compose](feelings-permission.md#emotion-compose), with their endings | your stake and feeling | the holder's stake and feeling |
| A `/ɡ/` sake's *my* reading ([personal possession](/sakes#personal-possession)) | your belonging | the holder's belonging |
| Another evidential | how you know | how the holder knows |
| The grounds after an inferring or pattern host's [`blarl`](#evidence-clause) | grounds you assert | the holder's grounds; you do not assert them |
| A comparison [bar](comparative-arity.md#bars) | your expectation | the holder's expectation |

> `zonal wawothumol goyem blolazun theyemuzozen.`
>
> [z-memo | [[w-relatedness-unmet-modifiable-AIMED-STILL | g-stimulus] | bl-Olazun]] | th-CLUES-Uzozen
>
> "I gather Uzozen resents Olazun over the memo, holding it in."

> `zolazun theyemuzozen thugom velurul.`
>
> z-Olazun | th-CLUES-Uzozen | th-LIVE | v-departure
>
> "I gather Uzozen is watching Olazun leave."

A [feeling with no object](feelings-permission.md#feeling-no-object) works the same way: the holder makes it someone else's feeling.

> `thuwathuruor theyemolazun.`
>
> th-competence-unmet-passing-UNPLACED-SURGING | th-CLUES-Olazun
>
> "I gather Olazun is anxious."

With the interlocutors as holder, the view is one you both share. On MEMORY this is the history you both know, so a sweeping *never* or *always* covers only the times you both remember:

> `zawan vebal hal thevumayan.`
>
> z-listener | v-hear | h-never | th-MEMORY-interlocutors
>
> "From what I remember of what we both saw, you've never listened."

MEMORY says the claim rests on your memory, and the holder says the view is one you both share. You can't know the listener's memory, only claim the view is shared, and the listener can answer with a memory of their own. `ham thevumayan` adds *as far as I know*.

Pronouns and speech moves stay yours: **`uwon`** is still you, and the sentence is still your turn. The holder's view is their view **now**. A channel with a holder still takes its own `/b/`, so `thebamuzozen blral` is *I hear Uzozen expects it*. A holder is not a [source](#source): a source in `/b/` is where a claim came to you from, and the claim stays yours to pass on; a holder makes the claim the holder's view.

**One clause, one holder.** The holder covers its clause and any dependent sentence inside it; a new `/x/` sentence is yours again unless it has its own seam word. A dependent with its own seam word switches holder for that sentence only. That is how you take a stance on someone else's stance: your stance in the main sentence, theirs in a dependent.

> `wawothum goyem zarl zolazun wawothum goyem theyemuzozen.`
>
> [w-relatedness-unmet-modifiable | g-stimulus] | z-that-clause | [z-Olazun | [w-relatedness-unmet-modifiable | g-stimulus]] | th-CLUES-Uzozen
>
> "It sits badly with me that, as I gather, Olazun sits badly with Uzozen."

The first sake word has no holder, so it is yours. The second is Uzozen's.

Only the seven channels and MAY take a holder: they are the ways you can have access to someone else's view. When they told you in their own words, a [cite](/spans#writing) keeps their wording instead.

**Compare with:** being upset **for** Uzozen is your own feeling about Uzozen's stake (the ON-BEHALF locus in [emotion compose](feelings-permission.md#emotion-compose)); with a holder the feeling is Uzozen's. *On behalf of* Uzozen is [proxy](/relations#proxy) (`hanom`). A `/b/` on [permission](feelings-permission.md#permission) or [consent](feelings-permission.md#consent) names someone who granted or agreed openly, not a holder whose mind you report. A [dictionary compound](/compounds#lexical-compounds) also joins with an ending letter, but it is a listed word; the holder seam is built on the spot, and only after these closed hosts.
