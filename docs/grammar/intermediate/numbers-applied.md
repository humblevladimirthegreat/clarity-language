# Numbers in use
<a id="numbers-applied"></a>

A number word can do more than count. This page shows how to write a code or label (*room 12*), a clock time or date, an amount of a unit (*two meters*), a range (*3 to 5*), and a percent. How the number word itself is built (markers, endings, writing) is in [numbers](/numbers); unspecified amounts, exponents, and the spoken comma are in [digitless and exponents](digitless-exponents.md).

### Letter codes {#letter-codes}

English names many things with letters as well as digits: a vitamin (*B12*), a flat (*4B*), a jet (*F-16*). A **`_`** code may hold letters too. Write each letter in lowercase, in shorthand, just like the digits: `g_b12`.

> `zuzozen dobebul g_b12 vagedal.`
>
> 🔊 `zuzozen dobebul grebubadol vagedal.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [d-pill | g-_b12] | v-eat
>
> "Uzozen takes a B12 pill."

Say each character as one syllable, after the marker **`re`**:

- A digit is its digit syllable (`ba` … `mo`).
- A consonant is its [letter name](phonotactics.md#letter-names) (`bu` … `me`, `tho`, `wa`, `yu`). A letter name never sounds like a digit, because it takes the [opposite vowel](/phonology#opposite-vowels): `bu` is *b*, `ba` is *1*.
- A vowel is its own sound after a short break, the catch in English *uh-oh*. The spelled form writes the break as `'`. Without it, `bu` + `u` would run into one long vowel, and `re` + `o` would sound like another marker.

<!-- cheat-sheet: numbers -->
| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| `z_b12` (🔊 `zrebubadol`) | letter, then digits | *B12* |
| `z_4b` (🔊 `zrehubul`) | digit, then letter | *4B* |
| `z_vba` (🔊 `zrevebu'al`) | vowel after a break | *VBA* |
| `z_e1` (🔊 `zre'ebal`) | code that starts with a vowel | *E1* |
| `z_thw` (🔊 `zrethowal`) | `th` is one letter | *THW* |
| `z_2,1` (🔊 `zredothebal`) | two fields | *version 2.1* |

Rules for letters in a code:

- No comma between letters and digits: the switch is audible (`z_b12`, not `z_b,12`).
- **`x`** is never a character, so a code before **`x`** is always a numeric prefix or plural **-x** (`z_b0x` covers B0 to B9, as in [plural labels](/digit-strings#plural-labels)).
- Only the **`_`** marker takes letters: a count, a rank, a sign, or a date has none.
- In a code, `e` is always the vowel letter, never an exponent.
- A letter at the end of a code is a character, not the ending. A code with a letter always shows its ending by a mark: `z_nl` is **-l**, `z@_nl` is **-n**, `z=_nl` is **-r**.

**A foreign code** keeps its letters when each one has an Agebon letter. English *p*, *t*, *k*, *s*, and *f* are the voiceless sounds of `b`, `d`, `g`, `z`, and `v`, so they take those letters: *F-16* is `z_v16`, and *P2* is `z_b2`. A code with a letter that has no Agebon sound (*c*, *i*, *j*, *q*, *x*) stays a [span](/spans#loans): *FBI* is <code>z@&lt;FBI&gt;</code>.
### Measure phrases {#measure-phrases}
<a id="units"></a>
<a id="unit-amount"></a>
<a id="si-units"></a>

To say **how much of a unit** (*two meters*, *three hours*), use the unit's noun and put the amount on it as a `/ɡ/` number, the same way a count sits on a noun. Each SI or everyday unit is the [abstract](/word-endings#abstract-m) sense of a published root, so the unit noun ends in **-m**. Say that word, not a Latin abbreviation (`m`, `kg`, `s`). Once the unit phrase has been mentioned, resume it with **-r**.

> `zuzozen bezerem gradol vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [b-meter | g-two] | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen walks two meters."

For a price (*for five grams*), put the same measure phrase in a `bl-` word after the [exchange](/relations#exchange) word **`heham`**.

The amount hosts nothing, so no `bl-` word comes after it. A plain `/b/` there is the recipient.

<!-- cheat-sheet: numbers -->
| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| `bezerem g+2` | extra noun (`/b/`) | *two meters* |
| `zazerem g+3`; `deberom g+5` | subject / object / … | *three seconds*; *five grams* |
| `gezerem g+2` after a noun | modifier on a noun | rare; prefer unit as `/b/` or a freestanding noun phrase |

The amount takes the usual number endings, so **-m** makes it fuzzy (`gradom` ≈ *about two*). The **unit** takes ordinary word endings.

Spelled out, `bagorum g+3` is `bagorum gragel` *three hours*.

### Ranges {#ranges}
<a id="number-ranges"></a>
<a id="numeric-ranges"></a>
<a id="from-to"></a>

To give a range of numbers (*from 3 to 5*, *3 through 5*), write the two ends with the [hook](/hooks) **`al`** between them: `A al B`. No join word closes it, and no extra word names the scale.

> `zragel al zrazal.`
>
> z-three | through | z-five
>
> "From 3 to 5." (both ends included)

Between two numbers, *3, including 5* has nothing to include into, so **`al`** reads as *through*: the band from A reaching and including B. This reading needs both endpoints to be the **same kind** of number (both amounts, both ranks, or both labels / times), each with digits. A number with no digits keeps the ordinary hook (`zral al zrazal` = *some number of them, including 5*), and so do mixed kinds.

To count a range of things, put the endpoints on the counted noun, the same way one count sits on it. The noun names the line:

> `zudozol gragel al grazal vowurel.`
>
> [z-dog | g-three] | through | g-five | v-walk
>
> "Three to five dogs walk."

> `zagorum gragel al grazal.`
>
> [z-hour | g-three] | through | g-five
>
> "Three to five hours."

A word after **B** describes **B** alone, and a word on the counted noun describes the whole range. To add a detail to the range itself, put a `/w/` word right before the hook ([detail on the hook](ending-grids.md#hook-w)).

**Exclude an end with `ul`.** *3, except 5* reads as the band that stops short of 5: the hook excludes the endpoint **after** it. The ends are read in the order you say them, so to exclude the low end, say it second.

<!-- cheat-sheet: numbers -->
| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| `zragel al zrazal` | both ends in | *3 to 5* (`[3, 5]`) |
| `zragel ul zrazal` | 5 left out | *3 up to but not including 5* (`[3, 5)`) |
| `zrazal ul zragel` | 3 left out | *more than 3, up to 5* (`(3, 5]`) |
| `zragel am zrazal` | soft ends | *roughly 3 to 5* |
| `zragel um zrazal` | soft exclusion | *3 up to about 5, not quite 5* |
| `zragel ual zrazal` | both ends out | *strictly between 3 and 5* (`(3, 5)`) |
| `zragel uol zrazal` | outside the band | *below 3 or above 5* |

**`ual`** and **`uol`** are the [range hooks](ending-grids.md#ranges), which also work on scales that are not numbers. An endpoint can still be fuzzy on its own (`zragel al zrazam` = *3 to about 5*).

**Compare with:** `zragel zrazal zaol` is *3, then 5*: two values in order, not a band. `zragel zrazal zal` is *3 and 5*. `zuzozen zolazun zol gamahum` ranks **people** on a scale. A place path (*from a station to a train*) uses two [place](of-relations.md#spatial-path) relation words, each with its `/b/`, not this hook.

### Percent and percentage points {#percent-and-percentage-points}

To give a percent of a whole (*25% of the cats*), end a **count** number (`ra` / `ru`) with **`yo`**. Put it on the noun for the whole, in the same `/ɡ/` slot as a count (`zagozolx gragel` = *three cats*).

> `zagozolx g+25%.`
>
> 🔊 `zagozolx gradozayol.`
>
> z-cat-x | g-25yo
>
> "25% of the cats."

<!-- cheat-sheet: numbers -->
| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| **`yo`** | percent (portion of a whole) | everyday percent figure (`25` in `25%`); those digits ÷ 100 |
| **`yu`** | percentage points (point-scale amount or delta) | point figure (`2` in `+2%*`); those digits ÷ 100 |

Digits follow the [normal digit rules](digitless-exponents.md#exponents) (including optional **`ye`** inside the percent/point reading). Say the digits you mean (`g+100%` → spoken `grabamomoyol`).

Order: integer digits, optional **`ye`** + fractional digits, then **`yo`** or **`yu`**. **`yo`** / **`yu`** sit on **`ra`** / **`ru`** only. At most one **`yo`** or **`yu`** group in the number word; leave **`wa`** / **`wu`** off that word. Endings still apply (**-l** exact, **-m** about, **-n** conventional label, **-r** resume).

In [preferred writing](number-markers.md#writing-preferred-shorthand): **`%`** → speech **`yo`**; **`%*`** → speech **`yu`** (sign still on **`ra`** / **`ru`**, written **`+`** / **`-`**).

#### Fractions (*half of*) {#fractions}

To name one part of a whole (*half of the cats*, *a third of the tea*), put a minus number **`g-N`** right after the noun. Here the **`ru`** marker divides, as it does in `/h/` **`h-N`**: with N of 2 or more, the word names one part in N.

> `zagozolx grudol vuhohol.`
>
> [z-cat-x | g-half-of] | v-sit
>
> "Half of the cats sit."

> `zududal grugel vuhohol.`
>
> [z-tea | g-third-of] | v-sit
>
> "A third of the tea sits there."

To say how many parts, put the count first and the fraction after it: **`g+N`** then **`g-M`** is *N parts of M*. English *two thirds* is the same two words in the same order.

> `zagozolx gradol grugel vuhohol.`
>
> [z-cat-x | g-two | g-third-of] | v-sit
>
> "Two thirds of the cats sit."

**`g-N`** alone is a fraction only right after a plain noun. After a [measure](#measure-phrases) unit, a minus number is still a negative amount (`bezerem grudol` *minus 2 meters*). The count makes the fraction plain in any slot, a unit included: a count then a minus is never a negative amount, since a unit takes one amount.

> `zuzozen vowurel bagorum grabal grugel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [b-hour | g-one | g-third-of]
>
> "Uzozen walks for a third of an hour."

*Half* of a unit also has a decimal route (`bezerem g+0.5`).
