# Ending grids {#ending-grids-sitting}

### Ending grids

Beginner already used closed **-l** and open **-m** for how complete B is, or how this sentence attaches to prior talk.

#### In-clause

<!-- cheat-sheet: joins-hooks -->
| | **-l** | **-m** |
|--|--------|--------|
| **a** (*including*) | `al`: including B | `am`: including B, and maybe more |
| **o** (*rather*) | `ol`: or rather B | `om`: or rather B, and maybe more |
| **e** (*instead*) | `el`: instead (B in place of A) | `em`: B instead of A, B being one of several possible replacements |
| **u** (*except*) | `ul`: except B | `um`: except B, and maybe more |

#### Discourse

At the front of a sentence, **-m** says this sentence is one of several you could say there: one more point among others, one way to reword, one possible replacement, one exception among others.

<!-- cheat-sheet: joins-hooks -->
| | **-l** | **-m** |
|--|--------|--------|
| **a** (*additionally*) | `al`: *Additionally, …* | `am`: *Among other things, …* |
| **o** (*in other words*) | `ol`: *In other words, …* | `om`: *To put it one way, …* |
| **e** (*instead*) | `el`: *Instead, …* | `em`: *Instead, for instance, …* |
| **u** (*except*) | `ul`: *Except, …* | `um`: *Except, among other exceptions, …* |

To make a whole `A HOOK B` phrase into a name (a store, a title, a handle), give the hook **-n** ([titled phrases](proper-names.md#titled-phrases)). Otherwise use **-l** or **-m**. The two sides keep their usual endings (kinds **-l**, abstracts **-m**, a name **-n**).

> `dedudal en dogudal.`
>
> NAME[d-tea | instead | d-coffee]
>
> "Coffee-Instead-of-Tea" (a cafe name)

**Compare with:** *no dog* / *not X* uses a prefixed [join](/joins#join-series-ending-shared) (`zodozel zul`). The hook **`ul`**, with no role letter, always takes a B out of A.

> `zodozel zul.`
>
> [z-dog | z-not]
>
> "No dog."

### Abstract landmarks (*in a crisis*, *out of debt*) {#abstract-landmarks}

English uses the same prepositions for states, plans, and conditions as for places: *in a crisis*, *on a policy*, *out of debt*. Ageben does the same. Put a noun in its abstract **-m** sense right after an extra-noun hook, and the hook keeps the picture it has with a place: a state is something you are **in**, a plan is a surface you stand **on**, a tendency is somewhere you head **toward**, a condition is something you get **out of**. So a new pairing needs no new word: read the hook's picture in an abstract space.

> `zalazun vavobol ual baram.`
>
> z-Alazun | v-go | [out-of | b-debt]
>
> "Alazun gets out of debt."

> `zazozon varuyol uol bonam.`
>
> z-Azozon | v-write | [against | b-policy]
>
> "Azozon writes against the policy."

| Hook | Abstract landmark | Example | English |
|------|-------------------|---------|---------|
| **`al`** *in* | a state or condition you are inside | `al babowum` | *in a crisis* |
| **`ael`** *on* | a plan, rule, or footing you stand on | `ael bonam` | *on policy* |
| **`el`** *at* | a point of concern or a status | `el bobevam` | *at issue* |
| **`oel`** *toward* | a tendency you head toward | `oel buzebum` | *toward a habit* |
| **`ul`** *from* | what something comes from or owes to | `ul buzobam` | *from heritage* |
| **`ual`** *out of* | a condition you leave | `ual baram` | *out of debt* |
| **`uel`** *through* | something you last through | `uel babowum` | *through the crisis* |
| **`ol`** *for* | what you favor | `ol bonam` | *for the policy* |
| **`aol`** *using* | what you go by | `aol bogarem` | *by tradition* |
| **`uol`** *against* | what you oppose | `uol bonam` | *against the policy* |

The frames read the same way, with the landmark as the general setting: `am babowum` *amid the crisis*, `uom bonam` *contrary to the policy*. A reading that needs a different picture is not a hook reading; say it with its own word.

**Compare with:** the landmark's **-m** picks its abstract sense (`babowum` *catastrophe*, where `babowul` is *bomb*); the hook's own **-m** (`am`, `uom`) makes the landmark the setting. The two choices are separate, and a word can take both.
### Detail on the hook (`/w/`) {#hook-w}

To add detail to the hook itself (*hesitantly excepting*, *never excepting*), put a `/w/` word right before the hook. A `/w/` word always describes the word **right after** it, and a hook takes one the same way a `/ɡ/` or `/h/` word does. A [restrictor](/restrictors) works there too. The hook's right-hand piece follows as usual.

On a same-role hook, the detail describes the *except* (or *including*, *rather*, *instead*). An `/h/` word in the clause still describes the verb.

> `zuvayal wuzugom ul zazozon.`
>
> z-family | [w-hesitation | except] | z-Azozon
>
> "The family, hesitantly excepting Azozon."

> `zuvayal wal ul zazozon.`
>
> z-family | [w-never | except] | z-Azozon
>
> "The family, never excepting Azozon."

A surprise word on the *including* hook gives English *even*: **`wemem al`** adds B and says openly that B is the one nobody expected.

> `zalazun wemem al zazozon vowurol.`
>
> z-Alazun | [w-amazement | including] | z-Azozon | v-walk
>
> "Alazun walks, and even Azozon does."

**Compare with:** `zuvayal ul zazozon hadurom vuguyol` puts haste on the **cooking**, not on the except.

On an extra-noun hook, `/w/` goes before the hook and `/b/` still comes right after it. A `/ɡ/` word after the landmark still describes the landmark.

> `zodozel vezuvel wal al bohehal.`
>
> z-dog | v-sleep | [[w-never | in] | b-house]
>
> "A dog sleeps, never in a house."

A discourse hook takes `/w/` the same way: `welazem al zazozon vowurol` is *Additionally, very much so, Azozon walks.* A `/b/` right after the hook still makes it an extra-noun hook, not a discourse hook.

### Ranges (`oel` / `ual` / `uol`) {#ranges}

When the items sit on a line with an order (people in a queue, days, chapters, numbers), English *A through B* names a **range**: A, B, and everything between. Put a stacked hook between two words in the same role: `A HOOK B`. Between same-role words these hooks name only ranges; with `/b/` on the right and no `/b/` on the left, they are [extra-noun hooks](/hooks#extra-noun) (*toward*, *out of*, *against*).

> `zazozon oel zahelan vowurol.`
>
> z-Azozon | through | z-Ahelan | v-walk
>
> "Everyone from Azozon through Ahelan walks."

<!-- cheat-sheet: joins-hooks -->
| Ageben | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| **`oel`** | A, B, and everything between | *A through B* | **o** ≈ at + **e** ≈ for: start at A, head for B |
| **`ual`** | only what lies between | *strictly between A and B* | **ua** ≈ take away both ends |
| **`uol`** | only what lies outside | *outside A–B* | **uo** ≈ undo the stretch from A to B |

The range runs from A to B, in the order you say them. Endings work as on other hooks: **-m** makes the ends soft (*roughly A through B*), and **-n** names the range as a whole. **-r** stands in for one member you are not naming, and under a [question](/questions#fill-ask-r) it asks which one:

> `zazozon uar zahelan vezuvel.`
>
> z-Azozon | some.strictly-between | z-Ahelan | v-sleep
>
> "Someone between Azozon and Ahelan sleeps."

A range **-r** always has a word on each side (`oer` / `uar` / `uor`). With nothing on the right, extra-noun **-r** is not a range.

> `zazozon ual zahelan vezuvel.`
>
> z-Azozon | strictly-between | z-Ahelan | v-sleep
>
> "Everyone between Azozon and Ahelan sleeps." (not Azozon or Ahelan themselves)

Between two numbers with digits, plain **`al`** and **`ul`** also make a range, because *3, including 5* has nothing to include into.

A word right after B describes B alone, not the range. To describe the range itself, put `/w/` right before the hook:

> `zazozon wemem oel zahelan vowurol.`
>
> z-Azozon | [w-amazement | through] | z-Ahelan | v-walk
>
> "Everyone from Azozon all the way through Ahelan walks." (a surprisingly wide range)

**Compare with:** `zazozon zahelan zal` is *Azozon and Ahelan*, the two ends only. `zazozon zahelan zaol` is *Azozon, then Ahelan*, an order with nothing between.
