# Role compounds {#roles}

When English would name a person, place or time from an event (*a teacher* from *teach*, *the one told*, *a sleeping place*, *bedtime*), Agebon builds that name from the event’s stem. Write a person or thing letter, then **`a`**, **`e`**, **`u`**, or **`o`**, then mid-word **`x`**, then the stem, then an ordinary [word ending](word-endings.md). **`a`** is the doer, **`e`** is the scene of the event (where or when), **`u`** is the one the event happens to, and **`o`** is the extra `/b/` party (who is told, or the other party of a relation).

The ending decides whether you mean anyone in that part or the one from a particular event. With **-l**, **-m**, or **-n**, the word is anyone who does it, with no claim about how often: *someone who teaches*. With **-r**, it is the person or place of the latest matching event: *the one teaching*. To say it is their usual role, the kind English names as a job (*a teacher*), add a scope vowel after the stem ([the usual role](#usual-role)).

## Beginner {#beginner}

### Role compounds: the doer (`a`) {#role-compounds}
<a id="agent-place-patient-recipient"></a>

English *someone who teaches* names a person from what they do, from the same idea as *teach*. Write a person or thing letter (`/z/` `/d/` `/b/`), then **`a`**, then mid-word **`x`**, then the event root, then an ordinary [word ending](word-endings.md). The result is one content word (a **role compound**) that you can use as a participant. (cue: **a** ≈ add (add a doer))

> `zaxuduyel varuvel.`
>
> z-agent-x-teach | v-run
>
> "Someone who teaches runs."

The last letter is the ordinary [word ending](word-endings.md): **-l** takes the stem’s everyday concrete sense, **-m** its abstract sense. `zaxuduyel` is *someone who teaches*; `zaxavuvul` is *someone who fights*; `zaxavuvum` is *someone who struggles*. No teaching or fight needs to come up earlier in the conversation, and the word says nothing about how often: one lesson is enough.

When the event is a dictionary word built from two roots, the role compound takes the whole word as its stem: `zaxanalawebol` is *someone who reads*, from `analawebol` *read*.

A noun that specifies the event goes in the same stem, before the event and joined to it with another **`x`**: `zaxudozoxowurel` is *someone who walks dogs* (the doer of *dog-walking*). The noun's part in the event is as open as in English (*dog walker*, *coffee maker*); for an exact part, use a [hook](hooks.md#extra-noun) or a second sentence. **-r** resumes the whole stem.

> `zaxudozoxowurel varuvel.`
>
> z-agent-x-dog-x-walk | v-run
>
> "Someone who walks dogs runs."

A role compound has one role vowel, left of the first **`x`**. It is never one piece of another compound.

### This instance (`-r`)

English *the one teaching* points at the doer of an event already mentioned. Put **-r** on the role compound: once the conversation has a matching event (*Uzozen teaches*), **-r** on the same stem means **that** doer, not just anyone who teaches.

> `zuzozen vuduyel.`
> `zaxuduyer vezuvol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-teach
> z-←agent-x-teach | v-sleep
>
> "Uzozen teaches. The one teaching sleeps."

To build the **-r** form, take the whole stem of the earlier verb. In `vuhohol` (*sit*), the stem is **`uhoho`**, so *the one sitting* is `zaxuhohor`: subject **`z`**, doer **`ax`**, the stem **`uhoho`**, and **-r**.

> `zuzozen vuhohol. zaxuhohor vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-sit . z-←agent-x-sit | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen sits. The one who sat walks."

### The undergoer (`u`)

English *the one seen* or *the one fought* names the person the event happens to. Use the same compound shape as the doer, but write **`u`** in the vowel slot. With **-l**, it is anyone it happens to (*someone seen*); with **-r**, it is the undergoer of the latest matching event. (cue: **u** ≈ undo (the one it happens to))

> `zuzozen dagozol vawebol.`
> `zuxawebor varuvel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-cat | v-see
> z-←patient-x-see | v-run
>
> "Uzozen sees a cat. The one seen runs."

### The scene (`e`)
<a id="the-place-e"></a>

English *a sleep-place* or *bedtime* names where or when an event happens, from the same idea as *sleep*. Use the same compound shape as the doer, but write **`e`** in the vowel slot. That named thing is the **scene of the event**: its place or its time, not the sleeper. (cue: **e** ≈ order (the scene the act is ordered in))

You can name such a scene even when no sleep has come up in the conversation:

> `zuzozen dexezuvol vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-scene-x-sleep | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sees a place where someone sleeps."

The same word can be a time. In `/h/`, `hexezuvol` is *at bedtime*.

Once the conversation has a matching event, **-r** means the scene of **that** event (where or when it happened), not a kind of scene. The sentence with the scene word is a new claim about that place (who sees it, who punches it); it does not add a place to the first verb.

> `zolazun varemul.`
> `zuzozen dexaremur vawebol.`
>
> z-Olazun | v-scream
> z-Uzozen | d-←scene-x-scream | v-see
>
> "Olazun screams. Uzozen sees the place where Olazun screams."

`zexeguyel` is a place where something is constructed (the **-l** sense of *construct*). A construction site as a kind of place, with no event in mind, is the dictionary word `degohul`.

**Compare with:** `zaxeguyel` is who constructs. What gets built did not exist before, so it is not an undergoer: it is the [result](#result). To say where a constructing in your sentence happens (*constructs in a house*), put the place on that clause with the extra-noun [hook](hooks.md#extra-noun) **`al`** plus `/b/`. The scene word is for naming the place itself.

### The extra `/b/` party (`o`)

English *the one told* names who gets the telling. In a sentence, that person is the **recipient**: a `/b/` word with no relation word before it ([extra nouns](clause.md#extra-nouns)). To name them from the verb stem, write **`o`** in the vowel slot. (cue: **o** ≈ one (that extra one))

> `zuzozen vezuyel.`
> `zoxezuyer varuvel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-tell
> z-←recipient-x-tell | v-run
>
> "Uzozen speaks. The one spoken to runs."

You can name that party with no prior telling: `zoxezuyel` is someone told something (the **-l** sense of *speech*).

Some stems name a **relation** (who is bound to whom), not an action. The other person of that relation is the `/b/` word right after the relation word. The same **`o`** names that person from the relation stem (*the other party of the bond*).

> `zuzozen gawom blolazun.`
> `zoxawor varuvel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [g-bond | bl-Olazun]
> z-←recipient-x-bond | v-run
>
> "Uzozen is bound to Olazun. The one Uzozen is bound to runs."

**Compare with:** *for someone* (who the act is meant to benefit) is the extra-noun hook **`el`** plus `/b/`, not the recipient.

| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| `…axROOT…` | names the doer of an event | *someone who teaches* / *the one teaching* | **a** ≈ add (add a doer) |
| `…exROOT…` | names the scene (place or time) of an event | *a place where someone sleeps* / *the place of that scream* | **e** ≈ order (the scene the act is ordered in) |
| `…uxROOT…` | names the undergoer of an event | *someone seen* / *the one seen* (this seeing) | **u** ≈ undo (the one it happens to) |
| `…oxROOT…` | names the extra `/b/` party | *someone told* / *the one told* | **o** ≈ one (that extra one) |

A role compound fills `/z/`, `/d/`, `/b/`, or `/ɡ/`. Under `/x/` it sets a [topic](pronouns.md#topic), as any compound does (`xaxuduyel` *now, about people who teach*), and under `/y/` with **-n** or **-r** it calls someone (`yaxobebun` calls the doctor).

Its stem is always an event. A [special pronoun](pronouns.md#special-pronouns) such as `uwon` (*the speaker*) names a person, not an event, so `zaxuwon` is not a word. The same root on **-l** is an ordinary content word with a verb sense (*perform*), so it can be a stem: `zaxuwol` is *someone who performs*.

With **-l**, **-m**, or **-n**, the word is **any** such participant: someone who does it, a place or time where it happens, someone it happens to. **-r** looks back to the latest verb, event noun, or relation with that whole stem (including a stem already inside a role compound) and names **this instance**. The vowel still picks which part you mean: doer, scene, undergoer, or extra `/b/` party.

### Without the stem: role pointers {#role-pointers-family}

A [role pointer](pronouns.md#role-pointers) is this same word with the event stem left out: the role vowel, **`x`**, a pointer vowel, and an ending (**-r** the participant itself, **-l** a new one of its kind, **-m** its part in the event). Instead of naming the event, the pointer vowel picks it by position: **`a`** the latest one, **`e`** this sentence’s own, **`o`** the other one, **`u`** the one that left the part unsaid. `zaxuhohor` is *the one who sat*; `zaxar` is *whoever did the latest thing*.

A scene pointer names the place or time of the latest event. If that clause has a place (*in a house*), the scene is that place:

> `zuzozen vezuvol al bahehal. zolazun dexar vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-sleep | [in | b-house] . z-Olazun | d-←scene.same | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sleeps in a house. Olazun sees the house."

With no place, a *during* time is the scene (`herem blenulul`, *during the storm*). With neither, `dexar` is the event’s own place or time, as `dexezuvor` would be. A place wins over a time, and with two places the first one is the scene. *Before*, *after*, *until*, *by*, and an [as-of](relations.md#as-of) pair name a time the event is measured from, not its scene.

**Compare with:** for the doer, the undergoer, or the extra party, a pointer looks back to whoever last had that part, skipping events without one ([role pointers](pronouns.md#role-pointers)). For the scene, it takes the latest event, whatever it has. Scene **`u`** (`zexur` *wherever it happened*) says nobody has named the place, where `zexar` is a place the conversation already gave.

### The event itself

English *a fight* names the event itself, not anyone in it. Put the event root on a thing letter with no role vowel: `davuvul` is *a fight*. Use **`ax`**, **`ex`**, **`ux`**, or **`ox`** only when you mean who fights, where or when the fight is, who is fought, or the extra `/b/` party.

> `zuzozen davuvul vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-fight | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sees a fight."

**Related form:** a verb’s own **-r** resumes that [event](pronouns.md) (`vavuvur` *that fighting*). Role **`ax`** / **`ux`** names the people.

### Saying someone does it
<a id="saying-someone-is-a-teacher"></a>

To say someone **is one who teaches**, put the same role compound on `/ɡ/`, as in [classification](predication.md#classification). The word says only that they do it, with no claim about how often. With **-r** on `/ɡ/`, it says they are the doer of the matching event (*is the one teaching*).

> `zuzozen gaxuduyel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | g-agent-x-teach
>
> "Uzozen is someone who teaches."

> `zuzozen gaxavuvul.`
>
> z-Uzozen | g-agent-x-fight
>
> "Uzozen is someone who fights."

> `zuzozen gaxavuvum.`
>
> z-Uzozen | g-agent-x-struggle
>
> "Uzozen is someone who struggles."

After another noun, the same `/ɡ/` word picks out which one you mean by that role.

> `zuzozen dagayul gaxavuvul vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [d-guard | g-agent-x-fight] | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sees a guard who fights."

**Compare with:** the plain root on `/ɡ/` is the act [in progress](predication.md#in-progress): `dagayul gavuvul` is *a fighting guard*, in the middle of a fight. English *the guard who fought (that time)* with extra participants or a place is [two sentences](dependents.md#which-noun), not this role word.

## Intermediate {#intermediate}

### The usual role (`the`) {#usual-role}

English *a teacher* says more than *someone who teaches*: teaching is that person's usual role, often a job. Write the role compound, then mid-word **`th`**, then **`e`**, then the ending. This is the [practiced role](predication.md#scope-occupation) label scope: it says the role is something they regularly do, not one act. (cue: **e** ≈ order (a sequence of practice))

> `zaxuduyethel varuvel.`
>
> z-agent-x-teach-th-role | v-run
>
> "A teacher runs."

`zaxavuvuthel` is *a fighter*, and `zaxudozoxowurethel` is *a dog walker*. A role compound without **`the`** never makes that claim, so one fight does not make someone a fighter.

**Compare with:** a role compound takes only one addition after its stem, so a usual role (**`the`**) does not also take [ability](intention.md#role-ability). Other [scope vowels](predication.md#scope-role) go in the same spot: **`tha`** is *this time only*, **`thu`** is a teacher type.

The other role vowels work the same way. With **`the`**, a scene is where the event usually happens: `dexezuvothel` is *a sleeping place*. To say someone **is** a teacher, put the same word on `/ɡ/`.

> `zuzozen gaxuduyethel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | g-agent-x-teach-th-role
>
> "Uzozen is a teacher."

### Instrument (`ae`) {#instrument}

Beginner already used four role vowels. Six two-vowel pairs in the same slot name six more parts of an event.

English *a writing tool* or *an opener* names the thing an event is done **with**. Write **`ae`** in the vowel slot. (cue: **ae** is the same pair as the hook **`ael`** *using*)

> `zuzozen daexarayel vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-instrument-x-write | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sees a writing tool."

With **-r**, it is the tool used in **that** event.

> `zuzozen vavuvul. zolazun daexavuvur vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-fight . z-Olazun | d-←instrument-x-fight | v-see
>
> "Uzozen fights. Olazun sees what Uzozen fights with."

**Compare with:** *writes with a pen* on the same clause is still the hook **`ael`** plus `/b/`. The compound names the tool as its own noun.

### Goal, source, path (`oe` / `ua` / `uo`) {#goal-source-path}

A movement has three more parts: where it heads, where it comes out of, and the way it goes. Each uses the two-vowel pair of the matching [extra-noun hook](hooks.md#extra-noun-intermediate).

| Agebon | Names | English | Cue |
|--------|-------|---------|-----|
| `…oexROOT…` | the **goal** of the event | *a finish line* (from *run*), *a target* | **oe**, as in **`oel`** *toward* |
| `…uaxROOT…` | the **source** it comes out of | *a spring* (from *pour*), *a quarry* | **ua**, as in **`ual`** *out of* |
| `…uoxROOT…` | the **path** it goes by | *a walkway* (from *walk*), *a route* | **uo**, as in **`uol`** *through* |

> `zuzozen doexaruvel vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-goal-x-run | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sees a finish line."

> `zolazun duoxowurel vawebol.`
>
> z-Olazun | d-path-x-walk | v-see
>
> "Olazun sees a walkway."

> `zuzozen dawudal vobuvul. zolazun duaxobuvur vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-wine | v-pour . z-Olazun | d-←source-x-pour | v-see
>
> "Uzozen pours wine. Olazun sees where it was poured from."

**Compare with:** the scene **`e`** is where the whole event happens. A goal, source or path is one end or the route of a movement. *Walks toward the station* on the same clause is the hook **`oel`** plus `/b/`.

### Result (`ao`) {#result}

English *a building* or *a dish* names the thing an event **makes**: it did not exist before. Write **`ao`** in the vowel slot. (cue: **ao** ≈ add one: one new thing in the world)

> `zuzozen daoxeguyel vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-result-x-construct | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sees a building."

With **-r**, it is what **that** event made.

> `zolazun veguyel. zuzozen daoxeguyer vawebol.`
>
> z-Olazun | v-construct . z-Uzozen | d-←result-x-construct | v-see
>
> "Olazun builds. Uzozen sees what Olazun built."

**Compare with:** the undergoer **`u`** is a thing the event changes, which was already there. The result **`ao`** is a thing the event brings into being.

### The one who pays (`ue`) {#bearer}

Some events cost someone who is not the one acted on. When a wallet is stolen, the wallet is what the stealing acts on, but its owner is the one who loses. Write **`ue`** in the vowel slot to name that person: the one the event goes **against**. (cue: **ue**, as in the hook **`uel`** *against*)

> `zuexurozum varuvel.`
>
> z-bearer-x-theft | v-run
>
> "A theft victim runs."

With **-r**, it is the one who pays for **that** event.

> `zuzozen vurozum. zuexurozur varemul.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-steal . z-←bearer-x-steal | v-scream
>
> "Uzozen steals. The one stolen from screams."

**Compare with:** the undergoer **`u`** is what the event acts on; the recipient **`o`** is who it is told or given to. **`ue`** is whoever bears its cost, whether or not they were acted on.

### Stacked vowels on a pointer {#stacked-pointers}

Each two-vowel pair also makes a [role pointer](#role-pointers-family): leave out the stem, and pointer vowel **`a`** reads the latest event. If that clause has the matching hook (**`ael`**, **`oel`**, **`ual`**, **`uol`**, **`uel`**) with its `/b/`, the pointer is that `/b/`; otherwise it is the event’s own tool, goal, and so on.

> `zuzozen vowurel oel bahehal. zolazun doexar vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [toward | b-house] . z-Olazun | d-←goal.same | v-see
>
> "Uzozen walks toward a house. Olazun sees the house."

> `zolazun veguyel. zuzozen daoxar vawebol.`
>
> z-Olazun | v-construct . z-Uzozen | d-←result.same | v-see
>
> "Olazun builds. Uzozen sees what Olazun built."

<!-- cheat-sheet: roles-comparing -->
| Agebon | Names | Its `/b/` comes from |
|--------|-------|----------------------|
| `daexar` | the tool of the latest event | **`ael`** |
| `doexar` | where it headed | **`oel`** |
| `duaxar` | where it came out of | **`ual`** |
| `duoxar` | the way it went | **`uol`** |
| `daoxar` | what it made | (always the event’s own) |
| `zuexar` | the one who paid for it | **`uel`** |

The other endings and pointer vowels of the [pronouns](pronouns.md#role-pointers) page go on these too. **-l** is a new one of that kind (`daexal` *another tool like that one*), and **-m** is that part’s share in the event (`daexam` *what the tool did*; `zuexam` *the cost they bore*, rather than the theft or the victim).

Pointer vowel **`u`** picks the latest event that left that part unsaid (`daexur` *whatever it was done with*). Pointer vowel **`o`** is *the other one*: `daexor` is *the other tool*, the nearest earlier event whose named tool is someone or something else. Only an event that names that tool, goal, source, or path with its `/b/` counts, so events that left it out are skipped, as **`a`** skips an event with nobody in the part.

> `zuzozen vuguyel ael banewul. zolazun vuguyel ael buzedal. zuzozen daexor vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-cook | [using | b-knife] . z-Olazun | v-cook | [using | b-spoon] . z-Uzozen | d-←instrument.other | v-see
>
> "Uzozen cooks with a knife. Olazun cooks with a spoon. Uzozen sees the other tool." (the knife)

The scene has no such forms: `zexor`, `zexol`, and `zexom` are not words.

### Viewpoint laterals (`DIR` × anchor) {#viewpoint-laterals}
<a id="spatial-laterals"></a>
<a id="my-left-your-left"></a>

English *left* / *right* / *ahead* / *back* can mean a compass heading (*west*) or a side of a viewpoint (*Uzozen’s left*, *the car’s left*). For the viewpoint reading, write the same arrow root, then mid-word **`th`**, then the person or thing whose facing counts as north, then an ordinary [word ending](word-endings.md). You can then say *walk left* without meaning *walk west*.

A person’s front is the direction they face. For an object, use the front its design or current use makes forward; if that does not settle a front, make the frame clear from context.

The [sake](sakes.md#sake-inventory) roots are never a direction: after one of them, **`th`** starts a sake word, even when a root-shaped piece follows ([emotion compose](sakes.md#emotion-compose)).

> `yel vowurel howuhethuzozen.`
>
> y-command | v-walk | h-west-th-Uzozen
>
> "Walk left." (Uzozen’s left)

Bare arrow words are compass points on a north-up map:

> `yel vowurel howuhel.`
>
> y-command | v-walk | h-west
>
> "Walk west."

Speaker and listener roots are the facing person when you mean *my left* / *your left*:

> `yel vowurel zawan howuhethawan.`
>
> y-command | v-walk | z-listener | h-west-th-listener
>
> "You, walk left." (your left)

For *their left*, put a [role pointer](pronouns.md#role-pointers) on **-r** after **`th`**: `howuhethaxar` is *on the latest doer's left*, and `howuhethaxer` is *on this sentence's own doer's left*.

> `zuzozen vowurel. zolazun vuhohol howuhethaxar.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk . z-Olazun | v-sit | h-west-th-←agent.same
>
> "Uzozen walks. Olazun sits on their left." (Uzozen's left)

| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| PoS | slot as usual (`/ɡ/` property, `/h/` path / manner, `/z/` `/d/` `/b/` side-region) | same slots as other content |
| DIR | an arrow-rose root | *north* / *ahead*, … |
| **`th`** | joins DIR to the viewpoint | viewpoint after **`th`** (cue: **th** ≈ *think*, whose point of view) |
| ANCHOR | what sets the facing frame: [special](pronouns.md#special-pronouns) **`uwo`** / **`awa`** / **`aya`**, a person’s name, a content **-r** of a person or oriented object, or a role pointer on **-r** | *Uzozen’s left*, *your left*, *the car’s left*, *their left* |
| Ending | ordinary ending is written at the end of the whole compound; its reference is the viewpoint anchor, not the direction | viewpoint kind / hedge / name / resume |

**Compare with:** naming a doer from an event puts a **vowel** left of **`x`** ([role compounds](#role-compounds): `zaxavuvul`). Viewpoint laterals put a **direction root** left of **`th`** and a facing person on the right.

### Arrow rose (compass vs face)

The examples above used *west* / *left*. The other seven arrow roots work the same way: alone, each is a compass heading; with **`th`** and a facing person, it is that direction from the person’s body.

<!-- cheat-sheet: roles-comparing -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| **`onuhel`** | compass heading, or ahead on a face | *north* / *ahead* | ⬆️ *up-arrow*: map north, or ahead if facing is north |
| **`azuhel`** | compass heading, or back on a face | *south* / *back* | ⬇️ *down-arrow*: map south, or back if facing is north |
| **`ahel`** | compass heading, or right on a face | *east* / *right* | ➡️ *right-arrow*: the sun’s path east, or right of facing |
| **`owuhel`** | compass heading, or left on a face | *west* / *left* | ⬅️ *left-arrow*: opposite east, or left of facing |
| **`unuhel`** | compass heading, or forward-right on a face | *northeast* / *forward-right* | ↗️ *northeast-arrow*: between north and east |
| **`anuhel`** | compass heading, or forward-left on a face | *northwest* / *forward-left* | ↖️ *northwest-arrow*: between north and west |
| **`ezuhel`** | compass heading, or back-right on a face | *southeast* / *back-right* | ↘️ *southeast-arrow*: between south and east |
| **`uzuhel`** | compass heading, or back-left on a face | *southwest* / *back-left* | ↙️ *southwest-arrow*: between south and west |

Indoor headings may follow a local map (building north). When you mean directions from a person’s body, name the viewpoint or use the listener (`…thazawan`, `…thehon`).

### Gravity (`ayuhe` / `aduhe`) {#gravity}

English *up* / *down* usually follows the pull of gravity, not anyone’s facing. Write **`ayuhe`** (skyward) or **`aduhe`** (toward the pull) as ordinary content words, with no **`th`** + facing person. Everyone shares that frame.

> `yel vowurel hayuhel.`
>
> y-command | v-walk | h-up
>
> "Walk up."

> `yayuhel.`
>
> y-up
>
> "Up!"

<!-- cheat-sheet: roles-comparing -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Same root as | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|--------------|-----|
| **`ayuhel`** | skyward, shared gravity frame | *up* | | ⏫: away from the pull |
| **`aduhel`** | toward the pull, shared gravity frame | *down* | | ⏬: toward the pull |
| **`evuhel`** | the vertical axis | *up–down axis* | | ↕️: the axis only |

### Viewpoint vs landmark

English *left of the tree, from Uzozen’s view* needs both a facing person and a landmark. Put the facing person after **`th`**; put the tree, door, or wall in `/b/`. The `/b/` word is the landmark, not whose left it is.

> `zanal gowuhethuzozen bledalel.`
>
> z-book | [g-west-th-Uzozen | bl-tree]
>
> "The book is left of the tree, from Uzozen’s view."

A bare compass word plus `/b/` is the region on that side of the landmark (`gowuhel bledalel` *west of the tree*).

Gravity works the same way: bare **`aduhe`** + `/b/` is *under* the landmark, and bare **`ayuhe`** + `/b/` is *above* / *over* it.

> `zudozol vezuvol haduhel bledalel.`
>
> z-dog | v-sleep | [h-down | bl-tree]
>
> "The dog sleeps under the tree."

> `zubezol vuhohol gayuhel bludozol.`
>
> z-bird | v-sit | [g-up | bl-dog]
>
> "A bird sits above the dog."

**Compare with:** locative *at* / *on* / *inside* name how the figure sits relative to the landmark ([locative relations](relations.md#locative-relations)), not a compass heading.

<!-- cheat-sheet: roles-comparing -->
| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| Bare DIR | compass / gravity | `gowuhel` *west*; `gayuhel` *up* |
| **`DIR th ANCHOR`** | that point on **ANCHOR’s** facing rose | `gowuhethawan` *listener-left* |
| DIR + **`th`** + **`o`** + `/b/` | that point on the **landmark’s** own rose | `gazuhethol blahehal` *behind the house* |
| **complex `/ɡ/` + `/b/`** | region on a side **of a landmark** | `gowuhethuzozen bledalel` *left of the tree, from Uzozen’s view* |

### The landmark's own front (DIR + `th` + `o`) {#landmark-facing}

English *behind the house* means the house's own back, not a compass heading and not the speaker's back. Write the arrow root, then **`th`**, then **`o`** in place of a facing person, then the ending. Put the landmark in a `bl-` word right after it. **`o`** is the same vowel that names the extra `/b/` party in [role compounds](#role-compounds): here, the `/b/` landmark is the one whose facing counts.

> `zudozol vuhohol gazuhethol blahehal.`
>
> z-dog | v-sit | [g-south-th-landmark | bl-house]
>
> "The dog sits behind the house."

> `zuzozen vowurel honuhethol blohol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [h-north-th-landmark | bl-door]
>
> "Uzozen walks in front of the door."

This is [label scope](predication.md#label-scope) **`tho`** on a direction: the direction relative to that landmark. Arrow roots take no other scope vowel.

The landmark's front is where its design or current use faces (a house's front door, a car's windshield). A DIR + **`th`** + **`o`** word always takes a `/b/` landmark.

<!-- cheat-sheet: roles-comparing -->
| Agebon | English |
|--------|---------|
| `gazuhethol blahehal` | *behind the house* (its own back) |
| `gonuhethol blahehal` | *in front of the house* |
| `gowuhethol blahehal` | *on the house's left* |
| `gowuhethuzozen blahehal` | *left of the house, from Uzozen's view* |
| `gowuhel blahehal` | *west of the house* |

### Endings and resume

Write the ordinary [word ending](word-endings.md) at the end of the whole compound. In a viewpoint lateral, the ending applies to the **viewpoint anchor**, not to DIR: **-l** / **-m** describe the anchor’s reference, **-n** names the anchor, and **-r** resumes it. A bare direction word still takes its ending on the direction itself.

| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| **-l** | indefinite / kind viewpoint anchor or unmarked path | left of a viewpoint |
| **-m** | soft / hedged viewpoint anchor | left of a hedged viewpoint |
| **-n** | named viewpoint anchor | that named viewpoint’s left |
| **-r** | resume the most recent matching viewpoint anchor in a **`DIR th ANCHOR`** compound, or the anchor alone when it is the latest match: ordinary [pronoun](pronouns.md) rules | that viewpoint’s left |

<!-- cheat-sheet: roles-comparing -->
| Agebon | English |
|--------|---------|
| `gowuhel` | *west* (shared map) |
| `gowuhethuzozen` | *Uzozen’s left* |
| `gowuhethawan` | *your left* (listener facing) |
| `gowuhethuwon` | *my left* (speaker facing) |
| `gowuhethayan` | *our left* (shared facing) |
| `gayuhel` / `hayuhel` | gravity *up* |
