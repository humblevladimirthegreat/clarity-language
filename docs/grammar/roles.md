# Role compounds {#roles}

When English would name a person or thing from an event (*a teacher* from *teach*, *the one told*, *a building*), Agebon builds that name from the event’s stem. Write a person or thing letter, then **`w`**, then a scope vowel, then a part consonant, then the stem, then an ordinary [word ending](word-endings.md). The part consonant is the role letter the participant had in the event's clause: **`z`** the doer, **`d`** what the event acts on or brings into being, and **`b`** the extra `/b/` party (who is told, or the other party of a relation).

The scope vowel says how far the label reaches. **`a`** is this episode: one act is enough (*someone who teaches*). **`e`** is a practiced role, the kind English names as a job (*a teacher*). Every role word picks one, so calling someone *a fighter* after a single fight takes a separate, visible choice. The ending decides whether you mean anyone in that part or the one from a particular event: with **-r**, it is the participant of the latest matching event (*the one teaching*).

## Beginner {#beginner}

### Role compounds: the doer (`z`) {#role-compounds}
<a id="agent-place-patient-recipient"></a>

English *someone who teaches* names a person from what they do, from the same idea as *teach*. Write a person or thing letter (`/z/` `/d/` `/b/`), then **`w`**, then the scope vowel **`a`**, then the part consonant **`z`**, then the event root, then an ordinary [word ending](word-endings.md). The result is one content word (a **role compound**) that you can use as a participant. The **`z`** is the doer's own role letter: the one who did it sat in `/z/`.

> `zwazuduyel varuvel.`
>
> z-episode-agent-teach | v-run
>
> "Someone who teaches runs."

The last letter is the ordinary [word ending](word-endings.md): **-l** takes the stem’s everyday concrete sense, **-m** its abstract sense. `zwazuduyel` is *someone who teaches*; `zwazavuvul` is *someone who fights*; `zwazavuvum` is *someone who struggles*. No teaching or fight needs to come up earlier in the conversation, and **`a`** says nothing about how often: one lesson is enough.

When the event is a dictionary word built from two roots, the role compound takes the whole word as its stem: `zwazanalawebol` is *someone who reads*, from `analawebol` *read*.

A noun that specifies the event goes in the same stem, before the event and joined to it with mid-word **`x`**: `zwazudozoxowurel` is *someone who walks dogs* (the doer of *dog-walking*). The noun's part in the event is as open as in English (*dog walker*, *coffee maker*); for an exact part, use a [hook](hooks.md#extra-noun) or a second sentence. **-r** resumes the whole stem.

> `zwazudozoxowurel varuvel.`
>
> z-episode-agent-dog-x-walk | v-run
>
> "Someone who walks dogs runs."

A role compound has one part consonant, right after its scope vowel. It is never one piece of another compound.

### One time or a practice (`a` / `e`) {#one-time-or-a-practice}
<a id="usual-role"></a>

English *a teacher* says more than *someone who teaches*: teaching is that person's practiced role, often a job. Write **`e`** in the scope slot instead of **`a`**. (cue: **e** ≈ order (a sequence of practice))

> `zwezuduyel varuvel.`
>
> z-role-agent-teach | v-run
>
> "A teacher runs."

`zwezavuvul` is *a fighter*, and `zwezudozoxowurel` is *a dog walker*. With **`a`**, a role compound never makes that claim, so one fight gives `zwazavuvul` (*someone who fought*), not a fighter.

| Agebon | Scope | English | Cue |
|--------|-------|---------|-----|
| `zwazuduyel` | **`a`** this episode | *someone who teaches* | **a** ≈ add (one act added) |
| `zwezuduyel` | **`e`** practiced role | *a teacher* | **e** ≈ order (a sequence of practice) |

### This instance (`-r`)

English *the one teaching* points at the doer of an event already mentioned. Put **-r** on the role compound: once the conversation has a matching event (*Uzozen teaches*), **-r** on the same stem means **that** doer, not just anyone who teaches.

> `zuzozen vuduyel.`
> `zwazuduyer vezuvol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-teach
> z-←episode-agent-teach | v-sleep
>
> "Uzozen teaches. The one teaching sleeps."

To build the **-r** form, take the whole stem of the earlier verb. In `vuhohol` (*sit*), the stem is **`uhoho`**, so *the one sitting* is `zwazuhohor`: subject **`z`**, then **`wa`**, the doer **`z`**, the stem **`uhoho`**, and **-r**.

> `zuzozen vuhohol. zwazuhohor vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-sit . z-←episode-agent-sit | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen sits. The one who sat walks."

The scope vowel reads the same on **-r**: `zwezuduyer` is *the one teaching, in their practiced role*.

### The undergoer (`d`) {#the-undergoer-d}
<a id="the-undergoer-u"></a>

English *the one seen* or *the one fought* names the person the event happens to. That person sat in `/d/`, so write **`d`** as the part consonant. With **-l**, it is anyone it happens to (*someone seen*); with **-r**, it is the undergoer of the latest matching event.

> `zuzozen dagozol vawebol.`
> `zwadawebor varuvel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-cat | v-see
> z-←episode-patient-see | v-run
>
> "Uzozen sees a cat. The one seen runs."

What an event brings into being sits in `/d/` too (*Olazun builds a house*), so **`d`** names it as well: `dwadeguyel` is *something built*. A verb's `/d/` is either what it acts on (*see*) or what it makes (*construct*), and the verb says which.

> `zolazun veguyel. zuzozen dwadeguyer vawebol.`
>
> z-Olazun | v-construct . z-Uzozen | d-←episode-patient-construct | v-see
>
> "Olazun builds. Uzozen sees what Olazun built."

### The extra `/b/` party (`b`) {#the-extra-b-party-b}
<a id="the-extra-b-party-o"></a>

English *the one told* names who gets the telling. In a sentence, that person is the **recipient**: a `/b/` word with no relation word before it ([extra nouns](clause.md#extra-nouns)). To name them from the verb stem, write **`b`** as the part consonant.

> `zuzozen vezuyel.`
> `zwabezuyer varuvel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-tell
> z-←episode-recipient-tell | v-run
>
> "Uzozen speaks. The one spoken to runs."

You can name that party with no prior telling: `zwabezuyel` is someone told something (the **-l** sense of *speech*), and `zwebezuyel` is *a confidant*, someone regularly told things.

Some stems name a **relation** (who is bound to whom), not an action. The other person of that relation is the `/b/` word right after the relation word. The same **`b`** names that person from the relation stem (*the other party of the bond*).

> `zuzozen gawom blolazun.`
> `zwabawor varuvel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [g-bond | bl-Olazun]
> z-←episode-recipient-bond | v-run
>
> "Uzozen is bound to Olazun. The one Uzozen is bound to runs."

**Compare with:** *for someone* (who the act is meant to benefit) is the extra-noun hook **`el`** plus `/b/`, not the recipient.

| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| `…wazROOT…` | names the doer of an event | *someone who teaches* / *the one teaching* | **z**: the doer sat in `/z/` |
| `…wadROOT…` | names what the event acts on or makes | *someone seen* / *the one seen* (this seeing); *something built* | **d**: it sat in `/d/` |
| `…wabROOT…` | names the extra `/b/` party | *someone told* / *the one told* | **b**: they sat in `/b/` |

A role compound fills `/z/`, `/d/`, `/b/`, or `/ɡ/`. Under `/y/` with **-n** or **-r** it calls someone (`ywezobebun` calls the doctor).

Its stem is always an event. A [special pronoun](pronouns.md#special-pronouns) such as `uwon` (*the speaker*) names a person, not an event, so `zwazuwon` is not a word. The same root on **-l** is an ordinary content word with a verb sense (*perform*), so it can be a stem: `zwazuwol` is *someone who performs*.

With **-l**, **-m**, or **-n**, the word is **any** such participant: someone who does it, something it acts on or makes, someone told. **-r** looks back to the latest verb, event noun, or relation with that whole stem (including a stem already inside a role compound) and names **this instance**. The part consonant still picks which part you mean: doer, undergoer, or extra `/b/` party.

### Without the stem: role pointers {#role-pointers-family}

A [role pointer](pronouns.md#role-pointers) is this same word with the event stem left out: **`w`**, the scope vowel, the part consonant, a pointer vowel, and an ending (**-r** the participant itself, **-l** a new one of its kind, **-m** its part in the event). Instead of naming the event, the pointer vowel picks it by position: **`a`** the latest one, **`e`** this sentence’s own, **`o`** the other one, **`u`** the one that left the part unsaid. `zwazuhohor` is *the one who sat*; `zwazar` is *whoever did the latest thing*. After the pointer vowel and ending the word ends, while a compound's stem goes on (`zwazar` against `zwazarayel`, *someone who writes*).

A pointer looks back to whoever last had that part, skipping events without one ([role pointers](pronouns.md#role-pointers)).

### The event itself

English *a fight* names the event itself, not anyone in it. Put the event root on a thing letter with no **`w`** and part: `davuvul` is *a fight*. Use **`waz`**, **`wad`**, or **`wab`** only when you mean who fights, who is fought, or the extra `/b/` party.

> `zuzozen davuvul vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-fight | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sees a fight."

**Related form:** a verb’s own **-r** resumes that [event](pronouns.md) (`vavuvur` *that fighting*). A role compound names the people.

### Saying someone does it
<a id="saying-someone-is-a-teacher"></a>

To say someone **is one who teaches**, put the same role compound on `/ɡ/`, as in [classification](predication.md#classification). With **`a`**, the word says only that they do it, with no claim about how often. With **-r** on `/ɡ/`, it says they are the doer of the matching event (*is the one teaching*).

> `zuzozen gwazuduyel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | g-episode-agent-teach
>
> "Uzozen is someone who teaches."

> `zuzozen gwazavuvum.`
>
> z-Uzozen | g-episode-agent-struggle
>
> "Uzozen is someone who struggles."

To say someone **is** a teacher, put the practiced-role word on `/ɡ/`:

> `zuzozen gwezuduyel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | g-role-agent-teach
>
> "Uzozen is a teacher."

After another noun, the same `/ɡ/` word picks out which one you mean by that role.

> `zuzozen dagayul gwazavuvul vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [d-guard | g-episode-agent-fight] | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sees a guard who fights."

**Compare with:** the plain root on `/ɡ/` is the act [in progress](predication.md#in-progress): `dagayul gavuvul` is *a fighting guard*, in the middle of a fight. English *the guard who fought (that time)* with extra participants or a place is [two sentences](dependents.md#which-noun), not this role word.

## Intermediate {#intermediate}

### Scope across scenes (`o`, `u`, pairs) {#scope}
<a id="scope-across-scenes"></a>

The scope slot after **`w`** takes every [label scope](predication.md#label-scope) vowel, not just **`a`** and **`e`**. They are the same vowels other labels write after mid-word **`th`**; on a role word they come first, and nowhere else. A **`th`** scope after a role word's stem (`zwazuduyethel`) is not a word.

| V1 | Reach | Example | English |
|----|-------|---------|---------|
| **`a`** | this episode: one act is enough | `zwazuduyel` | *someone who teaches* |
| **`e`** | practiced role | `zwezuduyel` | *a teacher* |
| **`o`** | this pair: toward one party, named in a `bl-` word on `/ɡ/` | `gwozuduyel blolazun` | *is a teacher to Olazun* |
| **`u`** | a type across scenes | `zwuzuduyel` | *a teacher type* |
| pairs | the scope stacks: **`ao`**, **`ae`**, **`oe`**, **`ua`**, **`uo`**, **`ue`** | `gwuezuduyel` | *not in that capacity* |

> `zuzozen gwozuduyel blolazun.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [g-pair-agent-teach | bl-Olazun]
>
> "Uzozen is Olazun's teacher."

On `/z/`, `/d/`, and `/b/`, pair scope **`o`** stands alone: the party is the one the clause already relates the person to. On `/ɡ/` it takes its `bl-` party, as on any [pair-scope label](predication.md#scope-relative).

> `zuzozen gwuezuduyel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | g-outside-role-agent-teach
>
> "Uzozen is not acting as a teacher here."

Scope works on [role pointers](pronouns.md#role-pointers) too. `zwazar` is *the latest doer*; `zwezar` is *the latest doer, in their practiced role*: after *Uzozen teaches*, `zwezar` is Uzozen as a teacher. With **-m** it gives a useful contrast: `dwazam` is *what they did (this time)*, and `dwezam` is *their practice, their craft*.

### The role you act in (`/h/`) {#capacity}

English *as a teacher, I …* names the role the act is done in, not a person standing in the clause. Write the same role compound, with a slot part (`z`, `d`, or `b`), on `/h/`: a person cannot be an adverb, so the word is the capacity the act is done in.

> `zuzozen vezuyel hwezuduyel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-tell | h-role-agent-teach
>
> "Uzozen speaks as a teacher."

The scope vowel does its usual work. Pair scope **`o`** takes its `bl-` party, as on `/ɡ/`.

| Agebon | English |
|--------|---------|
| `hwazuduyel` | *as the one teaching this time* |
| `hwezuduyel` | *as a teacher* |
| `hwozuduyel blolazun` | *as Olazun's teacher* |
| `hwuezuduyel` | *not as a teacher* (off duty) |
| `hwedobebul` | *as a patient* (the one a doctor treats) |
| `hwebezuyel` | *as a confidant* |

**Compare with:** resemblance is the [similative](relations.md#similative) (`hewom blwezuduyel` *like a teacher*). An ordinary `/h/` adverb is how the event goes (`hedurem` *hastily*).

**Compare with:** `zuzozen gwezuduyel.` says Uzozen **is** a teacher. `zuzozen vezuyel hwezuduyel.` says the telling is done in that role.

### Places, tools, and times {#places-tools-times}

A role compound names a participant: the doer, what the event acts on or makes, or the extra `/b/` party. A place, tool, or time is a landmark, brought in with an [extra-noun hook](hooks.md#extra-noun) (`al bahehal` *in a house*, `ael banewul` *using a knife*), and it has no role compound. Name it with words you already have:

| Job | Agebon | English |
|-----|--------|---------|
| a kind of tool, place, or time | an ordinary [compound](compounds.md#two-roots-one-word) with a head root: `darayexuwal` (write + tool), `dezuvoxadurel` (sleep + time) | *a writing tool*, *bedtime* |
| the landmark a clause named | resume its noun: after `ael banewul`, `danewur` | *that knife* |
| that landmark as a place or means | the hook's own [pointer](hooks.md#hook-resume): `ar` *in there*, `or` *there*, `ur` *from there*, `aer` *with it* | *there*, *with it* |
| a landmark nobody named | two sentences: a place exists, and the event happened there | *wherever it happened* |
| who pays, who it is for | the hook in a clause (`uel` *against*, `el` *for* plus `/b/`), then a resume | *the one it was stolen from* |

> `zuzozen vuguyel ael banewul. zahelan danewur vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-cook | [using | b-knife] . z-Ahelan | d-←knife | v-see
>
> "Uzozen cooks with a knife. Ahelan sees the knife."

> `zuzozen vuguyel ael banewul. zahelan vuguyel aer.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-cook | [using | b-knife] . z-Ahelan | v-cook | with.it
>
> "Uzozen cooks with a knife. Ahelan cooks with it too."

### Content of the event {#content-of-the-event}

English *the way it was done*, *what it left them*, *what came of it*, or *that remark* names a quality of an event, not a person in it. Write the same role-word shape, and put a part consonant for that quality after the scope vowel: the word is then a thing you can talk about, resume, or point to.

> `dwahowurel vawebol.`
>
> d-episode-manner-walk | v-see
>
> "Someone sees a way of walking."

A slot part on the same stem is still a participant: `dwazowurel` is *someone who walks*, as [role compounds](#role-compounds) name a doer.

The stem is still the event. The part says which quality of it you mean. Pointers drop the stem and read the latest matching event, as [role pointers](pronouns.md#role-pointers) do for people:

> `zuzozen vowurel hedurem. zahelan dwahar vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | h-haste . z-Ahelan | d-←episode-manner.same | v-see
>
> "Uzozen walks in haste. Ahelan sees the way it was done."

If that clause named no manner word, `dwahar` is the event's own manner: how walking went, unnamed. A restrictor, a time pole, or a [say word](sakes.md#say-words) is already doing its own job on `/h/`, so the pointer skips it.

| Part | Names | Compound | Pointer |
|------|-------|----------|---------|
| **`h`** | how, when, or where it happened | `dwahowurel` *a way of walking* | `dwahar` *the way it was done* |
| **`g`** | the state it leaves its participant in | `dwaguguyel` *how cooked it is* | `dwagar` *the state it left them in* |
| **`th`** | a reason, thought, or feeling about it | `dwathowurel` *a reason to walk* | `dwathar` *the reason for it* |
| **`w`** | how much or how intense it was | `dwawowurel` *the amount of walking* | `dwawar` *how much it was* |
| **`y`** | the turn that said it (question, command, remark) | `dwayowurel` *a remark about walking* | `dwayar` *that remark* |
| **`x`** | what followed from it | `dwaxowurel` *what comes of walking* | `dwaxar` *what came of it* |
| **`v`** | the event itself | (no compound: the event noun is the stem, `dowurel`) | `dwavar` *that event* |

Scope **`a`** / **`e`** / **`o`** / **`u`** still say how far the label reaches: `dwehowurel` is *someone's usual way of walking*, `dwuhowurel` *a walking style*.

A **`g`** pointer is the `/d/` participant's state when the event has one, and the `/z/` participant's otherwise. After *Uzozen punches Olazun*, `dwagar` is Olazun's state; after *Uzozen runs*, it is Uzozen's. **`w`** always reads the event's own extent: a `/w/` word grades the next adjective, never the event, so `dwawar` is *that much*. `dwagor` and `dwawor` are not words: no clause names a resulting state or an amount for *the other* to differ from.

A **`th`** pointer reads [sake](sakes.md) words, feeling roots, and [causation poles](causation.md) on `/th/`. A channel, MAY, the notional, or a holder is the speaker's stance on the *claim*, not a stance on the event, so the pointer skips those too. With no such word, `dwathar` is still *the reason for it*: the gap the spelling fills.

A **`y`** pointer reaches turns, not events: `dwayar` the latest turn, `dwayor` the one before it, `dwayer` this very turn, `dwayur` whatever was said. With **-m**, `dwayam` is what the saying did, apart from what it said.

If the next sentence after an event opened with a [consequence linker](dependents.md#sentence-linkers) (`xahem` *therefore*, `xahel` *it follows that*), an **`x`** pointer is that sentence's event:

> `zuzozen vanuvul. xahem zolazun varemul. zahelan dwaxar vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-punch . x-therefore | z-Olazun | v-scream . z-Ahelan | d-←episode-outcome.same | v-see
>
> "Uzozen punches. Therefore Olazun screams. Ahelan sees what came of it."

**Compare with:** a landmark is a thing (*the house*, *the knife*), reached by resuming its noun or with a hook pointer (`aer` *with it*). An **`h`** part is a quality of the event (*the haste*): *how* is `dwahar`. **`g`** is the state the event leaves its participant in; **`x`** is what happens next because of it.

### Viewpoint laterals (`DIR` × anchor) {#viewpoint-laterals}
<a id="spatial-laterals"></a>
<a id="my-left-your-left"></a>

English *left* / *right* / *ahead* / *back* can mean a compass heading (*west*) or a side of a viewpoint (*Uzozen’s left*, *the car’s left*). For the viewpoint reading, write the same arrow root, then mid-word **`th`**, then the person or thing whose facing counts as north, then an ordinary [word ending](word-endings.md). You can then say *walk left* without meaning *walk west*.

A person’s front is the direction they face. For an object, use the front its design or current use makes forward; if that does not settle a front, make the frame clear from context.

The [sake](sakes.md#sake-inventory) roots are never a direction: after one of them, **`th`** starts a sake word, even when a root-shaped piece follows ([emotion compose](sakes.md#emotion-compose)).

> `yel vowurel howuhethuzozen.`
>
> y-command | v-walk | h-west-th-Uzozen
>
> "Walk left." (Uzozen’s left)

Bare arrow words are compass points on a north-up map:

> `yel vowurel howuhel.`
>
> y-command | v-walk | h-west
>
> "Walk west."

Speaker and listener roots are the facing person when you mean *my left* / *your left*:

> `yel vowurel zawan howuhethawan.`
>
> y-command | v-walk | z-listener | h-west-th-listener
>
> "You, walk left." (your left)

For *their left*, [resume](pronouns.md#resume-r) the person on **-r** after **`th`**: `howuhethuzozer` is *on Uzozen's left*, said of someone already named.

> `zuzozen vowurel. zolazun vuhohol howuhethuzozer.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk . z-Olazun | v-sit | h-west-th-←Uzozen
>
> "Uzozen walks. Olazun sits on their left." (Uzozen's left)

| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| PoS | slot as usual (`/ɡ/` property, `/h/` path / manner, `/z/` `/d/` `/b/` side-region) | same slots as other content |
| DIR | an arrow-rose root | *north* / *ahead*, … |
| **`th`** | joins DIR to the viewpoint | viewpoint after **`th`** (cue: **th** ≈ *think*, whose point of view) |
| ANCHOR | what sets the facing frame: [special](pronouns.md#special-pronouns) **`uwo`** / **`awa`** / **`aya`**, a person’s name, or a content **-r** of a person or oriented object | *Uzozen’s left*, *your left*, *the car’s left*, *their left* |
| Ending | ordinary ending is written at the end of the whole compound; its reference is the viewpoint anchor, not the direction | viewpoint kind / hedge / name / resume |

**Compare with:** naming a doer from an event puts **`w`**, a scope vowel, and a part consonant before the stem ([role compounds](#role-compounds): `zwazavuvul`). Viewpoint laterals put a **direction root** left of **`th`** and a facing person on the right.

### Arrow rose (compass vs face)

The examples above used *west* / *left*. The other seven arrow roots work the same way: alone, each is a compass heading; with **`th`** and a facing person, it is that direction from the person’s body.

<!-- cheat-sheet: roles-comparing -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| **`onuhel`** | compass heading, or ahead on a face | *north* / *ahead* | ⬆️ *up-arrow*: map north, or ahead if facing is north |
| **`azuhel`** | compass heading, or back on a face | *south* / *back* | ⬇️ *down-arrow*: map south, or back if facing is north |
| **`ahel`** | compass heading, or right on a face | *east* / *right* | ➡️ *right-arrow*: the sun’s path east, or right of facing |
| **`owuhel`** | compass heading, or left on a face | *west* / *left* | ⬅️ *left-arrow*: opposite east, or left of facing |
| **`unuhel`** | compass heading, or forward-right on a face | *northeast* / *forward-right* | ↗️ *northeast-arrow*: between north and east |
| **`anuhel`** | compass heading, or forward-left on a face | *northwest* / *forward-left* | ↖️ *northwest-arrow*: between north and west |
| **`ezuhel`** | compass heading, or back-right on a face | *southeast* / *back-right* | ↘️ *southeast-arrow*: between south and east |
| **`uzuhel`** | compass heading, or back-left on a face | *southwest* / *back-left* | ↙️ *southwest-arrow*: between south and west |

Indoor headings may follow a local map (building north). When you mean directions from a person’s body, name the viewpoint or use the listener (`…thazawan`, `…thehon`).

### Gravity (`ayuhe` / `aduhe`) {#gravity}

English *up* / *down* usually follows the pull of gravity, not anyone’s facing. Write **`ayuhe`** (skyward) or **`aduhe`** (toward the pull) as ordinary content words, with no **`th`** + facing person. Everyone shares that frame.

> `yel vowurel hayuhel.`
>
> y-command | v-walk | h-up
>
> "Walk up."

> `yayuhel.`
>
> y-up
>
> "Up!"

<!-- cheat-sheet: roles-comparing -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Same root as | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|--------------|-----|
| **`ayuhel`** | skyward, shared gravity frame | *up* | | ⏫: away from the pull |
| **`aduhel`** | toward the pull, shared gravity frame | *down* | | ⏬: toward the pull |
| **`evuhel`** | the vertical axis | *up–down axis* | | ↕️: the axis only |

### Viewpoint vs landmark

English *left of the tree, from Uzozen’s view* needs both a facing person and a landmark. Put the facing person after **`th`**; put the tree, door, or wall in `/b/`. The `/b/` word is the landmark, not whose left it is.

> `zanal gowuhethuzozen bledalel.`
>
> z-book | [g-west-th-Uzozen | bl-tree]
>
> "The book is left of the tree, from Uzozen’s view."

A bare compass word plus `/b/` is the region on that side of the landmark (`gowuhel bledalel` *west of the tree*).

Gravity works the same way: bare **`aduhe`** + `/b/` is *under* the landmark, and bare **`ayuhe`** + `/b/` is *above* / *over* it.

> `zudozol vezuvol haduhel bledalel.`
>
> z-dog | v-sleep | [h-down | bl-tree]
>
> "The dog sleeps under the tree."

> `zubezol vuhohol gayuhel bludozol.`
>
> z-bird | v-sit | [g-up | bl-dog]
>
> "A bird sits above the dog."

**Compare with:** locative *at* / *on* / *inside* name how the figure sits relative to the landmark ([locative relations](relations.md#locative-relations)), not a compass heading.

<!-- cheat-sheet: roles-comparing -->
| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| Bare DIR | compass / gravity | `gowuhel` *west*; `gayuhel` *up* |
| **`DIR th ANCHOR`** | that point on **ANCHOR’s** facing rose | `gowuhethawan` *listener-left* |
| DIR + **`th`** + **`o`** + `/b/` | that point on the **landmark’s** own rose | `gazuhethol blahehal` *behind the house* |
| **complex `/ɡ/` + `/b/`** | region on a side **of a landmark** | `gowuhethuzozen bledalel` *left of the tree, from Uzozen’s view* |

### The landmark's own front (DIR + `th` + `o`) {#landmark-facing}

English *behind the house* means the house's own back, not a compass heading and not the speaker's back. Write the arrow root, then **`th`**, then **`o`** in place of a facing person, then the ending. Put the landmark in a `bl-` word right after it. **`o`** is the same vowel as pair scope on a label: here, the one `/b/` landmark is the one whose facing counts.

> `zudozol vuhohol gazuhethol blahehal.`
>
> z-dog | v-sit | [g-south-th-landmark | bl-house]
>
> "The dog sits behind the house."

> `zuzozen vowurel honuhethol blohol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [h-north-th-landmark | bl-door]
>
> "Uzozen walks in front of the door."

This is [label scope](predication.md#label-scope) **`tho`** on a direction: the direction relative to that landmark. Arrow roots take no other scope vowel.

The landmark's front is where its design or current use faces (a house's front door, a car's windshield). A DIR + **`th`** + **`o`** word always takes a `/b/` landmark.

<!-- cheat-sheet: roles-comparing -->
| Agebon | English |
|--------|---------|
| `gazuhethol blahehal` | *behind the house* (its own back) |
| `gonuhethol blahehal` | *in front of the house* |
| `gowuhethol blahehal` | *on the house's left* |
| `gowuhethuzozen blahehal` | *left of the house, from Uzozen's view* |
| `gowuhel blahehal` | *west of the house* |

### Endings and resume

Write the ordinary [word ending](word-endings.md) at the end of the whole compound. In a viewpoint lateral, the ending applies to the **viewpoint anchor**, not to DIR: **-l** / **-m** describe the anchor’s reference, **-n** names the anchor, and **-r** resumes it. A bare direction word still takes its ending on the direction itself.

| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| **-l** | indefinite / kind viewpoint anchor or unmarked path | left of a viewpoint |
| **-m** | soft / hedged viewpoint anchor | left of a hedged viewpoint |
| **-n** | named viewpoint anchor | that named viewpoint’s left |
| **-r** | resume the most recent matching viewpoint anchor in a **`DIR th ANCHOR`** compound, or the anchor alone when it is the latest match: ordinary [pronoun](pronouns.md) rules | that viewpoint’s left |

<!-- cheat-sheet: roles-comparing -->
| Agebon | English |
|--------|---------|
| `gowuhel` | *west* (shared map) |
| `gowuhethuzozen` | *Uzozen’s left* |
| `gowuhethawan` | *your left* (listener facing) |
| `gowuhethuwon` | *my left* (speaker facing) |
| `gowuhethayan` | *our left* (shared facing) |
| `gayuhel` / `hayuhel` | gravity *up* |
