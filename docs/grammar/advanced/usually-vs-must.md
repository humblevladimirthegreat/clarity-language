# Usually vs must {#usually-vs-must}

### Channels on a generalization {#universality}
<a id="universality-mood"></a>
<a id="generalization"></a>

English *always*, *usually*, *every*, and *never* make a general claim. The [universal fences](/universals-domains#universals-domains-generics) (`zual` / `zuam`) and the [restrictors](/restrictors) (`hual` / `huam` / `hal`) already say **who** or **when** the claim covers, and their ending says how many exceptions it allows: closed **-l** allows none, and open **-m** leaves them open, as on any list (*usually*, *as a rule*). No other word is needed for the claim itself.

> `zazozon huam vowurol.`
>
> z-Azozon | h-always.open | v-walk
>
> "Azozon usually walks."

To say **how you know** the general claim, add a [channel](/evidentiality#evidentiality). On a clause with a universal fence or restrictor, the channel is your warrant for the whole generalization, not for one case, and its [ending](/evidentiality#evidence-strength) says how much backs it.

> `zazozon hual vowurol thubor.`
>
> z-Azozon | h-always | v-walk | th-PATTERN.weak
>
> "Azozon always walks, from the few cases I have seen."

> `zual gwozeduyol veduyol thanal.`
>
> [z-everything | g-role-agent-teach] | v-teach | th-DEDUCED.strong
>
> "It follows that every teacher teaches." (every step could be shown)

> `zalazun hal vabuvul thebal blezunal.`
>
> z-Alazun | h-never | v-punch | [th-REPORTED.strong | bl-soccer]
>
> "Alazun never punches, per the soccer rules."

The fence says how far the claim reaches, and the channel says what it rests on. Keep the two apart: *always, as far as I have checked* is closed **`hual`** with a weak channel, because you claim no exceptions on thin evidence. Open **`huam`** does not claim that: it leaves the exceptions open.

| English | Fence or restrictor | Channel | Example |
|---------|---------------------|---------|---------|
| *usually*, *as a rule* | `huam` / `zuam` | any, or none | `zazozon huam vowurol.` |
| *always, as far as I have checked* | `hual` / `zual` | a weak channel | `zazozon hual vowurol thubor.` |
| *it follows that every …* | `zual` | DEDUCED.strong `thanal` | `zual gwozeduyol veduyol thanal.` |
| *by definition* | `zual` + `thehom blarl` | none | `zual geyel gwozeduyol thehom blarl zeyer veduyol.` |
| *under the rules of …* | `hal` / `hual` | REPORTED.strong with the rules in `/b/` | `zalazun hal vabuvul thebal blezunal.` |
| *cats are sleepy* (in general) | `zuam` + kind | any, or none | `zuam gogozel guzemel thubom.` |

A [cause or condition](/causation) on a general claim says how things work. It holds for **each** member or occasion, the same way the verb does. Inside the sentence after **`blarl`**, resume the kind with **-r** (`zogozer`) to mean *that same one*: each cat, in turn.

> `zual gogozel vezuvel thewom thohem blarl zogozer guzemel.`
>
> [z-everything | g-cat] | v-sleep | th-CAUSE | [th-if | bl-that-clause] | [z-←cat | g-sleepy]
>
> "Every cat sleeps when it is sleepy; that is how it works."

A **definition** works both ways: whatever has the property is that kind, and every member of the kind has it. Write it on a closed fence with **`thehom`** (*if and only if*, from [causation](/iff-fault#only-because)). The grounds sentence is the defining property. A definition needs no channel, because it is a decision about a word, not a report about the world.

> `zual geyel gwozeduyol thehom blarl zeyer veduyol.`
>
> [z-everything | g-person] | g-role-agent-teach | [th-iff | bl-that-clause] | z-←person | v-teach
>
> "By definition, a person is a teacher if and only if they teach."

The channel stays on the main sentence and covers the whole generalization. Put it before the pole, as with the [evidence clause](/evidentiality#evidence-clause): `zual gogozel vezuvel thubol thohem blarl …` is *going by a well-established pattern, every cat sleeps when …*.

**Compare with:** a general claim you are **not** giving as today's report is [FORMER](/notional-standing#former-climate) (`helam`). *Every cat must sleep* as a rule someone sets is a [requirement](/permission-consent#say-words) (`hwuvom`), not a law of how things work. *Maybe every cat sleeps* is [MAY](/knowing#may) on the same clause.
