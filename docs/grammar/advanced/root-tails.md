# Root tails {#root-tails-sitting}

### Root tails {#root-tails}

A root ends in a **tail**, its last two letters, that hints at what it names. The last consonant says what **kind** of thing the root's concrete sense is, and the last vowel says what **job** it does. On a five-letter root the first three letters keep it apart from others of the same kind; on a three-letter root the first vowel does.

The tail is a memory aid. No grammar reads it, and you never build a root from the tail. It just means that if you know what a thing is, you already know two letters of its root, and an unknown word in a text still tells you roughly what it names. A country or territory name has no tail: it is spelled from the English sound ([place-name roots](singability.md#place-name-roots)).

Each letter **echoes** a job it already has somewhere in the grammar: the letter points at that job as a memory aid, and grammar does not read it.

<!-- cheat-sheet: consonants -->
| Second consonant | Kind | Example | Echo |
|------------------|------|---------|------|
| `z` | creatures: animals, microbes | `ogoze` *cat* | [`/z/`](/clause#role-letters) is the one who acts |
| `y` | people: persons, kin, roles, and what only a person does | `eboyo` *baby* | [`/y/` + name](/speech-moves#vocative) calls someone |
| `m` | mind and feeling: faces, emotions, mental states | `evemu` *fear* | [**-m**](/word-endings#abstract-m) is the abstract ending |
| `b` | the body and what goes on it: parts, clothes, what you carry, medicine | `aheba` *hand* | [`/b/`](/clause#role-letters) is the bolted-on piece |
| `v` | doing, playing and moving: actions, toys, vehicles | `eruvo` *run* | [`/v/`](/clause#role-letters) is the action |
| `h` | places and fixtures: buildings, landscapes, rooms, headings | `oheha` *house* | [`/h/`](/clause#role-letters) is where |
| `l` | nature and raw stuff: plants, weather, sky, materials, colors | `edalo` *tree* | [**-l**](/word-endings#concrete-l) is the concrete ending |
| `d` | food and drink, with dishes and kitchen gear | `alede` *apple* | [`/d/`](/clause#role-letters) is what is done to (eaten) |
| `w` | tools and things you use | `anewu` *knife* | [`/w/`](/clause#role-letters) is *with* |
| `g` | signs and marks: symbols, letters, religion marks | `ewugu` *warning* | [`/ɡ/`](/clause#role-letters) is a grade, a mark on something |
| `r` | counting, time, money and rhythm | `ugoro` *clock* | `r` after a role letter starts a [number](/numbers#counts) |
| `n` | titles: mail, paper, labels, and named sports | `unona` *newspaper*, `ezuna` *soccer* | [**-n**](/proper-names#proper-name--n) is the name ending |

The last vowel is the job. It hangs on [joins](/joins): what the root **does to stuff** (gathers, travels the line, one piece, takes away). **a** holds (the list grows), **o** path or sequence (rank: the line stays), **e** one compact thing (choice: one piece), **u** takes away.

<!-- cheat-sheet: vowels -->
| Last vowel | Job | Examples |
|------------|-----|----------|
| `a` | holds or gathers: containers, groups, masses, what you go inside; or hollow, like a bowl | `abowa` *basket*, `aruda` *rice*, `eneza` *ant* (it lives in a colony) |
| `o` | order: ranked, in sequence, a stage or cycle, a path you travel or are led along; or long, like a road | `oraho` *road*, `ugoro` *clock*, `eruvo` *run*, `urulo` *rainbow* |
| `e` | one whole thing: round or compact, like a ball | `ogoze` *cat*, `alede` *apple* |
| `u` | takes away or works against: cuts, removes, harms, stings, forbids; or sharp, like a blade | `anewu` *knife*, `ezozu` *snake*, `evemu` *fear* |

When more than one job fits, try **u** first, then **a**, then **o**. A knife is one tool, but it cuts, so its root ends in `u`. When no job fits, the thing's outline decides: hollow `a`, long `o`, round or compact `e`, sharp `u`. A banana ends in `o` and an apple in `e`. For a feeling, it's which way the feeling points (toward others `a`, along a standing `o`, inward `e`); for an action, how it unfolds (with others `a`, along a course `o`, once on the spot `e`).

In every kind but feelings, the **middle vowel** usually says how big the thing is:

<!-- cheat-sheet: vowels -->
| Middle vowel | Size | Examples |
|--------------|------|----------|
| `a` | about your size or bigger | whale, ship, bed, bicycle |
| `o` | between your hand and you | dog, chair, guitar |
| `e` | no bigger than your hand | ant, key, apple, phone |
| `u` | nothing you could measure: a stuff, an act, a symbol, a time | rice, red, run, arrow, night |

This is a hint, not a rule: a few roots take another vowel to stay apart from a neighbor. Feelings have no size, so a feeling's middle vowel carries no meaning.

A [dictionary word](/compounds#lexical-compounds) made of two roots ends in its head root, so it keeps the head's tail. A few dictionary words end in a bare tail instead of a second root.

### Tail suffixes {#tail-suffixes}

A few dictionary words end in a bare tail instead of a second root. *Friend* is *hug* plus `ye`: `y` for a person, `e` for one thing marked by the left piece, so *a person known by hugs*. The two letters already say what kind of thing the word names, so it needs no second root such as *person*.

> `zahuyayel.`
>
> z-friend
>
> "a friend"

| Ageben | Pieces | English |
|--------|--------|---------|
| `zahuyayel` | *hug* + `ye` | friend |
| `zoyahehal` | *bed* + `ha` | bedroom |
| `zebezezal` | *bird* + `za` | flock |
| `zavogeyel` | *flag* + `ye` | citizen |
| `zahahayel` | *hut* + `ye` | neighbor |
| `zugeweyel` | *camera* + `ye` | celebrity |

The left piece is always a five-letter root, and there is no join letter: five letters, then the tail, then the ending. The tail vowel does the same jobs as on a root: **a** the thing holds or gathers the left, **o** it follows the left in order or along a path, **e** it is one thing marked by the left, **u** it takes the left away or works against it. The tail consonant is any kind but nature `l`, mind `m` and counting `r`; those words take a full second root.

When the thing is a more specific kind than its tail, the dictionary word keeps two full roots: *doorbell* ends in *bell*, not in a bare tool tail.

Like every dictionary word, you look these up. You never add a tail to a root on the spot; to join two meanings yourself, use **`x`**.
