# People, things and places

Recipes for English *as for*, *whose*, *different from*, official titles, and pointing back across roles. Every form here is taught on a grammar page; each recipe links what it needs.

## Talking about someone {#someone}

### As for (`hahugem`) {#as-for}

**Needs:** [Adverbs](clause.md#adverbs-h) · [Extra nouns](clause.md#extra-nouns)

English *as for X* or *regarding X* names who or what the sentence is about, even when X is not the subject or object. In Agebon, put **`hahugem`** *as for* in `/h/` and X in a `bl-` word right after it: `hahugem bluzozen` is *as for Uzozen*. The rest of the sentence keeps its free order. (cue: #️⃣ *hash*: a hashtag marks what the post is about.)

> `hahugem bluzozen zolazun dagozol vawebol.`
>
> [h-as-for | bl-Uzozen] | z-Olazun | d-cat | v-see
>
> "As for Uzozen, Olazun sees a cat."

**Compare with:** *as for* frames this one sentence and leaves the talk's topic alone. English *now, about Uzozen* sets the [topic](pronouns.md#topic) from here on (`xuzozen`).

### Which noun, with resume and kinds
<a id="which-noun-intermediate"></a>

**Needs:** [Which person or thing](dependents.md#which-noun) · [Resume](pronouns.md#resume-r) · [Role compounds](roles.md#role-compounds) · [Asides](spans.md#asides-th)

Beginner already used two sentences for English *who / that / which*. Prefer [resume **-r**](pronouns.md#resume-r) in the second sentence.

> `zagayul vuhohol. zuzozen vezuyel bagayur.`
>
> z-guard | v-sit . z-Uzozen | v-tell | b-←guard
>
> "A guard sits. Uzozen talks to that guard."

> `zuzozen dudozol vawebol al babohal. zudozor varuvel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-dog | v-see | [in | b-bank] . z-←dog | v-run
>
> "Uzozen sees a dog in a bank. The dog runs."

A place, tool, or *of*-relation on the noun is still an extra-noun [hook](hooks.md#extra-noun) or hosted `/ɡ/` + `/b/` (*the guard [in](hooks.md#extra-noun) a bank*, *a book written [with a hammer](hooks.md#extra-noun)*). A one-place role (*a guard who sits*, *a place where someone sleeps*) is a [role compound](roles.md#role-compounds) on `/ɡ/` with a non-resume ending; add **`the`** for a usual role (*a teacher*), the same pattern as *is a teacher*. Resume **-r** on that compound is **this instance** (*the one teaching*), not the occupation. A noun in the middle of an act (*a fighting guard*) is the plain event root on `/ɡ/` ([in progress](predication.md#in-progress)).

> `zuzozen dagayul gwazuhohol vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [d-guard | g-episode-agent-chair] | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sees a guard who sits."

For English *whose*, resume the person after the [whose hook **`em`**](hooks.md#genitive) in the second sentence. Use **`gogunum`** instead when the point is that the guard legally owns it.

> `zagayul vuhohol. zudozol em bagayur varuvel.`
>
> z-guard | v-sit . [z-dog | [used-by | b-←guard]] | v-run
>
> "A guard sits. The guard's dog runs."

English can pack this into one sentence: *The guard whose dog runs sits.*

English uses the same *who* clause for two jobs. When it picks out **which** one (*the guard who sits*, not the other guard), use the two-sentence pattern or a role compound, as above. When it only adds extra information about someone already known (*Uzozen, who walks, sings*), put that information in a noun [aside](spans.md#asides-th) **`g(…)`** right after the noun.

> `zuzozen g(zuzozer vowurel) vezuyol.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | g-ASIDE[z-←Uzozen | v-walk]] | v-sing
>
> "Uzozen, who walks, sings."

**Compare with:** ordinary `guhohol` is *chair* / *position* as a property, not *sitting*: the root's everyday sense is a chair, not an event, so it has no [in-progress](predication.md#in-progress) reading. For *sitting*, use the role word `gwazuhohol`. [Restrictors](restrictors.md) say when a **claim** counts (*never*, *only when it rains*), not which noun. Two hosted `/ɡ/` + `/b/` pairs on the **same** first noun still need another sentence ([complex chaining](clause.md#complex-chaining)).

### Stacked owners (*Uzozen's dog's bone*) {#stacked-owners}

**Needs:** [Whose (`em`)](hooks.md#genitive) · [Sequence `ae`](joins.md#sequence-ae)

When English stacks possessives, keep one **`em`** and list every owner in its `/b/`, closed by the sequence join **`bael`**. Put the **nearest** owner first: the first `/b/` uses the host, and each later `/b/` keeps the one before it.

> `zobel em budozol buzozen bael.`
>
> [z-bone | [used-by | [b-dog | b-Uzozen | b-in-order]]]
>
> "Uzozen's dog's bone."

**Compare with:** a set join in the same slot (`budozol buzozen bal`) says both use the bone directly. Only the sequence says *the dog's bone, and Uzozen's dog*.

### *The day when …* {#day-when}

**Needs:** [Adverbs](clause.md#adverbs-h) · [Resume](pronouns.md#resume-r)

English *the day when* / *the place where* is two sentences. Name the time or place first, then resume it with **-r** in `/h/` in the next sentence: *on that day*.

> `zolazun vawebol duzurem. zuzozen vowurel huzurer.`
>
> z-Olazun | v-see | d-day . z-Uzozen | v-walk | h-←day
>
> "Olazun sees the day when Uzozen walks."

### Different from {#different}

**Needs:** [Similative](relations.md#similative) · [Negation `u`](joins.md#negation-u)

For *unlike* / *different from*, use the [similative](relations.md#similative) **`gewom`** with the model in `/b/`, then close it with a negating join **`gul`**. The match to the model is denied.

> `zuzozen gewom blolazun gul.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [[g-like | bl-Olazun] | g-not]
>
> "Uzozen is different from Olazun."

### *What someone did* {#free-relative-what}

**Needs:** [The undergoer (`d`)](roles.md#the-undergoer-d) · [This instance (`-r`)](roles.md#this-instance-r)

English *what* in *Olazun sees what Uzozen writes* names the thing an event happened to, with no noun for it. State that event first, then point back at its undergoer with the **`u`** role compound in **-r**: *the thing written*.

> `zuzozen varayel. zolazun vawebol dwadarayer.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-write . z-Olazun | v-see | d-←episode-patient-write
>
> "Olazun sees what Uzozen writes."

Keep the two verbs different. **-r** points at the latest matching event, so `zolazun vawebol dwadawebor` would point at Olazun's own seeing.

**Compare with:** `dar` is *something* (a new, unnamed thing), not the thing from an event already said.

### *Someone's act* (*Uzozen's walk*) {#someones-act}

**Needs:** [Which person or thing](dependents.md#which-noun) · [Resume](pronouns.md#resume-r) · [Of relations](relations.md#of-relations) · [Negation](joins.md#negation-u)

English *'s* on an act (*Uzozen's walk*, *the monkey's tricks*) names who did it. **`em`** is for things in someone's use, and an act is not one. Say the act as its own sentence first, then resume its verb root with **-r** in the next sentence: *that walk*.

> `zuzozen vowurel. zolazun dowurer vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk . z-Olazun | d-←walk | v-see
>
> "Olazun sees Uzozen's walk."

When the act was denied (`vul`), the resume points at the act **as denied**: the not-doing. That is how English *the forgetting* or *the failure to call* comes back after *did not remember* or *did not call*.

> `zuzozen dolazun vevum vul. zevur gabedom.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-Olazun | [v-memory | v-not] . z-←memory | g-mishap
>
> "Uzozen didn't remember Olazun. The forgetting was a mishap."

When the thing only comes **from** B (*the sound of the drums*), use origin **`gaham`** + `/b/` instead.

> `zolazun dadowol gaham bludoralx vebal.`
>
> z-Olazun | [d-audio | [g-origin | bl-drum-x]] | v-hear
>
> "Olazun hears the sound of the drums."

### Named handles {#named-handles}

**Needs:** [Proper name `-n`](word-endings.md#proper-name--n) · [Multipart names](word-endings.md#multipart-names) · [Initials](numbers-applied.md#initials)

A long official title (bureau, act, titled group) may have a **handle**: one published root plus **-n**, used like any proper name, including on first mention. The long form is the same office spelled as a hyphenated name (`ROOT x ROOT` … + **-n**).

> `zanan vezuyel.`
>
> z-Anan | v-tell
>
> "Anan announces." (Anan is an office, named here for the first time)

| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| `zanan` | handle: one root + **-n** | default talk, including first mention |
| `zanaxolazun` | long title: hyphenated name (`ROOT x ROOT` … + **-n**) | full legal / ceremonial name of the same office |
| `z@_nl` | initials: each title root's first consonant, as a letter code | short name of the same office, like *FBI* |

> `zanaxolazun.`
>
> z-Ana-x-Olazun
>
> "Book-Courage" (the office's full title)

Resume the handle like any name: its whole stem + **-r** (`zanar`).

**Compare with:** a [mention](spans.md#mention) is the **word** (`glegel z[ana]` = *the word “ana”*). A foreign code whose letters all have Agebon letters is a native [letter code](numbers-applied.md#letter-codes) (`z_v16` *F-16*); one with a letter Agebon lacks stays an opaque span (<code>z@&lt;FBI&gt;</code>).

### An English name as Agebon {#english-name}

**Needs:** [Named `-n`](word-endings.md#named-n-beginner) · [Countries](word-endings.md#countries-traditions) · [Opaque](spans.md#loans) · [Respell](lexicon.md#respell)

English *Japan* as a place already has a published root: write `azabanan`. English *Sam* as a person is either a published root you pick as a name, a respelling that follows the English sound, or a span that keeps the original letters.

> `zolazun dazabanan vawebol.`
>
> z-Olazun | d-Azabanan | v-see
>
> "Olazun sees Japan."

To keep *Sam* as English spelling, write the span. To see the Agebon sound-spelling, type the name in the [lexicon](lexicon.md#respell).

> <code>z@&lt;Sam&gt; vowurel.</code>
>
> z-NAME.OPAQUE["Sam"] | v-walk
>
> "Sam walks."

**Compare with:** a learner’s name in the examples is a published root plus **-n**, chosen on [word endings](word-endings.md#named-n-beginner), not a respelling of their English name.

### Who does it: *student*, *singer*, *manager* {#agent-nouns}

**Needs:** [Role compounds (the doer)](roles.md#role-compounds) · [One time or a practice](roles.md#one-time-or-a-practice) · [Saying someone is a teacher](roles.md#saying-someone-is-a-teacher) · [The role you act in](roles.md#capacity)

English names a person by what they usually do (*a singer*, *a manager*). Agebon builds that noun from the event root: person letter, **`w`**, the practiced-role scope **`e`**, the doer **`z`**, the root, then the ending ([one time or a practice](roles.md#one-time-or-a-practice)). `zwezezuyol` is *a singer* from `ezuyol` *sing*, and on `/ɡ/` (`gwezezuyol`) it says someone is one. With scope **`a`**, `zwazezuyol` is only *someone who sings*. Only roots whose everyday sense is the event work this way, so one stem often covers several English jobs.

> `zwezeluyel varayel.`
>
> z-role-agent-learn | v-write
>
> "A student writes."

Rows with scope **`a`** name a part in one event (*winner*, *witness*, *passenger*), so that vowel is enough. The one acted on uses part **`d`**: `zwadenamul` is *a victim* (the one harmed). A plural audience or staff takes plural **-x** (`zwazebalx`).

A noun that specifies the job goes in the stem: `zwezudozoxowurel` is *a dog walker*. The noun's part in the job is as open as in English.

English *as a teacher, I …* puts the practiced-role compound on `/h/`: `hwezuduyel` is *as a teacher* ([the role you act in](roles.md#capacity)). `hwuezuduyel` is *not as a teacher*, speaking off duty.

**Compare with:** *like a teacher* is the [similative](relations.md#similative) (`hewom blwezuduyel`).

*Shareholder* is *owner* of a part: `zwazogunum gobem blahehal` (owner of a part of the house). *Colleague* is a worker tied to you: `zwezuzubal gohuyam bluwon`. *Relative* is a person who is part of the family: `zeyol gobem blavayal`.

**Compare with:** for *boss* or *chief*, the tie form names whom they lead: `zolazun gugobem bluzozen` is *Olazun is Uzozen's boss* ([social relations](relations.md#social-relations)).

### Kin words {#kin-words}

**Needs:** [Kin](numbers-applied.md#kin-generations) · [Adjectives `/ɡ/`](clause.md#adjectives-ɡ)

English *brother*, *sister*, *aunt*, *grandmother* name one layer of the family tree and a sex. Agebon says the layer with the kin number and the sex with **`gemugol`** *male* or **`gevugol`** *female*, after `zeyol` *person*. Put the person the tie is from in a `bl-` word right after the number.

> `zeyol gevugol grewamol bluzozen.`
>
> [z-person | g-female | [g-#-e0 | bl-Uzozen]]
>
> "Uzozen's sister."

| English | Agebon |
|---------|--------|
| *mother* / *mom* | `zeyol gevugol grewubal bluzozen` |
| *father* / *dad* | `zeyol gemugol grewubal bluzozen` |
| *brother* | `zeyol gemugol grewamol bluzozen` |
| *sister* | `zeyol gevugol grewamol bluzozen` |
| *aunt* (a sister of a parent) | `zeyol gevugol grewamol bleyol grewubal bluzozen` |
| *grandmother* (a parent of a parent) | `zeyol gevugol grewubal bleyol grewubal bluzozen` |
| *generation* | the kin number itself: `grewubal` parents, `grewamol` siblings, `grewabal` children |

For *uncle* and *grandfather*, use `gemugol` for `gevugol`.

### Young, male and female animals {#young-animals}

**Needs:** [Adjectives `/ɡ/`](clause.md#adjectives-ɡ)

English *puppy*, *kitten*, *mare* and *ewe* each pack an animal together with its age or sex. Agebon says the animal's root, then an adjective: **`gahoyel`** *young* (the child root), **`geboyel`** *newborn* (the baby root), **`gevugol`** *female*, **`gemugol`** *male*. These are the same adjectives the [kin words](#kin-words) use for *sister* and *brother*.

> `zudozol gahoyel vowurel.`
>
> [z-dog | g-child] | v-walk
>
> "A puppy walks."

Adjectives stack, so a female lamb is sheep, young, female.

> `zuzozen dehozal gahoyel gevugol vawebol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [d-sheep | g-child | g-female] | v-see
>
> "Uzozen sees a female lamb."

| English | Agebon |
|---------|--------|
| *puppy* | `zudozol gahoyel` |
| *kitten* | `zagozol gahoyel` |
| *lamb* | `zehozal gahoyel` |
| *foal* | `zohazel gahoyel` |
| *duckling* | `zudozal gahoyel` |
| *newborn calf* | `zagazal geboyel` |
| *mare* | `zohazel gevugol` |
| *stallion* | `zohazel gemugol` |
| *ewe* | `zehozal gevugol` |
| *lioness* | `zolazul gevugol` |
| *hen* | `zuhozal gevugol` |

Some young and some males already have a root of their own. Use it: `zuhezel` *chick* and `zarozel` *rooster*. A young animal that looks like a different creature has its own dictionary word too, such as `zubezelabezul` *caterpillar*.

### Buying and selling {#buy-sell}

**Needs:** [Exchange](relations.md#exchange)

English *buy* and *sell* are two views of one swap. Agebon has one trading verb, `veham`, and **`heham`** names what is given in return. Put what changes hands in `/d/` and what it is swapped for in `/b/`. Money in `/d/` and the goods in `/b/` is *buy*; the goods in `/d/` and money in `/b/` is *sell*.

> `zuzozen daral heham blanal veham.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-money | [h-in-exchange-for | bl-book] | v-convertibility
>
> "Uzozen buys a book."

> `zuzozen danal heham blaral veham.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-book | [h-in-exchange-for | bl-money] | v-convertibility
>
> "Uzozen sells a book."

*Sale*, *purchase* and *trade* name the same swap as a noun; say it as the sentence, then [point back](dependents.md#which-noun) to it.

### Give, get, lend, earn, owe {#give-get}

**Needs:** [The extra `/b/` party (`o`)](roles.md#role-compounds) · [Extra noun (`el`, `em`)](hooks.md#extra-noun) · [Exchange](relations.md#exchange) · [Coordinated lists](joins.md#respectively) · [Fault](causation.md#fault)

English *give*, *get*, *receive*, *provide*, *lend* and *borrow* are several views of one handing-over. Agebon keeps the giving verb `vewal` (*present*) and moves the view by where you put people. The thing sits in `/d/`, and the one who gets it sits in plain `/b/` after the verb ([recipient](clause.md#extra-nouns)). To name the parties from the verb itself, use the [role compounds](roles.md#role-compounds): **`zwazewal`** is the giver (*supplier*, *provider*) and **`zwabewal`** is the recipient (*receiver*). **-r** (`zwabewar`) is *this* recipient of the latest giving. *Lend* and *borrow* work the same way on `vegeral` (*credit*, with the *lend* sense): the borrower is the recipient.

| English | Agebon | Reading |
|---------|--------|---------|
| *give* / *provide* / *contribute* / *submit* / *hand in* | `zuzozen danal vewal bolazun.` | the book goes to Olazun |
| *get* / *receive* / *acquire* (a thing handed over) | the same sentence | the receiver is the `/b/` |
| *giver* / *supplier* / *provider* | `zwazewal` | doer of the giving |
| *receiver* | `zwabewal` | the one given to |
| *lend* / *borrow* | `zuzozen danal vegeral bolazun.` | the book is lent to Olazun |
| *borrower* | `zwabegeral` | the one lent to |
| *distribute* | `zuzozen danal vewal bolazun bahelan bal.` | the book goes to each of them |
| *earn* | `zuzozen varayel heham blaral.` | write in exchange for money |
| *owe* | `zuzozen garam blolazun.` | in debt to Olazun |
| *get* (go after something) | `zuzozen vowurel el banal.` | walk for the book |
| *contribute money* | `zuzozen daral vewal el bavayal.` | give money for a family's sake |
| *contribute to* (be part of the cause) | `…therar barl …` | one share among several causes |

> `zuzozen danal vewal bolazun.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-book | v-present | b-Olazun
>
> "Uzozen gives Olazun a book." / "Olazun gets a book from Uzozen."

> `zuzozen varayel heham blaral.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-write | [h-in-exchange-for | bl-money]
>
> "Uzozen earns money by writing."

> `zuzozen garam blolazun.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [g-debt | bl-Olazun]
>
> "Uzozen owes Olazun."

### Join, belong, comprise, replace, represent, prefer, involve, agree {#relating-verbs}

**Needs:** [Rank joins](joins.md#rank-joins) · [Join-act verbs](join-across-roles.md#join-act-verbs) · [Join-relations](join-across-roles.md#join-relations) · [Of relations](relations.md#of-relations) · [Whose](hooks.md#genitive) · [Exchange](relations.md#exchange) · [Proxy](relations.md#proxy) · [Simile](relations.md#similative)

Many English verbs (*join*, *belong*, *consist*, *replace*, *represent*, *prefer*) say how two things **stand to each other** more than what anyone does. ### Contribution, acquisition, murder, comparison and other act nouns {#act-nouns}

**Needs:** [The event itself](roles.md#the-event-itself) · [Role compounds](roles.md#role-compounds) · [Someone's act](#someones-act) · [Fault](causation.md#fault) · [Label scope](predication.md#label-scope) · [Proxy](relations.md#proxy) · [Exchange](relations.md#exchange)

English turns many acts into nouns (*contribution*, *acquisition*, *murder*, *comparison*). Agebon has no separate noun for them. Put the verb stem on a thing letter with no **`w`** and part to name the act itself, write **`d`** as the part to name the thing the act happens to, or say the act as a sentence and resume it with **-r**. A wrongness or a pattern is the pole or label on the act, not a different noun.

| English | Agebon | Reading |
|---------|--------|---------|
| *contribution* / *acquisition* (the act) | `zewal` | a giving, seen as a whole |

| *contribution* (a share in a cause) | `thahur blarl` | one share among several causes |
| *representation* (acting for someone) | `zuzozen ganom blolazun` | Uzozen, on behalf of Olazun |
| *reaction* / *response* | `zogewem` | the answer to what unlocked it |

| *murder* (a wrongful killing) | `zolazun vezobul thahul blarl zuzozen vugevul.` | a death, and the fault is Uzozen's shooting |
| *comparison* / *contrast* (setting two against each other) | `zezom` | contrast |
| *compare A with B* | `zuzozen dolazun vezom bahelan.` | sets Olazun against Ahelan |
| *preference* (the one preferred) | `zolamalugulel` | the one loved first |
| *tendency* (a type across scenes) | `zalamuthul` | lying as a type: a tendency to lie |

| *tends to* / *often lies* | `zuzozen valamul huam.` | usually lies |
| *a lying type* | `zuzozen valamuthul.` | the type-claim |

### Other, another, different {#other-different}

**Needs:** [Unspecified member](joins.md#unspecified-member-r-phrase) · [Similative](relations.md#similative) · [Different from](#different)

English *another dog* and *the other one* name something outside the ones already in play. Put **`zur`** (*something else*) after the kind: `zudozol zur` is *another dog*. Resemblance is the similative: `gewom` + `/b/` is *similar to*, and `hewom` is *similarly*. Denying it with **`gul`** (or **`hul`** on an adverb) gives *different* and *differently*.

> `zuzozen vawebol dudozol dur.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-see | [d-dog | d-something-else]
>
> "Uzozen sees another dog."

| English | Agebon |
|---------|--------|
| *similar* (*similar to Olazun*) | `gewom blolazun` |
| *similarly* (*walks like Olazun*) | `hewom blolazun` |
| *different* (*different from Olazun*) | `gewom blolazun gul` |
| *differently* | `hewom blolazun hul` |
| *differ* (*Uzozen and Olazun differ*) | `zuzozen zolazun zal gewom gul` |

## Pointing back across roles {#pointing-back}

### *Himself*, *herself*, *themselves* {#reflexive}

**Needs:** [Resume](pronouns.md#resume-r)

English *himself*, *herself*, *itself* and *themself* say the object is the same one as the subject. Put the subject's resume (**-r**) in `/d/`. Agebon does not mark sex or number in the resume, so one form covers all of them. With plural **-x** on the subject and the resume it is *themselves*, and with the inclusive *we* (`zayan`) it is *ourselves*.

> `zuzozen vawebol duzozer.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-see | d-←Uzozen
>
> "Uzozen sees themself."

> `zudozolx vawebol dudozorx.`
>
> z-dog-x | v-see | d-←dog-x-x
>
> "The dogs see themselves."

> `zayan vebal dayar.`
>
> z-interlocutors | v-hear | d-←interlocutors
>
> "We hear ourselves." (you and I)

**Compare with:** for *each other*, join the people and put `hehem` after the verb ([pronouns](pronouns.md#resume-r)).

### Cross-role inventory {#cross-role-inventory}

**Needs:** [Resume](pronouns.md#resume-r) · [Cross-role recast](pronouns.md#cross-role-recast)

Intermediate already recast one referent into a new slot. Each table is an **antecedent** role; rows are the **pronoun** role. The mechanism is still one **-r**.

> `zudozol vowurel. zowurer gelazom.`
>
> z-dog | v-walk . z-←walk | g-big
>
> "A dog walks. That walk is big."

#### Antecedent was a noun (`/z/`, `/d/`, or `/b/`)

| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| `/z/` `/d/` `/b/` … `-r` | ordinary 3rd-person slot | *he / she / it / they*; *that (one)* (`/b/` still follows the host relation) |
| `/v/` … `-r` | the same action again, now involving that entity | *do the same to / with it* |
| `/ɡ/` … `-r` | of that entity’s kind (not possession) | *of that kind* |
| `/w/` … `-r` | degree or frame on a property of that entity | *that* degree / frame |
| `/h/` … `-r` | occasion framed by that entity | *then* / *that way* (framed by that entity) |
| `/y/` vocative … `-r` | address that entity | vocative |
| `/x/` … `-r` | return: sets the [topic](pronouns.md#topic) again | *Going back to subject X* |

Examples:

> `zuzozen vemowul. zolazun duzozer vawebol.`
>

>
> z-radio | g-big . z-studio-mic | g-←radio
>

> `zerowol welazom gelazom. zuzewol werowor gelazom.`
>
> z-radio | [w-very | g-big] . z-studio-mic | [w-←radio | g-big]
>
> "A radio is very big. A studio mic is that big too."

> `zuzozen verowom. zolazun huzozer vemowul.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-broadcast . z-Olazun | h-←Uzozen | v-mute
>

> `zuzozen vemowul. yuzozer.`

> `zuzozen vemowul. zolazun vanuvul. xuzozer varemul.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-mute . z-Olazun | v-punch . x-←Uzozen | v-scream

## Talk moves and quoting {#talk-quoting}

### Arrow roots as talk-moves

**Needs:** [Arrow rose](roles.md#arrow-rose) · [Sentence linkers](dependents.md#sentence-linkers)

When *east* / *west* / *southwest* name a move in the talk, not a map point, write the published root **bare** (no **`th`** + facing person). Generic heading is **`ure`** (*direction*). Facing-relative ahead / back still uses **`DIR th ANCHOR`**.

> `zuzozen vowurel. xahem zolazun varuvel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk . x-therefore | z-Olazun | v-run
>
> "Uzozen walks. Therefore Olazun runs."

| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| **`ahem`** (bare) | talk moves on | *progress*; discourse **`xahem`** *therefore* |
| **`owuhem`** (bare) | talk comes back | *return* |
| **`uzuhem`** (bare) | talk pulls back | *retreat* |
| **`urel`** | heading in general | *compass* / *direction* |

**For *therefore*, use:** the [linker](dependents.md#sentence-linkers) **`xahem`**. Body *left* / *right* still needs **`DIR th ANCHOR`**.

### Literal content (fence words / fence marks)
<a id="literal-content"></a>
<a id="escape"></a>

**Needs:** [Opaque and loan words](spans.md#loans)

When a fence glyph (`[` / `]` / `(` / `)` / `<` / `>` / `{` / `}` / `=` / `|` / `#`) or a [tone mark](speech-moves.md#tone-marks) (`!` / `?`) must appear **as content**, wrap that token in an **opaque** `<…>` span. The outer span’s wording is the opaque **interior** (the wrapper is packaging, not extra cited words).

```agebon
d[ varayel d<]> ]
d[ d<|> ]
d[ vezuyel d<#> ]
```

`d[ vezuyel d<#> ]` is a whole cite whose last content character is `#` (a bare `#]` would be editorial close). A hyphen before a closer is ordinary content.

Opaque `d<…>` closes on the first `>`. If the blob **contains** `>`, write it as a cite instead, which closes on the first `]`:

```agebon
d[code > 1]
```

**For *ordinals*, use:** [number words](numbers.md) with `#`. Inside a span, `#` is an editorial closer only when it sits immediately before `]` / `)` / `>` / `|`. `|` is close-all.

### Role, title, address {#role-title-address}

**Needs:** [Role compounds](roles.md#role-compounds) · [Kind / role](predication.md#classification) · [Cite](spans.md#writing) · [Call someone](speech-moves.md#vocative) · [Vocatives](plurality.md#vocatives-y)

These three English nouns each hide two jobs. *Role* is the function someone plays. Say it as a kind on `/ɡ/`, or as the usual doer of the event with a role compound and **`the`**. *Title* is either the name of a work or a claim to own something. *Address* is either speaking to someone or a speech.

| English | Agebon | Reading |
|---------|--------|---------|
| *role* (the function someone plays) | `zolazun gwezuduyel.` | the usual role: role compound with **`the`** on `/ɡ/` |
| *title* (the name of a work) | `d@[enuron olazun]` | cite with **`@`** |
| *title* (a claim to own) | `zogunum` | *ownership*, the abstract sense of `ogunu` |
| *address* (speak to someone) | `yolazun.` | call them with `/y/` + their name |
| *address* (speak to a group) | `yeyonx.` | `/y/` + the kind as a title, **-nx** |
| *address* (a speech) | `vezuyel` | the telling itself |

> `zolazun gwezuduyel.`
>
> z-Olazun | g-role-agent-teach
>
> "Olazun is a teacher." / "Olazun's role is teacher."

**Compare with:** a street address is an ordinary named place (**-n**), with no separate form.

### Sentence, phrase, word {#words-about-words}

**Needs:** [Mention](spans.md#mention)

To talk about a word, a phrase or a whole sentence as a thing in itself, use a mention: the marker **`glegel`**, then the span. English *the word …*, *the phrase …* and *the sentence …* all use the same frame. The span is a cite of the Agebon, and its role letter still says what the mention does in the clause.

> `glegel z[udozo] gamezom.`
>
> [gl-MENTION | z-CITE["udozo"]] | g-small
>
> "The word “udozo” is small."

> `glegel z[zolazun vezuyel] gamezom.`
>
> [gl-MENTION | z-CITE[z-Olazun | v-tell]] | g-small
>
> "The sentence “zolazun vezuyel” is small."
