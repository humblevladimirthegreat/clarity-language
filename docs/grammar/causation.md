# Causation {#causation}

English *if*, *only if*, and *because* each tie a condition to an outcome. Agebon uses a pole word for each, and the poles keep three questions apart: whether the condition is **enough** for the outcome, whether the outcome **needs** it, and whether you claim the condition is a **fact**.

## Beginner {#beginner}

### If {#if}

English *if* says a condition is enough for an outcome: whenever the condition holds, the outcome holds too (*Uzozen walks if it rains*). Write the **outcome** first, then the *if* pole (root **`oho`**), then the condition as its `bl-` word. Saying *if* does not claim the condition is true, and other routes to the outcome may still work. It does not say how the condition leads there either: only that the link holds. (cue: 🚪 *door*: walk through only if it opens)

The pole's role letter follows the outcome. Use `/th/` (**`thohom`**) when the outcome is an **event** (the verb). Use `/ɡ/` (**`gohom`**) when the outcome is a **noun**. A common noun with a `/ɡ/` word after it says the thing exists ([*there is …*](predication.md#existence)), so a noun outcome says *there is X if there is Y*. Keep the pole and its `/b/` together.

> `zuzozen vowurel thohom blerulal.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [th-if | bl-rain]
>
> "Uzozen walks if it rains."

> `zerulal gohom blugalal.`
>
> z-rain | [g-if | bl-cloud]
>
> "There is rain if there are clouds."

As a plain noun, the same root is `zohol` *a door*.

**Compare with:** for *X is enough* about one item from a list of options (*a book will do*), use an inclusive [join](joins.md#single-item-phrase), not a pole.

### Only if {#only-if}

English *only if* (or *needs*) says the outcome cannot hold without the condition. Use the same shape with the *only if* pole (root **`ebo`**): **`thebom`** after a verb, **`gebom`** after a noun. Other things may also be required, and again you do not claim the condition is true. (cue: 🧻 *paper-roll*: without it the situation does not run)

> `zuzozen vowurel thebom blerulal.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [th-only-if | bl-rain]
>
> "Uzozen walks only if it rains."

> `zerulal gebom blugalal.`
>
> z-rain | [g-only-if | bl-cloud]
>
> "There is rain only if there are clouds."

As a plain noun, the same root is `zebol` *a paper-roll*.

**Compare with:** *if* (`thohom` / `gohom`) leaves other routes possible. *Only if* says the outcome needs this.

| Agebon | Use | English | Same root as | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|--------------|-----|
| **`thohom` / `gohom`** | enough for the outcome; condition not asserted | *if* | `ohol` *door* | 🚪: walk through only if it opens |
| **`thebom` / `gebom`** | the outcome needs this; condition not asserted | *only if* / *needs* | `ebol` *paper-roll* | 🧻: without it the situation does not run |

`/th/` attaches to the event. `/ɡ/` attaches to the noun.

## Intermediate {#intermediate}

### A following sentence
<a id="dependent"></a>

When the condition is a **whole sentence** (*if Olazun sleeps*), keep the same pole and use the stand-in **`blarl`** as its `/b/` word. The next sentence is the condition that **`blarl`** stands for. Keep the pole and **`blarl`** together, last in the main sentence. The condition sentence always names its own subject. See [dependent clauses](dependents.md#dependent-clauses).

```text
main sentence …  thohom / thebom / …  barl   [dependent sentence]
```

> `zuzozen vowurel thohom blarl zolazun vezuvol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [th-if | bl-that-clause] | z-Olazun | v-sleep
>
> "Uzozen walks if Olazun is asleep."

> `zuzozen vowurel thebom blarl zolazun vezuvol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [th-only-if | bl-that-clause] | z-Olazun | v-sleep
>
> "Uzozen walks only if Olazun is asleep."

Several conditions can sit inside `/b/` as a [join](joins.md) (`thohom blugalal blozulal boam` *if clouds and/or snow*).

**Related form:** to join two full sentences with *and also*, use a clause [join](joins.md) (`xam`), not a pole.

### Because, iff, and only because {#only-because}
<a id="because"></a>
<a id="iff"></a>
<a id="poles"></a>

Beginner's *if* and *only if* do not claim the condition is true. The table completes the set. Pick the pole by **which way the link runs** (the condition is enough, the outcome needs it, or both) and by whether you **claim** the condition is a fact. Poles take **-m**, on `/th/` after a verb and on `/ɡ/` after a noun. As plain words the roots take ordinary endings (`zahul` *a wall*; `zehem` *reciprocity*).

| Agebon | Use | English | Same root as | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|--------------|-----|
| **`thohom` / `gohom`** | enough; condition not asserted | *if* | `ohol` *door* | 🚪: walk through only if it opens |
| **`thahum` / `gahum`** | enough; condition asserted as the reason | *because* | `ahul` *wall* | 🧱: the cause is what the result sits on |
| **`thebom` / `gebom`** | needed; condition not asserted | *only if* / *needs* | `ebol` *paper-roll* | 🧻: without it the situation does not run |
| **`thebom thahum` / `gebom gahum`** | needed; condition asserted | *only because* / *depends on* | | 🧻 then 🧱: needed, and named as a fact |
| **`thehem` / `gehem`** | both ways; condition not asserted | *if and only if* | `ehel` *east-west* | ↔️: each side only with the other |

> `zuzozen vowurel thahum blarl zolazun vezuvol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [th-because | bl-that-clause] | z-Olazun | v-sleep
>
> "Uzozen walks because Olazun is asleep."

> `zerulal gahum blugalal.`
>
> z-rain | [g-because | bl-cloud]
>
> "There is rain because of the clouds."

> `zuzozen vowurel thehem blarl zolazun vezuvol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [th-iff | bl-that-clause] | z-Olazun | v-sleep
>
> "Uzozen walks if and only if Olazun is asleep."

*Only because* stacks two poles: **`thebom`** (the outcome needs it), then **`thahum`** with the condition (and it is a fact).

> `zuzozen vowurel thebom thahum blerulal.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | th-only-if | [th-because | bl-rain]
>
> "Uzozen walks only because of the rain."

A noun after a because pole names an **act or a thing**, not a person. For *because of Olazun*, say what Olazun did as a sentence after **`blarl`**. A name in that `/b/` slot is a named event or place (a storm, a city), never the person.

English *unless* means *if … not*. Keep **`thohom`**, but use the stand-in **`blurl`** instead of **`blarl`** ([stand-in vowels](dependents.md#stand-in)): the outcome holds unless the next sentence is true.

> `zuzozen vowurel thohom blurl zolazun vezuvol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [th-if | bl-lest-clause] | z-Olazun | v-sleep
>
> "Uzozen walks unless Olazun is asleep."

For *even if*, put the *although* pole **`hezom`** right before **`thohom`**. You still do not claim the condition is true, and the outcome holds either way.

> `zuzozen vowurel hezom thohom blarl zolazun vezuvol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | h-although | [th-if | bl-that-clause] | z-Olazun | v-sleep
>
> "Uzozen walks even if Olazun is asleep."

| Agebon | English |
|--------|---------|
| **`thohom blurl`** | *unless* |
| **`hezom thohom blarl`** | *even if* |

### Fault {#fault}

*Because* names what made the outcome happen. It does not say anyone did wrong. When you mean *it was their fault*, change the ending on the because pole. **-m** is the plain cause. **-l** says the reason broke a rule: someone did wrong. **-r** says it was one cause among others.

<!-- cheat-sheet: why-allowed -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| **`thahul`** | the reason broke a norm | *it's their fault* / *to blame* | **-l** ≈ locked on a broken rule |
| **`thahum`** | the reason made it happen; no fault claimed | *because* | **-m** ≈ the ordinary case |
| **`thahur`** | the reason is one share among others | *partly because* / *played a part* | **-r** ≈ one wall of several holding it up |

> `zuzozen velurul thahul blarl zolazun vezuyel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-departure | [th-because.fault | bl-that-clause] | z-Olazun | v-tell
>
> "Uzozen left because Olazun spoke, and Olazun was wrong to."

> `zuzozen velurul thahum blarl zolazun vezuyel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-departure | [th-because | bl-that-clause] | z-Olazun | v-tell
>
> "Uzozen left because Olazun spoke." (no one is to blame)

> `zuzozen velurul thahur blerulal.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-departure | [th-because.share | bl-rain]
>
> "The rain is part of why Uzozen left."

The rule from [*because*](#only-because) matters most here: the act goes in the `bl-` word, not the person (`thahul blarl zolazun vezuyel` *Olazun's speaking is to blame*, never `thahul blolazun`). So fault lands on what someone did, which leaves room for *Olazun did a wrong thing* without *Olazun is bad*. A thing or circumstance (`blerulal`) is fine there, because there is no person to blame.

Fault is still a claim. You can hedge it with [MAY](knowing.md#may) right before the pole (`thomam thahul blarl …`), or pair **`thebom`** with it for *only because of that fault*.

### CAUSE {#cause}
<a id="cause-mood"></a>

English *that is what makes it happen* points at **how** a result comes about (the mechanism), not only at a condition that holds. Add the stance word **`thewem`** (CAUSE, root **`ewe`**) right before a pole: the pole names the condition, and CAUSE says that condition is how the result is produced. CAUSE can also take its own `/b/` word for the one who makes it happen (below). (cue: ⚙️ *machine*: how the mechanism engages)

As plain words, the same root is `zewel` *a machine* and `vewel` *to operate / engage*.

> `zuzozen vowurel thewem thohom blerulal.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | th-CAUSE | [th-if | bl-rain]
>
> "Uzozen walks if it rains." (rain as the mechanism)

> `zolazun vobuvul thewem thahum blavulul.`
>
> z-Olazun | v-pour | th-CAUSE | [th-because | bl-fire]
>
> "Fire is what makes Olazun pour."

On `/w/` (`wewem`), right before an adjective, CAUSE applies only to that adjective: the mechanism produces that one property.

> `zuzozen wewem galunom vowurel.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | [w-CAUSE | g-exertion]] | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen walks, straining." (something is causing the strain)

**Compare with:** *because* / *if* name the condition (`thahum` / `thohom`). CAUSE says that the link is the mechanism.

#### Make someone do it {#make}

In English *Uzozen makes Olazun speak*, Uzozen makes it happen and Olazun does the speaking. Keep the one who does it (Olazun) as the subject of the verb. Put the one who makes it happen in a `bl-` word right after **`thewem`**, with no pole.

> `zolazun vezuyel thewem bluzozen.`
>
> z-Olazun | v-tell | [th-CAUSE | bl-Uzozen]
>
> "Uzozen makes Olazun speak."

When a pole follows **`thewem`**, the `/b/` belongs to the pole, as above (`thewem thahum blavulul` *because of fire, as mechanism*).

**Compare with:** *Get someone to* (ask, persuade) is tell + a *to* [stand-in](dependents.md#stand-in).

| Agebon | English |
|--------|---------|
| **`thewem`** + `/b/` | *make* (causer in `/b/`) |
