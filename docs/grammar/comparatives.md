# Comparatives, superlatives, and equatives
<a id="comparatives-page"></a>

English splits three nearby jobs on a quality like *challenging*. *More … than* and *the most* put people in **order** on that quality. *As … as* says they sit at the **same height**. *Both are challenging* only says **each has** the quality; it does not say who ranks higher or whether they match. Agebon writes the first two with a rank join from joins followed by the shared adjective, and the third with an ordinary *and*-list.

## Beginner {#beginner}
<a id="overview"></a>

### Comparatives (`e`)
<a id="comparatives"></a>
<a id="comparative-shared-scale"></a>

When English would say *Uzozen is more challenging than Olazun*, you put two people in order on one quality. Write both names, then the **rank join** **`zel`** (vowel **`e`**), then the adjective they share (`/ɡ/`). The first name sits higher on that quality than the second. Both may still sit high; the join only says who ranks higher. (cue: **`e`** ≈ order; the shared `/ɡ/` is the ladder)

> `zuzozen zolazun zel gamahum.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-Olazun | z-rank/more | g-challenge]
>
> "Uzozen is more challenging than Olazun."

> `zahelan zuzozen zel gelazom.`
>
> [z-Ahelan | z-Uzozen | z-rank/more | g-big]
>
> "Ahelan is bigger than Uzozen."

### Superlatives (`zel` + one name)
<a id="superlatives"></a>

When English would say *the most challenging*, you are still ranking, but you name only the winner. One name before `zel`, then the same shared adjective, means that person ranks highest in the group you have in mind.

> `zuzozen zel gamahum.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-rank/more | g-challenge]
>
> "Uzozen is the most challenging."

> `zahelan zel guzemol.`
>
> [z-Ahelan | z-rank/more | g-sleepy]
>
> "Ahelan is the sleepiest."

### Degree (*much* / *slightly*)
<a id="degree"></a>

English *much more* / *slightly more* adds **how large the gap is**, without giving a number. Write that extra detail as `/w/` immediately before the same shared adjective.

> `zuzozen zolazun zel wohalal gamahum.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-Olazun | z-rank/more | [w-ocean | g-challenge]]
>
> "Uzozen is much more challenging than Olazun."

### Equatives (`oe` + shared scale)
<a id="equatives"></a>
<a id="equative"></a>
<a id="as-as"></a>

When English would say *as challenging as*, you are not picking a winner. You are saying two people sit at the **same height** on the quality. Write both names, then the tie **`zoel`** (rank-join vowels **`oe`**), then the shared adjective (`/ɡ/`). Closed **-l** (`zoel`) is an exact match. Open **-m** (`zoem`) is *about as … as*. (cue: **`oe`** ≈ one + order; they share one rank)

> `zuzozen zolazun zoel gamahum.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-Olazun | z-equal-rank | g-challenge]
>
> "Uzozen is as challenging as Olazun."

> `zolazun zahelan zoel guzemol.`
>
> [z-Olazun | z-Ahelan | z-equal-rank | g-sleepy]
>
> "Olazun is as sleepy as Ahelan."

> `zuzozen zolazun zoem gabulol.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-Olazun | z-equal-rank.open | g-bright]
>
> "Uzozen is about as bright as Olazun."

**Compare with:** English *is ADJ* with no *as … as* is [classification](predication.md#classification): `zuzozen gamahum` *Uzozen is challenging*. Use **`oe`** when two people share a height on the quality. *Walks like a duck* is resemblance ([*like*](relations.md#similative) `hewom`), not the same score on a named quality.

**Compare with:** English *both are challenging* is an *and*-list plus a shared adjective (`zuzozen zolazun zal gamahum`): each has the quality. It does not rank them or say they match. Use **`oe`** only for *as … as*.

<!-- cheat-sheet: roles-comparing -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|--------|-----|---------|-----|
| Rank **`e`** (`zel`) | rank on a shared quality | *more … than* / *the …-est* | **`e`** ≈ order |
| Equality **`oe`** (`zoel` / `zoem`) | same height on the quality | *as … as* / *about as … as* | **`oe`** ≈ one + order (one shared rank) |

## Intermediate {#intermediate}

### Full comparative arity
<a id="comparative-arity"></a>

Beginner already used two names plus **`zel`** (*more … than*) and one name (*the …-est*). The same shared-scale pattern also uses reverse rank **`ue`** (*less … than* / *the least*) and **zero** names.

<!-- cheat-sheet: roles-comparing -->
| Agebon | Use | English | Cue |
|---------|-----|---------|-----|
| **Multi (2+)** + shared scale under **`e` / `ue`** | comparative ladder | earlier above later (`e`); later above earlier under **`ue`** | **`e`** ≈ order; **`u`** ≈ undo |
| **Single-item (1)** + shared scale under **`e` / `ue`** | superlative | *the …-est* / *the least …* under **`ue`** | one name is the extreme |
| **Standalone (0)** + shared scale under **`e` / `ue`** | empty superlative | *there is no biggest* / *there is no smallest* under **`ue`**; open **-m** = none comes to mind | empty list: nobody is on top |

A standalone ladder says only that nothing is on top. It does not say why (a tie, no end to the scale, or items that do not compare). To assert a tie, use [equative](#equatives) **`zoel`**. To say someone is on top without naming them, use **`zer`**.

<!-- cheat-sheet: roles-comparing -->
| Example | Reading |
|---------|---------|
| `zuzozen zolazun zahelan zem gamahum` | *Uzozen is more challenging than Olazun, who is more challenging than Ahelan* (open: others may fit in the ranking too) |
| `zuzozen zolazun zuel gamahum` | *Uzozen is less challenging than Olazun* |
| `zel gamahum` | *there is no most challenging* |
| `zem gamahum` | *no most challenging comes to mind* |
| `zuel gamahum` | *there is no least challenging* |
| `zuzozen zuel gamahum` | *Uzozen is the least challenging (of all)* |
| `zer gamahum` | *whatever ranks highest on challenging* (someone is on top; not saying who) |
| `zuzozen zolazun zer gamahum` | *whichever of Uzozen or Olazun ranks higher on challenging* |

**Compare with:** a rank join with no shared adjective is an ordinary [rank join](joins.md#rank-joins) (*A matters more than B*). Add the shared `/ɡ/` when you mean *more ADJ than*.

English *not as challenging as* says the first name is below the second, so it is **`zuel`**: `zolazun zuzozen zuel gamahum` is *Olazun is not as challenging as Uzozen*. For a firm tie, use [`zoel`](#equatives).

English *no more challenging than* says less: the first name is below the second or tied with them. Write the whole comparison as usual, then deny it with **`zul`**, the way you [deny a whole list](joins.md#exclusivity-and-denying-a-whole-list). What English often only hints (that neither is very challenging) is not said.

> `zolazun zuzozen zel gamahum zul.`
>
> [[z-Olazun | z-Uzozen | z-rank/more | g-challenge] | z-not]
>
> "Olazun is no more challenging than Uzozen."

### Place on a scale (`w#N`) {#place-on-a-scale}

English *the second biggest* names a place just below the winner. Keep the superlative frame (one name before `zel`) and put an **ordinal** in the degree slot, `/w/` right before the adjective: **`wredol`** (`w#2`) is *second*. The place is on the scale the adjective names. (cue: **`e`** ≈ order, as in `gredol` *the second*)

> `zuzozen zel wredol gelazom.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-rank/more | [w-2nd | g-big]]
>
> "Uzozen is the second biggest."

Under **`zuel`** the same place counts from the bottom, so `zuzozen zuel wredol gelazom` is *the second smallest*. Use it from *second* on, because first place is the plain superlative. Write it only with one name before `zel` or `zuel`. With two names, the ordinal has no group to rank within, and outside a rank fence an ordinal on `/w/` has no reading.

**Compare with:** an ordinal after a noun picks the Nth *thing* (`zudozol gredol` *the second dog*), not the Nth on a scale. A factor (*twice as big*) goes on `/h/`, not `/w/`.

### Manner scale (`/h/`) {#manner-scale}

When English would say *Olazun walks more intensely than Uzozen*, you rank **how** they walk, not a quality of the people. Keep the same rank join. Write a manner adverb (`/h/`) **immediately after** it, then the verb. The first name sits higher on that manner.

> `zolazun zuzozen zel huhunem vowurel.`
>
> [z-Olazun | z-Uzozen | z-rank/more | h-intensity] | v-walk
>
> "Olazun walks more intensely than Uzozen."

> `zolazun zel huhunem vowurel.`
>
> [z-Olazun | z-rank/more | h-intensity] | v-walk
>
> "Olazun walks the most intensely."

> `zolazun zuzozen zoel huhunem vowurel.`
>
> [z-Olazun | z-Uzozen | z-equal-rank | h-intensity] | v-walk
>
> "Olazun walks as intensely as Uzozen."

**Compare with:** `guhunem` after `zel` (no verb) is *more intense* as a quality of the people. After the verb, `huhunem` is an ordinary *how* they walk and ranks nothing.

### Amount scale (`gral`) {#amount-scale}

When English would say *more cats than dogs*, you rank **how many**, not a quality. Keep the rank join. For the shared word, write the [digitless number](digitless-exponents.md#digitless) **`gral`**: after a rank join it means *by amount*.

> `zagozolx zudozolx zel gral.`
>
> [z-cat-x | z-dog-x | z-rank/more | g-amount]
>
> "There are more cats than dogs."

> `zagozolx zudozolx zuel gral.`
>
> [z-cat-x | z-dog-x | z-rank/less | g-amount]
>
> "There are fewer cats than dogs."

> `zagozolx zudozolx zoel gral.`
>
> [z-cat-x | z-dog-x | z-equal-rank | g-amount]
>
> "There are as many cats as dogs."

The noun decides whether English says *many* or *much*: cats are counted, tea is measured. Agebon uses the same **`gral`** for both.

Open **`gram`** ranks by a **rough impression** instead of a count: *it looks like more cats than dogs*. Use **`gral`** when you counted or measured, and **`gram`** when you are going by how it seems.

> `zagozolx zudozolx zel gram.`
>
> [z-cat-x | z-dog-x | z-rank/more | g-amount.about]
>
> "It looks like there are more cats than dogs."

To say **how many more**, put the difference in a `bl-` word right after **`gral`**. A count needs no unit noun: the unit is the thing counted.

> `zagozolx zudozolx zel gral blragel.`
>
> [z-cat-x | z-dog-x | z-rank/more | [g-amount | bl-three]]
>
> "There are three more cats than dogs."

**Compare with:** after set join **`a`**, a shared word describes each item, so `zagozolx zudozolx zal gral` is *several cats and several dogs*. Only a **rank** join turns **`gral`** into the scale. For a named quantity such as weight or time, use its own word (`garolom`, `gadurem`), not **`gral`**.

### Frequency scale (`hral`) {#frequency-scale}

English *walks more often than* ranks how many **times** something happens. Write digitless **`hral`** (*multiple times*) in the manner slot, right after the rank join. Only digitless **`hral`** sets this scale; other number words do not rank how often.

> `zuzozen zolazun zel hral vowurel.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-Olazun | z-rank/more | h-how-often] | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen walks more often than Olazun."

### Time scale (`bral`) {#time-scale}

English *arrives earlier than* ranks **when** something happens. Write digitless **`bral`** (*later*) in the same place, right after the rank join. Rank **`e`** is *later than*, and **`ue`** is *earlier than*.

> `zuzozen zolazun zel bral vovurel.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-Olazun | z-rank/more | b-later] | v-arrival
>
> "Uzozen arrives later than Olazun."

> `zuzozen zolazun zuel bral vovurel.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-Olazun | z-rank/less | b-later] | v-arrival
>
> "Uzozen arrives earlier than Olazun."

### Covarying clauses (*the more …, the more …*) {#covarying}

English *the more Uzozen walks, the more Olazun sleeps* says two amounts rise together. Put digitless **`hral`** (*multiple times*) or another amount word in each clause, and join the clauses with **`xoel`**. As with the [frequency scale](#frequency-scale), the equal-rank join turns each amount into a scale and says the two scales move in step.

> `zuzozen hral vowurel xoel zolazun hral vezuvol.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | h-more-than-one | v-walk | x-equal-rank | z-Olazun | h-more-than-one | v-sleep]
>
> "The more Uzozen walks, the more Olazun sleeps."

For *the more …, the less …*, use **`xuel`**: when the first scale rises, the second falls.

> `zuzozen hral vowurel xuel zolazun hral vezuvol.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | h-more-than-one | v-walk | x-rank/less | z-Olazun | h-more-than-one | v-sleep]
>
> "The more Uzozen walks, the less Olazun sleeps."

**Compare with:** **`xoel`** / **`xuel`** state only that the two go together, not which one causes the other. To say the first causes the second, use a [causation](causation.md) pole.

### Factor (*twice as … as*) {#factor}

English *twice as big as* gives the **ratio** between the two heights. Use the [equative](#equatives) **`zoel`** and its shared adjective, then a factor number (`/h/` + marker **`ra`**, [numbers](numbers.md#number-as-adverb-by-marker)) **right after** the adjective. The first name sits at that many times the second's height.

> `zuzozen zolazun zoel gelazom hradol.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-Olazun | z-equal-rank | g-big | h-two]
>
> "Uzozen is twice as big as Olazun."

Inverse marker **`ru`** divides: `hrudol` is *half as*. The factor hosts nothing, so no `bl-` word comes after it.

> `zolazun zuzozen zoel gelazom hrudol.`
>
> [z-Olazun | z-Uzozen | z-equal-rank | g-big | h-divided-by-two]
>
> "Olazun is half as big as Uzozen."

English *three times bigger* usually means the same ratio: use **`zoel`** with `hragel`, not rank **`zel`**. After **`zel`**, a number `/h/` is not a factor; it is an ordinary *N times* on the clause. For a gap in units (*two meters taller*), put a measure noun on the scale instead of a factor.

### Comparing against a stance (bars) {#bars}

English *more than planned* and *later than planned* rank one thing against a **value a stance sets**, not against another person: the plan sets how much and when. Write that `/th/` stance word, or that [intention adverb](clause.md#intention-adverbs) on `/h/` (FORMER, ability, a say word), where the second name would go, right before the rank join. That word is the **bar**. The join closes everything before it, so the bar is part of the comparison: it says what you rank against. It is not your stance on the whole sentence.

> `zuzozen thagem zel bral vovurel.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | th-plan-itinerary | z-rank/more | b-later] | v-arrival
>
> "Uzozen arrives later than planned."

The same stance word **after** the fence has its ordinary reading, a stance on the whole sentence. With one ranked name, the fence is then a [superlative](#superlatives):

> `zuzozen zel bral vovurel thagem.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-rank/more | b-later] | v-arrival | th-plan-itinerary
>
> "I plan for Uzozen to arrive last."

A bar fence ranks **one** item against **one** bar: with a bar, there is no second name and no second bar. Any of the rank joins works (**`zel`**, **`zuel`**, **`zoel`**, and their open **-m** forms), and the bar keeps its own ending. The fence can rank an object or a recipient the same way:

> `zuzozen dodolx thagem del gral vagedal.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [d-strawberry-x | th-plan-itinerary | d-rank/more | g-amount] | v-eat
>
> "Uzozen eats more strawberries than planned."

> `zuzozen vezuyel bolazun thagem bel hral.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-tell | [b-Olazun | th-plan-itinerary | b-rank/more | h-how-often]
>
> "Uzozen tells Olazun more often than planned."

A [hook](hooks.md) and its `/b/` on the ranked item go right before the bar, the way they go right before a join word:

> `zanal om buwon thagem zel garolom.`
>
> [[z-book | [near | b-speaker]] | th-plan-itinerary | z-rank/more | g-heavy]
>
> "This book is heavier than planned."

<!-- cheat-sheet: roles-comparing -->
| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| name + bar + **`zel`** | above the bar | *more than planned* / *later than planned* |
| name + bar + **`zuel`** | below the bar | *less than planned* / *earlier than planned* |
| name + bar + **`zoel`** | at the bar | *as planned* |
| bar after the fence | a stance on the whole claim | *plans to be the most …* |

**Compare with:** a name before the rank join is someone you rank against (`zuzozen zolazun zel guzemol` *Uzozen is sleepier than Olazun*). A stance word in that place is the value that stance sets.

## Advanced {#advanced}

### Every bar {#stance-bars}
<a id="worse-than"></a>
<a id="best-effort"></a>

Intermediate ranked against a plan, and [sake bars](prescription.md#sake-bars) rank against what a sake needs. Any stance that **sets a value** can be the [bar](#bars), and each keeps its own endings and meaning. A channel word (how you know something: you saw it, remember it, were told it, worked it out) sets **the level that source led you to expect**. So *than usual*, *than I remember*, and *than advertised* each put one channel word in the bar slot.

> `zuzozen thubem zel guzemol.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | th-PATTERN | z-rank/more | g-sleepy]
>
> "Uzozen is sleepier than usual."

> `zuzozen zel guzemol thubem.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-rank/more | g-sleepy] | th-PATTERN
>
> "Uzozen is the sleepiest, going by the pattern."

<!-- cheat-sheet: roles-comparing -->
| Bar | Ranks against | English |
|-----|---------------|---------|
| met sake `thudatham`, `thobatham`, … ([sake bars](prescription.md#sake-bars)) | what that sake needs | *enough* / *too much* / *not enough* |
| LIVE `thugom` | how it looks | *than it looks* |
| MEMORY `thevum` | how you remember it | *than I remember* / *than last time* |
| PATTERN `thubem` | the trail of cases | *than usual* / *normal* |
| CLUES `theyem` | what was worked out | *than calculated* / *than it should be* |
| REPORTED `thebam` | what was said or written | *than advertised* / *than they said* / *than the stories say* |
| INTUITION `thabom` | your gut expectation | *than I expected* |
| FORMER `helam` | how it used to be | *than it used to be* / *increasingly* |
| NOTIONAL `theyum` | the imagined case | *than imagined* / *than in theory* |
| PLAN `thagem` | the plan or the schedule | *than planned* / *than scheduled* |
| WANT `thalel` / `thalem` / `thaler` | the level wanted | *than wanted* / *than I wanted* |
| ABIL `hodaxal` | what can be done | *as … as possible* |
| a requirement `hwavel` / `hwavem` / `hwaver` | the rule, the demand, or custom | *up to code* / *than demanded* / *than customary* |
| a permission `hwaval` / `hwavam` / `hwavar` | the rule, the grant, or what is tolerated | *over the limit* / *more than they allow* / *than tolerated* |
| consent `hwadam` | the agreed terms | *more than agreed* |
| speaker attitude (`thoguyem`, `thewemum`) | the case you hoped for or feared | *than hoped* / *than feared* |

The bar's ending keeps its own job: on a channel it is [evidence strength](knowing.md#evidence-strength) (`thubel` *than it reliably is*, `thuber` *than the few cases so far*).

> `zuzozen thevum zel gelazom.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | th-MEMORY | z-rank/more | g-big]
>
> "Uzozen is bigger than I remember."

> `zanal thebam zuel gagebom.`
>
> [z-book | th-REPORTED | z-rank/less | g-clarity]
>
> "The book is less clear than advertised."

> `zuzozen thabom zel guzemol.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | th-INTUITION | z-rank/more | g-sleepy]
>
> "Uzozen is sleepier than I expected."

> `zuzozen helam zel guzemol.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | h-FORMER | z-rank/more | g-sleepy]
>
> "Uzozen is sleepier than they used to be."

> `zanal thalem zuel garolom.`
>
> [z-book | th-WANT-unstated | z-rank/less | g-heavy]
>
> "The book is lighter than I wanted."

The bar's `/b/` works as it does on the clause. On REPORTED it is the source, and on a [say word](feelings-permission.md#say-words) over the act (`hwavem`, `hwavam`) it is the person who demands or grants. WANT takes no `/b/`: for someone else's wants, put a [holder](knowing.md#holder) on the clause. On PATTERN it is **whose cases** set the pattern: the population you compare against. With no `/b/`, PATTERN is the ranked item's own usual level, or the usual case in the situation.

> `zanal thebam blolazun zuel gagebom.`
>
> [z-book | [th-REPORTED | bl-Olazun] | z-rank/less | g-clarity]
>
> "The book is less clear than Olazun said."

> `zuzozen thubem blwezeluyelx zel gezuyol.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | [th-PATTERN | bl-role-agent-learn-x] | z-rank/more | g-sing]
>
> "Uzozen sings well for a learner."

On a rank join, an event root on `/ɡ/` is the scale itself: how well the act goes (*sings well*), not an act [in progress](predication.md#in-progress).

An adjective after that `/b/` describes it, as after any [host](clause.md#complex-chaining), so it narrows the population.

> `zuzozen thubem blwezeluyelx guzemol zel gezuyol.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | [th-PATTERN | [bl-role-agent-learn-x | g-sleepy]] | z-rank/more | g-sing]
>
> "Uzozen sings well for a sleepy learner."

The ranked item can be a whole kind. Write the [universal fence](joins.md#universals-domains-generics) first, then the bar: the whole closed fence counts as the one ranked item, the way an inner list sits inside an outer one ([fence nesting](joins.md#fence-nesting)). Open **`zuam`** is *people of that kind, as a rule*.

> `zuam gwazodamul thubem zel hral vabezum.`
>
> [[z-everything.open | g-episode-agent-tired] | th-PATTERN | z-rank/more | h-how-often] | v-flaw
>
> "Tired people make mistakes more often than usual."

To rank one kind against another, keep one kind as the item and put the other in the PATTERN bar's `/b/`.

> `zuam gwazenuvom thubem bleyolx godamum zel hemebem vuzubal.`
>
> [[z-everything.open | g-episode-agent-rest] | [th-PATTERN | [bl-person-x | g-burnout]] | z-rank/more | h-achievement] | v-sweat
>
> "Rested people work more successfully than exhausted people."

A CLUES or PATTERN bar can also take [`blarl`](knowing.md#evidence-clause): the next sentence is the grounds you worked the bar out from.

**Ability.** The ability word [`oda`](ability.md#ability-fallback) as the bar is the limit of **what can be done**. A tie against **`hodaxal`** is *as … as possible*. The ability vowel says how fixed that limit is, so rank **`zel`** against **`hodaxel`** is *more than can be done for now*, and against **`hodaxul`** *more than could ever be done*.

> `zolazun hodaxal zoel hedurem vowurel.`
>
> [z-Olazun | h-ABIL-able | z-equal-rank | h-haste] | v-walk
>
> "Olazun walks as hastily as possible."

**Rules and permission.** A requirement as the bar is the demand; a tie meets it. A permission is the limit; rank **`zel`** goes over it.

> `zahehal hwavel zoel gobobom.`
>
> [z-house | h-episode-event-REQUIRE-rule | z-equal-rank | g-strength]
>
> "The house is as strong as the rules require."

> `zududal hwaval zel gral.`
>
> [z-tea | h-episode-event-ALLOW-rule | z-rank/more | g-amount]
>
> "There is more tea than the rules allow."

**Hopes and fears.** A speaker-attitude word as the bar is the case you hoped for or feared.

> `zanal thoguyem zel gagebom.`
>
> [z-book | th-hope | z-rank/more | g-clarity]
>
> "The book is clearer than I hoped."

> `zanal thewemum zuel gagebom.`
>
> [z-book | th-concern | z-rank/less | g-clarity]
>
> "The book is even less clear than I feared."

Only these stances set a value. A [cause or condition pole](causation.md), [MAY](knowing.md#may), [MIRATIVE](notional-standing.md#mirative), [DECISION](ability.md#decision), and [ATTEMPT](ability.md#try) do not, so none of them is a bar. A want sets the level wanted; a decision or an attempt commits to an act and sets no level, so *more than decided* is the [plan](intention.md#plan-predict) bar.

### Vague amounts (*many* / *few*) {#vague-amounts}

English *many*, *few*, *often*, and *late* rank against an unstated baseline, and *too much*, *too late*, and *as fast as possible* against an unstated need or limit. Agebon always names the bar: put it in the fence and rank on [amount](#amount-scale), the [frequency scale](#frequency-scale), or the [time scale](#time-scale).

> `zagozolx thubem zel gral.`
>
> [z-cat-x | th-PATTERN | z-rank/more | g-amount]
>
> "There are many cats." (more than usual)

> `zuzozen thubem zel hral vowurel.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | th-PATTERN | z-rank/more | h-how-often] | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen often walks." (more often than usual)

<!-- cheat-sheet: roles-comparing -->
| English | Bar and rank |
|---------|--------------|
| *many* / *few* | PATTERN **`thubem`**, `zel` / `zuel` with `gral` |
| *often* / *rarely* | PATTERN **`thubem`**, `zel` / `zuel` with `hral` |
| *late* / *early* | PATTERN **`thubem`**, `zel` / `zuel` with `bral` |
| *than usual* (the same person or thing) | PATTERN **`thubem`** with no `/b/` |
| *too much* / *enough* / *not enough* | a met sake bar (`thudatham`), `zel` / `zoel` / `zuel` |
| *too late* / *too soon* | a met sake bar (`thudatham`), `zel` / `zuel` with `bral` |
| *as … as possible* | ABIL **`hodaxal`**, `zoel` |
| *more and more* / *increasingly* | FORMER **`helam`**, `zel` |

The bar is never dropped. A single-item `zagozolx zel gral` already means *the most cats* (a [superlative](#superlatives)).
