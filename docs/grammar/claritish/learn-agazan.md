# Learn the full language {#learn-agazan}

## What you can say now {#recap}

With a handful of drop-ins, your English now says:

- how sure you are, and what you'll do about it (*could be*)
- how you know (live, memory, record, pattern, clues, hearsay, gut, tale) and how strong the evidence is
- how far a label reaches (this time, a role, one relationship, a type)
- which need a thank-you or an apology is about
- who allowed something, who required it, and whether the person it affects agreed
- how settled a decision or a try is
- a feeling as a need, a place, and a motion, instead of one opaque label
- how you mean it (tone marks)

## These were never add-ons {#never-add-ons}

Every drop-in you learned is a real Agazan word, spelled the same way. In the full language they sit inside sentences built for them, and they stack: one sentence can carry a tone, a channel, a try, and a sake at once.

> `;zalahen thodom thudur vezehel thozothar.`
>
> ;z-Alahen | th-LIVE | th-ATTEMPT-trial | v-sing | th-pleasure-met-immediate
>
> "I'm watching dear Alahen give singing a try, and it's a joy right now."

## What Claritish can't do {#limits}

In English, these distinctions show up only when you remember to add them. Most of the time you won't, and the old shortcuts come back: the guess that sounds like a fact, the rumor that sounds like a sighting, the one act that becomes a person's nature. Agazan is built around these distinctions, so they are there every time you speak, write, or think in it.
