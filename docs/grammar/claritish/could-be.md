# Lesson 2: How sure are you? {#could-be}

## The gap {#gap}

English *I think* often hides a guess. *Probably*, *maybe*, and *definitely* are vague, and people read them very differently. Agazan has one word for *could be*. Its ending says what you plan to do about the guess.

## The forms {#forms}

| Drop-in | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| `thovum` | could be (the default) |
| `thovul` | could be, and I'll find out |
| `thovur` | could be, who knows (a passing thought) |

Put the drop-in where English would put *probably*: at the end of the clause, at the start, or next to the word it colors. It covers the clause it sits in. When you are not sure which ending fits, use `thovum`.

The last letter is an ending you will see again and again: **-l**, **-m**, or **-r**. Here it runs from the most settled to the most passing. **-l** is the most settled: you will settle it. **-m** is the default. **-r** is the most passing: a thought for right now only. A cue for the whole course: **-l ≈ lasting, -r ≈ right now.** None of the three is the "right" one. Checking, leaving it at *could be*, and letting it pass are all fine.

**Saying the words:** say every vowel as its own beat, the same way every time. `a` as in *spa*, `e` as in *bet*, `o` as in *no* (without the glide), and `u` as in *boot*. `th` is the sound in *this*. So `thovum` has two beats, one for each vowel.

## Examples {#examples}

> The package arrives Friday `thovum`.  
> She's upset with me `thovul`.  
> It'll rain `thovur`.  
> I left the stove on `thovul`.  
> The flight's delayed `thovum`.

*I left the stove on `thovul`* says you might have, and you're going back to check. *It'll rain `thovur`* is a thought you're not chasing.

## Practice {#practice}

Add the drop-in that fits the note.

**1.** *The store is closed.* (could be; you'll call and check)

::: details Show answer
The store is closed `thovul`.
:::

**2.** *He forgot.* (could be; you aren't saying more)

::: details Show answer
He forgot `thovum`.
:::

**3.** *Aliens built it.* (a passing thought)

::: details Show answer
Aliens built it `thovur`.
:::

::: tip Full Agazan
In a full sentence, the same word sits among words built to carry it.

> `zalahen thovum vedabal dahaben.`
>
> z-Alahen | th-MAY | v-departure | d-Ahaben
>
> "Alahen may be leaving Ahaben."
:::
