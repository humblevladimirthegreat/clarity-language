# Lesson 10: Can and can't {#can-and-cant}

## The gap {#gap}

*I can't* hides three different things: *not right now*, *not yet*, and *never*. Heard back, *I can't sing* can mean a sore throat or a lifelong fact. Agazan says which one you mean.

## The forms {#forms}

Add a hyphen to the English verb, then `x` and a vowel:

| Suffix | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| `-xa` | can: able |
| `-xe` | can't, just for now (usually can) |
| `-xo` | can't yet (the default when you are not sure) |
| `-xu` | can never: impossible |

Reminder: `x` sounds like the *s* in *vision*. The hyphen only shows that the word in front is English. The letters after it are spelled exactly as in Agazan. Here the vowel carries the meaning, so the endings from the other lessons do not come back.

Cue for the vowels: **a ≈ add** (the ability is in), **e ≈ order** (this moment's ranking), **o ≈ one** (one open *can't*), **u ≈ undo** (the ability is taken off).

Leave the suffix off when *can* is only polite filler. *Can you pass the salt* needs no `-xa`.

## Examples {#examples}

> I `swim-xa`, but I `drive-xe` with this cast on.  
> She `code-xo`. She starts the course in May.  
> I `sing-xu` in front of people.  
> He `lift-xe` today, his back is out.  
> The kids `read-xo`, but they're learning.

*I `drive-xe`* says the cast is the problem and it will pass. *She `code-xo`* keeps the door open without promising how soon, which plain *can't* never does. *I `sing-xu`* is the only one that closes the door.

## Practice {#practice}

Add the suffix that fits the note.

**1.** *I can't run.* (my ankle is sprained; it will heal)

::: details Show answer
I `run-xe`.
:::

**2.** *He can't read music.* (he hasn't learned it yet)

::: details Show answer
He `read-xo` music.
:::

**3.** *I can't drink milk.* (an allergy that will not change)

::: details Show answer
I `drink-xu` milk.
:::

**4.** *She can drive.* (she has her license)

::: details Show answer
She `drive-xa`.
:::
