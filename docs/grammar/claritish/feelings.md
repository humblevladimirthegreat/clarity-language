# Feelings in full {#feelings}

## The gap {#gap}

*I'm anxious* does not say whether the feeling is a wave that will pass or a mood that has settled in. *I'm scared* sounds as if the danger is already here, when it may never come. Ageben says how long the feeling lasts and whether its cause is real or only predicted, and adds six more pulls, such as freezing or keeping the peace.

## The forms {#forms}

Start from a feeling word from What a feeling wants: a thanks or sorry word, one vowel for the **urge** (what the feeling pulls you to do), and a last ending. This lesson adds three things.

**Six more urges.** They go in the same place as `a`, `e`, `o`, and `u`, and name pulls those four blur together. This is a menu; you don't need to memorize it. (It is on the cheat sheet.)

| Urge | It pulls you to | Belongs when |
|------|-----------------|--------------|
| `oe` | turn to someone: for comfort, or to share it | company would help, or good news wants telling |
| `uo` | push back on an outcome or a demand | a limit is being crossed |
| `ua` | step out of the fight: let go, yield | it can't be changed, or it isn't worth the cost |
| `ao` | use yourself to keep the peace | calming things down keeps someone safe |
| `ae` | stay on the spot: frozen, or keeping a lid on it | acting now would cost more than waiting |
| `ue` | you can't tell which way it pulls | you don't know yet |

`oe` covers sharing as well as comfort, and the sake says which: with a met sake you want to tell someone, with an unmet sake you want someone with you. As with the first four, no urge is the right one to have.

**How long the feeling lasts.** The last ending is the settled-to-passing scale again, now about the feeling itself:

| Ending | The feeling |
|--------|-------------|
| **-l** | has settled in: hours, days, a mood |
| **-m** | how long is left open (the default) |
| **-r** | is a flash or a wave, and will pass |

How long is not how strong. A strong feeling can pass in a moment, and a mild one can stay for days. The ending in the middle, from Thanks and sorry, is still about the situation, so the two can disagree: a settled feeling over a cost that has passed is worth a second look.

| `thuna` | `thu` | `m` | `uo` | `l` |
|---------|-------|-----|------|-----|
| autonomy | unmet | default ending | push back (urge) | settled in (how long) |

**A cost that may come.** A feeling word on its own says the cost or payoff is real. For fear, worry, or hope about something that has not happened, put `thomam` from How sure are you? right before the feeling word. Then you can say you are afraid without claiming the danger is here.

## Examples {#examples}

> The review is tomorrow `thomam thuwathumur`.  
> He rewrote my memo again `thunathumuol`.  
> We stopped speaking years ago `thawethuluar`.  
> My boss raised his voice `thomam thobathumaor`.  
> The deadline moved up `thuwathumael`.

| Drop-in | What it says | Instead of | The parts |
|---------|--------------|------------|-----------|
| `thuwathumur` | it may cost me competence, and I want to get away; a wave | *anxious* | competence unmet; move off; a wave |
| `thunathumuol` | my say keeps being taken, and I'm pushing back; it has settled in | *resentful* | autonomy unmet; push back; settled in |
| `thawethuluar` | a wave of grief over a loss that stays | *sad* | relatedness unmet for good; let go; a wave |
| `thobathumaor` | I may be in danger, and I want to calm him down; a flash | *scared* | physical unmet; keep the peace; a flash |
| `thuwathumael` | I can't keep up, and I'm frozen; it has settled in | *overwhelmed* | competence unmet; stay on the spot; settled in |

`thomam` keeps the review from sounding like a failure that has already happened, and **-r** says this wave will pass. `thawethuluar` pairs a loss that is for good with a feeling that comes and goes. `thobathumaor` names the pull to appease before you follow it.

## Practice {#practice}

Build the feeling word from the parts in the note.

**1.** *I passed the exam.* (competence met, default ending; I want to tell someone; a flash)
