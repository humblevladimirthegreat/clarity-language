# Dependents and continue

How a **next sentence** fills a slot in the sentence before it, how English *who / that / which* is two sentences, and how to keep the same speech move going.

## Beginner {#beginner}

You already write one clause with [role letters](clause.md#role-letters) and start a new speech move with [`/y/`](speech-moves.md#turn-y). This page attaches another sentence, or keeps going without a new act.

### Which person or thing (*who / that / which*)
<a id="which-noun"></a>

English can describe a noun with a whole clause that starts with *who*, *that*, or *which* (*the guard who sits*). Agebon has no such word. Write **two sentences** instead: first say the inner clause as an ordinary sentence, then use the same person or thing in the next sentence.

> `zagayul vuhohol. zuzozen vezuyel bagayul.`
>
> z-guard | v-sit . z-Uzozen | v-tell | b-guard
>
> "A guard sits. Uzozen talks to the guard."

That pair is English *Uzozen talks to the guard who sits.* Repeat the full word when you need the same person again in the next sentence.

**Compare with:** *a blue dog* is still one [adjective](clause.md#adjectives-ɡ) (`gubulel`). *Uzozen tells Olazun **that** a dog runs* is a dependent clause: *that* packages the next sentence as content, not as a tag on *dog*.

### Dependent clauses {#dependent-clauses}

Sometimes a whole sentence fills one role in another sentence. In *Uzozen tells Olazun **that the dog runs***, the part after *that* is what Uzozen tells: the direct object. (Olazun, the person told, is plain `/b/`.) That inner sentence is a **dependent clause**.

In the main sentence, a **stand-in** holds the dependent clause's place: **`darl`**, which is the role letter + **`a`** + **-rl**. It stands for the sentence that comes right after it, so the main sentence never has to wrap around the inner one.

Put **`darl`** last in the main sentence, and start the dependent clause immediately after it. Word order is free, so you can always arrange the other words to leave **`darl`** at the end.

The main sentence stops after **`darl`**, and the next full sentence is the content that **`darl`** stands for. Do not open that inner sentence with `/y/`: it is not a new turn, and the stand-in's vowel already says what kind of content it is (**`a`** for *that …*). The inner sentence names who does its verb (`/z/`), even where English drops that person (*wants to walk*): write the name again.

Here the dependent clause is the direct object, so the stand-in is **`darl`**:

> `zuzozen vezuyel bolazun darl zudozol varuvel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-tell | b-Olazun | d-that-clause | z-dog | v-run
>
> "Uzozen tells Olazun that the dog runs."

For English *whether*, change the vowel to **`o`**: **`dorl`**. The inner sentence is question-like, but the outer sentence is still a statement, so the whole stretch ends with a period.

> `zuzozen vawebol dorl zolazun vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-see | d-whether-clause | z-Olazun | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen sees whether Olazun walks."

**Compare with:** *the dog that runs* (which dog) is [two sentences](#which-noun), not **`darl`** on the noun. *If Olazun walks* sets a condition rather than filling a role, so it takes a pole word instead of a stand-in on its own (next section).

### Because, if, although (poles) {#poles}

Words like *because*, *if*, and *although* attach a dependent clause as a reason, a condition, a contrast, or a time. Agebon uses a pair for this: a **pole** word that names the link, then the stand-in **`blarl`** right after it. The pole is a relation word, so **`blarl`** is its hosted [extra noun](clause.md#extra-nouns) in `/b/`. Keep the pair together, last in the main sentence, with **`blarl`** at the very end.

The pole's role letter follows the clause page. *Because*, *if*, *only if*, and *if and only if* are your [stance](clause.md#stance-th) on why the claim holds, so they take `/th/`. *Although*, *while*, *until*, *before*, *after*, and *so that* describe the event itself, so they are adverbs on `/h/`.

> `zuzozen vezuyol thahum blarl zolazun vowurel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-sing | [th-because | bl-that-clause] | z-Olazun | v-walk
>
> "Uzozen sings because Olazun walks."

A pole uses the abstract sense of its root, not the everyday object, so it ends in **-m**.

You can also say:

<!-- cheat-sheet: linking-clauses -->
| Agebon | English | Same root as | Cue |
|--------|---------|--------------|-----|
| **`thahum blarl`** | *because* | `ahul` *wall* | 🧱: the cause is what the result sits on |
| **`thohom blarl`** | *if* | `ohol` *door* | 🚪: walk through only if it opens |
| **`thebom blarl`** | *only if* | `ebol` *paper-roll* | 🧻: without it the situation does not run |
| **`thehem blarl`** | *if and only if* | `ehel` *east-west* | ↔️: each side only with the other |
| **`hezom blarl`** | *although* / *even though* | `ezol` *zebra* | 🦓: two stripes, still one animal |
| **`herem blarl`** | *while* (at the same time) | `erel` *gemini* | ♊: two things at once |
| **`harum blarl`** | *until* | `arul` *timer* | ⏲️: stop when it rings |
| **`hevahum blarl`** | *by the time* | `evahul` *finish-line* | 🏁: done by the time you reach the flag |
| **`habam blarl`** | *before* | `abal` *backpack* | 🎒: packed first |
| **`halam blarl`** | *after* | `alal` *sand* | ⌛: the sand has already run through |
| **`hovam blarl`** | *so that* / *in order to* | `oval` *goal* | 🥅: you act so the shot counts |

A command or request makes no claim, so an *if* pole on it limits the instruction instead: do this only in that case. English often puts *if* first (*if X, do Y*); Agebon keeps the usual order, with **`blarl`** last.

> `yel vowurel thohom blarl zolazun vuhohol.`
>
> y-command | v-walk | [th-if | bl-that-clause] | z-Olazun | v-sit
>
> "If Olazun sits, walk."

English *despite* takes a noun instead of a clause (*despite Olazun*). Use the same **`hezom`** pole and put that noun in a `bl-` word where **`blarl`** would go. As with *although*, the obstacle is taken as fact, and you add no second word for it. The pole is on `/h/` because it describes the event, never on `/th/`. To attach it to a noun instead, use `/ɡ/`: **`gezom`**. (cue: 🦓 *zebra*: two stripes, still one animal)

> `zuzozen vowurel hezom blolazun.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [h-although | bl-Olazun]
>
> "Uzozen walks despite Olazun."

Ordinary `zezol` is still *a zebra*.

The sentence after **`blarl`** has to name an event or a thing, so it needs a noun or a verb. A stance word by itself does not fill it.

**Compare with:** discourse *however* and *therefore* start a new sentence after a finished claim; they are not *although*. *Whether Olazun walks* is **`dorl`**, not **`thohom`**: *if* sets a condition, and *whether* leaves a yes-or-no question open.

### Times (*while*, *before*, *after*, *until*, *by*) {#time-poles}

The time poles take a noun the same way. Put the event or period in a `bl-` word right after **`herem`** *during*, **`habam`** *before*, **`halam`** *after*, or **`harum`** *until*.

> `zuzozen vowurel herem blenulul.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [h-while | bl-thunderstorm]
>
> "Uzozen walks during the thunderstorm."

> `zuzozen vuhohol halam blenulul.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-sit | [h-after | bl-thunderstorm]
>
> "Uzozen sits after the thunderstorm."

<a id="by-deadline"></a>

*Until* and *by* both name an endpoint. **`harum`** *until* says this event lasts up to the endpoint. **`hevahum`** *by* says this event is done at the endpoint or sooner. (cue: 🏁 *finish line*: done by the time you reach the flag)

> `zuzozen vowurel hevahum blenulul.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [h-by | bl-thunderstorm]
>
> "Uzozen walks by the time of the thunderstorm." (done no later than that)

<a id="as-soon-as"></a>

For *as soon as* / *once*, keep **`halam`** *after* and put the haste word **`wedurem`** in front of it. The gap between the two events is as short as it can be.

> `zuzozen vowurel wedurem halam blarl zolazun vuhohol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [[w-haste | h-after] | bl-that-clause] | z-Olazun | v-sit
>
> "Uzozen walks as soon as Olazun sits."

*Since* meaning *because* is **`thahum blarl`**.

Agebon verbs have no past or future form. *Before*, *after*, *while*, and *until* place this event in time relative to the next sentence.

### So that (*in order to*) {#so-that}

<a id="event-purpose"></a>

English *so that* names the outcome an event aims at (*walks so that Olazun sits*). Use the pole **`hovam`** with **`blarl`**, the same pair shape as *because*, and put the outcome in the sentence after **`blarl`**. Unlike *because*, this does **not** claim the outcome happened. To attach the pole to a noun, use **`govam`**. (cue: 🥅 *goal*: you act so the shot counts)

> `zuzozen vowurel hovam blarl zolazun vuhohol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [h-so-that | bl-that-clause] | z-Olazun | v-sit
>
> "Uzozen walks so that Olazun sits."

English *in order to* drops the person who sits. Agebon still names them in the inner `/z/`:

> `zuzozen vowurel hovam blarl zuzozen vuhohol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [h-so-that | bl-that-clause] | z-Uzozen | v-sit
>
> "Uzozen walks in order to sit."

Ordinary `zoval` is still *a goal* (the net).

### Continue (`/x/`): keep the same speech move {#continue-x}

Often you want to add another sentence to the same statement, question, or command, such as *therefore …* after a claim. An `/x/` word does that: the new stretch **continues** the speech move you already started, instead of starting a new one. It is still a statement if you were stating, still a question if you were asking, and so on.

Only the first turn (or a default statement body) chooses the speech act. So the continued stretch needs no act word of its own, not even **`yal`**.

A **linker** is an `/x/` word that connects one sentence to the next, like English *therefore* or *however*. The first sentence may end with a period; the linker then **starts the next written sentence**, still under the same speech act:

> `zudozol vowurel. xahem zagozol varuvel.`
>
> z-dog | v-walk . x-therefore | z-cat | v-run
>
> "A dog walks. Therefore a cat runs."

**Compare with:** [`/y/`](speech-moves.md#turn-y) starts a **new** speech move (a new statement, question, or command). `/x/` keeps the move you already started.

### Writing and speech rhythm (periods) {#orthography-and-prosody-periods}

A **period** closes the sentence body in writing, whatever the speech act. Speech matches that edge with a **rest**: one silent beat. The act word, not the punctuation, sets question or command. `?` and `!` are [tone marks](speech-moves.md#tone-marks) that go before the words they color.

| Writing | Speech | Use | Cue |
|---------|--------|-----|-----|
| `.` | Rest: one silent beat | Closes the prior **body**. Next stretch still defaults to **`yal`** unless a new `/y/` turn, a written act word, or *yes* / *no* particle sets otherwise | Rest = the claim is done |
| Next `/y/` turn (*yes* / *no*, a call, written act word) | The period's rest, then the `/y/` word | New turn; firm **-l** / soft **-m** imply **`yal`** / **`yam`** | The `/y/` word = a new act |
| Bare body or hook + body after `.` | The period's rest only | Implied **`yal`** (or **`yam`** only if a soft particle / written **`yam`** said so) | No `/y/` word, so same statement |
| Soft statement / soft *yes* / *no* **-m** | Nothing extra: the **-m** is heard | **`yam`** (implied or written) | **-m** leaves the hold open |
| `/x/` continue (clause join) | **No** rest: the sentence goes on | Keep opener speech act; period after the **whole** joined stretch | No rest = still going |
| `/x/` linker after `.` (`xahem …`) | The period's rest, then the linker | Keep prior speech act; may start a new written sentence under that speech act | Linker glues; still the same act |
| `/x/` topic word after `.` (`xuzozen …`) | The period's rest, then the topic word | Keep prior speech act; a new topic starts | The topic word = the subject changed, not the act |
| **Stand-in** hang | **No** rest until the last dependent | Next sentence fills the slot; period after the **whole** stretch | Same hang as `/x/` continue |

**-l** vs **-m** on the act word / *yes* / *no* is how firmly you stand behind it (closed vs soft / open).

### Sentence linkers {#sentence-linkers}

Beginner already used *therefore*. It is one of six **sentence linkers**: `/x/` words that connect a finished sentence to the one after it. Each one uses its root's **abstract** sense, so it ends in **-m**.

<!-- cheat-sheet: linking-clauses -->
| English | Agebon | Abstract of | Cue | Example |
|---------|--------|-------------|-----|---------|
| *therefore* | **`xahem`** | `ahem` *progress* | ➡️: the sun’s path, so the talk moves on | `xahem` |
| *however* | **`xezom`** | `ezom` *contrast* | 🦓: two stripes, still one animal | `xezom` |
| *meanwhile* | **`xegorem`** | `egorem` *passage* | 🕰️: time passing beside the last claim | `xegorem` |
| *next* | **`xuvewem`** | `uvewem` *sequence* | 🎞️: the following frame | `xuvewem` |
| *but* | **`xegohum`** | `egohum` *blockage* | 🚧: the expected path is blocked | `xegohum` |
| *by the way* | **`xuvedam`** | `uvedam` *accessory* | 🍟: a side dish, not the main course | `xuvedam` |

*therefore* moves forward from the prior claim; *however* marks contrast; *meanwhile* puts the next claim at the same time; *next* is the next frame; *but* blocks the expected continuation (harder push-back than *however*). Those same roots keep their ordinary content readings under other role letters.

A linker reaches back to **one** sentence: the one that ends at the period before it. To link back to several claims at once, join them into that sentence with a [clause join](joins.md#clause-joins).

Three linkers also take **-l** for a **firm** link: the link holds with no room for doubt, on the same settled-to-passing scale as [time horizon](sakes.md#settled-to-passing). (cue: **-l** ≈ lasting: the link holds)

<!-- cheat-sheet: linking-clauses -->
| Agebon | Use | English |
|--------|-----|---------|
| **`xahel`** | the next claim must hold if the last sentence does: a deduction | *it follows that* / *necessarily, then* |
| **`xezol`** | the contrast stands whatever the last claim said | *nevertheless* / *even so* |
| **`xegohul`** | the last claim is blocked outright; the next claim replaces it | *on the contrary* |

> `zolazun vezuvol vul. xegohul zolazun varuvel.`
>
> z-Olazun | [v-sleep | v-not] . x-on-the-contrary | z-Olazun | v-run
>
> "Olazun isn't sleeping. On the contrary, Olazun is running."

No other root makes a linker. Any other `/x/` content word sets the topic: **-l** / **-m** / **-n** introduce it (`xuzozen` *now, about Uzozen*) and **-r** returns to it. **-r** on a linker is still a [resume](pronouns.md#resume-r) (the same linker again, *likewise*).

All of these `/x/` words, and the topic words, come only at the **start of a sentence**: only `/y/` turn words or a [fronted discourse hook](hooks.md#discourse-hooks) may come before them. They never follow a [clause join](joins.md#clause-joins), and they never open the sentence after a [stand-in](#dependent-clauses), because that sentence belongs to the dependent.

A linker and a topic word do not share a sentence. To change the topic and also use a linker, write the topic word as its own sentence, then start the next sentence with the linker (*Now, about Uzozen. However, Olazun runs.*):

> `xuzozen. xezom zolazun varuvel.`
>
> x-Uzozen . x-however | z-Olazun | v-run
>
> "Now, about Uzozen. However, Olazun runs."

**`xuvewem`** also opens a new frame, like English *so, …* at the start of a turn: the talk moves on and the topic is cleared.

> `zudozol vowurel. xuvewem zuzozen varuvel.`
>
> z-dog | v-walk . x-next | z-Uzozen | v-run
>
> "The dog walks. So, Uzozen runs."

**`xuvedam`** opens a side topic, like English *by the way, …*, and clears the topic too.

In a new turn with its own act word, the act word comes first and the linker starts the body:

> `yom xuvewem zawan vowurel.`
>
> y-soft-question | x-next | z-listener | v-walk
>
> "So, do you want to walk?"

> `yol xuvedam zawan vebal.`
>
> y-question | x-by-the-way | z-listener | v-hear
>
> "By the way, did you hear?"

### Result (*so … that*) {#result-pole}

English *so … that* ties a result to how much of something there is (*so tired that they sleep*), all in one sentence. Put the pole **`hahem`** after the word that carries the degree, then **`blarl`**, then the result as the next sentence. **`hahem`** uses the same root as *therefore*, which needs two sentences. Unlike **`hovam`** *so that*, the result really happened.

> `zuzozen welazom godamul hahem blarl zuzozer vezuvol.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | [w-very | g-tired]] | [h-so-result | bl-that-clause] | z-←Uzozen | v-sleep
>
> "Uzozen is so tired that they sleep."

> `zuzozen welazom hedurem vowurel hahem blarl zolazun varuvel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | [w-very | h-haste] | v-walk | [h-so-result | bl-that-clause] | z-Olazun | v-run
>
> "Uzozen walks so hastily that Olazun runs."

**Compare with:** *although* / *while* attach a dependent after **`blarl`** (`hezom blarl`, `herem blarl`; see [dependent clauses](#dependent-clauses)). *Despite Olazun* is the same pole with a `bl-` noun (`hezom blolazun`). *So that Olazun sits* is **`hovam blarl`**. Discourse **`xezom`** / **`xegorem`** / **`xahem`** glue a finished sentence to the next one.

### Stand-in vowels (`-rl` / `-rm`) {#stand-in}
<a id="clause-member"></a>

Beginner used **`darl`** / **`barl`** for *that …* and **`dorl`** for *whether …*. A stand-in's vowel says what kind of content the next sentence is, with the same four vowels as the [act words](speech-moves.md#speech-act-beginner): **`a`** a statement, **`o`** a question, **`e`** an instruction, **`u`** something to avoid. So a dependent clause can carry an instruction (*tells Olazun **to sit***) or a warning (*tells Olazun **not to sit***).

Unlike a `/y/` act word, a stand-in does not itself state, ask, or command: it only lets that kind of content fill a role in the outer sentence. Stand-ins are not joins. The ending says how much the next sentence covers: **`-rl`** means it is the whole content (locked); **`-rm`** leaves it open (the gist, or not the only thing).

<!-- cheat-sheet: linking-clauses -->
| Stand-in | Content type | **`-rl`** locked | **`-rm`** open |
|----------|--------------|------------------|----------------|
| **`a`** hold | statement-like (compare **`yal`**) | `darl` *that …* | `darm` *that …* (gist) |
| **`o`** pick | question-like (compare **`yol`**) | `dorl` *whether …* | `dorm` *whether …* (open) |
| **`e`** order | instruction-like (compare **`yel`**) | `derl` *to …* | `derm` *to …* (open) |
| **`u`** undo | avoidance-like (compare **`yul`**) | `durl` *that not / lest …* | `durm` *lest …* (open) |

You may leave out the dependent clause's `/z/` only when all of these hold:

1. The stand-in is **`derl`** / **`derm`**, or **`durl`** / **`durm`** telling someone *not to*.
2. It fills the outer sentence's `/d/` slot.
3. The outer sentence has one plain `/b/` (the person told).

The missing subject is then that `/b/`. It is never the listener and never the outer `/z/`. In every other case, write the inner `/z/`: after **`darl`** / **`dorl`**, after any pole (**`blarl`** / **`blurl`**), and when the outer sentence has no plain `/b/` (*wants to sit*, *tries to sit*). When the doer is someone other than that `/b/`, write them too, using resume **-r** if the name would repeat.

After a pole, the dependent is **`blarl`** (or **`blarm`** for gist): *because* / *if* / *although* / *so that* all use it. There are two exceptions, both with **`blurl`**: purpose-not **`hovam blurl`** (*so that … not*) and **`thohom blurl`** (*unless*). Stand-ins never take `/x/`; clause *and* is `xal` / `xan`.

> `zuzozen vezuyel bolazun derl vuhohol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-tell | b-Olazun | d-to-clause | v-sit
>
> "Uzozen tells Olazun to sit."

The person told is plain `/b/`, so **`derl vuhohol`** can leave out its subject: the sitter is Olazun. The outer sentence stays a statement. With no plain `/b/`, write the sitter:

> `zuzozen valem derl zuzozen vuhohol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-wish | d-to-clause | z-Uzozen | v-sit
>
> "Uzozen wishes to sit."

To quote someone's exact words instead, use a [span](spans.md).

English *lest* / *not to* (keep this from happening) uses vowel **`u`**: **`durl`**. When it tells someone not to act, the person told (`/b/`) can be the missing subject, as with **`derl`**. When it only keeps an outcome off, with no one told, write the inner `/z/`. The outer sentence stays a statement. *Don't sit!* as the whole move is still a [prohibition](speech-moves.md#speech-act-beginner) (**`yul`**), not this stand-in.

> `zuzozen vezuyel bolazun durl vuhohol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-tell | b-Olazun | d-lest-clause | v-sit
>
> "Uzozen tells Olazun not to sit."

When *so that* aims at keeping an outcome off, keep **`hovam`** and type `/b/` with **`blurl`**:

> `zuzozen vowurel hovam blurl zuzozen vuhohol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [h-so-that | bl-lest-clause] | z-Uzozen | v-sit
>
> "Uzozen walks so as not to sit."

English *unless* is *if … not*: keep the *if* pole **`thohom`** and type `/b/` with **`blurl`**. The following sentence is the condition that must **not** hold.

> `zuzozen vowurel thohom blurl zolazun vezuvol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [th-if | bl-lest-clause] | z-Olazun | v-sleep
>
> "Uzozen walks unless Olazun sleeps."

The sentence after a stand-in runs to the end of the written sentence, so a [clause join](joins.md#clause-joins) inside it stays in the dependent. *Whether Olazun walks or Ahelan runs* is **`dorl`**, then both clauses with **`xol`** between them.

> `zuzozen vawebol dorl zolazun vowurel xol zahelan varuvel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-see | d-whether-clause | [z-Olazun | v-walk | x-or-exactly-one | z-Ahelan | v-run]
>
> "Uzozen sees whether Olazun walks or Ahelan runs."

To join another clause to the main sentence instead, end the sentence and start the next one with the join.

**Compare with:** *tells that Olazun sits* is **`darl`**. *Tells Olazun to sit* is **`derl`**. *Walks so that Uzozen sits* is **`hovam blarl`**.

### Pointing back (`-rth`) {#stand-in-back}

A stand-in on **`-rl`** / **`-rm`** holds a slot for the sentence that comes **next**. To point **back** at content already said, end the stand-in in **`-rth`**: English *so* in *Uzozen says so*, or *that* in *Olazun doubts that*. Nothing follows it; the sentence ends as usual.

The vowel still says what kind of content it is, and the word stands for the most recent content of that kind:

<!-- cheat-sheet: linking-clauses -->
| Stand-in | Points back to | English |
|----------|----------------|---------|
| **`darth`** | the last statement | *so* / *that* |
| **`dorth`** | the last question | *that question* |
| **`derth`** | the last instruction | *that (instruction)* |
| **`durth`** | the last *not* / *lest* content | *not* (*I hope not*) |

> `zolazun vuhohol. zuzozen vezuyel darth.`
>
> z-Olazun | v-sit . z-Uzozen | v-tell | d-that-same-claim
>
> "Olazun sits. Uzozen says so."

> `zuzozen vezuyel bolazun durl vuhohol. zolazun valem durth.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-tell | b-Olazun | d-lest-clause | v-sit . z-Olazun | v-wish | d-that-same-prohibition
>
> "Uzozen tells Olazun not to sit. Olazun wishes not to." (not to sit)

The content can be anything a sentence says: a verbless sentence, a denied event, or several sentences chained with `/x/`. A [resume](pronouns.md#resume-r) **-r** picks up one word; **`-rth`** picks up what was said.

Other role letters work the same way as the forward stand-in: **`blarth`** after a pole (*because of that*) or after [*like*](relations.md#similative) **`hewom`** (*like that*), **`zarth`** as the subject. Stand-ins fill `/z/` `/d/` `/b/` only; there is no stand-in on `/ɡ/` `/h/` `/w/` `/th/`. *Such* is [whole-stem **-r**](pronouns.md#resume-r), and *then* or *so* is a pole + **`blarth`**.

### Time poles on a noun {#time-pole-on-noun}

To place a **thing** in time rather than the event (*the walk after the thunderstorm*), put the [time pole](#dependent-clauses) on `/ɡ/` right after that noun: **`gerem`** *during*, **`gabam`** *before*, **`galam`** *after*, **`garum`** *until*, **`gevahum`** *by*. The `/b/` after it is the event or period, as with the `/h/` pole.

> `zolazun dowurel galam blenulul vawebol.`
>
> z-Olazun | [d-walk | [g-after | bl-thunderstorm]] | v-see
>
> "Olazun sees the walk after the thunderstorm." (*after the thunderstorm* says which walk)

Ordinary `zabal` is still *a backpack*.

## Advanced {#advanced}

### Lexicalized stand-ins in other roles {#stand-in-roles}

A stand-in's vowel says what kind of content follows, and its first letter says which role that content fills. With **-rn** (or **-n** after two vowels, as in **`vaen`**), the stand-in becomes a fixed word for that kind of content: **`darn`** *a statement*, **`dorn`** *a question*, **`dern`** *a command*, **`durn`** *a prohibition*. Use the same vowels with another role letter when the following sentence fills that role. On `/v/`, the same **-rn** / **-n** endings make verbs that take the following sentence as their content.

Each content type has one lexicalized form; there is no closed / open pair here.

<!-- cheat-sheet: linking-clauses -->
| Agebon | Dependent content | English |
|--------|-------------------|---------|
| **`varn`** | statement-like (`a`) | *state…* |
| **`vorn`** | question-like (`o`) | *question…* |
| **`vern`** | instruction-like (`e`) | *command…* |
| **`vurn`** | avoidance-like (`u`) | *prohibit…* |
| **`vaen`** | accepts a statement | *confirm that…* |
| **`vuen`** | rejects a statement | *deny that…* |
| **`vaon`** | accepts a proposed action | *agree to…* |
| **`vuon`** | rejects a proposed action | *decline to…* |
| **`vuan`** | rejects the premise behind a question or claim | *dispute the premise that…* |

For example, the `/v/` word takes the following sentence as its content:

> `zuzozen vaen zolazun vuhohol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-confirm | z-Olazun | v-sit
>
> "Uzozen confirms that Olazun sits."

> `zuzozen vuon zuzozen vuhohol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-decline-to | z-Uzozen | v-sit
>
> "Uzozen declines to sit."

A `/z/` stand-in puts the whole following sentence in the subject slot. With a `/ɡ/` word describing that sentence, that is English *it is … that* / *it is … to*.

> `gamahum zarl zuzozen vowurel.`
>
> g-challenge | z-that-clause | z-Uzozen | v-walk
>
> "It is hard for Uzozen to walk."

The sentence after one of these verbs has its own subject when the chosen content type requires one, just as it does after **`darl`** / **`derl`**. Changing the role letter lets the same kind of content serve as an object, a described property, or another role the clause allows.

### Nested dependents {#nested-dependents}

A clause has at most one **stand-in**, at its end. But the dependent clause is a clause too, so it may end in its own **`blarl`**, and a further sentence follows it on the **right**.

> `zuzozen guzemol thahum blarl zolazun vowurel thahum blarl zahelan vezuvol.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | g-sleepy] | [th-because | bl-that-clause] | z-Olazun | v-walk | [th-because | bl-that-clause] | z-Ahelan | v-sleep
>
> "Uzozen is sleepy because Olazun walks because Ahelan sleeps." (Ahelan's sleep is why Olazun walks)
