# Hook compounds {#hook-compounds-sitting}

### Hook compounds (cite + extra-noun hook) {#hook-compounds}

English has single verbs for moving in relation to a place: *enter*, *leave*, *oppose*. Agebon makes them by **fusing** an extra-noun hook onto the end of a citation: *walk* + *from* (`vowurel ul buyahel` *walks from a station*) becomes one word, *leave*. Every hook in the extra-noun grid can fuse this way.

Keep the left word’s own **-l** or **-m**, then write the hook, with no mid-word **`x`**. The dictionary form is the **citation** (no role letter), because the same stem can sit under `/z/` or `/ɡ/` as well as `/v/`.

The left word is always a five-letter root from the dictionary. Three-letter roots never fuse, so a word that starts with one is never a hook compound; to say *from a map*, use the two words.

```text
CITE + HOOK
owurel + ul → owurelul
```

In a clause, add a role letter to that citation (`vowurelul`). The extra participant is an ordinary object (`/d/`), not `/b/` (that would still be a recipient after a verb).

> `zuzozen duyahel vowurelul.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-station | v-leave
>
> "Uzozen leaves a station."

> `owurelul`
>
> leave
>
> "leave" (the dictionary form)

The left ending is part of the stem, so two different left roots stay distinct once the hook is attached. In speech that ending starts the hook's syllable, so no consonant closes a syllable mid-word: `owurelul` is *o-wu-re-lul*.

| Hook | Free extra-noun | Fused **-l** | Fused **-l**, abstract `/d/` | Fused **-m** | Citation (from *walk*) |
|------|-----------------|--------------|------------------------------|--------------|------------------------|
| **`al` / `am`** | *in* / *amid* | *enter* | *enter a crisis* | *mill amid* | `owurelal` / `owurelam` |
| **`aol` / `aom`** | *on* / *over* | *mount* | *take up a policy* | *cover* | `owurelaol` / `owurelaom` |
| **`ol` / `om`** | *at* / *near* | *attend* | *attend to an issue* | *adjoin* | `owurelol` / `owurelom` |
| **`oel` / `oem`** | *toward* / *in the direction of* | *head for* | *head for a habit* | *orient* | `owureloel` / `owureloem` |
| **`ul` / `um`** | *from* / *away from* | *leave* | *leave a heritage behind* | *recede* | `owurelul` / `owurelum` |
| **`ual` / `uam`** | *out of* / *out from among* | *exit* | *get out of debt* | *pick out* | `owurelual` / `owureluam` |
| **`uol` / `uom`** | *through* / *by way of* | *traverse* | *get through a crisis* | *relay* | `owureluol` / `owureluom` |
| **`el` / `em`** | *for* / *used by* | *serve* | *serve a policy* | *have in use* | `owurelel` / `owurelem` |
| **`ael` / `aem`** | *using* / *by* | *wield* | *wield a tradition* | *channel* | `owurelael` / `owurelaem` |
| **`uel` / `uem`** | *against* / *contrary to* | *oppose* | *oppose a policy* | *defy* | `owureluel` / `owureluem` |

An abstract object keeps the hook's picture, as on the [free hook](ending-grids.md#abstract-landmarks):

> `zuzozen dobowum vowureluol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | d-catastrophe | v-traverse
>
> "Uzozen gets through the crisis."

> `zolazun dududal vowurelel.`
>
> z-Olazun | d-tea | v-serve
>
> "Olazun serves tea."

Other roots fuse the same way: `vezuyeluel` *contradict* is *tell* fused with *against*. The dictionary lists these words as citations (`owurelul`, not `vowurelul`).

**Compare with:** `vowurel ul buyahel` still *walks from a station* as two words. Same-role *including* / *rather* / *instead* / *except* never fuse; they are always separate hook words. A [lexical compound](compounds.md#lexical-compounds) like *doorbell* glues two content roots with a join letter; this glues a citation to an extra-noun hook.

### Hook prefixes (hook + `x` + stem) {#hook-prefixes}

English builds describing words from a relation and a thing: *anti-war*, *indoor*, *transoceanic*, *water-powered*. In Agebon, write the role letter, then the vowels of an extra-noun hook, then **`x`**, then a stem, then the ending. The stem is the hook's landmark, and the word names something that stands in that relation to it: `guexavuvul` is *against fighting* as one word. Where the free hook needs its own `/b/` word after the noun, the prefix packs the relation into a single word, so it can describe a noun, fill any noun slot, and become a dictionary word. (cue: **x** ≈ plus, the hyphen in *anti-war*)

> `zuzozen guexavuvul.`
>
> z-Uzozen | g-against-x-fight
>
> "Uzozen is anti-fighting."

> `zehaval gaexowulal.`
>
> z-ship | g-using-x-water
>
> "The ship is water-powered."

Plain **`x`** spells the hook's **-l** reading. An **`m`** right before the **`x`** picks the hook's **-m** frame, the same **`m`** that turns `uel` into `uem`: `guemxewulel` is *contrary to the wind*, headed into it. The stem keeps its own sense choice: **-l** for the concrete sense, **-m** for the abstract one, read the way an [abstract landmark](ending-grids.md#abstract-landmarks) reads after a free hook.

> `zuzozen guemxewulel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | g-contrary-to-x-wind
>
> "Uzozen is headed into the wind."

| Hook | Prefix | Concrete stem | Abstract stem |
|------|--------|---------------|---------------|
| `al` *in* | **`…ax…`** | `gaxahehal` *indoor* | `gaxobowum` *mid-crisis* |
| `aol` *on* | **`…aox…`** | `gaoxohalal` *seagoing* | `gaoxonam` *on-policy* |
| `ol` *at* | **`…ox…`** | `goxahehal` *at the house* | `goxabevam` *at issue* |
| `oel` *toward* | **`…oex…`** | `goexahehal` *houseward* | `goexuzebum` *habit-forming* |
| `ul` *from* | **`…ux…`** | `guxahehal` *from the house* | `guxuzobam` *heritage-derived* |
| `ual` *out of* | **`…uax…`** | `guaxowulal` *out of the water* | `guaxaram` *debt-free* |
| `uol` *through* | **`…uox…`** | `guoxohalal` *transoceanic* | `guoxobowum` *crisis-spanning* |
| `el` *for* | **`…ex…`** | `gexahehal` *household* | `gexonam` *pro-policy* |
| `ael` *using* | **`…aex…`** | `gaexowulal` *water-powered* | `gaexugarom` *tradition-based* |
| `uel` *against* | **`…uex…`** | `guexohalal` *sea-facing* (a seawall) | `guexonam` *anti-policy* |

| Hook | Prefix | Concrete stem | Abstract stem |
|------|--------|---------------|---------------|
| `am` *amid* | **`…amx…`** | `gamxohalal` *mid-ocean* | `gamxobowum` *crisis-era* |
| `aom` *over* | **`…aomx…`** | `gaomxohalal` *over the ocean* (a flight) | `gaomxonam` *policy-covered* |
| `om` *near* | **`…omx…`** | `gomxahehal` *near the house* | `gomxabevam` *problem-adjacent* |
| `oem` *in the direction of* | **`…oemx…`** | `goemxahehal` *facing the house* | `goemxuzebum` *habit-leaning* |
| `um` *away from* | **`…umx…`** | `gumxahehal` *away from the house* | `gumxuzebum` *habit-shedding* |
| `uam` *out from among* | **`…uamx…`** | `guamxayayal` *picked from the group* | `guamxayayam` *community-drawn* |
| `uom` *by way of* | **`…uomx…`** | `guomxohel` *bridge-routed* | `guomxewulem` *influence-routed* |
| `em` *used by* | **`…emx…`** | `gemxayayal` *group-owned* | `gemxayayam` *community-held* |
| `aem` *by* | **`…aemx…`** | `gaemxorahel` *road-borne* | `gaemxonam` *policy-channeled* |
| `uem` *contrary to* | **`…uemx…`** | `guemxewulel` *headwind* | `guemxonam` *off-policy* |

A pair differs the way the free hooks do: `goxahehal` *at the house* is there, and `gomxahehal` *near the house* is only close to it.

The role letter is the word's slot, as on any content word:

| Role letter | The word names | Example | English |
|-------------|----------------|---------|---------|
| `/ɡ/` | a property: in that relation to the stem | `gexegobom` | *body-positive* |
| `/z/` `/d/` `/b/` | someone or something in that relation | `zuexavuvul` | *an opponent of fighting* |
| `/h/` | how the clause stands to the stem | `hoexahehal` | *houseward* (of the movement) |

> `zuexavuvul vowurel.`
>
> z-against-x-fight | v-walk
>
> "An opponent of fighting walks."

> `zuzozen vowurel hoexahehal.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | h-toward-x-house
>
> "Uzozen walks houseward."

The stem may be an ordinary compound (`guexahehaxowurel` *against walking in houses*) or a [hook compound](#hook-compounds) citation:

> `zolazun guexowurelul.`
>
> z-Olazun | g-against-x-leave
>
> "Olazun is against leaving."

[Label scope](label-scope.md#label-scope) goes after the stem, as on any word, and **-r** resumes the whole word:

> `zolazun guexavuvuthal.`
>
> z-Olazun | g-against-x-fight-th-episode
>
> "Olazun is against fighting this time."

> `zuexavuvul vowurel. zuexavuvur vezuvol.`
>
> z-against-x-fight | v-walk . z-←against-x-fight | v-sleep
>
> "An opponent of fighting walks. That opponent sleeps."

A common hook prefix can enter the dictionary as a citation, with no role letter (`uexavuvul`). In speech **`x`** starts the stem's first syllable (*gue-xa-vu-vul*); with **`mx`**, the **`m`** closes the hook's syllable (*guem-xe-wu-lel*).

**Compare with:** the free hook keeps the landmark as its own word: `zumon uel buduvul vowurel.` is *someone against the duel walks*, and a later `buduvur` points back to that duel. The prefix makes one word for a kind: `zuexuduvul vowurel.` is *an opponent of dueling walks*, with no landmark to point back to.

### Hooked joins (hook + `x` + join) {#hooked-joins}

English bounds *everyone*, *someone*, and *nothing* with a preposition: *everyone at the house*, *something for me*, *everyone against me*. A free hook after a join does a different job: it is an extra of the whole clause, so `zual vezuvol ol bahehal.` says where the sleeping happens, not who counts ([universals and domains](rank-joins.md#universals-domains-generics)). To bound the join's own set, write the role letter, then the vowels of an extra-noun hook, then **`x`**, then the join, and put the hook's landmark right after the word as a `bl-` word. The join keeps its meaning and its ending, and the hook now says which members count: the ones that stand in that relation to the landmark. So *who counts* and *where it happens* get different spellings, and one clause can say both. (cue: the hook comes first, as in a [hook prefix](#hook-prefixes))

> `zoxual blahehal vezuvol.`
>
> [z-at-x-everything | bl-house] | v-sleep
>
> "Everyone at the house sleeps."

> `zual vezuvol ol bahehal.`
>
> z-everything | v-sleep | [at | b-house]
>
> "Everyone sleeps at the house."

> `zaxual bluzahal vezuvol ol bahehal.`
>
> [z-in-x-everything | bl-school] | v-sleep | [at | b-house]
>
> "Everyone in the school sleeps at the house."

| Piece | What it says | In `zoxual` |
|-------|--------------|-------------|
| role letter | the slot, as on any join: `/z/`, `/d/`, `/b/`, or `/h/` | `z` |
| hook vowels | an [extra-noun hook](ending-grids.md#extra-noun-intermediate), with its **-l** left unwritten | `o` *at* |
| `m` or `r` | the hook's **-m** frame, or its **-r** point back | none |
| **`x`** | the seam, as in a hook prefix | `x` |
| join vowels + ending | any [standalone join](joins.md#standalone-phrase), read as usual | `ual` *everyone* |

The landmark takes `bl-` because it completes the word before it, as after an `/h/` word. Any word that can be a `bl-` word works: a name (`bluzozen`), a content word (`blahehal`), a [special pronoun](pronouns.md#special-pronouns) (`bluwon`), a resume (`blahehar`), or a standalone join. A [SHARED](rank-joins.md#shared-after-the-join) `/ɡ/` comes after the landmark: `zoxual blahehal gagozol` is *every cat at the house*.

This lets a sweeping claim show how far it reaches. *Everyone's against me* names its bound (`zuexual bluwon`), and the join ending says whether exceptions are ruled out: closed `zuexual` is *every one of them*, open `zuexuam` is *as far as I know*. A listener can then check the bound, or ask for the exception.

In speech **`x`** starts the join's syllable (*zo-xu-al*). With `m` or `r`, that letter closes the hook's syllable (*zom-xu-al*, *zor-xu-al*). The [first-syllable stress](singability.md#rhythm-and-stress) keeps a hooked join apart from a join followed by a hook: `zarxar` (*someone in there*) is one word with one stressed beat, and in `zarx ar` (*some people, in there*) `ar` takes a fresh beat.

#### Which hook {#hooked-joins-hooks}

Every hook in the [extra-noun grid](ending-grids.md#extra-noun-intermediate) works, with the reading it has there. The relation holds between each member and the landmark. **-l** (no letter before the seam) is the exact relation, and `m` before the seam is the frame, as on a free hook.

| Hook | **-l** | Example | **-m** | Example |
|------|--------|---------|--------|---------|
| `a` | *in* | `daxar blahehal` *something in the house* | *amid* | `zamxar bluzahal` *someone amid the school* |
| `ao` | *on* | `daoxar bledohol` *something on the table* | *over* | `daomxar bledohol` *something over the table* |
| `o` | *at* | `zoxual blahehal` *everyone at the house* | *near* | `zomxual bluwon` *everyone here* |
| `oe` | *toward* | `zoexarx bluzahal` *some people heading to the school* | *in the direction of* | `zoemxarx bluzahal` *some people headed the school's way* |
| `u` | *from* | `duxar blahehal` *something that came from the house* | *away from* | `zumxual bluwon` *everyone away from me* |
| `ua` | *out of* | `duaxar blahehal` *something out of the house* | *out from among* | `zuamxar blayayal` *someone picked out of the group* |
| `uo` | *through* | `zuoxar blahehal` *someone passing through the house* | *by way of* | `duomxar blohel` *something that came by way of the bridge* |
| `e` | *for* | `dexar bluwon` *something for me* | *used by* | `demxar bluwon` *something I use* |
| `ae` | *using* | `zaexual blanewul` *everyone with a knife* | *by* | `zaemxual bledavel` *everyone going by train* |
| `ue` | *against* | `zuexual bluwon` *everyone against me* | *contrary to* | `zuemxual blonam` *everyone acting contrary to the policy* |

An [abstract landmark](ending-grids.md#abstract-landmarks) reads the same way: `zaxual blobowum` *everyone in crisis*, `zuexual blonam` *everyone against the policy*. A person landmark gives [here and there](discourse-hooks.md#deixis): `zomxual bluwon` *everyone here*, `zomxual blawan` *everyone there by you*, `zumxual blayan` *everyone over there*, and `zoxual bluwon` *everyone right where I am*.

**Compare with:** belonging and origin are [of-relations](of-relations.md#of-relations), not places. *Everyone in the family* (its members) is `zual gobem blavayal`, and *everyone from the town* (where they are from) is `zual gaham blahaham`. `zaxual blavayal` is everyone inside the family's space, and `zuxual blahaham` is everyone who came from the town, since `u` names where a path starts.

#### Endings and items {#hooked-joins-endings}

The join part keeps its readings, so every [join ending](joins.md#standalone-phrase) works, under any hook ending. The hook's ending sits before the seam and the join's at the end of the word, so the two never meet.

| Join ending | Agebon | English | Under `yol` |
|-------------|--------|---------|-------------|
| **-r** | `zoxar blahehal` | *someone at the house* | *who at the house?* |
| **-r**, other series | `zoxor blahehal` / `zoxur blahehal` / `zoxer blahehal` | *anyone at the house* / *someone else at the house* / *whoever ranks highest at the house* | *any? / who else? / which one most?* |
| **-r** + **-x** | `zoxarx blahehal` | *some people at the house* | *who all at the house?* |
| **-l** | `zoxal blahehal` | *nobody at the house* | |
| **-m** | `zoxam blahehal` | *nobody at the house, as far as I know* | |
| **-l** on `ua` | `zoxual blahehal` | *everyone at the house, no exceptions* | |
| **-m** on `ua` | `zoxuam blahehal` | *everyone at the house, as far as I know* | |

The two endings are separate choices: `zomxuam bluwon` is *everyone around here, as far as I know*. A standalone join as the landmark names the widest bound out loud: `zoxual blual` is *everyone, anywhere, no exceptions*, and `zoxuam blual` is *people anywhere, in general*.

> `yol dexar bluwon.`
>
> y-question | [d-for-x-wh | bl-speaker]
>
> "What is there for me?"

With items before it, a closed or open join lists the whole set within the bound, as any join does:

> `zuzozen zoxal blahehal vowurel.`
>
> [z-Uzozen | z-at-x-and | bl-house] | v-walk
>
> "Of everyone at the house, only Uzozen walks."

On **-r**, the join stands for one of the items, and the hook says which: the one in that relation.

> `yol zudozol zagozol zoxar blahehal.`
>
> y-question | [z-dog | z-cat | z-at-x-wh | bl-house]
>
> "Which of the dog and the cat is at the house?"

A hooked join sits where any join of its role letter sits. A plain `/b/` one is the recipient (`zuzozen vezuyel boxar blahehal.` *Uzozen tells someone at the house.*), and one right after an extra-noun hook is that hook's landmark:

> `zudozol vezuvol al baoxar bledohol.`
>
> z-dog | v-sleep | [in | [b-on-x-something | bl-table]]
>
> "The dog sleeps in something on the table."

#### Point back (hook **-r**) {#hooked-joins-point-back}

To bound a join by a place already named (*everyone there*), write `r` before the seam and no `bl-` word. The hook then points back to the landmark already in play, the way [`or`](discourse-hooks.md#hook-resume) does on its own: `zorxual` is *everyone there* as `or` is *there*. The hooks that point back on their own take it here: `zarxual` *everyone in there*, `zorxual` *everyone there*, `zurxual` *everyone from there*, `zerxual` *everyone it was for*, `zaorxual` *everyone on it*, `zaerxual` *everyone using it*, `zuorxual` *everyone passing through there*.

> `zuzozen vuhohol ol bahehal. zorxual vezuvol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-sit | [at | b-house] . z-there-x-everything | v-sleep
>
> "Uzozen sits at the house. Everyone there sleeps."

The landmark may be a free hook's `/b/`, any hosted `bl-` word, or an earlier hooked join's landmark; the latest one counts. A hooked join with `r` needs a landmark already in play, and every other hooked join needs its `bl-` word.

#### Bounding a time (`/h/`) {#hooked-joins-time}

English *never since …* and *always, until …* bound how often something happens by a stretch of time. On `/h/`, a hooked join's join is a bare [restrictor](restrictors.md), spelled as after the `h` of `hal` *never*, `ham`, `hual` *always*, `huam`, `har` *sometimes*, `hor`, `hur`, or `her`, and a time landmark bounds which times it covers. The restrictor still says how often; the hook says over which stretch.

<!-- Exception to house-cast: *you never listen* is the complaint these forms answer, so the listener is the point. -->
> `zawan vebal huxam blenulul.`
>
> z-listener | v-hear | [h-from-x-never.open | bl-thunderstorm]
>
> "You haven't listened since the thunderstorm, as far as I know."

<!-- cheat-sheet: joins-hooks -->
| Hook | Times it bounds | Agebon | English |
|------|-----------------|--------|---------|
| `a` | within the landmark's span | `haxual blenulul` | *always, during the thunderstorm* |
| `o` | at that moment | `hoxar blenulul` | *at some point at the thunderstorm* |
| `u` | from that point on ([since](hooks.md#since)) | `huxal blenulul` | *never since the thunderstorm* |
| `oe` | up to that point | `hoexual blenulul` | *always, until the thunderstorm* |
| `om` | around that moment (the frame of `o`) | `homxual blenulul` | *always, around the time of the thunderstorm* |

> `zuzozen vowurel hoexual blenulul.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [h-toward-x-always | bl-thunderstorm]
>
> "Uzozen always walked, until the thunderstorm."

> `zuzozen vezuyol homxual blenulul.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-sing | [h-near-x-always | bl-thunderstorm]
>
> "Uzozen always sings around the time of the thunderstorm."

A loose bound is often the honest one: *you always get like this at the holidays* usually means *around* them (`homxual`), and the listener can check that.

**Compare with:** [`harum`](dependents.md#time-poles) *until* places the event itself; `hoexual` bounds the *always*.

**Point back to a time.** With `r` before the seam, `ar`, `or`, and `ur` point back to the latest **time** landmark: the `bl-` word of a time pole (`herem`, `habam`, `halam`, `harum`, `hevahum`), the landmark of an earlier `/h/` hooked join, or a `blarl` in either. A place landmark does not count.

> `zuzozen vowurel halam blenulul. zawan vebal hurxal.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-walk | [h-after | bl-thunderstorm] . z-listener | v-hear | h-since.then-x-never
>
> "Uzozen walked after the thunderstorm. You haven't listened since then."

#### Bounded by a clause {#hooked-joins-clause}

To name the stretch with a sentence (*never, in all the time since we moved in*), put the stand-in **`blarl`** after an `/h/` hooked join, as [`ul barl`](hooks.md#since) takes one for *since*. The sentence after it is the time that bounds the restrictor. It runs to the period, so the hooked join comes last in the main clause.

> `zawan vebal huxal blarl zayan vavobel al bahehal.`
>
> z-listener | v-hear | [h-from-x-never | bl-that-clause] | z-interlocutors | v-go | [in | b-house]
>
> "You haven't listened since we moved into the house."

**Compare with:** a [hook prefix](#hook-prefixes) also puts a hook before **`x`**, but the stem after the seam is its landmark: `guexavuvul` is *anti-fighting*. In a hooked join the join after the seam is the head, and the landmark is the `bl-` word: `zuexual bluwon` is *everyone against me*. The shape after **`x`** tells them apart: a root, or only vowels and an ending. Use the prefix for a kind of member that can enter the dictionary (`zual goxahehal` *everyone at-home*), and the hooked join for one landmark (`zoxual blahehal` *everyone at that house*), which may be a name, a special pronoun, a resume, a standalone join, or a clause, and may be pointed back to.
