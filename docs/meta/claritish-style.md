# Claritish wording and voice

Wording policies for editors writing the **Claritish** track under **`docs/grammar/claritish/`**. What the track is, the lesson shape, what counts as a drop-in, placement, and the link rules: [Claritish track](grammar-docs.md#claritish-track).

[doc-style.md](doc-style.md) applies here unchanged: register, second person, punctuation over em dashes, one idea per heading, bold sparingly, Agazan in backticks, [plain language](doc-style.md#plain-language-no-assumed-linguistics). Claritish is the same voice aimed at a reader who has not opened the grammar and may never. This page adds only what is different, and owns the [hyphenated English host](#hyphenated-host), which exists in Claritish and nowhere in Agazan.

## Use the grammar's terms
<a id="grammar-terms"></a>

Name the feature the way the grammar names it. The reader is learning real Agazan words, so the English name they learn here must be the one they will meet again: **label scope** and **scope vowel**, **evidential channel**, **emotion locus** and **emotion motion**, **stance** word, **sake**, **tone mark**, **permission** / **requirement** / **consent**, **decision**, **try**. [terminology.md](../grammar/terminology.md) is the list; do not link to it from a lesson.

Do **not** coin a Claritish-only word for a feature that already has an English name. A track-local synonym costs the reader the term twice and makes the lesson stop transferring at the handoff.

| Smell | Prefer |
|-------|--------|
| *reach*, for how far a label applies | *label scope*; the vowel is the *scope vowel* |
| *channel*, introduced as if it were a Claritish word | *evidential channel* (*how you know*), then *channel* for short |
| *place* and *motion*, for a composed feeling | *emotion locus* and *emotion motion* |

Gloss the term in plain English in the **same sentence as first use**, exactly as [doc-style](doc-style.md#plain-language-no-assumed-linguistics) requires: *the scope vowel, which says how far the label reaches*. Using the grammar's name is not a licence to leave it unglossed.

Two terms the track owns, because the grammar has no name for them: **drop-in** (an Agazan word used inside an English sentence) and the **gap** section heading. Add no others.

**Naming a feature is not teaching its grammar.** Keep out the machinery the reader cannot use yet: role letters, hosting, slots and word order, joins, spans, citation forms, the concrete / abstract split. A lesson says what the word means and where it goes in the English sentence, and stops.

## The gap names an English failure
<a id="the-gap"></a>

`## The gap` is two or three sentences on the English habit that misleads, with the real English words in italics (*I think* hides a guess; *he's a liar* turns one act into someone's nature). Agazan enters only in the last sentence, as one clause saying what it names instead.

The failure is psychological, not structural. A gap is a thinking trap the reader will recognize from their own week: the guess that sounds like a fact, the rumor that sounds like a sighting, the one act that becomes a nature. "English lacks a word for X" is not a gap on its own.

| Smell | Why it fails | Prefer |
|-------|--------------|--------|
| Opens with the Agazan form or inventory | Nothing yet says why the reader wants it | Name what English loses first; the forms come under `## The forms` |
| "Agazan marks evidentiality." | A feature announcement; no trap, and an unglossed term | "*He said it's fixed* and *I saw it's fixed* sound the same once you repeat them." |
| A gap that is only tidiness (*English is inconsistent here*) | Gives the reader no reason to change how they talk | Name the cost: what gets believed, or who gets blamed |

## Forms, then placement, then endings
<a id="forms"></a>

`## The forms` leads with the small table, then one sentence on where the drop-in goes in the English sentence, then the endings.

- **Give a default, and an escape.** Say which form to use when the reader cannot tell which fits ("when you are not sure which ending fits, use `thovum`").
- **Long menus say they are menus.** A locus or channel inventory is followed by *you don't need to memorize it*.
- **Say when to leave it out.** Every lesson marks at least one place the drop-in is unnecessary, and none of them implies that plain English is a mistake: *none of the three is the "right" one*; *leave the suffix off when the scope doesn't matter*.
- **Rule out the near miss.** Where the obvious English reading is wrong, deny it in one short sentence (*motion is not strength*).
- **Endings** are the settled-to-passing scale, with the cue **-l ≈ lasting, -r ≈ right now**, and each lesson notes when the scale comes back. Lesson 5 grades the source instead.
- **Pronunciation** is taught once, in Lesson 1, in English keywords. Later lessons do not repeat it.

## The hyphenated English host
<a id="hyphenated-host"></a>

The one place Claritish adds to an English word is [label scope](../grammar/predication.md#label-scope), written with a hyphen: English word + `-th` + scope vowel + **-l** (`lied-thal`). The hyphen marks an English host, the letters after it are spelled exactly as Agazan spells them, and the ending is always **-l**.

The hyphen belongs to Claritish only. Agazan writes label scope mid-word on an Agazan root with no hyphen (`valahathal`), so a lesson teaching the suffix never implies the hyphen survives into the full language.

**Marking:** a hyphenated English host (`lied-thal`) is an English span, so the build does not parse it. Table rows for the suffix use `-thal`, not `…-thal` (a template that does not parse). A tone mark attaches to the English word it colors and is written bare (`%You're the worst.`), not as a backtick span.

## Examples and practice
<a id="examples-practice"></a>

About five one-line examples in a single blockquote, two trailing spaces per line. They are ordinary adult situations (a shipped draft, a delayed flight, a rewritten memo, a sister laid off), not toy sentences, and they carry no Agazan beyond the drop-in. The [house cast](grammar-docs.md#house-cast) stays out of the lessons; names appear only in the full sentence on the closing page.

After the block, one short paragraph unpacks two or three of the examples, not all five, and picks the ones where the ending or the scope does the work.

Practice items give the English sentence in italics, then a parenthesized note saying **what the speaker means**, never which form to write ("(could be; you'll call and check)", not "(use `thovul`)"). A note may name the parts when the word is composed, as in Lesson 7. Answers go in `::: details Show answer`.

## No Agazan sentences, no glosses
<a id="no-glosses"></a>

Lessons carry no full Agazan sentences and no morph gloss lines. Explain a drop-in by what it means, never by cutting it into Agazan morphemes. Where a word is built from parts, label the parts in plain English in a table (Lesson 7's need / locus / motion row), and that is the ceiling. The single glossed Agazan sentence on the closing page is the only one in the track.

## Back-references
<a id="back-references"></a>

A lesson may name an earlier lesson by number when it reuses its form ("start from a thanks or sorry word from Lesson 4"), as a reminder of one word, not a recap. No links between lessons, no links into the grammar, and no previews of lessons ahead outside the list on the intro page.
