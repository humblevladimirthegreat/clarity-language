# Register samples

Editors only — not linked from grammar pages. Working sheet for Phase 2b of the expressiveness review (`docs/proposals/expressiveness-review.md`).

Eight English samples (≈150 words each) in registers the [syntax test corpus](syntax-test-corpus.md) does not cover. Cite rows as `RS-n` (sample) or `RS-n.m` (sentence or turn *m* within it). Translate with the published grammar and lexicon; sort each blocker the same way as the corpus ([When a sentence will not translate as is](syntax-test-corpus.md#when-a-sentence-will-not-translate-as-is)): parser miss, grammar gap, missing vocabulary. Log grammar gaps in the ledger, lexicon-only gaps as `L-nn` rows for the TODO lexicon items.

The English is original to this file. Sentences are numbered so a translation can go line by line. Names are placeholders (Mara, Jun, Ines, Tomas, Dr. Okafor); use the house cast where a name is needed in Agazan ([house cast](grammar-docs.md#house-cast)) and translate the meaning, not the spelling. A sentence whose English form is deliberately not mirrored (copula, tense, articles) is **covered** if the meaning has a natural route.

## RS-1 — Casual chat

Two friends, Mara and Jun, on a walk.

1. **Mara:** Hey, you made it! I wasn't sure you'd come.
2. **Jun:** Yeah, sorry I'm late. The bus was kind of a mess.
3. **Mara:** Oh no. Was it the same driver as last time?
4. **Jun:** I think so, but I'm not sure. Anyway, I'm here now.
5. **Mara:** Good. So, do you want to get coffee first, or just walk?
6. **Jun:** Honestly, coffee sounds great. I haven't eaten anything yet.
7. **Mara:** Nothing at all? You should have said something. There's a bakery around the corner.
8. **Jun:** Right, but I didn't want to keep you waiting.
9. **Mara:** You're ridiculous. Come on, my treat.
10. **Jun:** Really? Thanks. Next one's on me, okay?
11. **Mara:** Deal. By the way, did you ever hear back about that job?
12. **Jun:** Not yet. Maybe next week, if they're quick. Fingers crossed.
13. **Mara:** They'd be lucky to have you, you know.
14. **Jun:** Stop it. You're making me blush.

## RS-3 — How-to instructions

Making a simple tomato sauce.

1. Before you start, wash your hands and clear a space on the counter.
2. Chop one onion and three cloves of garlic. Cut them as small as you can, because they cook faster that way.
3. Heat two tablespoons of oil in a wide pan over medium heat. When the oil shimmers, add the onion.
4. Stir often. If the onion browns too quickly, lower the heat.
5. After about five minutes, add the garlic and cook for one more minute. Do not let it burn; burnt garlic tastes bitter.
6. Pour in one large can of crushed tomatoes and half a cup of water. Add a pinch of salt.
7. Let the sauce simmer, uncovered, for twenty to thirty minutes, until it thickens.
8. Taste it. If it is too sharp, add a small spoonful of sugar. If it is bland, add more salt.
9. Be careful when you stir: hot sauce can splash.
10. Serve immediately, or let it cool and store it in the fridge for up to four days.

## RS-4 — News paragraph

A local news item, invented.

1. The city council voted 7 to 2 on Tuesday night to close the Harbor Street bridge for repairs, ending months of debate.
2. The bridge, which was built in 1962, carries about 14,000 vehicles a day.
3. According to a report released last month, several support beams have cracked and must be replaced.
4. Work is expected to begin in March and could last up to eight months, officials said.
5. "We understand this will be difficult for residents," said council member Dr. Okafor, who voted in favor. "But delaying the work would cost more and put people at risk."
6. Shop owners near the bridge, who fear losing customers, say they were not consulted.
7. The mayor has promised that a free shuttle will run between the two shores while the bridge is closed.
8. It is not yet clear who will pay for the shuttle. The council will meet again on the 12th to discuss funding.
9. Residents can share comments online until the end of the month.

## RS-5 — Story narration

Original short passage.

1. When Nadia reached the top of the hill, the sun had already set, and the valley below was filling with mist.
2. She stood still for a long time, listening. Somewhere a dog was barking, and the wind moved through the grass like a hand through hair.
3. "Is anyone there?" she called. Nobody answered.
4. She had walked all day to get here, and her feet ached. She sat down on a flat stone and took off her boots.
5. As she rubbed her heels, she noticed a small light moving among the trees. It came closer, slowly, as if it were unsure of her.
6. "Who are you?" she whispered.
7. The light stopped. Then a voice, thin and old, said, "I was about to ask you the same thing."
8. Nadia laughed in spite of herself. Her fear, which had been heavy a moment before, felt suddenly foolish.
9. "I'm only a traveler," she said. "I lost the road hours ago."
10. "Then you are in the right place," said the voice. "Nobody finds this hill unless they are lost."

## RS-6 — Argument / opinion

An op-ed excerpt on short work weeks.

1. Many people believe that a four-day work week would make companies less productive. I think they are mistaken.
2. Admittedly, the evidence is still limited. Most trials have been small, and the companies that volunteered were probably already open to change.
3. Even so, the results are hard to ignore. Workers reported less stress, and the firms that took part kept their output, sometimes even raising it.
4. Why would that be? Tired people make more mistakes, and rested people work better than exhausted ones. This is not a new idea.
5. Critics say that some jobs simply cannot be compressed. A hospital cannot close on Fridays. That is true, but it is not an argument against trying elsewhere.
6. I should say that I may be biased: I have worked long hours for years, and I would like to believe it was not necessary.
7. Still, if the choice is between a small risk and a system that burns people out, I know which one I would take.
8. If we never test the idea, we will never know whether it works. Surely that is reason enough to try.

## RS-7 — Support conversation

A friend, Ines, speaking with Mara after a hard day. (Compassion theme.)

1. **Mara:** I don't even know why I'm upset. It's such a small thing.
2. **Ines:** It doesn't sound small to me. Do you want to tell me what happened?
3. **Mara:** I forgot my sister's birthday. She didn't say anything, but I could tell she was hurt.
4. **Ines:** That sounds painful. It makes sense that you feel guilty.
5. **Mara:** Everyone else remembers these things. I'm a terrible sibling.
6. **Ines:** I hear that you're being hard on yourself. Can I offer another way to look at it?
7. **Mara:** Okay.
8. **Ines:** You forgot a date, and you feel bad because you love her. Those are two different things. One is a mistake; the other is who you are.
9. **Mara:** I guess so. I just don't know what to say to her.
10. **Ines:** You could tell her the truth: that you're sorry, and that she matters to you. You don't have to do it today.
11. **Mara:** Thank you for listening. I really needed someone to.
12. **Ines:** I'm glad you told me. I'm here whenever you want to talk more.

## RS-8 — Decision memo

An internal note by a team lead. (Empowerment / rationality theme.)

**To:** the project team. **Re:** which vendor to choose.

1. We have to decide by Friday whether to renew our contract with Vendor A or switch to Vendor B. This memo sets out what I know, what I do not know, and what I recommend.
2. Vendor B costs about 20% less per year. However, switching would take roughly two months, and during that time we would probably lose some speed.
3. I do not know how reliable Vendor B is. Two references were positive, but both were chosen by Vendor B, so I give them limited weight.
4. My guess is that there is a 60% chance the switch pays off within a year. I could be wrong; I have been overconfident about estimates before.
5. I would rather choose than drift. Staying is also a decision, and we should own it as one.
6. My recommendation: run a one-month pilot with Vendor B on a single project. If it meets the three criteria below, we switch. If it does not, we renew with Vendor A and stop reopening the question.
7. The criteria are: response time under one hour, no more than one outage in the pilot, and a support rating of at least four out of five.
8. Please tell me by Wednesday if you disagree. Nobody needs to agree with me to be heard; I want the reasons, not just the vote.
