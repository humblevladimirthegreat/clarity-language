# Proposal: Claritish (Agazan drop-ins for English)

**Status:** PROPOSED  

**Design authority:** remains [`docs/grammar/`](../grammar/introduction.md). Claritish adds no forms. Each drop-in is an existing Agazan word, spelled exactly as the grammar page that teaches it spells it.

## Motivation

The full language takes a long time before it pays off. Most of Agazan's psychological value sits in a few closed words: stance words, endings and tone marks. English has no word for these, yet each one makes sense by itself. If English speakers can drop those words into ordinary English sentences, they get the benefit on day one, and each word they learn is already real Agazan.

## Goals

1. Speakers get **immediate benefit**: calibrated confidence, honest sourcing, less labeling of people, clear consent, and kinder conversation bids.
2. **Nothing is wasted.** Every form is spelled exactly as in Agazan, so learning Claritish is learning Agazan.
3. **Excitement.** Each lesson ends with a taste of the same idea in a full Agazan sentence. The last page invites the learner to start the full language.
4. **No new grammar.** A drop-in sits in an English sentence without any Agazan syntax around it.

## Non-goals

- Agazan syntax in English (role letters, hosted `/b/`, joins, spans, hooks, role compounds, numeric derivation).
- Respelling for English readers, or marking Agazan words with italics, quotes or brackets. Drop-ins are written plainly.
- A replacement for the grammar track. Claritish is an on-ramp.

## Rules

1. **A drop-in is a closed Agazan word** from a taught stance, pronoun, number or tone-mark set, spelled exactly as in Agazan.
2. **Placement is free and follows English.** Put the word where an English adverb or tag would sit: after the clause (`She'll come th+70.`), before it, or next to the word it colors.
3. **Scope is the nearest clause.** A stance drop-in covers the English clause it sits in.
4. **Suffixes on English words are allowed** for [label scope](../grammar/predication.md#label-scope): an English word + `th` + scope vowel + ending (`liedthal`). In Claritish this is the only suffix. It is a Claritish adaptation, because Agazan attaches the seam only to an Agazan root.
5. **Tone marks** go before what they color, as in Agazan: `%` before a word or a free-standing `%` before the rest of the sentence.
6. **English punctuation stays English.** Sentences don't have to end in an Agazan period.

## Lessons

Follow the lessons in order. Each one is a single page with: the gap in English, the forms (a small table), five or so English example sentences, a short practice, and a **Full Agazan** box that shows one Agazan sentence using the form, as a teaser.

**No links into the grammar pages.** The grammar has to be learned in order, so lesson pages never link to a stage page, not even from the Full Agazan box. The only way from Claritish into the grammar is the closing plug after Lesson 10. The **Source** lines below are for editors only and don't appear on the lesson pages. The order goes from zero-cost marks, through the rationality and compassion words, to social forms, and finishes on fun forms.

### Lesson 1: Tone marks

**Gap:** tone is lost in text. Is it a joke, sincere, or said with emphasis?

| Form | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| `%` | joking, teasing |
| `;` | warm, affectionate |
| `&` | contrast: this one, not another |
| `?` | unsure about this word |
| `?!` | surprised and doubtful |

> %You're the worst.  
> ;Call me when you land.  
> I said &Tuesday.  
> He brought a ?casserole.

**Why first:** there's nothing to pronounce, and people can use it in their very next text. It also teaches the Agazan rule that a mark goes *before* what it colors. **Source** (editors): [tone marks](../grammar/speech-moves.md#tone-marks).

### Lesson 2: How sure are you?

**Gap:** English *probably* / *maybe* / *definitely* are vague, and people read them very differently.

| Form | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| `th+N` | N% likely (`th+70`) |
| `th+` / `th-` | likely / unlikely |
| `thovom` | could be |
| `thovol` | could be, and I'll find out |
| `thovor` | could be, who knows (and I'm fine not knowing) |

> The package arrives Friday th+80.  
> She's upset with me thovol.  
> It'll rain th-.

**Why here:** it's the clearest rationality win, and it introduces the **-l / -m / -r** endings (seeking / default / not seeking). **Sources** (editors): [stance numbers](../grammar/numbers.md#number-as-stance-by-marker), [MAY](../grammar/knowing.md#may).

### Lesson 3: How do you know?

**Gap:** English doesn't mark whether a claim was seen, remembered, inferred or heard. Rumors take on the authority of eyewitness reports.

| Form | Channel |
|------|---------|
| `thodum` | watching it now |
| `thunom` | from memory |
| `therum` | on record / scheduled |
| `thabem` | from the pattern |
| `thevem` | inferred from clues |
| `themam` | someone told me |
| `thahum` | gut feeling |
| `thazom` | per the story |

The ending swap is the payoff: **-l** strong evidence, **-m** default, **-r** weak (`themar` a rumor, `thunol` a vivid memory, `thahur` a faint hunch).

> The server is down thodum.  
> Marco quit themar.  
> The meeting got moved therul.  
> This bug is in the parser thevem, th+60.

The last example shows that evidence strength and likelihood are separate. **Why here:** the endings from Lesson 2 are reused with a new meaning, so the system starts to feel learnable. **Source** (editors): [evidentiality](../grammar/knowing.md#evidentiality), [evidence strength](../grammar/knowing.md#evidence-strength).

### Lesson 4: Labels (once, pattern, to whom, name only)

**Gap:** *He's a liar* turns one act into someone's nature. English needs a whole clause to take that back.

Add `th` + a scope vowel + an ending to the English word:

| Suffix | Reach |
|--------|-------|
| `…thal` | this one occasion |
| `…thel` | a pattern (not a fixed nature) |
| `…thol` | true in one relationship |
| `…thul` | a name only, not an explanation |

> He liedthal. He's not a liarthel.  
> I was lazythal this morning.  
> She's rudethol, to him at least.  
> My "procrastination"thul is mostly exhaustion.

**Why here:** this is the flagship compassion feature, and it's the most striking thing to show people. The spelling is the Agazan seam exactly, so `valahathal` looks familiar right away. **Source** (editors): [label scope](../grammar/predication.md#label-scope).

### Lesson 5: Thanks and sorry that say why

**Gap:** *thanks* and *sorry* don't say what the act did for the person.

| Form | Sake |
|------|------|
| `aba` | autonomy |
| `udu` | competence |
| `ona` | relatedness |
| `ozu` | pleasure |
| `uho` | survival / safety |

Thanks = sake + `tham` (met): `thonatham` *that meant a lot to me*, `thuduthal` *that sets me up for good*. Sorry = sake + `thum` + `bohen` (unmet, *yours*): `thonathum bohen` *that hurt your sense of connection*. **-l / -m / -r** grade how long it lasts or how serious it is (`…thur bohen` *my bad*).

> You covered my shift — thuhotham.  
> I cancelled again. Thonathum bohen.  
> Thanks for teaching me the shortcut, thuduthal.

**Why here:** the five sakes are the heart of the psychology, and naming a need is a skill people can use every day. `bohen` is the first Agazan word outside the stance set; teach it as a set phrase. **Source** (editors): [thanks and sorry](../grammar/sakes.md#thanks-sorry), [sake inventory](../grammar/sakes.md#sake-inventory).

### Lesson 6: Allowed, required, agreed

**Gap:** English *can* / *may* / *should* / *OK* blur who allowed something, and whether consent was given or assumed.

| Form | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| `thegal` / `thegam` / `thegar` | allowed by rule / by a person / tolerated |
| `therel` / `therem` / `therer` | forbidden by rule / by a person / frowned on |
| `themehol` / `themehom` / `themehor` | required by rule / by a person / by custom |
| `thuxegal` / `thuxegam` / `thuxegar` | the affected person agreed in advance / said yes (revocable) / is assumed OK |

> I borrowed your car thuxegar — I should have asked for thuxegam.  
> You can leave early thegam.  
> We wear ties themehor, not themehol.

**Why here:** the same **-l / -m / -r** scale (rule / person / unspoken) comes back a third time. **Sources** (editors): [permission](../grammar/sakes.md#permission), [requirement](../grammar/sakes.md#requirement), [consent](../grammar/sakes.md#consent).

### Lesson 7: Decisions and tries

**Gap:** *I've decided* sounds final, and *I'll try* sounds like a hedge. Neither says how locked in the speaker is.

| Form | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| `thehal` / `theham` / `thehar` | decided for good / decided, can still change / decided for now |
| `thudol` / `thudom` / `thudor` | committed to trying / tries / trial run (failing is useful to know) |

> I'm going vegetarian thudor.  
> We're moving to Denver thehal.  
> I'm taking the job theham.

**Why here:** a trial run lowers the stakes of starting something, which fits the empowerment theme. **Sources** (editors): [decision](../grammar/intention.md#decision), [try](../grammar/intention.md#try).

### Lesson 8: Conversation and *we*

**Gap:** English has no quick way to say "I only have a minute," and *we* is ambiguous about whether it includes the listener.

| Form | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| `xan` | I have time, we can stay |
| `xen` | I have a few minutes / leaving soon |
| `xon` | one quick thing / about to leave |
| `xun` | just saying hi / leaving now |
| `ohan` | *we* = you and I |

> Hey — xon — did you sign the form?  
> Xan. What's up?  
> Ohan should fix this before Friday.

Claritish drops the name from the bid, because English speakers rarely say their own name. The bare `x` + vowel + **-n** is the Agazan bid without its host name. **Why here:** these are social forms that work best once a few people in a group know them. **Sources** (editors): [conversation length](../grammar/x-compounds.md#conversation-length), [special pronouns](../grammar/pronouns.md#special-pronouns).

### Lesson 9: Feelings in three parts

**Gap:** one English emotion word (*anxious*, *resentful*, *proud*) hides three things: which need is at stake, where the feeling sits or what it points at, and how it moves. A single label can be a judgment in disguise.

Build on the sake words from Lesson 5. Put `g` + sake + `tha` (met) or `thu` (unmet) + ending right after the English noun the feeling is about. Then add a **locus** and a **motion ending**:

| Locus | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| `a` | mine, held inside |
| `uo` | caught from someone or the room, not mine |
| `ae` | felt in a role, not personally |
| `ua` | the group's feeling, and I share it |
| `ao` | the atmosphere of the situation |
| `u` | I can't place where it comes from |
| `o` | aimed at someone |
| `oe` | reaching to change something |
| `ue` | pushing back |
| `e` | felt on someone's behalf |

| Motion | Meaning |
|--------|---------|
| **-r** | surging, in waves |
| **-m** | flowing, steady |
| **-l** | still, muted, numb |

> The draft guduthamar. (proud: competence met, inside, surging)  
> Tomorrow's review guduthuraor. (anxious: competence at stake for now, over the room, surging)  
> That meeting gonathumuom — I think I picked it up from Priya. (irritated, but caught, not mine)  
> The memo gonathumuer. (objecting to it)  
> Today guduthurul, and I can't say why. (uneasy, unplaced, still)

**Why here:** this is the richest compassion and self-understanding tool, and it's great for journaling. It comes late because it combines the sakes from Lesson 5 with the ending logic from earlier lessons, and it gives learners the most to build. Present the locus and motion tables as a menu, not something to memorize. **Source** (editors): [emotion compose](../grammar/sakes.md#emotion-compose).

### Lesson 10: Fun numbers

**Gap:** none that's serious. This lesson is a reward and a teaser for the number system.

| Form | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| `g+1e` | a gazillion |
| `g#1e` | the umpteenth |
| `y+e` | To infinity! |
| `y#e` | Finally! |

> I've told you g#1e times.  
> Y#e, the build passed.

**Source** (editors): [numbers](../grammar/numbers.md).

### Closing: learn the full language

A short final page, not a lesson. It ends Claritish and is the only place that links into the grammar.

- Recap what the learner can now say: confidence, sources, labels, needs, consent, decisions, conversation length, feelings.
- Show that these were never add-ons. Each drop-in is a real Agazan word, and the full language uses them the same way inside sentences built for them. Show one full sentence that stacks several of them, with a morph line.
- Tease what Claritish can't do: a whole language designed around these distinctions, easy to sing, and unambiguous enough for tools to parse.
- **Plug:** one clear call to action, *Start learning Agazan*, linking to the start of the learning path ([introduction](../grammar/introduction.md#how-to-learn)).

## Open questions

1. **The ending on label-scope suffixes.** The lessons fix **-l** (`liedthal`). Should an abstract English word take **-m** (`kindnessthem`), as Agazan does for abstract meanings, or should Claritish keep one ending so there's less to learn?
2. **Bare bids and the join words.** `xan` and `xon` are also Agazan clause joins. As standalone greetings in English they're easy to tell apart. Should the full-Agazan box on Lesson 8 point this out?
3. **`bohen` in Lesson 5.** Is one set-phrase noun acceptable, or should sorry wait for the full language?
4. **Where it lives.** It could be a separate site section outside the stage order, like the recipe track. Should [Why Agazan](../grammar/why-agazan.md) link to Lesson 1 as the easy entry point?
5. **Journaling.** Should the earlier journaling Claritish become the practice for Lessons 5 and 9?
