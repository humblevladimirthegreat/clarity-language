# Proposal: Hook prefixes (*anti-*, *pro-*, *trans-*)

**Status:** PROPOSED. Nothing here is implemented. Role compounds already use role letter + **`w`**, so the role-letter + vowel + **`x`** + stem shape is free. This proposal claims that shape for hook prefixes.  
**Related:** [hook compounds](../grammar/hooks.md#hook-compounds), [extra-noun hooks](../grammar/hooks.md#extra-noun), [stacked hook vowels](../grammar/hooks.md#extra-noun-intermediate), [compounds](../grammar/compounds.md), [left and right of `x`](../grammar/compounds.md#families-by-shape), [singability constraints](../grammar/phonology.md#singability-constraints).

## Problem

English builds many words from a relation plus a thing: *anti-war*, *pro-choice*, *indoor*, *transoceanic*, *homeward*, *water-powered*. Agebon has these relations as extra-noun hooks (**`uel`** *against*, **`el`** *for*, **`al`** *in*, **`uol`** *through*), but it fuses a hook only as a suffix, onto a citation, and the result is a verb ([hook compounds](../grammar/hooks.md#hook-compounds): `owurelul` *leave*, `owureluel` *oppose*). To describe a thing by its relation to something else, a speaker has to write a hook and a `/b/` after the noun (`behaham om buwon` *the village near me*). There is no single word for *anti-fighting*, and so no dictionary of such words.

## The shape

Role letter + hook vowel(s) + seam + stem + ending. The seam is **`x`** for the hook's **-l** reading and **`mx`** for its **-m** frame.

> `zuzozen guexavuvul.`
>
> z-Uzozen | g-against-x-fight
>
> "Uzozen is anti-fighting."

> `zuzozen guemxewulel.`
>
> z-Uzozen | g-contrary-to-x-wind
>
> "Uzozen is headed into the wind."

The hook vowels name the relation, the seam joins them to the stem, and the stem is the hook's landmark. The word names something that stands in that relation **to** the stem. **`x`** is the compound seam (**x** ≈ plus), and English writes these words with a hyphen too: *anti-war*, *pro-choice*.

Plain **`x`** is the default and carries the **-l** hook. An **`m`** before the seam picks the **-m** frame, the same letter that picks it on the free hook (**`uel`** → **`uem`**, so **`uex`** → **`uemx`**). There is no **`lx`** seam: plain **`x`** already spells the **-l** reading.

This mirrors the existing hook compound:

| | Hook compound (exists) | Hook prefix (new) |
|-|------------------------|-------------------|
| Order | stem, then hook | hook, then stem |
| Shape | citation + hook: `owurelul` | role letter + hook + **`x`** or **`mx`** + stem: `guexavuvul` |
| The stem is | the event | the landmark |
| The word names | the event, done in that relation to a `/d/` | something in that relation to the stem |
| English | *leave*, *oppose*, *enter* | *anti-fighting*, *indoor*, *homeward* |

## The twenty prefixes

Each hook in the extra-noun grid gives two prefixes: hook vowels + **`x`** for the **-l** hook, and hook vowels + **`mx`** for its **-m** frame. The vowels and cues are the hooks' own.

### Plain `x`: the -l hooks

The literal column uses a stem's concrete sense (**-l**) and a place or physical contact. The metaphorical column uses an abstract sense (**-m**): a state, a policy, a debt.

| Hook | Prefix | Reading | Literal | Metaphorical |
|------|--------|---------|---------|--------------|
| **`uel`** *against* | **`uex`** | opposed to the stem | `guexohalal` *sea-facing* (a seawall) | `guexonam` *anti-policy* |
| **`el`** *for* | **`ex`** | in favor of, meant for | `gexahehal` *for the home*, household | `gexonam` *pro-policy* |
| **`al`** *in* | **`ax`** | inside the stem | `gaxahehal` *indoor* | `gaxobowum` *mid-crisis*, in crisis mode |
| **`aol`** *on* | **`aox`** | on the stem's surface | `gaoxohalal` *seagoing*, on the ocean | `gaoxonam` *on-policy*, following the policy |
| **`ol`** *at* | **`ox`** | located at | `goxahehal` *at-home* | `goxabevam` *at issue* |
| **`oel`** *toward* | **`oex`** | heading to | `goexahehal` *homeward* | `goexuzebum` *habit-forming* |
| **`ul`** *from* | **`ux`** | starting from, sourced at | `guxahehal` *from home*, home-sourced | `guxuzobam` *heritage-derived* |
| **`ual`** *out of* | **`uax`** | coming or made out of | `guaxowulal` *out of the water* | `guaxaram` *debt-free*, out of debt |
| **`uol`** *through* | **`uox`** | across, by way of | `guoxohalal` *transoceanic* | `guoxobowum` *crisis-spanning*, lasting through the crisis |
| **`ael`** *using* | **`aex`** | running on, by means of | `gaexowulal` *water-powered* | `gaexugarom` *tradition-based* |

### `mx`: the -m frames

The **-m** frame makes the landmark the general setting, not the exact contact, source, tool, or opponent, as on the free hook.

| Hook | Prefix | Reading | Literal | Metaphorical |
|------|--------|---------|---------|--------------|
| **`uem`** *contrary to* | **`uemx`** | set against the stem's frame | `guemxewulel` *headwind*, into the wind | `guemxonam` *off-policy*, contrary to the policy |
| **`em`** *used by* | **`emx`** | in the stem's use | `gemxayayal` *group-owned* | `gemxayayam` *community-held* |
| **`am`** *amid* | **`amx`** | set amid the stem | `gamxohalal` *mid-ocean*, out among the waves | `gamxobowum` *crisis-era*, set amid the crisis |
| **`aom`** *over* | **`aomx`** | over the stem, covering it | `gaomxohalal` *over-ocean* (a flight) | `gaomxonam` *policy-covered* |
| **`om`** *near* | **`omx`** | near the stem | `gomxahehal` *near-home*, nearby | `gomxabevam` *problem-adjacent* |
| **`oem`** *in the direction of* | **`oemx`** | facing the stem, not reaching it | `goemxahehal` *home-facing* | `goemxuzebum` *habit-leaning* |
| **`um`** *away from* | **`umx`** | away from the stem | `gumxahehal` *away from home* | `gumxuzebum` *habit-shedding* |
| **`uam`** *out from among* | **`uamx`** | picked out from among | `guamxayayal` *picked from the group* | `guamxayayam` *community-drawn* |
| **`uom`** *by way of* | **`uomx`** | routed by way of the stem | `guomxohel` *bridge-routed* | `guomxewulem` *influence-routed* |
| **`aem`** *by* | **`aemx`** | carried by the stem as channel | `gaemxorahel` *road-borne* | `gaemxonam` *policy-channeled*, carried through policy |

Each metaphorical reading keeps the same picture as the literal one, so a learner who knows the hook can read a new prefix word without a dictionary. A pair differs the way the free hooks do: `goxahehal` *at-home* is in the house, and `gomxahehal` *near-home* is only close to it.

## Metaphorical readings belong to the hooks

These metaphorical readings are not special to prefixes. They are what each extra-noun hook means when its landmark is abstract, wherever the hook appears. [hooks.md](../grammar/hooks.md#extra-noun) teaches the extra-noun hooks only with places, tools, and people today, and says nothing about an abstract landmark. This proposal adds that reading to the hook itself, so the free hook, the hook compound, and the hook prefix all share it:

| Hook | Abstract landmark reading | Free hook | Hook compound (suffix) | Hook prefix |
|------|---------------------------|-----------|------------------------|-------------|
| **`al`** *in* | in a state or condition | `al bobowum` *in a crisis* | `owurelal` *enter* (a crisis) | `gaxobowum` *mid-crisis* |
| **`aol`** *on* | on a plan, rule, or footing | `aol bonam` *on policy* | `owurelaol` *mount* (a policy, take it up) | `gaoxonam` *on-policy* |
| **`ol`** *at* | at a point of concern or status | `ol babevam` *at issue* | `owurelol` *attend* (an issue) | `goxabevam` *at issue* |
| **`oel`** *toward* | tending toward | `oel buzebum` *toward a habit* | `owureloel` *head for* (a habit) | `goexuzebum` *habit-forming* |
| **`ul`** *from* | derived from, owing to | `ul buzobam` *from heritage* | `owurelul` *leave* (a heritage) | `guxuzobam` *heritage-derived* |
| **`ual`** *out of* | out of a condition | `ual baram` *out of debt* | `owurelual` *exit* (debt) | `guaxaram` *debt-free* |
| **`uol`** *through* | lasting through, by way of | `uol bobowum` *through the crisis* | `owureluol` *traverse* (a crisis) | `guoxobowum` *crisis-spanning* |
| **`el`** *for* | in favor of | `el bonam` *for the policy* | `owurelel` *serve* (a policy) | `gexonam` *pro-policy* |
| **`uel`** *against* | opposed to | `uel bonam` *against the policy* | `owureluel` *oppose* (a policy) | `guexonam` *anti-policy* |
| **`ael`** *using* | based on, by means of | `ael bugarom` *by tradition* | `owurelael` *wield* (a tradition) | `gaexugarom` *tradition-based* |

The **-m** frames extend the same way, and their **`mx`** prefixes share the reading: `am bobowum` *amid the crisis* and `gamxobowum` *crisis-era*; `uem bonam` *contrary to the policy* and `guemxonam` *off-policy*.

The rule for an abstract landmark: keep the literal picture and read the landmark as a region, a surface, a point, or a path in an abstract space. A state is a container (**`al`**), a plan is a surface to stand on (**`aol`**), a tendency is a destination (**`oel`**), a condition is something to get out of (**`ual`**). A reading that needs a different picture is not a hook reading; it takes its own word.

The stem keeps its own sense choice: **-l** the concrete sense, **-m** the abstract. `guexuduvul` is *anti-dueling*, and `guexuduvum` is *anti-conflict*. A stem may be any root, including a three-letter one, or an **`x`** compound: `guexahehaxowurel` is *anti-house-walking*.

## Role letters

The role letter is the word's slot, as on any content word.

| Role letter | Reading | Example | English |
|-------------|---------|---------|---------|
| `/ɡ/` | a property: in that relation to the stem | `gexegobol` | *pro-body*, body-positive |
| `/z/` `/d/` `/b/` | someone or something in that relation | `zuexavuvul` | *an opponent of fighting* |
| `/h/` | the clause stands in that relation | `hoexahehal` | *homeward* (of the movement) |

Scope works as on any word, after the stem with the **`th`** seam: `zuexavuvuthel` is *a committed opponent of fighting* (the practiced reach), and `zuexavuvuthal` is *against fighting this time*. **-r** resumes the whole word, as usual.

The `/h/` reading says the same as the free hook and its `/b/` on the clause (`oel bahehal` *toward the house*). The fused form is the kind (*homeward*), and the free form names the landmark. Both are allowed.

## Compare with the free hook

`zumon uel buduvul` and `zuexavuvul` overlap. The free phrase puts a landmark in play that later words can point back to (`buduvur`), and it takes adjectives on the landmark. The prefix makes one word that names a kind: it can enter the dictionary, take scope, and sit in any slot. Use the prefix for *an anti-war person* as a category, and the phrase for *someone against that duel*.

## Why plain `x` is the -l prefix

The **-l** hooks are the everyday ones (*in*, *at*, *from*, *against*), so their prefixes take the shortest seam and keep every syllable open (`gue-xa-vu-vul`). The **-m** frame adds one letter, the **`m`** it adds on the free hook, so a learner who knows `uem` can read `uemx`. In English terms, **`uex`** is *anti-*, **`uemx`** is *contra-*, **`amx`** is *inter-* (*amid*), **`omx`** is *para-* (*near*).

**`lx`** is not a seam. It would spell the same reading as plain **`x`** with a closed syllable added, on the commonest prefixes.

## Spelling and parsing

- **Left of the seam:** one hook vowel or one hook pair. A full root is at least three letters and alternates consonant and vowel, so the [left and right of `x`](../grammar/compounds.md#families-by-shape) test that tells ordinary compounds from other `x` families also tells a hook prefix from an ordinary compound.
- **Right of the seam:** a stem of at least three letters. A single vowel and an ending (role letter + vowel + **`x`** + vowel + ending) is not a hook prefix; it is unclaimed ([unassigned-reserved](../meta/unassigned-reserved.md)).
- **Vowel pairs that are not hooks** (**`ea`**, **`eo`**, **`eu`**, **`oa`**, **`ou`**, **`au`**, and so on) before **`x`** or **`mx`** have no reading. List them in `unassigned-reserved.md`.
- **`lx`** after hook vowels has no reading, closed by design (above). Any other consonant before the seam (**`nx`**, **`rx`**) has no reading either.
- **Roots:** no root contains **`x`**, and every root starts and ends with a vowel, so neither seam is ever inside a root, and **`mx`** right after the role letter's vowels is always this seam.
- **Not the plural:** word-final **-mx** is the [indefinite group](../grammar/plurality.md) on an abstract word. The seam always has a stem after it, so the two never meet: `gumxuzebum` is a prefix word, `zuzebumx` is *some habits*.
- **Hosted:** a hook prefix in a `bl-` slot is `bluexavuvul`, or `bluemxonam`.
- **Syllables:** **`x`** opens the stem's first syllable (`gue-xa-vu-vul`), as in any **`x`** compound. With **`mx`**, the **`m`** closes the hook's syllable (`guem-xo-nam`). That is a new syllable-final consonant inside an ordinary word, beside the lexical join before **`r`**; [singability constraints](../grammar/phonology.md#singability-constraints) must list it with the allowed clusters.
- **The old role-compound spellings are free:** `zaxuduyel` has no reading, and here it would be *in-teaching*; `daexarayel` would be *write-powered*. Role compounds already use **`w`** (`zwazuduyel`).

## Derivation in the dictionary

Like hook compounds, common hook prefixes become dictionary words, listed as citations with no role letter (`uexavuvul`). A dictionary entry may narrow the sense, as long as the hook still reads plainly.
