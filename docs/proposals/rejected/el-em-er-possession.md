# Proposal: `el` / `em` / `er` possession series

**Status:** REJECTED. Taking `el` would cost the everyday *for*, and the inalienable slot already exists as `gabom`. Adopted instead: `em` narrowed to use / access, plus a care tie `gahazum` — see [hooks](../../grammar/hooks.md#genitive) and [social relations](../../grammar/relations.md#social-relations).

## Idea

Turn the extra-noun `e` hook into a possession series whose ending is a time horizon, the way [sakes](../../grammar/sakes.md) endings are (**-l** lasting / **-m** unstated / **-r** immediate):

| Form | Proposed meaning |
|------|------------------|
| **`el`** + `/b/` | inalienable: part of B, can't be taken away (*Azawan's hand*) |
| **`em`** + `/b/` | alienable (*Azawan's dog*) |
| **`er`** + `/b/` | temporary or steward (*my seat tonight*, *my patient*) |

## Why it was attractive

- One ending pattern for possession and sakes: learners would reuse a known cue.
- Makes alienability and temporariness visible, which supports non-attachment (a thing held for now is not part of me) and defusion.

## Why it was rejected

1. **Loses the short *for*.** `el` is Beginner *for* (*cooks for Alahen*), and every extra-noun hook slot is taken. The fallback, `hegulam` + `/b/`, is a three-syllable relation word for one of the most frequent prepositions. The fused hook *serve* (`owogalel`) would also need a new meaning.
2. **Inalienable already has a home.** `gabom` (*part of*) covers body parts and parts of wholes. A second form would overlap it.
3. **Alienable `em` forces a choice on every *'s*.** A strictly alienable `em` gives no neutral way to say *B's* when the speaker does not care about alienability. The adopted rule narrows `em` to use / access instead, which still keeps people and feelings out.
4. **Temporary and steward are different axes.** *My seat tonight* is a time horizon; *my patient* is a duty of care toward a person. The adopted design keeps them apart: temporary use is ordinary `em`, and care is a tie root on `/ɡ/` (`gahazum`).
5. **Inalienable `el` invites fusion with feelings.** Learners would reach for it with *Azawan's anger*, which works against the defusion goal. Feelings go through emotion compose instead.

**Minor:** `er` + `/b/` would have been parseable (a following `/b/` separates it from the point-back `er` *for that*), so the -r reading was not a blocker.
