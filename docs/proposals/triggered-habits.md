# Proposal: Triggered habits

**Status:** PROPOSED. Nothing here is built.  
**Related:** [feature criteria](../meta/feature-criteria.md), [purpose](../grammar/purpose.md#trap-identity) (a moment as identity), [restrictors](../grammar/restrictors.md) (*always* / *only when*), [ability](../grammar/ability.md) (*can't* kinds), [label scope](../grammar/label-scope.md), [sakes](../grammar/sakes.md), cigarette `uzebu` in [`lexicon-published.csv`](../../data/lexicon-published.csv) (abstract *habit*).  
**Design authority:** none until absorbed. TODO dictionary line *habits: triggered*.

A closed overlay so *habit* names a **cue then an act**, not a trait you have. Optional and targeted: you can still say the act with *always*. The mood is for when you mean a pull.

## Problem

English *I have a habit of…*, *I'm a nail-biter*, *I always reach for my phone* pack three jobs into one claim:

- the **act** repeats
- something **cues** it (a time, a place, a feeling, an unmet sake)
- the link is **who you are**, or is too automatic to choose against

Restrictors already cover how often (`hual` *always*, `hal` *only when*). Label scope already limits how far a label reaches (*lazy this time*). Neither makes you name the cue, so *I have a habit* still reads as a possession or a kind of person. The cigarette abstract *habit* (“repeated hard-to-break act”) is that shortcut: a person *has* a habit, and the seed is shame-adjacent (addictive draw).

The healthier frame is ordinary psychology of habit as **context-cued automaticity** (Wood & Neal 2007, *Psychological Review*; implementation intentions, Gollwitzer 1999). A habit is a link you can notice and interrupt, including a useful morning routine. It is not a vice and not a diagnosis.

## Feature criteria

| Test | This form |
|------|-----------|
| Helps with language goals | Empowerment (the pull is a link, not identity) and compassion (no “bad habit” default). Rationality: the cue is a claim you can check. |
| Common problem | Most people talk about habits; treating them as character is everyday FAE, not an edge case. |
| Easy to use | One adverb mood on `/h/`, cue in `/b/`, one ending. One paragraph plus two examples. |
| Avoids shame | Endings grade how bound the link is, not whether the act is good. Coffee at the kettle and a cigarette at stress use the same word. No socially correct default. |
| Reminders where needed | Optional. *Always* and *only when* stay. Reach for this mood when you mean cue-response. |

A recipe of restrictor plus because (`hal` + `thahum`) can say the same facts in two words. That is allowed ([design decisions](../meta/design-decisions.md): two ways are fine). The overlay earns its keep only as the **nudge**: you cannot use this mood without choosing a cue (or openly leaving it unstated), the way *can't* forces *right now / yet / never*.

## Proposed form

Closed overlay on the cigarette root (`uzebu`), same pattern as ATTEMPT on the test tube: ordinary `-m` on `/h/` is the mood; other role letters keep the content abstract.

| Piece | Job |
|-------|-----|
| `/h/` + cigarette + `-m` | this act is a cued response (HABIT) |
| `/b/` right after | the **cue** (a time, a place, a thing, a sake, a clause stand-in) |
| no `/b/` | cue unstated (weaker nudge; keep so the word is still usable) |
| ending | how bound the link is, settled-to-passing: **-l** the cue usually pulls the act; **-m** unstated; **-r** the cue is there and you can still choose |

Sketch (spellings follow the current five-letter root until an editor shortens it):

> `zuzozen vuzebul huzebum bluyamum.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-cigarette | h-HABIT | bl-tension
>
> "Uzozen smokes, as a habit cued by tension."

> `zuzozen vovewol huzebur bluzemol.`
>
> z-Uzozen | v-phone | h-HABIT.interruptible | bl-sleepy
>
> "Uzozen reaches for the phone when sleepy, and can still choose."

**Compare with:** `vuzebul hual` is *always smokes* (frequency, no cue). `vuzebul hanurul hal` is *smokes only at night* (when the claim counts, not that night pulls the hand). `guzebuthal` is *a habit this time* (label scope on the noun). FORMER + *always* is *used to* ([used to](../grammar/say-tense.md#used-to)).

`/h/` not `/th/`: the act is something others could watch, like ATTEMPT and the say words ([D-46](../meta/design-decisions.md)). Someone else's habit as a claim about their mind still needs a [holder](../grammar/evidentiality.md#holder) if you are guessing the cue you cannot see.

No `/w/` overlay in the first sitting. *Habitually angry* is restrictor `wual` plus label scope. Stacking a cue onto an adjective is a later cell if this one ships.

## Rejected here

- **Recipe only** (restrictor + because). Allowed as a second route; it does not force naming the cue.
- **Label scope on *habitual*.** Limits reach; still no cue.
- **Overlay on *sloth* / *laziness*.** Species-as-character, and it grades the person.
- **Good-habit / bad-habit vowels.** Shame; D-16-adjacent evaluation.
- **Types of resistance** (thoughts, behavior, attention) as extra endings. Dictionary TODO, not this overlay. Name the cue; the act’s role letter already says doing vs a `/th/` thought.
- **Requiring `/b/` always.** Would make “I know it’s cued, I cannot name what” unsayable, against easy-to-use.
